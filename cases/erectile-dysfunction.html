📋 Clinical Stem — Erectile Dysfunction
A 52-year-old man, embarrassed and reluctant, who books a "quick" appointment and only at the end mentions 6 months of erectile difficulty — with several untreated vascular risk factors
David Okonkwo, 52, comes in nominally about a repeat prescription, and as he is about to leave says, "while I'm here… things aren't working in the bedroom." On gentle questioning he describes 6 months of gradually worsening erectile difficulty, now affecting most attempts, with loss of his early-morning erections. He is a smoker, has untreated borderline-high blood pressure noted opportunistically last year, a strong family history of heart disease, a desk job and little exercise, and his waist is expanding. He drinks 25 units a week. His mood is "a bit flat" and it is straining his relationship. He hopes for "those blue tablets" and to keep the conversation brief.
This stem tests the ability to: create a safe, non-embarrassing space for a sensitive presentation raised as a "by the way"; recognise gradual-onset ED with loss of morning erections in a man with multiple vascular risk factors as ORGANIC and as a cardiovascular sentinel symptom that mandates full CV risk assessment (QRISK), not merely a PDE5i prescription; screen and act on mood; perform the relevant examination and investigations (BP, BMI/waist, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, morning testosterone); prescribe a PDE5 inhibitor safely (nitrate/nicorandil check); and address lifestyle and the underlying drivers. The SCA challenge is to honour his wish for "the blue tablets" and a brief consultation while doing the cardiovascular and psychological work the symptom demands.
Scenario A — Organic ED + vascular risk (this stem) Gradual onset, lost morning erections, vascular risk factors. PDE5i + full CV risk assessment (QRISK), treat risk factors, lifestyle, screen mood. The symptom is a window onto cardiovascular health.
Scenario B — Psychogenic ED Younger man, sudden onset, preserved morning/spontaneous erections, situational (fine with masturbation, not with partner), performance anxiety/relationship stress. PDE5i can help, but address psychological factors ± psychosexual therapy.
Scenario C — Hypogonadism Low libido, fatigue, reduced morning erections, ± small testes. Morning total testosterone (repeat low + LH/FSH/prolactin/SHBG); exclude pituitary cause; consider testosterone replacement under guidance; PDE5i response may be blunted if very low.
Scenario D — Drug-induced ED Onset after starting a thiazide, beta-blocker, SSRI, finasteride or antipsychotic. Review and, where possible, switch the offending drug; don't just add a PDE5i over the top.
Scenario E — Red-flag urology Priapism >4h (emergency), Peyronie's (painful curvature), penile/testicular lump, or ED with prominent LUTS + abnormal prostate → respective urological/prostate pathways.
Key variables to adapt for Onset (gradual organic vs sudden psychogenic); morning erections (lost = organic; preserved = psychogenic); vascular risk factors and QRISK; medication review (ED-causing drugs); libido/fatigue → testosterone; mood/relationship distress; nitrate/nicorandil use (PDE5i contraindication); age; LUTS; priapism/Peyronie's red flags; the "by the way" presentation and embarrassment.

"Has this been worrying you — about your health, or about the relationship?"
His concerns may be about masculinity, the relationship, ageing, or a fear of serious illness. Naming them — and the strain on his relationship and mood — makes him feel understood and opens the door to addressing the psychological dimension, not just the physiology.
🎯 Expectations
"You mentioned the tablets — let me explain how they work and what else will help them work better."
He expects a quick prescription. Naming this lets you deliver it (most men can have a PDE5i) while framing lifestyle change, risk-factor treatment and mood as the things that make the tablets work better and protect his health — a deal he can buy into.
1D — Psychosocial context
🫂 The "by the way" symptom — embarrassment, masculinity and missed opportunity

Men often raise ED as an afterthought, at the door, because of embarrassment, stigma and fear. How the clinician responds in that moment determines whether the consultation succeeds. Brushing it off, or simply printing a sildenafil prescription to end an awkward moment, both miss the point: ED is treatable AND it is a flag for cardiovascular disease and sometimes depression. The work is to normalise the topic warmly, give the treatment he came for, and use the open door to assess his heart and his mood — turning an embarrassing moment into a genuinely valuable consultation.

🧍 Masculinity & shame

ED can feel like a threat to identity. A calm, normalising, blame-free manner is therapeutic in itself and makes the rest of the assessment possible.

"This is one of the commonest things I see, and it's very treatable — there's absolutely nothing to be embarrassed about. I'm glad you brought it up."
❤️ The cardiovascular link

Reframing ED as a useful early warning gives a positive reason to engage with risk-factor work and lifestyle change.

"The same blood vessels that supply the penis supply the heart — so this can actually be an early heads-up. Sorting your blood pressure, the smoking and your weight will help the erections and protect your heart at the same time."
💞 Relationship strain

ED affects partners and relationships; acknowledging this — and offering to involve the partner or psychosexual support — addresses a key driver.

"This kind of thing can put a strain on a relationship — how are things between you? Sometimes it helps to involve your partner, and there's specialist support if that would be useful."
🧠 Mood

Low mood both causes and results from ED; screening and treating it (mindful of SSRI sexual effects) is part of the plan.

"You mentioned feeling a bit flat — that often goes hand in hand with this. Can I ask a bit more about your mood, because treating that can really help too?"

7A — Address the patient's expectation first: validate → explain → negotiate
🤝
David wants "the blue tablets" and a short consultation — give him the tablet, and use the open door for his heart
1
Validate — name the request and say yes to it

David's expectation is reasonable: a PDE5 inhibitor is first-line for almost all ED, and he is very likely to be able to have one today. Saying so early takes the anxiety out of the consultation. If he senses the tablet is being held back until he "earns" it with blood tests, he may disengage and simply buy sildenafil from a pharmacy or, worse, an unregulated website — and the cardiovascular opportunity is lost.

"You were hoping for the tablets — and the good news is that, as long as you're not on certain heart medicines, I can prescribe them today. Thank you for bringing it up; it's one of the commonest things men see us about."
2
Explain — why the symptom matters beyond the bedroom

The penile arteries are small, so they show the effects of furred, stiffened blood vessels earlier than the coronary arteries. Gradual ED with loss of morning erections in a 52-year-old smoker with raised blood pressure and a family history of early heart attacks is a cardiovascular warning. QRISK3 includes erectile dysfunction as a risk variable for exactly this reason (NICE NG238 recommends QRISK3 for cardiovascular risk assessment). Framed as good news — an early warning — this becomes motivating rather than frightening.

"The blood vessels that supply the penis are narrower than the ones that supply the heart, so they often show changes first. That makes this a useful early heads-up. With your dad and brother's history, I'd really like to use it to check and protect your heart — the same things that help your heart will help the erections too."
3
Negotiate — a plan he can say yes to

Offer a package, not a lecture: a prescription today with a nitrate check and proper instructions; blood pressure and weight measured now; bloods (HbA1c, lipids, morning testosterone) booked; a QRISK3 calculation once results are back; and one lifestyle target he chooses. Keep it brief if he wants brief — the follow-up appointment is where the rest of the work happens.

"So how about this: I'll give you the tablets today and show you how to get the best out of them. I'll check your blood pressure now and book some blood tests, and when you come back in a few weeks we'll see how the tablets are working and go through your heart-risk score together. Does that sound fair?"
Key principle: The tablet is not the reward for doing the cardiovascular work — it is the bridge to it. Prescribing today (when safe) earns the trust that brings David back for the QRISK3, blood-pressure and smoking conversations. The only reasons to withhold a PDE5 inhibitor today are nitrate/nicorandil (or riociguat) use, unstable cardiac symptoms, or a condition where sexual activity itself is inadvisable.

7C — Non-medication management: mechanism + evidence + tailored advice
In organic ED, lifestyle change is not preamble to the prescription — it treats the endothelial disease the tablet only bypasses. For David, stopping smoking, cutting alcohol, losing central weight and becoming active each lower his cardiovascular risk and can improve erectile function. Psychosexual and relationship support addresses the anxiety and strain that keep even organic ED going.
🚭
Stop Smoking
Target: quit date set; NHS stop smoking support
Mechanism

Smoking damages the endothelium, reduces nitric oxide availability and accelerates atherosclerosis in the penile and coronary arteries alike. Nitric oxide is the signal that PDE5 inhibitors amplify — so smoking directly blunts the tablet's effect.

Practical

Very brief advice (ask, advise, act), then referral to local NHS stop smoking support: behavioural support plus varenicline, combination nicotine replacement or a nicotine-containing vape gives the best quit rates. For David, link it to what he cares about: "stopping is one of the best things you can do for your erections and your heart."

Stopping smoking is the single biggest change in his cardiovascular risk that he controls
🏃
Aerobic Activity
Target: 150 min/week moderate activity, built up gradually
Mechanism

Regular aerobic exercise improves endothelial function, lowers blood pressure, improves insulin sensitivity and reduces visceral fat. Observational studies consistently link higher activity with lower ED risk, and trials show improvement in erectile function scores with supervised aerobic training.

Practical

David has a desk job and does little. Start with brisk walking — a lunchtime walk, getting off the bus a stop early — and build up to the UK CMO recommendation of 150 minutes a week of moderate activity plus two strength sessions. If he has any exertional chest pain, assess this before advising increased exertion.

Improves erections, BP and cardiovascular risk together
⚖️
Weight & Waist
Target: 5–10% weight loss; waist reducing
Mechanism

Central obesity drives insulin resistance, hypertension and dyslipidaemia, and lowers circulating testosterone (lower SHBG and aromatisation of testosterone to oestradiol in adipose tissue). Weight loss improves all of these.

Practical

Record BMI and waist today. Offer the NHS Digital Weight Management Programme or local tier 2 service if eligible; a Mediterranean-style diet supports both weight and cardiovascular risk. In Esposito's RCT of obese men with ED, the intensive weight-loss and activity programme restored normal erectile function in about a third.

7D — Prescribing guide: PDE5 inhibitor ladder (BSSM 2018 · BNF/SmPC · Drug Tariff)
A PDE5 inhibitor is first-line for ED of any cause unless contraindicated (BSSM ED guideline, Hackett et al., J Sex Med 2018). Choice between agents is mainly about timing and cost: sildenafil is short-acting and cheapest; tadalafil lasts longer and can be taken once daily. Always complete the safety check first: nitrates, nicorandil, riociguat, recent cardiovascular events, and alpha-blockers. Doses: per BNF.
Step 1 — Sildenafil (first choice for most)

Generic sildenafil, on demand — dose per BNF

  • Take about an hour before sex; onset is delayed by a heavy or fatty meal
  • Needs sexual stimulation — it is not an aphrodisiac
  • No more than one dose in 24 hours
  • Titrate within the licensed range before judging failure; allow several attempts at the maximum tolerated dose
NHS: generic sildenafil can be prescribed for any man with ED — no SLS endorsement needed. Sildenafil 50 mg is also a pharmacy (P) medicine (Viagra Connect, MHRA reclassification 2017) for men 18+.
Step 2 — Tadalafil or vardenafil (alternative)

Generic tadalafil on demand or once daily; generic vardenafil — doses per BNF

  • Tadalafil: long duration of action (up to ~36 hours) — suits men who want spontaneity; less affected by food
  • Once-daily low-dose tadalafil suits frequent sexual activity; it is also licensed for LUTS due to BPH
  • Vardenafil: similar to sildenafil in timing; caution with QT-prolonging drugs
  • If one PDE5 inhibitor fails despite correct use, trying another is reasonable before referral
From 1 October 2025, generically written NHS prescriptions for tadalafil and vardenafil no longer need the SLS endorsement (Drug Tariff change). Branded Viagra®, Cialis® and Levitra® remain restricted to Part XVIIIB (Schedule 2) criteria.
Step 3 — Specialist second-line options

Urology / specialist ED service

  • Alprostadil — intracavernosal injection, intraurethral pellet or topical cream (usually specialist-initiated; injection technique must be taught)
  • Vacuum erection device — useful where drugs are contraindicated or ineffective
  • Penile prosthesis — for refractory ED after full assessment
  • Psychosexual therapy alongside any of these
Before calling a PDE5 inhibitor a failure, check technique, dose, timing with food, stimulation, testosterone status and number of attempts — most "failures" are fixable.
⛔ The safety check before any PDE5 inhibitor
  • Contraindicated: any nitrate (GTN spray, isosorbide mononitrate/dinitrate) or nicorandil — profound, potentially fatal hypotension. Also recreational amyl nitrite ("poppers"). Ask directly; check the repeat list
  • Contraindicated: riociguat (soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator for pulmonary hypertension); previous NAION (loss of vision from non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy)
  • Avoid / seek cardiology advice: recent MI or stroke, unstable angina, hypotension, severe heart failure, or any patient in whom sexual activity is inadvisable
  • Caution: alpha-blockers (doxazosin, tamsulosin) — symptomatic postural hypotension; stable on the alpha-blocker first, start at a low PDE5i dose and separate timing per BNF/SPC
  • Caution: anatomical penile deformity (Peyronie's), conditions predisposing to priapism (sickle cell disease, myeloma, leukaemia), bleeding disorders/active peptic ulcer
  • Interactions: strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g. some HIV protease inhibitors, clarithromycin, ketoconazole) raise PDE5i levels — check BNF
NHS prescribing rules (Drug Tariff Part XVIIIB — "SLS")
  • Generic sildenafil — any man with ED on the NHS, no endorsement (since 2014)
  • Generic tadalafil and vardenafil — no SLS endorsement needed since 1 October 2025
  • Branded products and other listed ED treatments — NHS only for men meeting Schedule 2 criteria (e.g. diabetes, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, poliomyelitis, prostate cancer, prostatectomy or radical pelvic surgery, severe pelvic injury, single-gene neurological disease, spina bifida, spinal cord injury, renal failure treated by dialysis or transplant), or in specialist services for men with severe distress; prescription endorsed "SLS"
  • Check local ICB formulary — some advise a monthly quantity; OTC purchase is an option some men prefer for privacy
Verify current wording in the Drug Tariff Part XVIIIB before endorsing — the list changes.

7G — Psychosocial impact: identity, relationship, work and driving
🫂
Erectile dysfunction — a private symptom with public consequences for identity, relationship and health

ED rarely affects only erections. It touches a man's sense of himself, his relationship, his mood and sometimes his work. David minimised it by raising it at the door; the consultation's job is to show him it matters and that help is straightforward.

🧍
Identity & Masculinity

Many men experience ED as a loss of masculinity or as a sign of ageing. Shame delays presentation, often for years, and drives men towards unregulated online products of uncertain content. A matter-of-fact, normalising manner is itself therapeutic.

"Lots of men your age have this, and it says nothing about you as a man — it's usually about blood flow, and it's very treatable."
💞
Relationship & Partner

Partners may misread ED as loss of attraction or infidelity; avoidance of intimacy follows and the strain feeds performance anxiety. Involving the partner, where the man wishes, often improves outcome and gives the partner a chance to ask questions.

"Sometimes partners worry it's about them. Would it help for your wife to come to the next appointment, so she can hear it's a physical problem and what we're doing about it?"
🧠
Mood & Self-Worth

ED and depression are linked in both directions, and men are less likely to report low mood. David's "fed up" is worth a PHQ-9 and a gentle question about hopelessness. If an antidepressant is needed, discuss sexual side effects openly so he doesn't stop it silently.

"You said you've been feeling fed up. How low has it got? Have there been times you've felt hopeless or that life isn't worth it?"
💼
Work & Stress

A desk job with long hours encourages inactivity, snacking and drinking to unwind — all drivers here. Work stress also feeds psychogenic components. Practical changes (walking meetings, lunchtime walks, fewer evening drinks) fit into a working week.

"What's a realistic change you could make during the working day — maybe a walk at lunchtime to start with?"
🚗
Driving

ED itself has no DVLA implications. Relevant only indirectly: warn that PDE5 inhibitors can cause dizziness or visual disturbance — don't drive if affected. If the cardiovascular assessment finds angina, DVLA car-licence rules for angina apply (stop driving while symptomatic at rest or at the wheel); if any condition affecting fitness to drive is found, advise per DVLA guidance.

"The tablets can occasionally cause dizziness or blurred vision — if that happens, don't drive until it's settled."
💷
Cost, Privacy & Online Buying

Some men prefer to buy sildenafil privately to avoid a GP record or embarrassment. Pharmacy supply is legitimate, but unregulated online "herbal" products may contain undeclared PDE5 inhibitors — dangerous for anyone on nitrates. Generic sildenafil on NHS prescription is inexpensive and comes with the cardiovascular check.

"You can get it from a pharmacy if you'd prefer, but please avoid internet products — some contain hidden ingredients that are dangerous with heart tablets."

⚠ Scenario-specific phrases — use these verbatim

🔴 Emergency — priapism or chest pain
"If you ever get an erection that lasts more than four hours, or is painful and won't go down, that's an emergency — go straight to A&E, or call 999; don't wait to see if it settles. 👁 Sudden loss of vision or hearing
"Very rarely, these tablets have been linked to sudden loss of sight in one eye, or sudden hearing loss. If that happens, stop taking them and get seen urgently the same day — eye casualty or A&E."

🔍 1 — Organic vs psychogenic
Organic

Gradual onset, lost morning erections, vascular risk factors. = CV sentinel → QRISK, treat BP/lipids/diabetes, lifestyle. Check morning testosterone if low libido/fatigue.

Psychogenic

Sudden, situational, preserved morning erections, performance anxiety/relationship. PDE5i can help; address psychological factors ± psychosexual therapy.

💊 2 — Treat

PDE5 inhibitor first-line (sildenafil/tadalafil) — counsel on use; try several times at adequate dose. ⛔ Absolute contraindication: nitrates & nicorandil. Treat drivers: BP, lipids, diabetes, switch ED-causing drugs. Lifestyle: stop smoking, reduce alcohol, weight, activity. Mood/relationship: screen and treat. If PDE5i fails: optimise + urology (intracavernosal, vacuum, implant). Confirmed hypogonadism → testosterone (specialist). Priapism >4h = emergency.
