Immunisation schedule — what is due, at what age, in 2026

The England routine schedule after the July 2025 and January 2026 changes (MenB and PCV timing, no Hib/MenC, the new 18-month visit and MMRV), the selective and pregnancy programmes, adult eligibility for shingles, RSV, pneumococcal, flu and COVID-19, and how to catch up someone with an uncertain history.

Two big changes since 2025. From 1 July 2025: the second MenB dose moved from 16 to 12 weeks, the first PCV dose moved from 12 to 16 weeks, and the Hib/MenC (Menitorix) booster at one year was withdrawn. From 1 January 2026: a new 18-month appointment (4th dose of the 6-in-1 vaccine), chickenpox (varicella) added as MMRV, and the second MR-containing dose brought forward from 3 years 4 months to 18 months for younger children. This page shows the England schedule; Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are very similar but check local letters.

Selective programmes and pregnancy

Pregnancy

Pertussis

dTaP/IPV in every pregnancy from 16 weeks; ideally from about 20 weeks (after the anomaly scan) to 32 weeks. Can still be given later, including up to birth, but gives less protection to the baby.

Pregnancy

RSV (Abrysvo)

One dose from 28 weeks of every pregnancy; can be given up to birth. Offer at the 28-week contact, often alongside pertussis.

Pregnancy · seasonal

Flu and COVID-19

Inactivated flu vaccine at any stage of pregnancy during the flu season (from 1 September 2026). Pregnancy alone is not a COVID-19 eligibility group in autumn 2026 — offer only if immunosuppressed.

Infants at risk

BCG

Infants at increased risk of TB (for example a parent or grandparent born in a country with high TB incidence) — usually by 28 days of age, once the newborn SCID screening result is known where that screening applies.

Infants at risk

Hepatitis B

Babies of mothers with hepatitis B: monovalent dose at birth (plus immunoglobulin if high risk) and at 4 weeks, the routine 6-in-1 doses, then a monovalent dose and a blood test for HBsAg at 1 year.

Children at clinical risk

Flu and others

Flu from 6 months for children in clinical risk groups. Asplenia, complement disorders and severe immunosuppression need extra pneumococcal, meningococcal and Hib doses — see Green Book chapter 7.

Catch-up and uncertain status

  • Unless there is reliable written or verbal evidence, assume the person is unimmunised and vaccinate in line with the UK schedule for their age. Extra doses are not harmful.
  • Never restart an interrupted course — carry on where it stopped, keeping minimum intervals (usually 4 weeks between primary doses).
  • Use the UKHSA algorithm for individuals with uncertain or incomplete immunisation status (updated September 2026) to build an age-appropriate catch-up plan. From age 10, use Td/IPV rather than the 6-in-1 or 4-in-1 vaccines.
  • Arrivals from abroad: compare their record with the UK schedule (the WHO publishes each country's schedule), offer MMR, polio-containing and MenACWY doses as needed, and consider BCG, TB screening, hepatitis B testing and HIV testing on risk.
  • Contraindications are few: confirmed anaphylaxis to a previous dose or component; live vaccines (MMRV, MMR, LAIV, rotavirus, BCG, yellow fever) are contraindicated in significant immunosuppression and, for adults, in pregnancy. See Green Book chapter 6.
