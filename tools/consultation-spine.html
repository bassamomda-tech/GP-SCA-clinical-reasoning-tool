📘 Using the spine

Work through the nine parts in order. Red flags first, then the history, examination and investigations the presentation needs, then explain, agree a plan and safety-net. Check the plan against the protocol or pathway for the diagnosis — the spine does not choose treatment.

Model consultation write-ups

For exam practice only. Each is a model write-up of a fictional SCA case for one diagnosis, in the same nine parts. The [bracketed] parts are the details a candidate would take from the case. Read it as a worked example — it is not a record for a real patient, and doses must be checked in the current BNF.

Education and reference only — you remain the clinician of record. The spine is a generic teaching structure for each presentation, drawn from UK primary-care guidance; it does not take patient details, and does not write, code or decide anything for a particular patient. The model write-ups are exam practice with fictional cases. Always check against the current NICE guidance, the BNF and your own assessment of the patient in front of you.
