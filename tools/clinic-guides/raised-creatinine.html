Raised creatinine or falling eGFR CLINIC GUIDE

A raised serum creatinine, or a low or falling eGFR, in adults 18+. Not for children, pregnancy, or people on dialysis or with a kidney transplant (renal team).

1Is it real — and is it new?NG148 · NG203

  • Find the baseline: a recent stable pre-illness creatinine, or a representative value from the previous 7–365 days. Old results are the most useful test.
  • Stage on creatinine, not eGFR: eGFR assumes a steady state and lags in AKI.
  • New low eGFR, well, no baseline: repeat U&E within 2 weeks to exclude AKI; CKD only if it persists for >3 months.
  • Falsely raised: meat meal before the test, creatine supplements, high muscle mass, heavy exercise; trimethoprim or cimetidine block secretion (creatinine rises, GFR does not fall).
  • Falsely reassuring: low muscle mass in frail, amputee or wasted patients. Consider cystatin C when muscle mass makes eGFR unreliable (NG203).
  • Step change on known CKD = AKI on CKD until proven otherwise.

5Work-up checklistNG148 · NG203 · NG12

  • U&E + K⁺; previous results
  • Urine dipstick before any catheter
  • Urine ACR
  • FBC, CRP, bicarbonate
  • Calcium, LFTs, glucose
  • ECG if K⁺ ≥6.0
  • Myeloma screen if 60+ with clues
  • CK if long lie
  • Renal USS (indications below)
  • KFRE in CKD G3–G5

USS: AKI with possible obstruction or no clear cause within 24 h (6 h if pyonephrosis). CKD: accelerated progression, haematuria, obstructive symptoms, family history of polycystic kidneys (over 20), eGFR <30. Never use the KFRE during AKI.

MetformineGFR 45–59: maximum 2 g a day. 30–44: maximum 1 g a day, review. <30: stop. Hold in AKI or dehydration.

10Follow-up and monitoring checklistNG148 · NG203

  • AKI at home: U&E 24–48 h, then until back to baseline
  • New low eGFR: repeat within 2 weeks, then confirm at 3 months
  • Held drugs restarted with a U&E; renoprotective drugs not left off
  • After AKI: U&E, ACR and BP at about 3 months; watch for CKD for at least 3 years
  • ACEi/ARB start or increase: U&E and K⁺ at 1–2 weeks
  • CKD: eGFR and ACR at least yearly, more often by G and A category
  • KFRE in G3–G5; plot the eGFR trend, not the snapshot
  • Code the AKI or CKD; written sick-day advice; NSAID warning on the record
  • Chase USS, myeloma screen, ACR — named clinician responsible
  • Annual: BP to target, statin, NSAID and dose review, vaccinations

12Special groupsNG203 · NG148 · SPC

  • Heart failure: review diuretics with care — stopping can cause overload; restart the ACEi/ARB early.
  • Frail or low muscle mass: a "normal" creatinine may hide poor function; consider cystatin C.
  • Could become pregnant: ACE inhibitors and ARBs are teratogenic — discuss contraception and plans before starting.
  • Lithium: AKI → hold and check an urgent level; psychiatry plan before restarting.
  • Diabetes: hold metformin and the SGLT2i when unwell; restart when eating and drinking.

13Pitfalls and never-eventsNG148 · NG203

  • Never label CKD on one eGFR, or use the KFRE during an AKI.
  • Never stop an ACEi/ARB for the expected rise (<30% creatinine).
  • Never give fluid to an overloaded patient.
  • Never combine an ACEi with an ARB.
  • A held ACEi/ARB never restarted is a common, silent harm.
  • An AKI alert is acted on the same day, not filed.

14Safety-net — say it, then document itNG148

Same dayPassing much less urine; cannot keep fluids down; very drowsy; new swelling or breathlessness

15Say it like thisplain English

"Your kidney blood test has changed. Often this is from being dehydrated or from a medicine, and it usually recovers. Stop the ibuprofen and pause [tablets] until we recheck on [date]; we'll tell you when to restart them."
