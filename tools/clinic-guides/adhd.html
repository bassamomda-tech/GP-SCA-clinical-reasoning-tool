ADHD in children and adults CLINIC GUIDE

The primary care role: recognise, refer well, support, then shared-care prescribing and monitoring. Children 5+, young people and adults (under-5s: parent training). Diagnosis and starting drugs are specialist tasks.

Page 1 · Recognise, refer and support suspected ADHD → good referral → support starts now

!Act todayNG87 · SPC

  • On a stimulant or atomoxetine: syncope, exertional chest pain, palpitations or marked breathlessness → same-day assessment; ongoing chest pain or collapse → 999.
  • Suicidal thoughts or self-harm (atomoxetine warning; also in any adolescent) → same-day assessment and crisis/CAMHS route; immediate danger → 999. Ask directly; do not use risk tools or scales to predict suicide (NG225).
  • Jaundice, dark urine, abdominal pain or pruritus on atomoxetine → stop it and arrange same-day assessment.
  • Brief staring spells with abrupt return → possible absence seizures: urgent paediatric epilepsy referral (NG217), not an ADHD referral. Sudden onset or regression → not ADHD: safeguarding and urgent paediatric or neurological assessment.

1Is it ADHD? Confirm the patternNG87 §1.3 · DSM-5

  • DSM-5: ≥6 of 9 inattentive and/or ≥6 of 9 hyperactive-impulsive symptoms (5 if aged 17+), for ≥6 months, several present before age 12.
  • Two or more settings (home, school, work, relationships, social) with at least moderate impairment. One setting only → look for the setting-specific cause.
  • Diagnosis is specialist: full clinical, psychosocial, developmental and psychiatric assessment, never from a rating scale alone.
  • Girls: inattentive, quiet, disorganised, underachieving; masking and anxiety hide it. Adults: often present after their child's diagnosis; restlessness, impairment at work, money, driving.

3Cardiology advice before any drugNG87 §1.7

Seek cardiology advice before medication if there is:

  • congenital heart disease or previous cardiac surgery;
  • sudden death in a first-degree relative under 40 suggesting cardiac disease;
  • exertional breathlessness, exertional or fright/noise-induced syncope, or rapid regular palpitations;
  • cardiac chest pain, signs of heart failure, a murmur, or hypertension.

History and examination normal → no routine ECG is needed before stimulants, atomoxetine or guanfacine.

4Before referral: the referral packdo not delay for any one item

  • Parent/carer and school reports or scales where feasible (Conners, SNAP-IV, SDQ are adjuncts, not tests); occupational account for adults
  • Onset and duration, birth and developmental history, school reports, family history of ADHD
  • Comorbidity: autism, tics, anxiety, low mood, substance use, sleep
  • Psychosocial context, adverse experiences, any safeguarding concern
  • Height and weight on centile charts (the pre-treatment baseline)
  • BP and pulse against the normal range for age; cardiovascular history and examination
  • Hearing, vision; neurological examination for tics
  • Impairment in concrete terms: what it stops them doing

Refer to: children → locally commissioned specialist ADHD service (community paediatrics or CAMHS); adults → specialist adult ADHD service. Do not diagnose or start medication in primary care.

Page 2 · Shared care and review agreement → drug sequence → safe supply → monitor → contact the specialist

7Before you take on shared careNG87 · NHS England 2018

  • A written shared-care agreement is in place, and you accept it
  • Specialist titration is complete; the drug, brand, formulation and dose are stated
  • Who monitors what, and how often, is written down
  • The specialist stays available for advice and dose changes
  • Baseline height, weight, BP and pulse are recorded

Shared care is by agreement. If it is not agreed, the specialist continues to prescribe. Never start or change a dose without specialist instruction.

8The NG87 drug sequence (specialist-initiated)doses are titrated by the specialist; check the SPC and local formulary

Children 5+ · firstMethylphenidate (MR or IR).
Children · secondLisdexamfetamine after a 6-week trial of methylphenidate at an adequate dose without enough benefit. Dexamfetamine if lisdexamfetamine works but its long effect is not tolerated.
Adults · firstLisdexamfetamine or methylphenidate. Next: atomoxetine after separate 6-week trials of both, or intolerance.
Non-stimulantsChildren: atomoxetine or guanfacine if both stimulants fail or are not tolerated. Adults: no guanfacine without tertiary service advice.

9Controlled-drug and supply rulesMisuse of Drugs Regs · MHRA DSU Sep 2022

  • Methylphenidate, lisdexamfetamine, dexamfetamine = Schedule 2: prescription valid 28 days; quantity normally ≤30 days; not on repeat dispensing.
  • Prescribe MR methylphenidate by brand: release profiles differ, so switching can change symptom control (MHRA).
  • Supply problems: check the current national supply position; switch only with specialist advice.
  • Safe storage; ask about diversion, especially with substance use in the household. Atomoxetine and guanfacine are not controlled drugs.
  • Misuse or diversion risk: the specialist may prefer lisdexamfetamine or a non-stimulant; never increase supply on request.

11Review checklistevery shared-care review

  • BP and pulse vs normal range for age: before and after each dose change, and every 6 months
  • Height every 6 months in children and young people
  • Weight: aged 10 and under every 3 months; over 10, at 3 and 6 months after starting, then 6-monthly; adults 6-monthly
  • Plot height and weight on growth charts; faltering → specialist
  • Benefit and impairment at home, school or work; adherence
  • Appetite, sleep, mood, tics, cardiovascular symptoms
  • Atomoxetine: suicidal thoughts, liver symptoms, sexual dysfunction
  • Misuse, diversion, safe storage; driving
  • Yearly: medication reviewed by a clinician with ADHD expertise, including whether it is still needed
  • Teenagers: plan transition to adult services early (a common point of drop-out)

12Contact the specialisturgency tiers

  • Same day: cardiac symptoms, suicidal thoughts, suspected liver injury, new seizures.
  • Within days: sustained tachycardia, arrhythmia or raised BP; growth faltering or marked weight loss; suspected misuse or diversion.
  • Routine: poor response, rebound, tics, wish to stop, pregnancy plans, transition.

Height significantly affected → specialist may plan a break over school holidays for catch-up growth (NG87).

13Never-eventsNG87 · MHRA · SPC

  • Never: diagnose ADHD or start medication in primary care · change a dose without the specialist · prescribe a Schedule 2 stimulant without a current shared-care agreement · let a stimulant run without BP, pulse and growth checks · stop guanfacine abruptly · combine with an MAOI (or within 14 days).
  • Driving: adults must tell the DVLA if ADHD or its treatment affects safe driving; advise and record it.
  • Comorbid anxiety, tics or substance misuse: the specialist may titrate more slowly and monitor more closely (NG87).
