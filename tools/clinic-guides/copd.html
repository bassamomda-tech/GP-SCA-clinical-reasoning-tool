Page 1 · Diagnose and start risk factor + symptoms → spirometry → stop smoking, rehab, first inhaler

!Act todayNG115 · NG114 · NG12

  • 999: breathless at rest with confusion or drowsiness, cyanosis, exhaustion, or SpO₂ well below their usual baseline. Known CO₂ retainer or at risk → controlled oxygen, target 88–92%; say the target to the crew.
  • Sudden pleuritic pain and breathlessness → think pneumothorax or PE. New ankle oedema, raised JVP → cor pulmonale or heart failure: same-day assessment.
  • Exacerbation at home: more bronchodilator; prednisolone 30 mg daily for 5 days if breathlessness interferes with daily activities; antibiotic only on the NG114 judgement — amoxicillin 500 mg three times a day, doxycycline 200 mg then 100 mg daily, or clarithromycin 500 mg twice a day, 5 days in total.
  • Aged 40+ with unexplained haemoptysis → suspected cancer pathway (NG12). Aged 40+ with 2 or more unexplained cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, weight loss or appetite loss (1 or more if ever smoked) → chest X-ray within 2 weeks.

1Confirm the diagnosisNG115

Suspect: over 35, a risk factor (usually smoking) and exertional breathlessness, chronic cough, regular sputum, frequent winter "bronchitis" or wheeze.

Confirm: post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.7 on quality-assured spirometry. A label without spirometry needs it now.

FEV₁ is not the whole severity: weigh breathlessness (MRC), exacerbations, BMI, exercise capacity and comorbidity. GOLD groups and CAT bands are international context only, not NICE rules.

2Work-up at diagnosisNG115

  • Chest X-ray
  • FBC (anaemia, polycythaemia, eosinophils)
  • BMI
  • MRC dyspnoea grade
  • SpO₂ when stable
  • Smoking history (pack-years)

Selectively: alpha-1 antitrypsin if early onset, little smoking or family history · ECG, echo or natriuretic peptide if cor pulmonale or heart failure suspected · sputum culture if persistently purulent · CT if other lung disease suspected.

3Asthmatic features?NG115 · decides the ICS question

Asthmatic or steroid-responsive features = any of:

  • previous secure diagnosis of asthma or atopy;
  • a higher blood eosinophil count (NICE sets no cut-off);
  • FEV₁ varying by at least 400 ml over time;
  • diurnal PEF variation of at least 20%.

None of these → ICS is not part of the first dual step. COPD with asthma → keep an ICS-containing regimen.

4The two treatments that change the courseNG115

  • Stop smoking: ask and offer support at every contact — behavioural support plus medicine (varenicline, NRT; e-cigarettes as a quit aid). The most effective way to slow decline.
  • Pulmonary rehabilitation: offer to everyone functionally disabled by COPD — usually MRC grade 3 and above — and after a hospital admission for an exacerbation. Not suitable if unable to walk, unstable angina or recent MI.

8Targets — what good looks likeNG115

  • Not smoking (or actively quitting).
  • Rehabilitation offered and completed if MRC 3+.
  • Fewest possible exacerbations; none needing admission.
  • Correct technique on every device; each drug earning its place.
  • A written plan the person can explain back.

9Before every step-upNG115

  • Smoking — still smoking? Everything else is secondary
  • Technique and adherence — watched; device suits their breath
  • Rehab — offered, attended, completed?
  • Second diagnosis — heart failure, anaemia, lung cancer, bronchiectasis, anxiety
  • Asthmatic features re-assessed (box 3)

10Annual review checklistyearly · stage 4 twice a year

  • Smoking status and motivation to quit
  • MRC grade and symptom control
  • Exacerbations, steroid and antibiotic courses, admissions
  • Inhaler technique observed; effect of each drug
  • FEV₁ and FVC; BMI
  • SpO₂ when stable (≤92% → oxygen assessment)
  • Need for pulmonary rehabilitation
  • Complications: cor pulmonale, anxiety, depression
  • Vaccines (box 5); action plan and rescue pack
  • Advance care planning when disease is advanced

11The inhaler ladderNG115 · GOLD eosinophil cut-offs are international, not NICE

Step 1 · relieverSABA or SAMA as needed. Stop a SAMA when a LAMA starts.
Step 2 · dualNo asthmatic features → LABA + LAMA. Asthmatic features → LABA + ICS.
Step 3 · tripleOn LABA + LAMA: consider triple after 1 severe (admitted) or 2 moderate exacerbations in a year, or a 3-month trial for symptoms harming quality of life — back to dual if no benefit. On LABA + ICS: offer triple for either.
Step 4 · specialistStill exacerbating on optimised triple, with smoking and rehab addressed → respiratory review: azithromycin prophylaxis, roflumilast, or a biologic within its NICE TA (e.g. mepolizumab, TA1166).

12Rescue packsNG115

Oral steroid + antibiotic to keep at home if the person:

  • has had an exacerbation within the last year and remains at risk;
  • understands when and how to take them, and the risks;
  • knows to tell the practice each time and ask for a replacement.

3 or more steroid and/or antibiotic courses in a year → find out why. Review after each use.

14Oxygen — who to assessNG115

Assess when stable if: FEV₁ <30% · cyanosis · polycythaemia · peripheral oedema · raised JVP · SpO₂ ≤92% on air (consider if FEV₁ 30–49%).

LTOT criteria (non-smokers; two ABGs at least 3 weeks apart, stable, on optimal treatment): PaO₂ <7.3 kPa, or 7.3–8 kPa with polycythaemia, peripheral oedema or pulmonary hypertension. Used for at least 15 hours a day.

No LTOT for people who keep smoking despite cessation support (fire risk); assess home fire risk for all. Oxygen does not help breathlessness without hypoxaemia.

15When to referNG115

  • Diagnostic doubt; onset under 40 or family history (alpha-1 antitrypsin).
  • Rapid decline in FEV₁; haemoptysis; suspected cor pulmonale.
  • Frequent exacerbations despite optimised triple therapy.
  • Oxygen assessment; rehabilitation; lung volume reduction assessment.
  • Palliative care when breathlessness and admissions dominate.

16Special groups and never-eventsNG115 · BTS emergency oxygen

  • COPD with asthma: treat the asthma too — keep an ICS-containing regimen; do not withdraw ICS because the obstruction is fixed.
  • Heart failure or ischaemic heart disease: a cardioselective beta-blocker is not contraindicated by COPD.
  • Advanced disease: plan ahead while well — preferred place of care, ceiling of treatment; opioids for refractory breathlessness, with palliative care input.
  • Never: label COPD without post-bronchodilator spirometry · give uncontrolled high-flow oxygen to someone at risk of hypercapnia (target 88–92%) · add ICS without a recorded reason · prescribe a SAMA alongside a LAMA · assess for oxygen during or just after an exacerbation · prescribe 14-day steroid courses by habit.
