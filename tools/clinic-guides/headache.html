Giant cell arteritis (50+, new headache, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness): prednisolone 40–60 mg now, before tests. Raised ICP (papilloedema; worse lying down, coughing or straining; vomiting) · new headache in pregnancy or after birth (maternity) · immunosuppression or HIV · red painful eye with haloes (eye unit now).		 Progressive, sub-acute loss of central neurological function (for example personality or cognitive change, progressive weakness) → consider urgent direct-access MRI brain (CT if MRI contraindicated), done within 2 weeks.
Headache alone is not an NG12 criterion: the neurological sign is. Known cancer with a new headache → assess urgently.

Carbon monoxide: others at home affected, better away from home, a gas or solid-fuel appliance, no CO alarm → get everyone out, stop the appliance, ventilate; assess the same day.

1Focused history — questions that discriminateCG150

  • How fast did it peak? Seconds to minutes = thunderclap. Gradual over weeks = think raised ICP or medication overuse.
  • Is it new or different from the usual headaches? A changed pattern is a new presentation.
  • Position and straining: worse lying down, on coughing or sneezing, or on standing (low pressure).
  • Aura: gradual over at least 5 minutes, lasting 5–60 minutes, fully reversible? Motor weakness, double vision or one-eye loss is not typical aura.
  • Age 50+: jaw pain on chewing, scalp tenderness, visual symptoms, weight loss.
  • Days counted: headache days and acute-medicine days a month, including codeine bought over the counter.
  • Context: pregnancy or recent birth, cancer, HIV or immunosuppression, anticoagulants, head injury, fever.
  • Carbon monoxide: anyone else at home (or a pet) unwell, better away from home, appliances serviced, CO alarm fitted?

4Investigations checklistCG150 · BSR 2020

  • None for typical migraine or tension-type headache with a normal examination
  • ESR, CRP, FBC if GCA possible — but never wait for them to start steroids
  • Pregnancy: BP and urine protein; pregnancy test where relevant
  • Glucose if diabetes or hypoglycaemic symptoms
  • Headache diary for at least 8 weeks (CG150)
  • No imaging for reassurance: MRI via NG12 criteria or the specialist

9Never-eventsCG150 · NG228 · BSR

  • Reassure a thunderclap headache because it has settled.
  • Delay steroids in suspected GCA for an ESR, biopsy or scan; a normal ESR does not exclude GCA.
  • Label a new headache over 50, or a changed pattern, as "tension" without examining.
  • Offer opioids for migraine or tension-type headache, or escalate prevention while overuse continues.
  • Image a typical primary headache for reassurance alone.
  • Miss carbon monoxide: always ask who else at home is unwell.
  • Start a triptan for atypical aura (weakness, double vision, one-eye loss) before stroke and TIA are excluded.
  • Start or continue combined hormonal contraception in migraine with aura.
  • Arrange an outpatient scan for a thunderclap headache instead of same-day emergency assessment.

10Safety-net and follow-up checklistrecord it in the notes

  • 999: sudden worst headache, new weakness, numbness or speech change, confusion, fit, fever with stiff neck or rash
  • Same day: jaw pain on chewing, scalp tenderness or visual change (50+); worse lying down or straining; new headache in pregnancy or after birth
  • Headache diary and medicine-day count given (8 weeks)
  • Acute-medicine limit stated and written: simple analgesics <15, triptans or combinations <10 days a month
  • CO question asked; CO alarm advice given
  • MOH withdrawal: review booked at 4–8 weeks
  • Diagnosis re-checked at review; a changed pattern = start again from the red box
  • Written information on the likely diagnosis

11Prescribing pointersSPC · MHRA · CG150

Sumatriptan50 mg (100 mg if needed) at headache onset; repeat only if it worked and recurred, ≥2 hours apart; max 300 mg in 24 hours
Metoclopramide10 mg up to three times a day, max 5 days; dystonia risk in young people
Codeine, co-codamolNot for migraine or tension-type headache; counts towards the 10-day limit
Steroids in GCAStart before referral; agree bone, glucose and BP protection with the specialist

12Say it like thisafter a normal assessment

"Your examination is normal and the pattern fits a common headache rather than anything dangerous. A scan wouldn't add anything today. What will help is a diary of headache days and painkiller days — and if the headache ever comes on suddenly like a thunderclap, or you get weakness, confusion or a fever with a stiff neck, call 999."
