Microcytosis: the low MCV CLINIC GUIDE

Interpreting an MCV below the reference range (usually <80 fL) in adults 18+. Not for pregnancy (maternity thresholds), children, known transfusion-dependent thalassaemia or CKD on dialysis (renal team).

1Confirm it is realBSG 2021

  • Look back first: a long-standing, stable low MCV with a normal Hb points to a carrier state; a new or falling MCV points to iron loss.
  • Ferritin misleads when there is inflammation, infection, liver disease or malignancy: it rises as an acute-phase protein. Add CRP and transferrin saturation (TSAT); repeat iron studies once an acute illness has settled.
  • Recent iron, IV iron or transfusion raises ferritin: note the date before interpreting.
  • Serum iron varies through the day and with meals: do not decide on it alone.
  • MCH is often a more reliable marker of iron deficiency than MCV (BSG).
  • Anaemia: Hb <130 g/L men, <120 g/L non-pregnant women (WHO); use local laboratory ranges.

5Work-up checklistBSG 2021 · NG20 · NG12

  • FBC with MCH, RDW; film if atypical
  • Ferritin — the key test
  • CRP + TSAT if inflammation likely
  • tTG IgA + total IgA in all IDA
  • FIT in IDA, and 60+ with anaemia
  • Urinalysis for blood in IDA
  • U&E/eGFR; LFTs if ferritin high
  • HPLC if ferritin normal + low MCV

Reticulocyte Hb helps where available. Coeliac serology only while eating gluten. Hb analysis with ferritin, not instead of it.

7Iron deficiency — who needs GI investigationBSG 2021 · NG12 · HTG690 · NG20

Every adult with IDAFIT; ≥10 µg Hb/g → colorectal suspected cancer pathway. Coeliac serology and urinalysis.
Men, post-menopausal womenGastroscopy + colonoscopy (CT colonography if unsuitable) unless there is a clear cause — even if FIT is negative.
Pre-menopausal womenUsually menstrual: treat and recheck. Endoscopy if aged 50+, red flags, strong family history or IDA out of proportion.
Negative endoscopyPersistent or recurrent IDA, or poor response → small bowel (capsule) and renal tract investigation.

8Treat the source tooBSG 2021 · NG88 · SPC · MHRA

  • Heavy periods: manage per NICE NG88 (LNG-IUS, tranexamic acid) alongside iron.
  • NSAIDs: stop if possible; gastroprotection if essential.
  • IV iron (local pathway): oral iron ineffective, not tolerated, malabsorbed, or urgent. Observe for at least 30 minutes afterwards (SPC). Ferric carboxymaltose: check phosphate with repeat dosing or risk factors (MHRA).
  • Take iron away from tea, coffee, calcium and antacids where practical; adherence comes first.

9Follow-up checklistBSG 2021 · NHS SCT

  • Hb at 2–4 weeks on iron: no rise → adherence, ongoing loss, malabsorption, wrong diagnosis
  • FIT returned and actioned; unreturned kit chased
  • Endoscopy and coeliac results reviewed and recorded
  • Iron for 3 months after Hb normalises; then ferritin
  • Then FBC periodically (about every 6 months at first) to catch recurrence
  • Trait: coded; partner testing offered; HbA2 repeated after iron if iron deficient at testing
  • Declines investigation: informed decision and safety-net recorded
  • Return the same day: black tarry stools, vomiting blood, chest pain, faint, worsening breathlessness

11Special groupsBSG 2021 · NHS SCT

  • Pregnancy: different Hb and ferritin thresholds — use the maternity pathway; carrier found → partner testing now.
  • IBD, coeliac, bariatric surgery: oral iron often poorly absorbed; IV iron via specialist or local pathway. Previous surgery does not stop the search for other causes.
  • Frail or major comorbidity: weigh the risks and benefits of endoscopy with the patient; record the decision.
  • Young men with IDA: investigate as for older adults.

12Pitfalls and never-eventsBSG 2021 · NG12

  • Never put IDA down to diet in a man or post-menopausal woman without GI investigation.
  • Never treat a negative FIT as an all-clear when concern persists; it does not see the upper GI tract.
  • Never give iron for a low MCV without checking ferritin: iron does not help thalassaemia trait.
  • Do not rely on the Mentzer index alone; use HPLC.
  • A normal ferritin with a raised CRP does not exclude iron deficiency.
  • Black stools on iron are expected; tarry, sticky stools with dizziness are not.
  • A rising Hb on iron does not mean the cause has been found.
  • Oesophagitis on gastroscopy does not explain IDA on its own: complete the colonoscopy.
  • IDA on anticoagulants or antiplatelets is still investigated.

13Say it like thisplain English

"Your red blood cells are smaller than usual. The commonest reason is low iron, and then the important question is where the iron went, so I'm arranging some tests. If your iron is normal, it may be something you were born with that runs in families. That is harmless, but worth knowing before a pregnancy."
