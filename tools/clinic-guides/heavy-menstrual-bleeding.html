Heavy menstrual bleeding CLINIC GUIDE

For women with heavy periods in primary care, from first consultation to review. Not for post-menopausal bleeding, bleeding in pregnancy or bleeding on HRT (separate pathways).

!Act today — the emergency and the cancer routesNICE NG12 (updated Apr 2026) · NG88

  • Acute heavy bleeding with dizziness, tachycardia, hypotension or collapse → emergency admission (999 if collapsed or shocked).
  • Unexplained post-menopausal bleeding aged 55+, not attributable to HRT → suspected cancer pathway (endometrial); under 55, consider one. Bleeding on HRT → HRT pathway.
  • Pelvic or abdominal mass not obviously fibroids, or ascites → suspected cancer pathway (ovarian). Cervix that looks consistent with cancer → consider the suspected cervical cancer pathway, whatever the screening history.
  • Iron-deficiency anaemia out of proportion to the bleeding → do not blame the periods: FIT (≥10 µg Hb/g → colorectal pathway, NG12) and coeliac serology.

1Diagnose by impact — not by volumeNICE NG88

HMB is excessive menstrual blood loss that interferes with physical, social, emotional and material quality of life. Measuring blood loss is not needed.

Ask: flooding · clots · changing protection hourly · double protection · changing at night · days off work or school. This is the baseline for judging treatment.

3Work-up — every womanNG88

  • FBC in all, alongside treatment
  • Ferritin: no longer advised against (NG88 Jul 2026)
  • hCG if pregnancy possible
  • Chlamydia test if at risk
  • Cervical screening up to date
  • Pregnancy plans — ask early

Not routinely: thyroid tests (only if symptoms) · female hormone tests · a coagulation screen without a suggestive history.

4Examine and image — targetedNICE NG88

Examine (abdominal and pelvic) if there are related symptoms, abnormal bleeding patterns, suspected structural disease, or before an LNG-IUS. Uncomplicated HMB: drug treatment can start without it.

TestUse when
Pelvic ultrasoundUterus palpable abdominally · history or examination suggests a pelvic mass · examination inconclusive or difficult
Transvaginal USSuspected adenomyosis: significant dysmenorrhoea, bulky tender uterus
Outpatient hysteroscopySuspected submucosal fibroid, polyp or endometrial pathology: persistent intermenstrual bleeding or endometrial risk factors

No blind endometrial biopsy as the routine test; MRI is not first-line.

5Start treatment now — split by pregnancy plansNG88 · SPC

Contraception acceptableLNG-IUS 52 mg first: no pathology, fibroids under 3 cm not distorting the cavity, or adenomyosis. Treats pain too.
LNG-IUS declined or unsuitableNon-hormonal: tranexamic acid, an NSAID. Hormonal: CHC (UKMEC), cyclical oral progestogen.
Trying to conceiveNon-hormonal only: tranexamic acid with or without mefenamic acid.
Fibroids 3 cm or moreConsider specialist referral; tranexamic acid and/or NSAID meanwhile.

Usual adult doses: tranexamic acid 1 g TDS for up to 4 days from the start of bleeding (max 4 g/day) · mefenamic acid 500 mg TDS during bleeding. Treat in parallel with any investigation.

8What success looks likeNG88

  • Impact measures improved against her own baseline: no flooding, fewer protection changes, no days lost.
  • Haemoglobin corrected and iron stores replenished.
  • A method that also fits her contraception and pregnancy plans.
  • Review medical treatment after an adequate trial — usually around 3 months, sooner if severe or worsening.

9Before changing treatmentNG88

  • Adherence — tranexamic acid from day 1 of bleeding, every cycle?
  • LNG-IUS — irregular bleeding early on is expected; is loss improving?
  • New pattern — intermenstrual or post-coital bleeding, pain, a mass
  • Contributors — copper IUD, anticoagulants, missed contraception
  • Iron — still deficient? Taking it correctly?
  • Pregnancy — possible? Plans changed?

10Review checklistat ~3 months, then yearly

  • Impact score against baseline (flooding, changes, days lost)
  • FBC (ferritin where used) and iron plan
  • Side effects and tolerability
  • LNG-IUS: threads, bleeding pattern, device expiry
  • On CHC: BP, BMI, UKMEC recheck (migraine with aura, smoking 35+, VTE risk)
  • Pregnancy and contraception plans
  • New red flags: IMB, PCB, mass, post-menopausal bleeding
  • Perimenopause: does the plan still fit?
  • Failed treatment → re-examine, ultrasound or hysteroscopy, refer

11The treatment ladderNICE NG88 · SPC · CoSRH UKMEC

Step 1 · LNG-IUSFirst-line where contraception is acceptable and there is no significant structural disease. Counsel about early irregular bleeding.
Step 2 · other drugsTranexamic acid, NSAID, CHC or cyclical oral progestogen — alone or combined, per contraindications.
Step 3 · investigateTreatment fails → re-examine; ultrasound or hysteroscopy by the NG88 criteria; review the diagnosis.
Step 4 · specialistEndometrial ablation (family complete), fibroid treatment, hysterectomy. GnRH analogue is specialist-initiated.

14Special groups and never-eventsNG88 · CoSRH

  • Never require a measured blood loss before treating.
  • Never offer oral progestogen in the luteal phase only, or danazol, for HMB.
  • Never leave the FBC unactioned, or assume iron deficiency is "just the periods".
  • Never leave a woman waiting for referral untreated.
  • Trying to conceive: hormonal options and ablation are not suitable; tranexamic acid ± NSAID.
  • On anticoagulants: joint decision with the prescriber; do not just stop it.
  • Adolescents heavy from menarche: think von Willebrand disease.

15Acute heavy bleeding — no compromiseNG88 · local pathway

  • Exclude pregnancy; check pulse, BP and Hb.
  • Assess thrombotic risk and possible structural or malignant cause before any hormone.
  • Use a locally approved temporary regimen — not a long-term plan.
  • Severe or persistent → same-day gynaecology advice.
