Stable angina CLINIC GUIDE

For the new diagnosis and the annual review. Adults 18+ with angina due to coronary artery disease. Not for suspected ACS, heart failure management or after a recent MI or stent (follow the cardiology plan).
NICE CG126 (2011, updated 2016) · CG95 (updated 2016) · NG238 · MHRA · DVLA
Reviewed Oct 2026 · next review Apr 2027

Page 1 · Confirm and start chest pain → is it stable angina? → CTCA → GTN + first drug + prevention

!Act now — this is not stable anginaNICE CG95 · CG126

  • Current chest pain, pain with haemodynamic compromise, syncope, severe breathlessness or dynamic ECG changes → 999. Do not delay transfer for an ECG or bloods.
  • New rest pain, crescendo pattern (more often, longer, lower threshold) or rapidly worsening symptoms that have now settled → same-day acute assessment, not a dose change.
  • Pain still there 5 minutes after the second GTN dose → 999. Chest pain with a new murmur → urgent echo and cardiology review (possible aortic stenosis).
  • Chest pain within 24 h of sildenafil or vardenafil (48 h tadalafil) or after poppers → no GTN; sit down, 999 if it persists, tell the crew what was taken.

1Is it angina?NICE CG95

FeatureCount it
1 · Constricting discomfortChest, neck, shoulder, jaw or arm
2 · Brought on by exertionOr emotion; note triggers (cold, meals)
3 · Relieved by rest or GTNWithin about 5 minutes

3 = typical · 2 = atypical · 0–1 = non-anginal. Confirm it is stable: predictable, exertional, relieved by rest. Do not "trial GTN" as a test — oesophageal spasm responds too.

Grade it (CCS): I strenuous exertion only · II slight limitation · III marked limitation · IV any activity or at rest.

2Confirm the diagnosisNICE CG95 (2016)

No known CAD → resting 12-lead ECG and refer to the rapid access chest pain clinic.

Clinical pictureFirst test
Typical or atypical anginaCTCA (64-slice or above)
Non-anginal + ST–T changes or Q wavesCTCA
Non-anginal, normal ECGNo cardiac test; look elsewhere
CTCA non-diagnostic or uncertainFunctional imaging
Known CAD, recurrent symptomsCardiology or functional imaging, not a repeat CTCA

Start GTN and a first-line drug while waiting if symptoms need it; manage risk factors from day one.

3Work-up — every new diagnosislook for the amplifier

  • 12-lead ECG (old MI, AF, LVH)
  • FBC — anaemia amplifies angina
  • TFTs, HbA1c, U&E
  • Lipids (non-HDL)
  • BP, pulse, weight
  • Echo if murmur, HF signs, prior MI

Worse angina without new coronary disease? Look for anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, new AF, uncontrolled BP, heart failure, infection before assuming progression.

4GTN — teach it, check recallNICE CG126 §1.3

PainStop, sit down, GTN 1–2 sprays under the tongue
5 minutesStill there → second dose
5 moreStill there → 999
BeforeUse before planned exertion (hills, sex)

Headache and flushing are common and settle. Light-headed → sit or hold on to something. Never with a PDE5 inhibitor or poppers: no nitrate for 24 h after sildenafil or vardenafil, 48 h after tadalafil; avanafil per its SPC. Ask every patient.

5First drug — symptom controlNICE CG126 §1.3

Beta-blocker or calcium-channel blocker — choose by comorbidity, contraindications, preference and cost. Do not routinely start other anti-anginals first.

ChoiceSuitsAvoid / caution
Bisoprolol usually 5–10 mg ODPost-MI, HFrEF, high resting rateBradycardia, heart block, asthma
Amlodipine 5 → 10 mg ODCoexisting hypertensionAnkle oedema

Review 2–4 weeks after each change; titrate to symptoms, HR, BP and side effects. "Failed" at half dose is not failed. Not tolerated → switch class.

6Secondary prevention — the second trackNICE CG126 · NG238

Aspirin 75 mg ODConsider, weighing bleeding risk and comorbidities. Not routinely on top of an anticoagulant
Atorvastatin 80 mgLower dose if interactions, side-effect risk or preference (NG238)
ACEiConsider with diabetes; offer or continue for its own indication — not for angina alone
BP, diabetesTo target on their own pathways

Anti-anginals ease symptoms; statin and antiplatelet reduce events. Every review checks both.

Bus/lorry: must tell DVLA

"You have two kinds of medicine. The spray and heart tablets ease the pain. The statin and aspirin lower your risk of a heart attack — they only work if you keep taking them, even when you feel well. And pain still there five minutes after the second spray is a 999 call."

8Targets — both tracksNG238 · NG136 · CG126

TrackAim
SymptomsFewest attacks on the fewest drugs; stable pattern; usual activities kept
Heart rate, BPTitrate to symptoms and side effects — no fixed heart-rate target on its own
LipidsLDL ≤2.0 or non-HDL ≤2.6 mmol/L; recheck 2–3 months after a change
BP, diabetesTo target on their own pathways (NG136, diabetes guidance)

Lipids above target → check adherence, dose, interactions and tolerance, then consider ezetimibe (NG238).

9Before every step-upis it still stable?

  • Pattern — rest pain, crescendo or a lower threshold → red box, not titration
  • GTN use — rising sprays, or avoiding activity to avoid pain
  • Dose — is the first drug at its maximum tolerated dose?
  • Adherence — taking it? Stopped because of side effects?
  • Amplifier — FBC, TFTs, ECG for AF, BP

10Annual review checklistevery patient, every year

  • Symptom frequency, CCS class, GTN sprays per week, pattern change
  • GTN technique and 5-5-999 recall; PDE5 inhibitor or poppers use
  • Pulse, BP, weight; side effects (fatigue, ankles, ED, dizziness)
  • Lipids, HbA1c, U&E; statin and aspirin still taken
  • Smoking status, activity, mood, cardiac rehab offered if needed
  • Influenza vaccine if eligible; DVLA position if a Group 2 driver
  • Record CCS class, current step and next action

13When to referNICE CG95 · CG126 §1.4

999 / same dayRed box: current pain, crescendo or new rest pain, syncope
Chest pain clinicNew suspected angina, no known CAD → CTCA
CardiologySymptoms not controlled on optimal medical treatment (1 or 2 anti-anginals + secondary prevention) → consider PCI or CABG
Heart TeamLeft main or complex three-vessel disease; PCI-versus-CABG uncertainty
EchoMurmur, heart-failure signs, previous MI
Cardiac rehabAfter MI or revascularisation; fear-driven inactivity

PCI and CABG relieve symptoms; for many stable patients they add no survival over optimal tablets, but some anatomy benefits — cardiology decides.

11The anti-anginal ladderNICE CG126 §1.3 · doses per SPC

Step 3 · if both unsuitableMonotherapy with a long-acting nitrate, ivabradine, nicorandil or ranolazine.

12Side effects — fix, don't abandonSPC · MHRA

Fatigue, cold hands (beta-blocker)Lower dose or switch to a CCB; taper, never stop suddenly
Bradycardia, dizzinessReduce dose; check for heart block on ECG
Ankle oedema (amlodipine)Lower dose or try another dihydropyridine
GTN headacheParacetamol; usually settles; sit down
Any ulcer on nicorandilStop nicorandil; ulcers heal only after withdrawal
Erectile dysfunctionAsk; PDE5 inhibitor only with no nitrate or nicorandil

14Safety-net — what they must knowCG126

  • Pain still there 5 minutes after the second spray → 999.
  • New pain at rest, or pain coming more often or more easily → contact us the same day.
  • Using the spray more than before → tell us early.
  • Never stop the heart-rate tablet suddenly; never use the spray after Viagra-type drugs or poppers.

15Special groups and never-eventsMHRA · DVLA · CG126

  • HFrEF: beta-blocker; avoid verapamil, diltiazem. Asthma: usually no beta-blocker → CCB.
  • DVLA: Group 2 drivers must notify; relicensing needs at least 6 weeks free of angina plus functional test requirements.
  • Never: nitrate or nicorandil with a PDE5 inhibitor · routine beta-blocker + verapamil/diltiazem · stop a beta-blocker abruptly · treat a crescendo pattern as a dose problem · let the statin quietly lapse.
  • After MI, PCI or CABG: follow the cardiology antiplatelet plan and stop dates; reconcile every letter.
  • Rising GTN use or shrinking activity may be deterioration even when the patient says "fewer attacks" — reassess the pattern.
  • Aspirin is not for primary prevention in people without CVD.
