Asthma, aged 12 and over CLINIC GUIDE

For the new diagnosis and the annual review. Adults and young people aged 12+. Not for children aged 5–11 (different pathway), severe asthma under specialist care or hospital management of an attack.

!Act today — grade the attack on numbersBTS/SIGN 158 (acute asthma)

SeverityAny one ofAction
Life-threateningPEF <33% best/predicted · SpO₂ <92% · silent chest · cyanosis · poor respiratory effort · arrhythmia · exhaustion · altered consciousness · hypotension999 now; treat while waiting — oxygen, nebulised or spacer salbutamol, prednisolone. Stay with the patient.

ModerateWorse symptoms, PEF >50–75%, no severe featuresTreat in practice; prednisolone 40–50 mg for at least 5 days; review soon.

Lower threshold to admit: previous near-fatal asthma (ICU or ventilation), pregnancy, living alone or poor support. Stridor, angio-oedema or rash after a drug or food → anaphylaxis: IM adrenaline 500 micrograms first. Never let a normal-looking patient talk you out of the numbers.

Aged 12–16: if PEF is not diagnostic, NG245 adds a house dust mite skin-prick test, or total IgE plus eosinophils. ICS lowers FeNO and eosinophils and normalises spirometry — test before starting ICS where safe, but never delay treating someone acutely unwell. Code "suspected asthma" until confirmed.

2Baseline assessmentrecord once, revisit yearly

  • Occupation: better on days off?
  • Smoking and vaping
  • Rhinitis, reflux, BMI
  • Attacks, oral steroid courses, admissions, ever ICU
  • Reliever count from records
  • NSAID or beta-blocker triggers
  • Control questionnaire score (ACT/ACQ)
  • Mood, breathing-pattern disorder

3Flag who is at riskNG245 · MHRA DSU Apr 2025

  • SABA over-use: more than 2 inhalers a year.
  • 2 or more courses of oral steroids a year.
  • 2 or more ED visits or any admission for asthma.
  • Non-adherence to ICS; previous near-fatal attack; psychosocial problems.
  • As-needed SABA more than twice a week → review and adjust treatment (MHRA).

Tailor care to these people: earlier review, a checked action plan and a named follow-up.

4First treatment — no SABA-only regimenNG245

New diagnosisAIR: low-dose ICS/formoterol as needed only
Very symptomatice.g. waking at night, or a severe attack at presentation → start low-dose MART
On SABA aloneMove to AIR or another ICS-containing regimen — do not reissue
On MARTOne inhaler for maintenance and relief; no separate SABA routinely

Example: budesonide/formoterol 200/6 Turbohaler (licensed as reliever from age 12): 1 inhalation when symptomatic, a second after a few minutes if needed. More than 8 inhalations a day is not normally needed; up to 12 for a limited period (SPC). Licences, strengths and maximum doses differ between products — check each SPC.

5Inhaler technique — every reviewNG245

  • Watch them use it at every review and every change: the commonest cause of "treatment failure".
  • Check adherence from prescription records, not memory.
  • Dry-powder devices need a fast, deep breath; MDIs a slow breath with a spacer.
  • Weak inhale or poor coordination → MDI + spacer; rinse and spit after ICS doses.
  • Match the device to the person, then keep it: avoid switching device without a review.

6Moving existing patientsNG245 · plan, don't switch blind

  • SABA only, no ICS anywhere: the dangerous pattern — book a review and move to AIR (or MART if uncontrolled).
  • ICS + SABA, well controlled: not wrong. Offer AIR or MART at a planned review after checking diagnosis, technique, adherence, device and licence.
  • Every switch: show the new device, rewrite the action plan, review soon.

Page 2 · Review and step the annual review: control → technique → step up or down → plan

8Target — controlled asthmaNG245 definition

Uncontrolled = any one of:

  • any attack needing oral corticosteroids;
  • reliever needed on 3 or more days a week;
  • waking at night with asthma on 1 or more nights a week.

Target: none of these, normal activity, no time off work or school. Use the same validated questionnaire each time so the scores compare.

9Before every step-upNG245

  • Technique — watched, right device for their breath
  • Adherence — prescription record; steroid fears explored
  • Triggers — smoking, vaping, work, pets, rhinitis, NSAIDs, beta-blockers (eye drops too)
  • Diagnosis — ever objectively confirmed? Think dysfunctional breathing, inducible laryngeal obstruction, COPD, heart failure
  • Comorbidity — obesity, reflux, anxiety and depression

11Side effects — fix, don't abandonSPC · MHRA

Thrush, hoarse voice (ICS)Rinse and spit after maintenance doses; spacer; recheck technique
Tremor, palpitations (formoterol)Usually settles; if not, ask how often they really need it
Cough on inhalingSlow the inhalation, or switch DPI to MDI + spacer
Mood, sleep, behaviour (montelukast)Warn at the start; stop if they change
Frequent oral steroidsTreat as uncontrolled asthma: step up or refer; think bone protection

12Annual review checklistat least yearly, and after any attack

  • Control questionnaire score (ACT or ACQ) recorded
  • Attacks, oral steroid courses, ED visits, admissions in 12 months
  • SABA/AIR use counted from the prescribing record
  • Inhaler technique observed; adherence from records
  • Action plan reviewed, updated and understood
  • Smoking and vaping; occupation; rhinitis; weight
  • FeNO where available if control is poor or a change is planned
  • Flu vaccine; COVID-19 or RSV only if otherwise eligible
  • Any attack in 12 months = uncontrolled → box 9, then step up
  • Controlled for a sustained period → discuss stepping down
  • Record current step, control and next action

13After every attackBTS/SIGN 158 · NG245

  • Seen in hospital → GP or nurse review within 2 working days of discharge
  • Prednisolone course being taken; symptoms and PEF improving
  • Find the trigger; check technique and adherence
  • Step up if indicated; update the action plan; recount steroid courses

14When to referNG245

  • Diagnosis suspected but not confirmed by tests 1–3 → bronchial challenge.
  • Suspected occupational asthma → occupational asthma specialist; record the exposure first.
  • Uncontrolled on moderate MART with raised FeNO or eosinophils.
  • Still uncontrolled after LTRA and LAMA trials.
  • Repeated oral steroid courses or admissions despite the box 9 checks.

15Special groups and never-eventsNG245 · BTS/SIGN 158 · MHRA

  • Pregnancy: continue ICS or MART — uncontrolled asthma is the bigger risk to the baby. Acute asthma in pregnancy → treat as usual and involve the obstetric team early.
  • Young people 12–17: check each product's licence for age; involve them in the action plan.
  • Never: SABA without an ICS · a LABA without an ICS · a step-up before technique and adherence are checked · reissuing SABA on request without review · stopping ICS to "test" without a plan.
  • Non-selective beta-blockers (including eye drops) and NSAIDs in known sensitivity can trigger attacks.
