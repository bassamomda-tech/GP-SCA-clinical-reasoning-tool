Raised HbA1c or glucose CLINIC GUIDE

A raised HbA1c or plasma glucose in adults 18+. Not for children (same-day paediatric referral), pregnancy or gestational diabetes (antenatal pathway, NICE NG3), or review of known diabetes.

1Confirm it is realNG28 · WHO 2011

  • Asymptomatic: repeat to confirm, ideally within 2 weeks; diabetes needs a second result ≥48 mmol/mol. Symptomatic (thirst, polyuria, weight loss): one result is enough.
  • Random glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L diagnoses diabetes only with symptoms. Capillary meter readings are never diagnostic: use venous plasma glucose.
  • HbA1c reflects 8–12 weeks, so it misleads when glucose rose recently or red-cell life is altered.
  • HbA1c unreliable → use glucose: symptoms under 2 months, suspected type 1, acute illness, steroids or antipsychotics, pancreatic damage or surgery; pregnancy; anaemia, haemolysis, recent transfusion or blood loss, haemoglobinopathy; advanced CKD, HIV treatment. Seek laboratory advice.
  • Stress hyperglycaemia: raised glucose in acute illness with a normal HbA1c — recheck HbA1c 3 months after recovery.

3Clinical clues — is it type 2?NG28 · NG17

Pace and symptomsThirst, polyuria, thrush. Onset over weeks or rapid weight loss → insulin deficiency.
Type 1 / LADAKetones, weight loss, autoimmune disease, poor response to tablets. Age or BMI never excludes it.
Drugs and historySteroids, antipsychotics, tacrolimus; pancreatitis, alcohol; past gestational diabetes.
ExamineWeight, BP, hydration; feet (pulses, monofilament); Cushingoid features; acanthosis.

5Work-up checklistNG28 · NG203 · NG238

  • Ketones and glucose today if symptomatic
  • Repeat HbA1c or venous plasma glucose
  • U&E, eGFR: gates metformin and SGLT2i
  • Urine ACR: early-morning sample
  • Lipids, QRISK3, BP
  • LFTs (fatty liver), FBC
  • Urine dipstick: ketones, infection
  • TSH, ECG if clinically indicated
  • GAD antibodies if type 1 or LADA possible
  • Ferritin, TSAT if haemochromatosis
  • Foot check: monofilament, pulses
  • Eye screening referral at diagnosis

Never delay same-day diabetes-team assessment, or insulin, waiting for antibodies; C-peptide later if the type is still uncertain.

8SGLT2 inhibitor and metformin safetyNG28 · MHRA · JBDS

Before the SGLT2iAsk about previous DKA, dehydration and a very-low-carbohydrate or ketogenic diet. Do not start while unwell. Warn about genital thrush.
Sick-day rulesVomiting, diarrhoea or poor intake: pause the SGLT2i and metformin; never stop insulin. Restart when eating and drinking normally for 24–48 h.
Ketones, not glucoseUnwell on an SGLT2i: check blood ketones even if glucose is normal. ≥3.0 mmol/L or DKA symptoms → 999.
RemissionWeight loss improves HbA1c and can bring remission: NHS Type 2 Path to Remission where eligible. Screening continues.

9Follow-up and monitoring checklistNG28 · MHRA

  • Asymptomatic first result ≥48: repeat before coding diabetes
  • Metformin tolerability once titrated, before adding the SGLT2i
  • HbA1c at 3 months; then 3–6-monthly until stable, then 6-monthly
  • Above target: adherence, lifestyle, intercurrent illness, right target, right type — then intensify
  • Annual care processes: HbA1c, BP, lipids, ACR, eGFR, BMI, smoking, feet, eye screening
  • Non-diabetic hyperglycaemia: annual HbA1c; stress hyperglycaemia: HbA1c 3 months after recovery
  • Metformin: check B12 if symptoms of deficiency; consider periodic monitoring if risk factors (e.g. higher dose, longer duration, low-normal baseline)
  • Rethink the type: rapid tablet failure, ongoing weight loss or ketones at any point
  • Chase antibodies, CT, ACR and the eye-screening invitation
  • On a sulfonylurea or insulin: ask about hypoglycaemia and driving at every review

11Special groupsNG28 · NG3 · DVLA

  • Pregnant or planning pregnancy: same-day antenatal diabetes team if pregnant; pre-conception care before stopping contraception. HbA1c is not used to diagnose gestational diabetes.
  • Frail or limited life expectancy: relaxed target agreed with patient and carer; avoid hypoglycaemia; caution with an SGLT2i.
  • Ketosis-prone type 2, notably in people of African or African-Caribbean heritage: DKA can occur in "type 2" — check ketones when unwell.
  • Driving: insulin → tell the DVLA; on tablets, car drivers usually need not unless DVLA hypoglycaemia triggers apply; Group 2 drivers on a sulfonylurea or insulin must tell the DVLA.

12Pitfalls and never-eventsNG28 · NG17 · NG12

  • Never label diabetes on one asymptomatic HbA1c, or on a capillary meter reading.
  • Never start metformin and "review in 3 months" in a 60+ with weight loss, or with ketones.
  • A normal glucose does not exclude DKA on an SGLT2 inhibitor: check ketones.
  • A normal HbA1c does not exclude diabetes after recent onset, in anaemia or haemoglobinopathy.
  • An HbA1c of 48 in a frail care-home resident on a sulfonylurea is a harm marker.

13Safety-net — say it, then document itNG28 · JBDS

Call 999Vomiting, tummy pain, fast deep breathing, drowsiness or confusion — even if the sugar is not very high on a "flozin"
Same dayVery thirsty, passing lots of urine and losing weight; cannot keep fluids down; a new sore, redness or swelling on a foot
HyposOn a sulfonylurea or insulin: shaky, sweaty or confused → fast sugar, then a snack; tell us about any severe or repeated hypo

14Say it like thisplain English

"Your blood test shows your sugar has been running high over the last two to three months. We'll recheck it to be sure, and look at your heart, kidneys, eyes and feet too. If you start vomiting, get tummy pain, fast deep breathing or feel very drowsy, call 999 — that can happen even when the sugar isn't very high."
