Hyperthyroidism in adults CLINIC GUIDE

For the new low TSH, symptom control while awaiting the specialist, and shared monitoring of antithyroid drugs. Adults 18+. Not for pregnancy (joint specialist care), children, thyroid eye disease treatment or thyroid cancer.
NICE NG145 (2019) · MHRA DSU Feb 2019 · NG12 · RCOG GTG76 (2025)

Page 1 · Diagnose and start low TSH → confirm → find the cause → control symptoms → specialist

!Act todayNG145 · MHRA · NG12

  • Thyroid storm: known or suspected thyrotoxicosis with fever, marked tachycardia or AF, plus confusion or agitation, heart failure, vomiting, diarrhoea or jaundice → 999. A clinical diagnosis — do not wait for TFTs.
  • On carbimazole or PTU with sore throat, mouth ulcers, fever or any infection → stop the drug, same-day FBC (neutrophils). Unwell or septic → neutropenic sepsis pathway now.
  • Severe upper abdominal pain or vomiting on carbimazole → stop, same-day assessment for pancreatitis; never re-expose (MHRA).
  • Reduced vision, colour loss or corneal exposure with thyroid eye disease → same-day ophthalmology. Rapid AF with hypotension, chest pain or breathlessness → 999.
  • Unexplained thyroid lump → consider a suspected cancer pathway referral (NG12); a low TSH does not exclude cancer.

3Work-up — every new diagnosisNG145

  • TSH, FT4, FT3
  • TRAb (TSH-receptor antibodies)
  • FBC baseline before any antithyroid drug
  • LFTs baseline
  • ECG if palpitations or irregular pulse
  • Pregnancy status and plans
  • Drug review: amiodarone, lithium, biotin, iodine/kelp
  • Eye check: acuity, colour vision, diplopia

Baseline FBC is the only way to tell later if a low neutrophil count is new. Ultrasound only for a nodule or structural concern.

6Symptom control and the first drugNG145 · MHRA · SPC

Propranolol 10–40 mg three or four times daily for tachycardia, tremor, anxiety; wean once euthyroid. Check for asthma, bradycardia, heart block, decompensated heart failure.

Carbimazole is the antithyroid drug of choice, started by or on the advice of the specialist (or local shared care); maintenance usually 5–15 mg daily. Consider PTU instead after carbimazole reactions, with a history of pancreatitis, or if pregnant or trying within 6 months.

At the first prescription: the sore-throat rule, verbally and in writing, and effective contraception (MHRA).

8Treatment pathway and monitoringNG145 · specialist-led

Step 1 · symptom controlBeta-blocker if no contraindication. Thyroiditis: this is the whole treatment.
Step 2 · antithyroid drugCarbimazole by titration or block-and-replace (specialist choice); Graves' course 12–18 months, then trial off.
Step 3 · definitiveRadioiodine is first-line for Graves' unless a drug course is likely to bring remission or it is unsuitable. Surgery for compression, suspected cancer or when radioiodine is unsuitable.
Radioiodine unsuitablePregnant or breastfeeding, or pregnancy planned within 6 months; active eye disease; suspected cancer; compression. Hypothyroidism afterwards is expected.

10Before changing the doseNG145

  • Right cause? Thyroiditis treated with carbimazole does nothing
  • Adherence — tablets actually taken?
  • Free hormones, not TSH, guide early changes
  • Overshoot — tired, cold, weight gain → reduce
  • Relapse after stopping → definitive treatment talk

  • Rash, itch, joint pain, jaundice, abdominal pain
  • Women who could become pregnant: contraception and plans
  • Eyes: new pain, diplopia, vision change; smoking

14Special groups and never-eventsMHRA · NG145 · RCOG GTG76

  • Pregnancy: carbimazole raises the risk of congenital malformations, especially in the first trimester and at high doses. Effective contraception; in pregnancy only after strict benefit–risk review, lowest dose, no added levothyroxine (no block-and-replace). Joint endocrine–obstetric care.
  • Older people: weight loss and AF without classic features — check TFTs in new AF.
  • Never: give carbimazole for thyroiditis · titrate on TSH alone early on · skip the written sore-throat warning · re-expose after pancreatitis.

Safety-net: "Sore throat, mouth ulcers or fever — stop and call us today. Yellow skin, dark urine or bad tummy pain — stop and call us today. High fever with a racing heart and confusion — 999. Planning a pregnancy — tell us before you try."
