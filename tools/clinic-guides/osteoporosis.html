Osteoporosis and fracture risk CLINIC GUIDE

For case-finding, starting bone protection and the treatment review. Adults, mainly aged 50+ and postmenopausal women. Not for children, pregnancy-associated osteoporosis, osteomalacia, Paget's disease or advanced CKD bone disease.
NICE NG259 (Jul 2026; replaced CG146) · TA464 · NOGG 2024 · MHRA · NG12
Reviewed Oct 2026 · next review Apr 2027

Page 1 · Assess and start case-find → FRAX → NOGG band → DXA if needed → treat

!Act todayNG12 · MHRA · NOGG

  • Sudden severe back pain or height loss → think vertebral fracture: image the spine. Aged 60+ with persistent bone pain (especially back) or an unexplained fracture → myeloma tests (FBC, calcium, ESR or plasma viscosity, serum protein electrophoresis, serum free light chains) per NG12.
  • New thigh, hip or groin pain on long-term bisphosphonate or denosumab → X-ray both femurs (atypical femoral fracture).
  • Denosumab overdue or about to stop → arrange the dose or a specialist-planned follow-on now: stopping or delaying raises the risk of multiple vertebral fractures (MHRA).
  • New dysphagia on an oral bisphosphonate → stop it and investigate (NG12) — not just a switch.

1Who to assessNICE NG259

  • Assess at 50+ or postmenopausal with a previous fragility fracture or current or frequent systemic glucocorticoids.
  • Consider assessing all women 65+ and men 75+, and others 50+ or postmenopausal with: 2 or more falls in the last year · first-degree relative with a hip fracture · BMI <18.5 · current smoker · alcohol >14 units/week · another cause (e.g. RA, untreated early menopause, hypogonadism, malabsorption, aromatase inhibitors, androgen deprivation).
  • Under 50 and premenopausal: assess after a hip or vertebral fragility fracture or 2 or more major osteoporotic fractures; consider with another major risk factor.

A fragility fracture = from standing height or less. Every one in an adult 50+ → fracture liaison service where available.

2Quantify — tool first, DXA selectivelyNG259 · NOGG 2024

FRAX or QFracture for ages 40–90; QFracture for 30–39. Use the same tool throughout one assessment.

Straight to DXA (age 30+): previous hip or vertebral fragility fracture, a major osteoporotic fracture in the last 2 years, or 2 or more fragility fractures.

NG259: consider DXA if 10-year major osteoporotic fracture risk is ≥10%. NOGG thresholds are FRAX-based — don't plot QFracture onto them. T-score ≤−2.5 = osteoporosis.

4Work-up before treatmentNOGG · secondary causes

  • FBC, ESR or CRP
  • U&E, eGFR
  • Bone profile: Ca, PO₄, ALP
  • LFTs, TFTs
  • Vitamin D
  • Coeliac serology
  • Men: testosterone, LH, FSH
  • Dental check before starting

Selectively: myeloma tests (red box) · PTH if calcium abnormal · cortisol tests if Cushingoid · spine imaging for height loss.

Correct vitamin D and calcium before zoledronate or denosumab (hypocalcaemia risk).

TA464: bisphosphonates for adults at higher risk, deciding case by case on fracture risk, adverse-effect risk and preference.

6First drugTA464 · SPC

Alendronate 70 mg once weekly: on waking, fasting, whole tablet with a full glass of plain water; stay upright; no food, drink or other medicines for at least 30 minutes. Risedronate 35 mg weekly is the oral alternative.

Oral unsuitable → zoledronate 5 mg IV once yearly (creatinine clearance ≥35 mL/min, well hydrated) or denosumab 60 mg SC every 6 months.

Calcium 700–1200 mg/day, diet first; vitamin D 800 units/day if at risk (NOGG).

9Fracture on treatment? Check firstNOGG

  • Adherence — many stop in the first year
  • Technique — fasting, plain water, 30 minutes
  • Calcium and vitamin D replete?
  • Secondary cause missed — myeloma, PTH, coeliac
  • Falls — new sedatives, vision, programme attended?

10Annual review checklistevery patient, every year

  • Adherence and the weekly ritual; GI symptoms
  • Falls in the last year; falls programme
  • New fractures, back pain, height
  • Calcium intake, vitamin D, renal function
  • Dental health, jaw pain; ear pain or discharge
  • Thigh or groin pain (atypical fracture)
  • Denosumab: next dose booked before it is due
  • Glucocorticoid courses since last review
  • Is the 5-year (or 3-year IV) review due?

11The treatment ladderTA464 · NOGG · SPC

Step 1 · oralAlendronate 70 mg weekly (or risedronate 35 mg weekly). First-line for most people.
Step 2 · IVZoledronate 5 mg yearly if oral is not tolerated, contraindicated or adherence is unrealistic.
Step 3 · denosumab60 mg SC every 6 months when bisphosphonates are unsuitable. Lifelong plan needed.
Specialist · anabolicVery high risk: teriparatide or romosozumab first, then an antiresorptive.

12Side effects — keep protection goingMHRA · SPC

RefluxCheck technique → risedronate → IV
Jaw osteonecrosisRare; dental check first, good oral hygiene; non-healing socket → review
Ear canal osteonecrosisEar pain, discharge or infection on long-term use → assess (MHRA)
Atypical femoral fractureThigh or groin pain → X-ray both femurs; stop if confirmed
HypocalcaemiaPrevented by correcting vitamin D first

14Special groups and never-eventsMHRA · NOGG

  • Glucocorticoids: assess risk when starting; bone protection with the steroid at ≥7.5 mg/day for 3+ months.
  • Denosumab: rebound vertebral fractures after stopping or delaying — flag it like an anticoagulant.
  • Never: just stop denosumab · take alendronate with food, tea or calcium · file a vertebral fracture on an X-ray report · plot QFracture on NOGG · give zoledronate with creatinine clearance <35.

15Situations that change the review — and the safety-netNG259 · NOGG · MHRA

Not treated (low risk)Reassess fracture risk when risk factors change — a new fracture, steroids, falls

Safety-net: "Sudden bad back pain or losing height — call us the same day. A new ache in the thigh or groin that won't settle — call us, we'll X-ray it. Food sticking when you swallow — stop the tablet and call. Your injection is due every 6 months — never let it slip."
