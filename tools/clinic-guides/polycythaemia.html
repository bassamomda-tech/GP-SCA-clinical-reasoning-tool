1Is it real and persistent?BSH 2019

Single high resultRepeat a venous FBC to confirm persistence (interval per local pathway); review Hb, Hct, WCC, platelets, MCV and film
Dehydration, acute illnessLow plasma volume: repeat when well and hydrated
DiureticsRelative erythrocytosis: review the need
Smoking, CO exposureCarboxyhaemoglobin raises Hb and makes pulse oximetry misleading: consider a carboxyhaemoglobin level
Testosterone, anabolic steroids, EPODrug-driven: ask directly
AltitudeHct is higher at altitude

Haematocrit is better than Hb for identifying raised red-cell mass. Persistent erythrocytosis should be investigated, not repeatedly re-tested.

5Work-up checklistBSH 2019

  • Repeat venous FBC + film
  • JAK2 V617F + serum EPO
  • U&E, LFTs, calcium
  • Ferritin: iron deficiency can mask PV
  • SpO₂ at rest; spirometry if COPD suspected
  • Carboxyhaemoglobin in smokers or CO exposure
  • Urinalysis; renal ultrasound if indicated
  • Sleep assessment if OSA suspected

Normal daytime SpO₂ does not exclude nocturnal hypoxia. No routine CT, AFP or marrow in primary care.

6Reading the result in four stepsBSH 2019

1 · Symptoms or clot?Headache, visual change, chest pain, breathlessness, focal neurology, leg swelling → 999 or same day before anything else.
2 · Real and persistent?Repeat venous FBC after correcting dehydration, diuretics and smoking. Above 0.52 / 0.48 on repeat → investigate.
3 · Clonal?JAK2 with EPO. Positive JAK2, or high platelets, WCC or a spleen → urgent haematology.
4 · Which secondary cause?Lungs, sleep, smoking, testosterone, kidneys. Unexplained, young or lifelong → haematology advice.

7Management by causeBSH 2019 · NICE TA921 · NG209

CauseFirst lineWho leads, and the target
Polycythaemia vera (confirmed)Venesection to Hct <0.45 (men and women); low-dose aspirin unless contraindicated; cytoreduction (e.g. hydroxycarbamide, interferon) for high-risk disease (age, previous thrombosis) or poor control; ruxolitinib if hydroxycarbamide fails or is not tolerated (TA921)Haematology-led. GP prescribes hydroxycarbamide only under formal shared care. Do not start aspirin in suspected PV before specialist advice
Secondary (hypoxia, OSA, drugs, renal)Treat the cause: COPD care, sleep pathway and CPAP, stop smoking, review testosterone or steroids, renal or cardiac referralPrimary care if asymptomatic with a known cause. No routine venesection; no aspirin for the Hct alone. The PV target does not apply
RelativeAddress hydration, diuretics, smoking, alcohol, weight; control BP and cardiovascular riskPrimary care; no venesection. Persistent and unexplained → JAK2/EPO or haematology advice
Smoking-relatedBehavioural support plus varenicline, cytisinicline, combination NRT or a nicotine vape (NG209)Quitting may lower the Hct

8Cardiovascular risk — always part of the planBSH 2019 · NG209

SmokingThe biggest modifiable driver in both PV and secondary erythrocytosis. Offer support and medication at every contact.
BP and lipidsTreat per NICE. QRISK does not include polycythaemia, so it may underestimate risk in PV.
Weight and sleepWeight loss can improve OSA and relative erythrocytosis; refer suspected OSA to the sleep pathway.
Diuretics and fluidsReview diuretics; avoid dehydration, but no fixed fluid volume in heart failure, CKD or fluid restriction.

9Follow-up and monitoring checklistBSH 2019

  • Repeat Hct after correcting dehydration, diuretics, smoking
  • JAK2 and EPO results actioned; positive → urgent referral
  • PV target: Hct <0.45 — set and monitored by haematology
  • Rising Hct, platelets or WCC on treatment → inform haematology
  • Hydroxycarbamide: FBC and hold thresholds per shared-care protocol only
  • Low ferritin after venesection is expected: no iron in PV without haematology advice
  • Annual: thrombosis, bleeding, spleen, itch, BP, lipids, smoking
  • Secondary: recheck Hct once the cause is treated; refer if rising or symptomatic

11Special situations in known or suspected PVBSH 2019 · NG158

PregnancyKnown or suspected PV, or planning pregnancy → early joint haematology and obstetric care. Do not start or stop hydroxycarbamide or ruxolitinib without specialist advice.
Surgery, travelTell the surgical team and haematology. Long journeys: keep mobile and hydrated; drug prophylaxis only after individual risk assessment.
Clot on treatmentStroke, MI, PE or DVT despite treatment → emergency care, then haematology review of control and escalation.
TransformationGrowing spleen, falling Hb or platelets, tear-drop cells or blasts on the film → urgent haematology (same day if blasts).

12Pitfalls and never-eventsBSH 2019

  • Never diagnose PV from a raised Hct alone, or exclude it on a negative JAK2.
  • Never venesect secondary erythrocytosis in primary care, or aim for a normal Hct in hypoxic lung disease.
  • Never give iron in PV without haematology advice.
  • Do not label persistent unexplained erythrocytosis "relative" without EPO and JAK2.
  • Normal SpO₂ in a smoker does not exclude carboxyhaemoglobin or nocturnal hypoxia.
  • Do not adjust cytoreduction outside the agreed shared-care pathway.

13Self-care and the conversationBSH 2019 · NG209

Stop smoking · avoid dehydration, especially in heat, illness and travel · keep moving on long journeys · control BP and weight · avoid high altitude if possible.

"Your blood is thicker than it should be — there are more red cells than normal. Often that's from something like smoking, a lung or sleep problem, or being a bit dry, and we'll check for those. Sometimes the bone marrow makes too many on its own, so I'm also sending a blood test for that. Thick blood can clot, so tell us straight away about headaches, sight changes, chest pain or a swollen leg."
