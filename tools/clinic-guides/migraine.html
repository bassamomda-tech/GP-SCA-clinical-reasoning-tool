!Not migraine until proven otherwise — act todayNICE CG150 · NG228

  • Thunderclap (sudden severe headache peaking within 1–5 minutes), even if it has settled → 999. Also 999: fever with neck stiffness or non-blanching rash, new focal deficit, reduced consciousness, a first seizure.
  • Atypical aura — motor weakness, double vision, visual symptoms in one eye only, poor balance, reduced consciousness, sudden (not gradual) onset → same-day assessment for TIA, stroke or retinal disease. Do not treat it as migraine or start a triptan.
  • New headache aged 50+ with jaw claudication, scalp tenderness or visual symptoms → same-day GCA pathway. Papilloedema or headache worse lying down or on straining → same day. New headache in pregnancy or after birth → same-day maternity assessment.

1Confirm it is migraineCG150 Table 1

  • Without aura: ≥5 attacks lasting 4–72 hours, with ≥2 of: unilateral · pulsating · moderate or severe · worse on routine activity; plus nausea or vomiting, or photophobia and phonophobia.
  • Aura (visual, sensory or speech): fully reversible, develops over at least 5 minutes, lasts 5–60 minutes; may occur without headache.
  • Examination: BP, fundi, neurological examination — should be normal.
  • Headache diary for at least 8 weeks: frequency, duration, severity, every acute medicine (prescribed and bought), triggers, link to periods.
  • Menstrual migraine: attacks mainly from 2 days before to 3 days after the start of a period, confirm with a diary over at least 2 cycles.

2Work-up — every new diagnosisCG150

  • BP, fundi, neuro exam
  • Diary started (8 weeks)
  • Acute-medicine days, incl. OTC
  • Aura? Record yes / no
  • Contraception and pregnancy plans
  • Mood, sleep, asthma, CVD (shape drug choice)

No imaging for reassurance in typical migraine with a normal examination (CG150). Investigate red flags, atypical aura or a changed pattern instead.

3Count the two numbersCG150 Table 1 · ICHD-3

PatternDefinition (per month)
EpisodicHeadache on fewer than 15 days
Chronic migraineHeadache on ≥15 days for more than 3 months, with migraine features on ≥8 of them
Medication overuse (MOH)Headache on ≥15 days in a known headache disorder, plus for more than 3 months: triptans, opioids, ergots or combination analgesics ≥10 days, or paracetamol, aspirin or NSAID ≥15 days

MOH is the commonest reason migraine looks "refractory". Ask about codeine and combination products bought over the counter.

4Who needs preventionCG150

Discuss prevention when attacks are frequent, prolonged or disabling, when acute treatment is not working or not tolerated, or when medication overuse is building. Deal with MOH first (box 11).

Offer a shared decision between the three NICE options (box 8). Count success as fewer migraine days, not zero.

5Treat the attack — early, in the headache phaseCG150 · SPC · MHRA

First choiceOral triptan + NSAID, or triptan + paracetamol
One drug onlyTriptan, NSAID, aspirin 900 mg or paracetamol
Add antiemeticMetoclopramide or prochlorperazine, even without nausea
Oral failsNon-oral antiemetic + non-oral NSAID or triptan

Sumatriptan 50 mg (100 mg if needed) at onset of headache, not during aura. Repeat only if it worked and the headache came back, at least 2 hours later; max 300 mg in 24 hours. No response → no second dose for that attack.

Metoclopramide 10 mg up to three times a day, for no more than 5 days; acute dystonia is more common in young people.

One triptan fails on several attacks → try a different triptan before giving up on the class. Do not offer opioids or ergots.

Rimegepant (TA919): only if ≥2 triptans have not worked well enough, or triptans are contraindicated or not tolerated and NSAIDs and paracetamol have not worked.

8Prevention ladderNICE CG150 (2025): consider propranolol, topiramate or amitriptyline after discussing each

Step 1 · one NICE optionChoose by comorbidity, pregnancy potential, overdose risk and preference. Titrate slowly to a therapeutic dose.
Step 2 · next optionNot effective or not tolerated → discuss the next NICE option, then the third. Seek specialist advice before combining preventives.
Step 3 · acupunctureUp to 10 sessions over 5–8 weeks if the drug options are unsuitable, not tolerated or have not worked.
Step 4 · headache clinic≥3 preventives have not worked, are not tolerated or are unsuitable → refer against each NICE appraisal's own criteria (box 14).
DrugUsual doseCheck / monitorWatch

Prevention, episodicMigraine days down by ≥50% (the NICE TA yardstick at 12 weeks)

Review the need for prevention 3–6 months after starting (CG150).

10Before every step-upCG150

  • Overuse — count acute days from the diary first
  • Dose and time — titrated to a therapeutic dose and given long enough?
  • Adherence and side effects that stopped the titration
  • Acute plan — taken early, with an NSAID, antiemetic if needed?
  • Diagnosis — a changed pattern is a new presentation; re-check red flags
  • Hormones — menstrual pattern, new aura on CHC

11Medication-overuse headacheCG150

  • Stop all overused acute medicines abruptly for at least 1 month; warn that headache will get worse before it improves.
  • Consider starting prevention at the same time. Review at 4–8 weeks.
  • Strong opioids, previous failed withdrawal or major comorbidity → specialist support for withdrawal.

12Side effects and fixesSPC · CG150

TriptanChest or throat tightness, tingling; avoid in coronary, cerebrovascular or peripheral vascular disease and uncontrolled BP
PropranololTiredness, cold hands, vivid dreams; avoid in asthma; dangerous in overdose
TopiramateTingling, word-finding and memory problems, mood change, weight loss, kidney stones; eye pain or blurred vision → stop, same-day review
AmitriptylineDrowsiness, dry mouth, constipation; take early evening; toxic in overdose
MetoclopramideAcute dystonia → stop; 5-day limit

13Migraine review checklistevery review, at least yearly

  • Diary: migraine days and acute-medicine days a month
  • Acute plan: right triptan, taken early, with NSAID or paracetamol
  • Prevention: dose, response, side effects; still needed at 3–6 months?
  • Aura status vs contraception (CHC is UKMEC 4 with aura)
  • Topiramate: Pregnancy Prevention Programme met
  • Codeine or co-codamol on repeat → plan to stop
  • Mood, sleep, BP; pregnancy plans
  • Red flags re-checked; agreed medicine-day limit recorded

15Special groups and never-eventsUKMEC · MHRA · CG150

  • Aura + combined hormonal contraception = UKMEC 4: aura adds to ischaemic stroke risk. Never start CHC; aura on CHC → stop it and switch (POP, implant, IUS, Cu-IUD).
  • Topiramate: not for migraine in pregnancy; anyone who could become pregnant needs the PPP. Valproate: never for migraine in pregnancy; PPP for anyone who could become pregnant (MHRA).
  • Pregnancy: paracetamol first; triptan or NSAID only after discussing risks (no NSAID in the third trimester). Seek specialist advice for prevention.
  • Menstrual migraine: frovatriptan 2.5 mg twice a day or zolmitriptan 2.5 mg two or three times a day on expected days if standard acute treatment is not enough (CG150).
  • Never: opioids or ergots for attacks · escalate prevention while overuse continues · judge a preventive after 2 weeks at the starting dose.
