Hypocalcaemia: the low calcium CLINIC GUIDE

Albumin-adjusted calcium below the reference range in adults 18+. Not for children or neonates, pregnancy (specialist advice), dialysis (renal team) or hospital IV treatment.

Symptoms usually start below about 1.9 mmol/L but depend on how fast calcium falls. Correct low magnesium and potassium too: they add to the arrhythmia risk.

1Confirm it is realuse local laboratory ranges

  • Low albumin lowers total calcium while ionised calcium is normal: read the laboratory's adjusted calcium, never the total alone.
  • EDTA contamination (wrong tube order or decanting) gives a spuriously very low calcium with a very high potassium: repeat before acting if the patient is well.
  • Hyperventilation (alkalosis) lowers ionised calcium and causes tingling with a normal total.
  • Ionised calcium (blood gas) if unwell, critically ill, or the result does not fit the picture.
  • When to repeat: symptoms or <1.90 → do not wait for a repeat; 1.90–2.09 → same-day repeat with assessment; mild and well → repeat with the work-up bloods. Never prescribe calcium on one total calcium.

Normal range is laboratory-specific, commonly about 2.20–2.60 mmol/L. In acute pancreatitis a calcium <2.0 marks severe disease (Glasgow–Imrie).

High PTH = the parathyroids are responding (secondary). Low or inappropriately normal PTH = the parathyroids are failing (hypoparathyroidism): specialist territory.

5Work-up checklistsend together

  • Adjusted Ca + albumin: confirm
  • Intact PTH: the branch point
  • Magnesium: low Mg blocks PTH
  • Phosphate: high in hypopara, CKD
  • 25-OH vitamin D
  • U&E, eGFR: CKD
  • ALP: raised in vitamin D deficiency
  • ECG if symptomatic, acute or severe
  • Coeliac serology if malabsorption
  • Drug history: denosumab, PPI

25-OH vitamin D: <25 nmol/L deficient · 25–50 may be inadequate in some · >50 sufficient. 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D is specialist-directed.

Vitamin D risk groups: darker skin, housebound or care home, pregnancy and breastfeeding, malabsorption, anticonvulsants or rifampicin.

7Self-care and the conversationNHS · ROS

Diet: 700 mg calcium a day for adults (milk, cheese, yoghurt, fortified plant milks, tinned fish with bones, calcium-set tofu). Vitamin D: everyone should consider 10 micrograms (400 IU) daily in autumn and winter; all year if rarely outdoors. Sources: oily fish, egg yolk, fortified foods.

"Your calcium is a bit low, most likely because you are short of vitamin D, which helps you absorb calcium. The vitamin D course should fix it, and we will recheck your blood in a few weeks. If you get tingling round your mouth, cramps or twitching hands, get seen the same day."

9Follow-up and monitoringSPC · MHRA · specialist plan

  • Mild: adjusted Ca, PTH, phosphate at 2–4 weeks; target ≥2.1; stop calcium when the cause is fixed and intake is adequate
  • After loading: 25-OH vitamin D only if adherence, malabsorption or severe deficiency is a concern
  • Denosumab: Ca, eGFR, vitamin D before starting; Ca before each dose; extra check within 2 weeks of the first dose if predisposed (CrCl <30, dialysis)
  • Prescribe calcium and vitamin D alongside denosumab
  • Hypoparathyroidism: Ca, eGFR, urine calcium per specialist plan; dose changes specialist-led
  • After neck surgery: follow the operating team's plan

11Safety-net and special groupsSociety for Endocrinology · BTA

  • 999: hands or feet go into spasm · noisy or difficult breathing · fit · palpitations with fainting. Same day: new tingling round the mouth or fingertips, cramps, or twitching.
  • After thyroidectomy: tingling in the first days is low calcium until proven otherwise; seizure in the first 24–48 hours is hypocalcaemia until proven otherwise. Hypoparathyroidism: if calcium or calcitriol cannot be kept down (vomiting, diarrhoea), contact the endocrine team or attend hospital urgently; no self-increase of calcitriol; carry a medical alert card.

12Pitfalls and never-eventsMHRA · SPC

  • Never give oral calcium alone for Ca <1.90 or symptoms · never assess tingling or a Ca <2.10 by telephone only · never miss magnesium in "resistant" hypocalcaemia.
  • Never start denosumab with uncorrected hypocalcaemia or vitamin D deficiency (MHRA).
  • Do not treat a low total calcium caused by low albumin · do not start alfacalcidol automatically in CKD G3b.
  • Calcium carbonate taken with a PPI, fasting, is poorly absorbed · take a bisphosphonate on an empty stomach, apart from calcium (SPC).
