1Is the result real?BSH 2014 · CKS

Recent meal or supplementSerum folate reflects the last few days' intake: a normal level can hide chronic deficiency, and a low one may follow a few poor days
Haemolysed sampleRed cells are rich in folate: haemolysis can falsely raise serum folate. Repeat if the result does not fit
Already on folic acidThe level means little: do not use it to monitor
B12 deficiencyCan distort folate results: always read B12 and folate together
Red-cell folateNot routinely needed in primary care

A mildly low folate in a well person with a normal FBC: dietary advice and a repeat test is reasonable; treat if the cause persists or there is anaemia.

5Work-up checklistCKS · NG239 · NG20

  • B12 (total or active): before folic acid where feasible
  • FBC + MCV, reticulocytes
  • Blood film if macrocytosis or cytopenias
  • Ferritin: coeliac and diet cause both
  • tTG IgA + total IgA while eating gluten
  • LFTs + GGT: alcohol, liver
  • TFTs if macrocytosis unexplained
  • U&E; CRP if inflammation suspected

Ask about diet, alcohol, bowel symptoms, medicines, pregnancy plans and haemolytic disease. Examine mouth (glossitis, angular cheilitis), nodes, liver and spleen, and vibration and joint position sense.

6Reading the result in four stepsCKS · NG239

1 · Unwell or neurological?Neurology, pancytopenia, sepsis or Wernicke features → same day or 999 first.
2 · What is the B12?Low or unknown with neurology → treat B12 first. Never start folic acid alone in a macrocytic patient with an unknown B12.
3 · Why?Diet and alcohol are commonest; always consider coeliac disease and medicines, especially methotrexate.
4 · Who else needs folic acid?Pregnancy planning, high neural tube risk, chronic haemolysis, methotrexate.

8Self-care and the conversationCKS · NG247

Eat leafy greens, broccoli, pulses and fortified cereals; steam rather than boil · cut down alcohol · anyone who could become pregnant: take folic acid before conception · come back the same day with tingling, numbness, unsteadiness, fever or bleeding.

"Your folate — a B vitamin your body needs to make blood cells — is low. We've checked your B12 too, because the two go together and the order of treatment matters. A course of tablets for a few months usually sorts it, but we also need to find out why it dropped."

9Follow-up and monitoring checklistCKS · SPS

  • B12 result seen before or alongside the first folic acid prescription
  • Recheck FBC within about 8 weeks if there was anaemia or macrocytosis
  • Target: Hb and MCV back to normal, symptoms gone
  • Do not recheck serum folate while on folic acid
  • No response → B12, iron, haemolysis, alcohol, thyroid, MDS, adherence, malabsorption
  • Stop after the course once corrected and the cause is fixed; continue if the cause persists
  • Methotrexate: folic acid on every repeat; FBC, LFTs, U&E per shared-care protocol
  • Coeliac: annual review with FBC, ferritin, B12, folate

10Who to test, and when to think againCKS · NG20 · NG247

Test whenMacrocytosis or anaemia, sore tongue or mouth, unexplained fatigue with poor diet, alcohol misuse, malabsorption.
Do not test to decidepregnancy folic acid: everyone planning pregnancy needs it, whatever the level. No routine folate test at booking.
CoeliacLow folate with low iron is a classic pattern: test tTG IgA while still eating gluten; refer if positive.
No responseFBC not improving → recheck B12, iron and adherence; consider haemolysis, hypothyroidism, MDS; haematology advice.

12Pitfalls and never-eventsNG239 · NG247 · MHRA

  • Never give folic acid alone to a macrocytic or neurologically symptomatic patient before B12 is known: it can mask B12 deficiency while nerve damage progresses.
  • Never prescribe methotrexate without weekly folic acid.
  • Never wait for a folate result before starting pregnancy folic acid.
  • Folic acid does not remove the teratogenic risk of valproate: follow the MHRA pregnancy prevention programme.
  • Do not stop antiepileptics abruptly; plan pregnancy with neurology.
  • Severe malnutrition: thiamine before refeeding.

13Special groupsNG247 · SPS · MHRA · CG100

PregnancyLow folate alone can be managed in primary care if well: supplement and check B12, diet, coeliac and adherence. Same-day maternity review if severe anaemia, vomiting or another concern.
MethotrexateCheck folic acid at every repeat. Toxicity (ulcers, cytopenias, sepsis, renal decline) → withhold, urgent bloods, specialist today. Folinic acid rescue is specialist-led.
AntiepilepticsReview folate in those on phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital or primidone, especially if planning pregnancy. Valproate: pregnancy prevention programme.
Alcohol, malnutritionAUDIT-C and brief intervention. Wernicke features → 999 with parenteral thiamine. Severe malnutrition → thiamine before refeeding; dietitian.
