1Assess — every infantNG1 · QS112

  • Effortless or forceful? Colour (green, blood)?
  • Age at onset; getting worse?
  • Feeds: type, volume, frequency, technique
  • Intake in mL/kg/day against weight
  • Distress, back arching, feed refusal
  • Weight plotted against the centiles
  • Hydration, wet nappies, general state
  • Abdomen, fontanelle, tone, temperature
  • Eczema, blood or mucus in stool, diarrhoea
  • Atopy in the family

2GOR or GORD?NICE NG1 §1.1

GOR (physiological): effortless regurgitation in a well baby; usually starts before 8 weeks, may be frequent, and resolves in 90% before age 1. No investigation or treatment needed.

GORD: reflux with marked, persistent distress, feeding difficulty or refusal, faltering growth, or another complication.

Do not investigate or treat as GORD for one symptom without overt regurgitation (crying, feeding difficulty, faltering growth, chronic cough or hoarseness alone).

4Reassurance is the treatmentNG1 §1.3

  • Explain that reflux is common, normal and gets better without treatment, usually by the first birthday.
  • It is not caused by the parents and not a sign of illness in a growing baby.
  • Arrange review if new concerns: marked distress, feeding problems, faltering growth, or not settling by 1 year.
  • Holding upright after feeds is fine while the baby is awake and watched.
  • Sleep: always on the back, flat, in a clear cot. No wedges, raised cot ends, side or front sleeping.

"Bringing up milk is really common in happy, growing babies. It's not something you're doing wrong, and it nearly always settles by their first birthday."

5Feeding steps — in orderNG1 §1.3 · QS112 statements 2–4

1 · Review feedsFeeding history, technique, teat flow. Reduce feed volumes only if excessive for weight.
2 · Smaller, more oftenSmaller, more frequent feeds while keeping an appropriate total daily amount.
3 · Thickened formulaThickened formula as a trial (thickened with rice starch, cornstarch, locust bean gum or carob bean gum).
4 · AlginateIf unsuccessful, stop the thickener and try an alginate for 1–2 weeks.

Breastfed: a breastfeeding assessment by a trained person first; if distress continues, alginate trial for 1–2 weeks.

Alginate helped → continue, but try stopping at intervals. Never thickener and alginate together. Check the product's sodium content and instructions.

6Acid suppression — only to NICE criteriaNG1 §1.3 · updated Oct 2019

Do not offerConsiderThen
A PPI or H2RA for overt regurgitation alone, or for crying or distress alone, in an otherwise well infantA 4-week trial of a PPI or H2RA if overt regurgitation plus unexplained feeding difficulty, distressed behaviour or faltering growthReview at 4 weeks: no clear benefit → stop. Symptoms persist or come back after stopping → consider specialist referral (possible endoscopy)

Before a trial: rule out overfeeding, finish the feeding steps, consider CMPA. A failed trial means stop and rethink, not a higher dose or a second drug. Crying, feeding difficulty or faltering growth without visible regurgitation needs another explanation, not a PPI.

Dose by weight and age; licensing varies by product and formulation — check the SPC and your local formulary.

9Before any medicineNG1 §1.3

  • Red flags rechecked — forceful, green or bloody vomit
  • Overfeeding excluded: intake checked against weight
  • Feeding steps done in order, one at a time
  • Overt regurgitation present, not distress alone
  • CMPA considered if allergic or GI features
  • Indication, trial length and review date recorded

10Problems and fixesNG1 · SPC

Thickener and alginate both in useStop one: they are used one at a time
Feeds cut too farCheck total daily intake for weight; faltering growth risk
Alginate continued for monthsTry stopping; most reflux settles
PPI on repeat with no reviewReview the indication; stop if no clear benefit
Reflux medicine requested for a thriving babyExplain why it won't help posseting; agree the feeding plan
Parents exhaustedAcknowledge it; health visitor support; agree a review date

11Review checklistat each review until settled

  • Weight plotted and compared with the last
  • Regurgitation: frequency, volume, effortless or forceful
  • Distress, feed refusal or aversion
  • Feeding step reached and whether it helped
  • Alginate: still needed? Try stopping
  • PPI/H2RA: review at 4 weeks — benefit, or stop
  • New allergic or GI features → think CMPA
  • Red flags and safe sleep reinforced with parents
  • Still regurgitating at 1 year → reconsider the diagnosis

13Refer and never-eventsNG1 · MHRA DSU Dec 2019

  • Same day: red-flag vomiting (green, projectile under 2 months, blood), dehydration, unwell or febrile infant.
  • Paediatrics: persistent faltering growth with overt regurgitation; feeding aversion; no response to, or relapse after, a 4-week PPI/H2RA trial; still regurgitating after 1 year.
  • Specialist (possible endoscopy): haematemesis not explained by swallowed blood (same day if unwell).
  • Domperidone: no longer licensed under 12 years or under 35 kg (MHRA 2019). Metoclopramide is contraindicated under 1 year (MHRA 2013). No prokinetic without specialist paediatric agreement (NG1).
  • Never: acid suppression for posseting or crying alone · thickener plus alginate · cot tilting or wedges · indefinite PPI repeats.

15Safety-net — in the parents' wordsNG1 red flags · QS112

Sinister vomiting"Green or yellow-green vomit, blood in the vomit, or forceful vomiting that is getting worse — get seen the same day."
Not feeding or growing"Refusing feeds, keeps vomiting, fewer wet nappies, or not gaining weight — contact us the same day."
Unwell baby"Floppy, very hard to wake, pauses in breathing, turning blue, a fit — call 999. Fever, a bulging soft spot or swollen tummy — same day."
Expected course"In a happy, growing baby this almost always settles by the first birthday. Come back if it hasn't."
