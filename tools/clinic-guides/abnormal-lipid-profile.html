1Is it real? Confirm before you labelNG238 · CG71

Fasting or notA non-fasting full profile is enough for risk assessment. Fasting matters only for TG: repeat fasting if TG is ≥4.5 mmol/L or the result is unexpected.
Repeat firstNever label on one result. FH needs at least 2 LDL measurements after secondary causes are excluded.
Wrong momentAcute illness, infection, surgery or a recent MI lowers cholesterol for a time; pregnancy raises it. Recheck when well.
LDL unreliableCalculated LDL fails when TG is above 4.5 mmol/L: use non-HDL (total minus HDL) or a direct LDL.

5Work-up checklistNG238 · QS100

  • Lipids: TC, HDL, non-HDL, TG
  • TSH
  • HbA1c
  • U&E, eGFR + urine ACR
  • LFTs (ALT, GGT)
  • BMI, waist, BP, smoking
  • Alcohol (AUDIT-C)
  • Drug review
  • Family history of premature CHD
  • QRISK3, only where valid

6Is this FH?CG71 · NG238

Suspect: TC >7.5 or LDL >4.9 (adult) and/or CHD <60 in patient or first-degree relative. No QRISK3.

Simon BroomeTC >7.5 or LDL >4.9, plus
DefiniteTendon xanthomata (patient or 1st/2nd-degree relative) or DNA-confirmed
PossibleMI in a 1st-degree relative <60 or 2nd-degree <50, or TC >7.5 in an adult relative

Possible/definite or DLCN >5 → FH service: DNA, cascade testing. Search records: <30, TC >7.5; 30+, TC >9.0.

8Targets and next stepsNG238 · CG71

GroupTarget at 2–3 months
Primary prevention>40% fall in non-HDL from baseline
Secondary preventionLDL ≤2.0 or non-HDL ≤2.6 mmol/L
FH≥50% fall in LDL from the untreated baseline

Not at target: adherence → lifestyle → secondary cause → step up the statin → ezetimibe (full ladder in the High cholesterol protocol).

Lifestyle, alongside a statin: Mediterranean-style diet; unsaturated for saturated fat; no plant sterols or stanols for CVD prevention; 150 min/week activity; alcohol ≤14 units/week; stop smoking.

Low HDL: no specific drug; address smoking, inactivity, weight, insulin resistance and TG. A high HDL does not cancel a raised LDL or non-HDL.

Mixed pattern (raised LDL or non-HDL and TG): exclude diabetes, hypothyroidism, alcohol and drugs; use non-HDL, not calculated LDL; premature CVD in the family → think familial combined hyperlipidaemia → lipid clinic.

Raised TG: manage on the High triglycerides guide.

9Follow-up and monitoringNG238 · NHS England

  • Lipids 2–3 months after starting or changing treatment
  • Transaminases: baseline, within 3 months and at 12 months; then only if indicated
  • ALT raised but <3× ULN: not a reason to stop or withhold; stop only if it stays >3× ULN
  • CK only with muscle symptoms. Baseline >5× ULN → repeat after 7 days; still >5× → do not start
  • Severe muscle pain, weakness or dark urine → stop the statin; same-day CK and renal function
  • On treatment: CK 4–10× ULN or intolerable → stop 4–6 weeks, rechallenge; >10× ULN → urgent; <4× → look for other causes
  • Baseline CK raised but <5× ULN → start at a lower dose
  • Annual medicines review ± non-fasting non-HDL
  • Not on a statin: reassess QRISK3 and lipids periodically

11Special groupsCG71 · NG238

  • Pregnancy: lipids rise normally, so do not interpret them; statins are not used in pregnancy — stop them before conception.
  • FH family: relatives, including children of a parent with FH, are tested through the specialist FH service.
  • HIV: QRISK3 underestimates risk; check antiretroviral interactions before choosing a statin.
  • Severe mental illness, antipsychotics: risk underestimated; lipids often worsen on treatment.

12Pitfalls and never-eventsNG238 · CG71

  • Never run QRISK3 in FH, established CVD, CKD or type 1 diabetes.
  • Never trust calculated LDL when TG is above 4.5 mmol/L.
  • Never stop a statin for a mild ALT rise, or check CK routinely.
  • Never label on one result taken during acute illness.
  • Do not miss hypothyroidism, nephrotic syndrome or cholestasis behind a high cholesterol.

13Say it like thisplain English

"Your cholesterol is one part of your risk of a heart attack or stroke, not a disease on its own. We'll check it isn't caused by something else, like your thyroid, and work out your overall risk before deciding together whether a tablet would help."
