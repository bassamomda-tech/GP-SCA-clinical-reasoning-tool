Pancytopenia in adults CLINIC GUIDE

Low Hb, low white cells and low platelets together, in adults 18+. Not for children and young people (paediatric pathway), patients on chemotherapy (use the oncology hotline) or established aplastic anaemia or MDS care.
NICE NG12 · CG151 · NG239 (2024)

1Is it real?repeat within days if well

Low platelets only on analyserEDTA clumping gives false thrombocytopenia: check the film or repeat in a citrate tube
Clotted or delayed sampleCan lower platelets and distort the count: repeat
Recent viral illnessTransient falls are common: repeat FBC and film once well
Low neutrophils, Black or Middle Eastern heritageConstitutionally lower neutrophils are common, but explain only an isolated mild neutropenia, not pancytopenia
Alcohol, liver diseaseDirect marrow toxicity and splenic pooling: still investigate

A blood film reviewed by the laboratory is mandatory: the analyser cannot see blasts, dysplasia or hypersegmented neutrophils. Refer in parallel — do not wait for every result.

4Work-up checklistsend in parallel with referral

  • FBC + differential, reticulocytes
  • Blood film (laboratory review)
  • B12 + folate; ferritin
  • LFTs, bilirubin, LDH, urate
  • U&E, calcium, TFTs
  • CRP, ESR
  • HIV, hepatitis B and C, EBV, CMV
  • DAT if haemolysis possible
  • Blood cultures if febrile (hospital)
  • Coagulation if bleeding

Ask about onset, bleeding, infections, B symptoms, drugs, alcohol and travel. Examine for pallor, petechiae, nodes, liver, spleen, mouth ulcers and jaundice. Marrow biopsy is a hospital test.

6Reading the result in four stepsNG12 · CG151

1 · Septic or bleeding?Fever with low neutrophils → 999. Bleeding or platelets <20 → same day.
2 · What does the film show?Blasts or schistocytes → same day. Dysplasia → urgent haematology.
3 · Simple cause?B12, folate, drugs, alcohol, viral illness. Only a clear cause with a reassuring film stays GP-led.
4 · Nodes or spleen?Examine. Unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly → suspected cancer pathway referral.

7Management of the commonest causesNG239 · CKS · BSH · MHRA · SPC

CauseFirst lineThen
B12 deficiencyHydroxocobalamin 1 mg IM three times a week for 2 weeks, then every 2–3 months; neurological features: 1 mg on alternate days until no further improvement, then every 2 months, with specialist adviceRecheck FBC; all three lines should recover. No recovery → haematology
Folate deficiencyFolic acid 5 mg daily, usually for 4 months — only once B12 is normal or being replacedFind the cause: diet, alcohol, coeliac
Drug-inducedStop the drug now. Carbimazole: same-day advice; do not switch to propylthiouracil. Clozapine: act today with the monitoring service and mental health team (a red result means stop). Methotrexate: folinic acid rescue is specialist-ledSerial FBC with haematology; record the reaction; Yellow Card
AlcoholStop alcohol; replace B12 and folate if low; thiamine where indicatedRecheck FBC; refer if not recovering or spleen enlarged
Marrow failure, leukaemia, MDSHaematology-led: transfusion, growth factors, immunosuppression, chemotherapy or transplantGP: sepsis safety-net, vaccines, shared-care drugs

8Follow-up and monitoring checklistBSH · CG151

  • Film result read and actioned the same day
  • Referral sent in parallel; result chased; patient knows the plan
  • B12 or folate cause: recheck FBC; target all three lines normal; no recovery → refer
  • Drug stopped: serial FBC until recovery; allergy or adverse reaction recorded
  • Neutropenic patients: written safety-net and 24-hour haematology number recorded
  • Under haematology: shared-care drug monitoring per protocol
  • No live vaccines when immunocompromised; inactivated vaccines are fine
  • Mood, fatigue and fertility questions addressed

10Who needs an FBC — and who needs it fastNICE NG12

Adults: very urgent FBCWithin 48 hours for pallor, persistent fatigue, unexplained fever, persistent or recurrent infection, generalised lymphadenopathy, unexplained bruising, bleeding or petechiae, hepatosplenomegaly.
Count says leukaemiaFBC or film reported as acute leukaemia, at any age → immediate specialist assessment (standard practice, not an NG12 criterion).
Children and young peopleUnder 25 with unexplained petechiae or hepatosplenomegaly → immediate specialist assessment (paediatric or young adult pathway).
LymphomaAdults with unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly → consider a suspected cancer pathway referral.

11Special groupsCG151 · MHRA · SPC

On chemotherapyExpected low counts: any fever or feeling unwell → the 24-hour oncology or haematology hotline or 999, not GP telephone advice alone.
Antithyroid drugsCounsel on carbimazole to report sore throat, mouth ulcers or fever: urgent FBC, stop if neutropenic.
ClozapineMandatory monitoring via the brand service (CPMS, DMS or ZTAS; WBC and neutrophils, not ANC-only); low counts → act today on its instructions (red → stop).
TravelFever after malaria-area travel → same-day malaria testing. Jaundice, big liver and spleen after travel → think leishmaniasis; specialist advice.

12Pitfalls and never-eventsNG12 · NG239 · CG151

  • Never treat a febrile neutropenic patient with oral antibiotics at home.
  • Never attribute pancytopenia to B12 or folate without a film, or give folic acid alone.
  • Never continue carbimazole or methotrexate, or leave a clozapine count unactioned, while the cause is unclear.
  • Do not wait for all first-line bloods before referring unexplained pancytopenia.
  • A "mild" stable pancytopenia in an older person can still be MDS.
  • Avoid NSAIDs and aspirin while platelets are low; plan dental extractions with haematology.

13Self-care and the conversationCG151 · BSH

Fever, shivering or feeling very unwell → 999 or straight to hospital · new bruises, spots, bleeding gums or breathlessness → contact us the same day · avoid NSAIDs, aspirin and contact sports · good food hygiene and handwashing.

"All three types of blood cell are lower than they should be. That can come from something simple, like a vitamin shortage, but it can also mean the bone marrow — the body's blood factory — isn't working properly, so I'm asking the blood specialists to look at it quickly. Until we know, a temperature or feeling shivery means hospital straight away."
