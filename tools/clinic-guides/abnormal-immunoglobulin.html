Abnormal immunoglobulins CLINIC GUIDE

Adults 18+ with a paraprotein, abnormal serum free light chains (FLC), polyclonal rise or low immunoglobulins. Not for children, known myeloma under active treatment (haematology-led) or established CVID care (immunology-led).

5Work-up checklistNG12 · NG35 · BSH 2023

  • SPEP + immunofixation: class, light chain
  • Serum FLC κ, λ and ratio
  • FBC, film if cytopenias
  • Adjusted calcium, albumin
  • Creatinine, eGFR
  • Quantitative IgG, IgA, IgM
  • ESR or plasma viscosity
  • Urine BJP only if FLC unavailable
  • Urine PCR if low IgG or oedema
  • tTG IgA + total IgA together

Imaging is arranged by haematology: whole-body MRI first-line, low-dose CT if unsuitable; no isotope bone scan (NG35).

6MGUS: putting risk in proportionMayo model · BSH 2023

Most incidental bands are MGUS. Progression to myeloma or a related disorder is about 1% a year; most people never progress.

Risk factor (Mayo)Why it matters
Paraprotein ≥15 g/LLarger clone
Non-IgG (IgA, IgM)IgM progresses to lymphoma or Waldenström
Abnormal FLC ratioClone making excess light chain

Original Mayo data: 20-year progression about 5% with none of these, about 58% with all three. Haematology sets the risk group and the follow-up plan.

7Incidental paraprotein — the four stepsNG35 · BSH 2023 · Myeloma UK

1 · Type itImmunofixation (class, κ or λ) and quantify in g/L; serum FLC and ratio, renal range if CKD.
2 · Look for CRABFBC, adjusted calcium, creatinine; ask about bone or back pain, infections, weight loss, neuropathy.
3 · Choose the tierCRAB, FLC ratio ≥100, IgG >15, IgA/IgM >10, IgD/IgE → suspected cancer pathway. Otherwise routine.
4 · Follow the letterHaematology stages risk; GP monitors only if delegated, with clear re-referral triggers.

9Follow-up and monitoring checklistBSH 2023 · Myeloma UK

  • Haematology plan on file: risk group, tests, interval
  • Recheck: paraprotein, FBC, calcium, creatinine, FLC if in plan
  • Same laboratory platform; record the trend
  • Re-refer: rise >25% and ≥5 g/L, new anaemia, renal decline, high Ca, bone pain
  • Also re-refer: neuropathy, nephrotic syndrome, HF, macroglossia, purpura (MGCS)
  • Stop routine monitoring only on haematology advice or when no longer in the patient's interest
  • Flu, pneumococcal and Shingrix per Green Book if immunosuppressed
  • Safety-net: back pain with leg weakness or bladder change → 999

10Safety-net for the patientBSH 2023 · Myeloma UK

  • Contact us promptly, not at the next test: new bone or back pain, thirst or passing more urine, unusual tiredness, repeated infections, foamy urine or swelling.
  • 999 or A&E: back pain with leg weakness, numbness, or loss of bladder or bowel control.
  • Myeloma UK has plain-English MGUS information.

12Shared care after a myeloma diagnosisNG35 · MHRA · Green Book

  • Lenalidomide: VTE prophylaxis as the haematology protocol sets; Pregnancy Prevention Programme run by the haematology centre.
  • Infection: treat promptly; no live vaccines on active treatment; prophylaxis as haematology advises.
  • Steroids: check glucose and BP. Bone treatment: renal function before each zoledronic acid dose; dental care, ONJ awareness.
  • Kidneys: hydration; avoid NSAIDs. Early advance care planning; Myeloma UK and Macmillan support.

13Pitfalls and never-eventsNG35 · BSH 2023 · NG20

  • Never reassure on a normal SPEP alone when myeloma is suspected: add serum FLC. Never diagnose myeloma from the paraprotein size or an isolated abnormal FLC ratio.
  • Never read an FLC ratio in CKD against the standard range · never trust a negative tTG IgA without a total IgA · never delay life-saving transfusion for IgA-deficient components.
  • Never start bisphosphonate or denosumab for suspected myeloma in primary care · never lose a delegated MGUS patient from recall · avoid NSAIDs and iodinated contrast when renal function is at risk.
  • Never assume every IgA-deficient patient needs IgA-deficient blood (only after a previous severe reaction, with transfusion medicine) · never label selective IgA deficiency as CVID · no universal IgG target in CVID.

14Say it like thisMGUS

"The blood test found a protein made by one family of immune cells. In most people this stays harmless for life, and it is not cancer. We check it with blood tests so that we would spot the small number of people where it starts to change."
