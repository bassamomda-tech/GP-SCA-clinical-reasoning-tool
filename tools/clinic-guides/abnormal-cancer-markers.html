Raised tumour markers CLINIC GUIDE

Adults 18+ with a raised PSA, CA125, CEA, AFP, hCG or other tumour marker in primary care. Not for children, population screening programmes or specialist surveillance decisions (follow the written oncology plan).

Isolated CEA, AFP, CA19-9 or LDH values are not referral criteria. Markers rarely diagnose cancer alone: symptoms, examination, FIT and imaging drive the decision.

4Work-up checklistNG12 · NG131 · CG122

  • Why was it sent? Symptoms, history
  • Examination: abdomen, pelvis, DRE
  • Urine dip ± MSU before PSA
  • Repeat PSA only if confounded
  • Urgent USS abdomen and pelvis if CA125 at or above threshold
  • FIT for bowel symptoms
  • LFTs with CEA, AFP or CA19-9
  • Pregnancy test with hCG
  • FBC, film if haematological
  • Family history; BRCA, Lynch

7Suspected ovarian cancer — the NG12 routeNG12 (2026) · CG122

Examine firstAscites or a pelvic or abdominal mass → suspected cancer pathway now; do not wait for CA125.
Aged 40+Persistent or frequent symptoms → CA125. At or above the NG12 (2026) age threshold → urgent direct-access USS.
Aged 39 or underDo not use CA125 alone; consider urgent USS of abdomen and pelvis for persistent symptoms.
USS resultSuggests cancer → suspected cancer pathway. Normal → other causes, safety-net, review date.

8Follow-up and documentation checklistNG12 · specialist plans

  • Result, units and range recorded with the reason for testing
  • Benign causes and confounders considered
  • Action, destination, urgency and who reviews the result
  • Referral seen and outcome received; system flag for review
  • Planned review within a time frame agreed with the person (NG12)
  • Surveillance follows the written specialist plan; tests happen
  • Stop repeat markers that have no indication or plan
  • Safety-net: return if symptoms persist, change or worsen

10PSA in men without symptomsPCRMP · UK NSC

  • No national PSA screening programme. Offer an informed discussion: false positives, overdiagnosis, MRI or biopsy may follow.
  • A man aged 50+ who still wants a test after counselling may have one.
  • Weigh age, ethnicity, family history, BRCA2, comorbidity and life expectancy. Known BRCA2 or strong family history → specialist or clinical genetics advice.

12Pitfalls and never-eventsNG12 · CG122 · NG131 · OV05

  • Never use tumour markers to screen people without symptoms (except informed PSA) · never refer, or reassure, on an isolated CEA, CA19-9, AFP or LDH.
  • Never wait for CA125 when there is ascites or a mass · never let a normal CA125 or negative FIT override persistent symptoms · never use CA125 alone under 40.
  • Never test PSA during active UTI or prostatitis · do not arrange a bone scan just because PSA is above 10 · PSA velocity, density and free-to-total ratio are secondary-care tools.
  • After ovarian cancer, a CA125 rise alone does not trigger retreatment (MRC OV05): pass it to oncology without alarm.

13Say it like thisraised marker

"This blood test can be raised for lots of reasons, and most of them are not cancer. On its own it can't tell us what is going on, so I'd like to arrange the right scan or referral to find out properly, and I'll make sure we follow it up."
