1Confirm it is realJBDS · specialist practice

  • In diabetes: treat any capillary glucose <4.0 mmol/L with symptoms now; no lab confirmation needed.
  • Without diabetes: never diagnose from a meter alone. Document Whipple's triad: symptoms, a low laboratory glucose at the time, and relief when it is corrected.
  • Pseudohypoglycaemia: meters are least accurate at low values; cold or poorly perfused fingers; sugar on the skin; a venous sample left unseparated keeps using glucose (fluoride tube needed); CGM lags and can read falsely low when the sensor is lain on.
  • Mimics: anxiety, arrhythmia, TIA or stroke, seizure.
  • When to repeat: recheck capillary glucose 10–15 minutes after each treatment. Non-diabetic: take the lab sample during the next episode, before treatment, only if safe.

5Work-up checklistJBDS · NG17 · NG28

  • Capillary glucose: treat first
  • HbA1c: is it too low?
  • U&E, eGFR: drug clearance
  • LFTs, GGT: liver, alcohol
  • Injection sites: lipohypertrophy
  • CGM or diary: timing pattern
  • Gold score (awareness)
  • Cortisol ± ACTH if Addisonian

No diabetes, during a spontaneous episode and before treatment if safe: lab glucose, insulin, C-peptide, proinsulin, β-hydroxybutyrate, sulfonylurea screen. A 72-hour fast, mixed-meal test and imaging are specialist-only.

7Treating the episodeJBDS (2023) · SPC

1 · Conscious, can swallow15–20 g fast-acting glucose: glucose tablets (count by product), 150–200 mL fruit juice, or a 60 mL glucose shot. Check drink labels: some have been reformulated with less sugar.
2 · Recheck at 10–15 minStill <4.0 → repeat, up to 3 cycles. Can swallow but cannot eat: 40% glucose gel into the cheek.
3 · Once >4.0Long-acting carbohydrate: a slice of bread, two biscuits, or the next meal if due. Not chocolate (fat slows absorption).
Cannot swallow999, nothing by mouth. Glucagon 1 mg IM or 3 mg nasal by a trained carer; IV glucose by a skilled professional. Glucagon is less reliable with sulfonylureas, liver disease, alcohol or starvation.

On acarbose: use glucose (tablets, gel, solution), not sucrose. Never omit the next insulin dose; review it.

9Self-care and the conversationJBDS · NG17

Carry glucose at all times (car, bedside, work). Type 1: glucagon at home, carers trained, expiry checked. Alcohol: eat before and with it; check glucose overnight and before morning insulin. Exercise: check before, during and after; lows can come hours later.

"Your sugar dropped too low. Take something sugary straight away, check again after 15 minutes, then eat a snack like a sandwich or biscuits. Always carry glucose, and we will look at your tablets or insulin so this does not keep happening."

10Follow-up, driving and monitoringDVLA · NG17

  • After a severe episode: review cause, doses, glucagon training, CGM, driving
  • Annual: count level 1–3 events; Gold score; glucagon in date; carries glucose
  • Driving, insulin: check glucose within 2 hours before driving and every 2 hours on a journey; glucose in the car
  • Do not drive during or straight after a hypo; wait the DVLA recovery period
  • Group 2 (HGV, bus): full awareness, no severe hypo in 12 months; stricter checks
  • Give written DVLA advice and record it
  • Sulfonylurea: eGFR and HbA1c at least yearly; avoid glibenclamide in older people

12Pitfalls and never-eventsJBDS · MHRA

  • Never give oral glucose to someone who cannot swallow · never send home a high-risk sulfonylurea hypo after one sugary drink.
  • Never stop basal insulin in type 1 on sick days · SGLT2 inhibitor: stop in acute illness; check ketones if DKA suspected, even with normal glucose (MHRA).
  • Do not diagnose non-diabetic hypoglycaemia on a meter reading, or image before biochemistry.
  • Slow recovery after correction is not "hypo brain damage" until stroke, seizure, sepsis, intoxication and other causes are excluded.

13Safety-net and sick daysJBDS · MHRA · TREND-UK

  • 999 (tell carers): cannot swallow, unconscious or fitting; still low after 3 treatments; not waking up properly once glucose is normal. Same day: a hypo on a sulfonylurea that needed help or keeps coming back; new confusion or weakness.
  • Sick days: type 1 never stops basal insulin (urgent advice if vomiting, not eating or ketones) · insulin changes in type 2 are individualised · sulfonylurea: withhold or seek advice when not eating · pumps: never stop without a basal-insulin plan · test glucose more often.
  • Older or frail on insulin or a sulfonylurea: more lows, fewer warnings; review at least yearly and de-intensify early.
