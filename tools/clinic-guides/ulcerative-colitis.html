Ulcerative colitis in adults CLINIC GUIDE

The primary care role in known UC: flares, maintenance, monitoring and shared care. Adults 18+. Not for the first diagnosis (refer), children, or inpatient care of acute severe colitis.

Page 1 · Assess the flare and start grade it → exclude infection → treat by extent → review early

!Act todayTruelove and Witts · BSG 2025

  • Acute severe UC = ≥6 bloody stools a day plus any one of: temperature >37.8°C, HR >90, Hb <105 g/L, ESR >30 mm/h → same-day admission for IV steroids (BSG 2025). Phone the on-call gastroenterologist; do not wait for stool results.
  • Abdominal distension with systemic toxicity (toxic megacolon), peritonism or a rigid silent abdomen, or heavy bleeding with shock → 999.
  • Fever, sore throat or infection on a thiopurine or biologic → same-day FBC and review. Painful red eye with photophobia → same-day ophthalmology; pyoderma gangrenosum → same-day dermatology.
  • Avoid loperamide, codeine and other opioids in an active flare: they can precipitate toxic megacolon.

Extent (from the last colonoscopy) decides the route: proctitis (rectum only) · left-sided (to the splenic flexure) · extensive (beyond it). Ring the IBD nurse: many patients hold a written flare plan.

2Flare, infection or neither?NG199 · BSG 2025

  • Stool culture + C. difficile in every flare, ideally before steroids; never delay admission for them.
  • Ask: travel, food contacts, recent antibiotics or hospital stay, NSAIDs, stopped 5-ASA.
  • Confirmed C. difficile → oral vancomycin 125 mg QDS for 10 days (NG199) and IBD team advice.
  • On a thiopurine or biologic and not settling → specialist: CMV colitis is a mimic.
  • Inflammation or IBS overlap? Faecal calprotectin helps when stable; it never delays an admission.

4Work-up — every flareBSG 2025

  • Temp, HR, BP, weight
  • FBC, CRP (ESR if severe)
  • U&E/eGFR (hydration, 5-ASA)
  • LFTs + albumin
  • Ferritin and iron studies
  • Stool culture + C. difficile

Selectively: calprotectin · B12, folate, vitamin D · pregnancy test before treatment changes. Do not request colonoscopy in a severe flare from primary care (specialist flexible sigmoidoscopy).

UC is no exemption from NG12: rectal mass → consider a suspected cancer pathway referral (no FIT needed); change in bowel habit, iron-deficiency anaemia or abdominal mass → FIT, ≥10 µg Hb/g → suspected cancer pathway (HTG690). FIT can be positive from colitis alone, so discuss persistent concern with the IBD team.

6Induce remission — by extentNG130

ProctitisTopical 5-ASA (suppository). Not in remission at 4 weeks → add oral 5-ASA.
Left-sidedTopical 5-ASA. At 4 weeks → add high-dose oral 5-ASA, or switch to it + a short topical steroid.
ExtensiveTopical + high-dose oral 5-ASA. Not settling → time-limited oral steroid.
ModerateOral prednisolone, typically 40 mg OD with a planned taper; keep 5-ASA.

Prescribe 5-ASA by brand: formulations are not interchangeable gram for gram. Steroid courses are time-limited (NG130); never use them for maintenance. Budesonide MMX only for selected mild–moderate disease, never for a severe flare.

9Before calling it refractoryNG130 · BSG 2025

  • Adherence to 5-ASA — the commonest cause; once-daily dosing helps
  • Formulation and route — right brand, dose and topical technique for the extent
  • Infection — stool culture, C. difficile; CMV if immunosuppressed
  • NSAIDs, antibiotics, recently stopped smoking
  • Functional overlap — calprotectin normal? think IBS
  • Cancer — new pattern, weight loss or anaemia → NG12 (box 4)

10Annual review checklistevery patient, every year

  • Stool frequency, blood, urgency; steroid courses in the year
  • 5-ASA adherence and side effects; brand correct
  • FBC, ferritin, U&E/eGFR, LFTs; shared-care bloods up to date
  • Surveillance (not proctitis alone): first colonoscopy ~10 years after symptom onset (CG118; BSG 2025 says ~8); PSC → from PSC diagnosis, then annually
  • Bone: cumulative steroid exposure, vitamin D, fracture risk
  • Vaccines: flu, pneumococcal, COVID-19, shingles if eligible (Green Book)
  • Smoking, mood, fatigue, work; pregnancy plans and contraception
  • IBD nurse contact and written flare plan in place

11The maintenance ladderNG130 · BSG 2025 · thiopurines and advanced therapies are specialist-initiated

Step 1 · 5-ASAOral 5-ASA for left-sided and extensive UC; topical and/or oral for proctitis. Lowest effective dose; continue for most patients unless the IBD team advises otherwise.
Step 2 · thiopurine≥2 flares in 12 months needing systemic steroids, relapse on 5-ASA, or after one acute severe flare (NG130). TPMT before starting.
Step 3 · advanced therapyBiologic or small molecule chosen by the IBD team under the relevant NICE technology appraisal. GPs never start, stop or change the dose.
Step 4 · surgeryColectomy (pouch or ileostomy) for refractory disease, dysplasia or emergency. Curative for UC: present it as an option, not a failure.

12Side effects — and the fixSPC · MHRA

Rising creatinine (5-ASA)Discuss with the IBD team; stop if nephritis suspected
Low WCC or platelets (thiopurine)Withhold and phone the IBD team today
Itch without rash in pregnancy (thiopurine)Check for intrahepatic cholestasis (MHRA 2025)
Skin changes (thiopurine)Sun protection; low threshold to check skin lesions
Fever or infection (biologic)Same-day review; the usual signs may be blunted
Mood, sleep, glucose (steroid)Shorten the course with the IBD team; never stop abruptly

14Special groups and never-eventsMHRA · NG130 · BSG 2025

  • Pregnancy: aim for remission before conception; continue 5-ASA and thiopurines on specialist advice; methotrexate is contraindicated. Joint IBD–obstetric care.
  • Methotrexate (occasionally used in IBD): once weekly, with the day written on the prescription (MHRA).
  • Never: steroids as maintenance · a thiopurine without TPMT · antimotility drugs in a flare · repeat steroid courses without referral.
  • Pouch or ileostomy with new diarrhoea, urgency or bleeding → back to the pouch/IBD team; do not restart systemic UC treatment yourself.
  • Live vaccines on immunosuppression: check the Green Book and the IBD team first.
