Vitamin D deficiency CLINIC GUIDE

Low vitamin D with a high calcium: do not simply replace it. Think PHPT, malignancy, granulomatous disease (sarcoid, TB, lymphoma) and seek advice first.

1Confirm it is real — and neededROS · SACN · NG247

  • Test only when the result changes management: suspected osteomalacia, bone pain or proximal myopathy, malabsorption, persistent deficiency on treatment, complex osteoporosis, before IV zoledronate or denosumab per protocol.
  • Do not test well adults just because they are dark-skinned, housebound, older, pregnant or on an enzyme inducer: give 10 micrograms (400 IU) daily without a test.
  • Levels are seasonal (lowest after winter) and assays differ between laboratories: compare like with like.
  • 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D is not a test of deficiency; it is specialist-directed.
  • Unexpected result with a normal PTH and calcium → repeat before a loading course; check adherence and magnesium.

5Work-up checklistwhen testing is justified

  • 25-OH vitamin D
  • Adjusted calcium: before treating
  • Phosphate: low in osteomalacia
  • ALP: raised in osteomalacia
  • PTH: secondary HPT
  • eGFR: CKD
  • LFTs: liver disease
  • Magnesium if Ca low
  • Coeliac serology if malabsorption
  • X-ray if Looser zones suspected

Proximal myopathy: stand from a chair without using the arms, climb stairs, watch the gait.

6Before an antiresorptiveMHRA · SPC

  • Denosumab: correct hypocalcaemia and ensure adequate calcium and vitamin D first; monitor closely if eGFR <30.
  • IV zoledronate: renal function, calcium and vitamin D before the infusion.
  • Oral bisphosphonate: correct deficiency, but loading is not automatic if mild and treatment is urgent.

9Follow-up and monitoringROS · SPC

  • Adjusted calcium about 1 month after loading ends: it may unmask PHPT
  • No routine repeat 25-OH D in a well adult; consider at about 3 months if symptoms persist, malabsorption, osteomalacia, poor adherence
  • Target: >50 nmol/L is adequate for most
  • No blanket annual 25-OH D or PTH tests
  • PTH may lag: still high once replete → check Ca, eGFR; endocrinology
  • Stop high-dose treatment at the end of loading; continue maintenance while the risk persists, not automatically lifelong
  • Before denosumab or IV zoledronate: Ca, eGFR and vitamin D corrected

11Safety-net and special groupsSACN · NG247 · NHS

  • 999: a fit, severe muscle spasms, noisy or difficult breathing, or confusion. Same day: tingling round the mouth or fingers, or cramps; on high-dose vitamin D, nausea, vomiting, thirst or passing a lot of urine. Review within 2 weeks if bone pain or weakness persists or worsens.
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: 10 micrograms (400 IU) daily; do not test routinely or for a risk factor alone; Healthy Start vitamins are free for those eligible. Breastfed infants: 8.5–10 micrograms daily from birth; formula-fed babies taking more than 500 mL a day do not need drops.
  • Care homes and housebound: 10 micrograms daily all year, without testing. Enzyme-inducing antiepileptics: review supplementation and fracture risk; any drug change needs neurology input. Bariatric surgery: specialist protocol; calcium citrate is preferred.

12Pitfalls and never-eventsSPS · ROS

  • Never load without checking calcium and renal function · never replace vitamin D in hypercalcaemia or suspected sarcoid without advice.
  • Never leave a loading dose on repeat · never treat osteomalacia as "just a loading course".
  • Do not use alfacalcidol or calcitriol for simple deficiency, or in CKD G3a–G5 without renal advice.
  • Do not prescribe vitamin D just to prevent falls (NG249), infections or other non-bone conditions.
  • Prescribe by dose in IU or micrograms, not brand.

13Self-care and the conversationNHS · SACN

Few foods contain much vitamin D: oily fish, egg yolk, fortified spreads and cereals. Sunburn is never needed; supplements are safer. Tie a daily dose to an existing habit; set a reminder for weekly doses.

"Your vitamin D is low, which is very common in the UK because there isn't enough sunshine in winter. A short course of higher-dose tablets tops you up, then a small daily dose keeps you there. You don't need repeated blood tests unless you still feel unwell."
