Generalised anxiety and panic disorder CLINIC GUIDE

Page 1 · Assess and start worry or panic → right disorder → stepped care → first drug

!Act todayCG113 · NG225

  • Suicidal intent, a plan, or cannot keep safe → same-day crisis assessment; immediate danger → 999. Ask directly about intent, plan, means and protective factors. Do not use risk tools or scales to predict suicide (NG225): assess the person.
  • Chest pain that could be ACS → 999; do not label new or atypical chest symptoms as panic. Possible PE → assess on its own pathway.
  • Thyroid storm (hyperpyrexia, tachycardia, agitation, confusion) → 999. Alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal (tremor, sweating, confusion) → same-day assessment: seizure risk.
  • Psychosis, mania or severe self-neglect → same-day mental-health assessment.

1Diagnose clinically, measure with scoresCG113 · CG123

  • GAD: excessive, hard-to-control worry about several areas, most days for at least 6 months, with restlessness, fatigue, poor concentration, irritability, muscle tension or poor sleep, and distress or impairment.
  • Panic disorder: recurrent unexpected attacks peaking within minutes, then persistent fear of further attacks and changed behaviour.
  • GAD-2 ≥3 prompts assessment (CG123). GAD-7 measures severity and change; it does not diagnose.
  • Score PHQ-9 too: if depression coexists, treat the more severe (primary) disorder first.

2Work-up: targeted, not a panelby history and examination

  • TSH if thyroid features
  • ECG if palpitations, syncope or chest symptoms
  • Glucose if hypoglycaemic symptoms; FBC if anaemic
  • AUDIT-C; cannabis, stimulants
  • Caffeine and energy drinks
  • Medicines: salbutamol, steroids
  • Pregnancy; perimenopause (NG23)
  • Past mania, psychosis
  • Domestic abuse, safeguarding
  • Stressors: debt, housing, caring

Rare mimics (phaeochromocytoma, temporal-lobe epilepsy) only when the picture points there. Once a cardiac cause has been properly assessed, stop re-investigating typical recurrent panic.

5Stepped care for GADCG113 §1.2

Step 1 · allExplain the diagnosis and the worry cycle; active monitoring. Don't medicalise mild, recent worry.
Step 2 · low intensityGuided self-help, computerised CBT, psychoeducational groups. NHS Talking Therapies self-referral.
Step 3 · markedInadequate response or marked impairment: CBT or applied relaxation (12–15 weekly sessions), or an SSRI, by preference.
Step 4 · specialistComplex, refractory or high-risk: CMHT and specialist therapy or drug combinations.

7If medication is chosen: sertraline firstCG113 · SPC

Sertraline is the most cost-effective SSRI for GAD (off-label for GAD: explain and record). Start 25–50 mg once daily (the lower start if panic, frailty or past intolerance), then titrate per the SPC up to 200 mg. Use an SSRI licensed for panic disorder where you can.

Warn: anxiety and agitation can rise in the first 1–2 weeks; it passes. Under 30: small rise in suicidal thinking, so review within 1 week, then weekly for the first month.

Not benzodiazepines for routine GAD or panic; no antipsychotic for GAD in primary care.

9Before switching or referringsix checks

  • Adequate trial: right dose, actually taken, long enough
  • Mimics: thyroid, caffeine, alcohol, stimulants, cannabis withdrawal
  • Right diagnosis: panic, PTSD, OCD, social or health anxiety; bipolarity
  • Depression underneath, untreated
  • Therapy actually accessed, not drug alone
  • Self-medication with alcohol or benzodiazepines

11Review rhythm and what "better" meansCG113

WhenDo
Under 30See within 1 week, then weekly for the first month
First 3 monthsReview effect and side effects every 2–4 weeks; then every 3 months
Panic: no better by 12 weeksOffer an antidepressant from the other class, or another therapy
If effectiveGAD: continue for at least 1 year (high relapse risk). Panic: at least 6 months after the optimal dose

Success = restored function, not zero worry. Judge after an adequate dose for an adequate time; early switching is serial under-treatment.

12Annual review checklistand at every relapse

  • GAD-7 and PHQ-9, with the trend recorded
  • Function: work, relationships, what anxiety stops them doing
  • Suicidal thoughts asked about directly
  • Adherence, side effects; sexual dysfunction asked about
  • Alcohol (AUDIT-C), cannabis, caffeine
  • Therapy: referred, accepted, completed?
  • Pregabalin: dose creep, early requests, opioids or alcohol
  • Any benzodiazepine: plan to reduce and stop
  • Sodium if older, low weight or on diuretics with an SSRI
  • Pregnancy plans and contraception, especially on pregabalin
  • Relapse plan agreed; slow taper when stopping

15Never-events and special groupsCG113 · MHRA · CSM 1988

  • Benzodiazepines: not for routine GAD or panic; crisis only, lowest dose, 2–4 weeks, fixed quantity and end date.
  • Panic disorder: no benzodiazepines, sedating antihistamines or antipsychotics.
  • Pregabalin: Class C, Schedule 3 since April 2019. With opioids or other CNS depressants → respiratory depression. Pregnancy: possible small rise in malformations; use contraception (MHRA 2022).
  • Never stop pregabalin or an SNRI abruptly · start an SSRI without asking about past mania · leave an under-30 unreviewed for the first week.
