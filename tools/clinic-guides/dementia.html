Dementia CLINIC GUIDE

Page 1 · Recognise, diagnose and refer memory concern → reversible causes → memory service → support

!Act todayNG97 · CG103

  • Acute or fluctuating change in confusion, alertness or behaviour over hours to days → delirium until proven otherwise, including delirium on top of dementia: same-day assessment; admit if systemically unwell or unsafe. Look for infection, constipation, retention, dehydration, pain, hypoxia, new drugs.
  • Rapidly progressive decline over weeks to months → urgent neurology (prion and other rapid causes).
  • Lewy body or Parkinson's dementia: avoid antipsychotics, including haloperidol: they can worsen motor features and cause severe sensitivity reactions (NG97).
  • Safeguarding: neglect, financial abuse, unsafe driving or wandering → act the same day.

1Assess in primary careNG97 §1.2

  • History from the person and someone who knows them well: cognitive, behavioural and psychological symptoms, and the effect on daily life.
  • Medication review, especially anticholinergic burden; physical examination; blood and urine tests for reversible causes (box 2).
  • Use a validated brief structured cognitive instrument, for example:
10-CS10-point cognitive screener
6CIT6-item cognitive impairment test
6-item screener · MISMemory Impairment Screen
Mini-Cog · TYMTest Your Memory

A normal score does not rule out dementia. Hearing, language, education and anxiety all affect scores.

2Reversible-cause work-upNG97 · the usual screen

  • FBC
  • U&E, eGFR
  • LFTs
  • Calcium
  • Glucose
  • TFTs
  • Vitamin B12 and folate
  • Urine if delirium suspected
  • Hearing and vision
  • Depression, anxiety, sleep
  • Delirium screen (4AT as an aid)
  • Anticholinergic medicines

Further tests only on clinical suspicion. Record vascular risk factors: they matter for vascular and mixed dementia.

They coexist: delirium on dementia is common, and depression can mimic or accompany it.

4Refer to the memory serviceNG97 §1.2

  • Refer once reversible causes have been investigated and dementia is still suspected, for diagnosis and subtype.
  • Structural imaging is part of diagnosis unless dementia is well established and the subtype is clear (usually arranged by the specialist service).
  • Young-onset or atypical features → specialist referral; rapid decline → urgent neurology.
  • Suspected depression as the main problem → treat, then reassess.

8Cognitive enhancersNICE TA217 · NG97 §1.5 · initiated under local memory-service or shared-care arrangements

Mild–moderate ADDonepezil, galantamine or rivastigmine. Usually the one with the lowest acquisition cost.
Moderate ADMemantine if an AChE inhibitor is not tolerated or contraindicated. On an AChE inhibitor: consider adding memantine.
Severe ADMemantine; on an AChE inhibitor, offer memantine as an add-on. Do not stop an AChE inhibitor because of severity alone.
DLB · PDD · otherDLB: donepezil or rivastigmine; memantine if not tolerated. PDD: AChE inhibitor. Not for vascular alone or FTD.
DrugHow givenMonitorWatch
DonepezilOnce daily at night; start low, step up after about a month (SPC)Pulse, weight, benefitNausea, diarrhoea, vivid dreams (take in the morning), cramps, bradycardia
RivastigmineCapsules twice daily or a daily patch; titrate per SPCWeight, pulseGI effects, weight loss; patch skin reactions; re-titrate after a gap of more than 3 days
GalantamineModified-release once daily with food; titrate per SPCPulse; renal and hepatic functionNausea, dizziness, bradycardia; avoid in severe renal or hepatic impairment
MemantineOnce daily; weekly step-up per SPCRenal function (dose by CrCl), BPDizziness, headache, constipation, drowsiness; caution in epilepsy

Already on an AChE inhibitor with established AD: primary care may add memantine without specialist advice (NG97). Lecanemab, donanemab: MHRA-licensed 2024, not recommended by NICE; reappraisal paused July 2026 for commercial talks. Not routinely on the NHS: check the current position.

9Before starting or continuing an AChE inhibitorNG97 · SPC

  • Pulse: bradycardia or heart block → ECG and advice
  • Syncope or falls, conduction disease
  • Rate-limiting drugs: beta-blockers, verapamil, diltiazem, digoxin
  • Peptic ulcer or NSAID; asthma or COPD (cholinergic effects)
  • Decline? Exclude reversible causes before accepting progression

11Anticholinergic burdenNG97

Review at diagnosis and every medication review; reduce or replace where you can, balancing symptom control and withdrawal. No single burden scale is preferred.

Common culprits: oxybutynin and other bladder antimuscarinics · tricyclics (amitriptyline) · sedating antihistamines (chlorphenamine) · some antipsychotics · procyclidine · some GI antispasmodics.

12Distress and behaviour: antipsychotics lastNG97 §1.7 · MHRA

  • First, a structured assessment: pain, delirium, infection, constipation, retention, drugs, hearing, vision, hunger, boredom, depression, unmet needs, environment, how care is given.
  • Non-drug approaches every time.
  • Antipsychotic only if risk of harm to self or others, or agitation, hallucinations or delusions causing severe distress, after non-drug measures. Discuss the risk: about 12 extra strokes and 11 extra deaths per 1,000 treated for 6–12 weeks (NICE decision aid).
  • Lowest dose, shortest time; reassess at least every 6 weeks; stop if no clear ongoing benefit.
  • Avoid in Lewy body or Parkinson's dementia (specialist decision only). No valproate for agitation or aggression.
  • Mild to moderate depression or anxiety in mild to moderate dementia → consider psychological treatment; no routine antidepressant for mild to moderate depression.

13Annual review checklistevery patient, every year (QOF-relevant)

  • Function and change: ADLs, mobility, falls, continence, weight, swallowing
  • Cognition and behaviour: scores are not the only measure
  • Medicines: enhancer benefit and side effects; anticholinergic burden; any antipsychotic
  • Pulse; syncope or falls on an AChE inhibitor
  • Pain, mood, sleep, hallucinations, distress
  • Carer: strain, health, respite, carer's assessment
  • Safeguarding, finances, driving, home safety
  • Advance care planning, LPA, ReSPECT; review goals
  • Vascular risk factors, individualised to frailty and goals
  • Record the care plan and the named coordinator

15Never-eventsNG97 · MHRA · DVLA

  • Never call an acute change "just the dementia" · start an antipsychotic in Lewy body or Parkinson's dementia without specialist advice · leave an antipsychotic unreviewed beyond 6 weeks · add an anticholinergic without weighing cognition · rule out dementia on a normal screening score · assume a relative can decide without a registered LPA.
  • End of life: revisit goals, place of care and anticipatory medicines as decline progresses.
