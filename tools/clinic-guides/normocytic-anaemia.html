Normocytic anaemia CLINIC GUIDE

Interpreting a low Hb with a normal MCV (usually 80–100 fL) in adults 18+. Not for pregnancy, children, dialysis or ESA care (renal team) or known sickle cell disease (hospital pathway).

1Confirm it is realrepeat if unexpected

  • Anaemia: Hb <130 g/L men, <120 g/L non-pregnant women; use local laboratory ranges. Look back: new, falling or long-standing?
  • Repeat the FBC with reticulocytes and a film if unexpected, before labelling it.
  • Dilution: pregnancy, fluid overload or IV fluids lower the Hb without true anaemia.
  • Early bleeding and early iron deficiency are normocytic before the MCV falls.
  • Mixed deficiency (iron + B12 or folate) cancels out to a normal MCV: a high RDW is a clue. Check ferritin, B12 and folate together.
  • Ferritin rises with inflammation, infection, liver disease, malignancy: read it with CRP and TSAT.
  • A haemolysed sample raises LDH and potassium: repeat before calling it haemolysis.

5Work-up checklistNG203 · NG239 · BSG 2021

  • FBC + film, reticulocytes
  • Ferritin, CRP; TSAT if inflamed
  • B12 and folate
  • U&E/eGFR, LFTs, bilirubin
  • Urinalysis (blood, protein)
  • TSH if clinically indicated
  • FIT if 60+ or iron deficient
  • Electrophoresis + light chains if suggested

Haemolysis suspected: add LDH, haptoglobin, DAT. Coeliac serology if iron deficient or malabsorption.

6Reticulocytes — the fork in the roadBSH · NG203

High retics + jaundiceHaemolysis: LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin, DAT, film. Schistocytes + low platelets = TTP, immediate.
High retics, no haemolysisBleeding: GI, menstrual, urinary. FIT; ferritin will fall later.
Low retics, isolated anaemiaChronic disease, CKD, thyroid, early or mixed deficiency: treat what you find.
Low retics + other cytopeniasMarrow failure, MDS, leukaemia, myeloma: film and urgent haematology.

9Follow-up checklistBSG 2021 · NG239 · NG203

  • Hb at 2–4 weeks on iron: a rise of ≥10 g/L in 2 weeks confirms iron deficiency
  • No rise → adherence, ongoing loss, coeliac, wrong diagnosis, marrow
  • Iron for 3 months after Hb normalises
  • B12: review at about 3 months; do not use B12 levels to monitor injections
  • FIT and haemolysis results returned and actioned
  • Chronic disease: monitoring follows the disease and shared-care plan
  • Declines investigation: informed decision and safety-net recorded
  • Still unexplained after first-line work-up → haematology
  • Return the same day: breathless, chest pain, faint, black stools, jaundice or dark urine, new bruising or bleeding

11Pitfalls and never-eventsNG203 · NG239 · BSG 2021

  • Never blame CKD from the eGFR alone: investigate other causes first.
  • Never give folic acid alone before B12 is excluded or treated.
  • Never give empirical iron that delays assessment of possible marrow disease.
  • A normal ferritin with a raised CRP does not exclude iron deficiency.
  • A normal MCV does not exclude deficiency: mixed states hide.
  • Ignoring a raised reticulocyte count misses bleeding and haemolysis.
  • FIT <10 does not end the search when concern persists.
  • Do not transfuse, or withhold transfusion, on the Hb number alone.
  • Do not start steroids for suspected autoimmune haemolysis unless haematology advises.

12Special groupsNG203 · BSH · NG12

  • Pregnancy: different Hb thresholds — use the maternity pathway. Sickle cell disease: acute anaemia, fever, chest or neurological symptoms → same-day hospital, following the patient's own plan.
  • G6PD deficiency: sudden haemolysis after an infection, drug or fava beans — stop the trigger, urgent bloods, discuss with haematology. On anticoagulants: anaemia is still investigated, not blamed on the drug.
  • CKD: iron deficiency is common and treatable; ESA and HIF-PHI are renal-team decisions. Older adults: anaemia at 60+ → FIT, and think of myeloma and MDS.

13Say it like thisplain English

"Your blood count is low, but the cells are a normal size, so this is more about why your body isn't making enough, or is losing some, than about a single missing vitamin. These tests will narrow it down, and treating the cause is what brings the count back up."
