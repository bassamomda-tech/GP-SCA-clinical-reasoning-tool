Page 1 · Assess the flare and start complication first → infection → work-up → phenotype → induce

!Act today — and no steroid until a complication is excludedNG129 · BSG 2025

  • 999: peritonism or suspected perforation · complete obstruction (colicky pain, vomiting, distension, no flatus) · shock or GI bleeding with instability · sepsis.
  • Suspected abscess (tender mass, often right iliac fossa, fever, high CRP, not responding) → same-day surgical/gastroenterology assessment, usually CT. Steroids can hide sepsis and worsen an abscess.
  • Perianal abscess (tender fluctuant swelling, fever) → same-day colorectal surgery for drainage; antibiotics alone are not definitive.
  • Fever on a thiopurine, methotrexate or biologic → same-day FBC and review; on anti-TNF with cough, sweats and weight loss think TB. Painful red eye → same-day ophthalmology.

2Exclude infection, then quantifyBSG 2025

  • New or worse diarrhoea → stool culture + C. difficile before steroids; never delay emergency care.
  • Faecal calprotectin when stable: a low value makes colonic inflammation less likely but does not exclude small-bowel disease or a complication.
  • Ask about NSAIDs, opioids, missed maintenance doses, smoking.
  • IBS-type symptoms are common in Crohn's: steroids add harm, not benefit.

3Work-up — every flareBSG 2025

  • Temp, HR, BP, weight, BMI
  • FBC, CRP, albumin
  • U&E/eGFR, LFTs
  • Ferritin, iron studies
  • B12, folate, vitamin D
  • Stool culture + C. difficile

Imaging is specialist-led: CT acutely for abscess, perforation or obstruction; MR enterography for small-bowel extent. B12 matters most with terminal ileal disease or resection.

NG12 still applies: change in bowel habit, iron-deficiency anaemia or abdominal mass → FIT, ≥10 µg Hb/g → suspected cancer pathway (HTG690); rectal mass → consider referral, no FIT. Unexplained anal mass or ulceration → consider an anal cancer referral. Unexplained lymphadenopathy on a thiopurine → consider the lymphoma pathway.

6Induce remissionNG129 · uncomplicated flare only

First flare or 1 in 12 monthsConventional steroid (prednisolone) per the IBD flare plan.
Ileocaecal or right colonMild–moderate, steroid unsuitable → budesonide (less effective).
≥2 flares in 12 monthsor steroid can't be tapered → add azathioprine or mercaptopurine.
Thiopurine not possibleIntolerant or TPMT deficient → add methotrexate (specialist).

Never a thiopurine or methotrexate alone to induce remission (NG129). No budesonide or 5-ASA in a severe flare. Document the steroid taper and review within days: not improving → same-day specialist reassessment, not a higher dose.

9Flare not settling? Check firstNG129 · BSG 2025

  • Complication — abscess, stricture, fistula (no more steroid)
  • Infection — C. difficile, enteric pathogens; CMV if immunosuppressed
  • Adherence — is the maintenance drug actually taken?
  • Smoking — still smoking? re-offer support
  • Drugs — NSAIDs, opioids, antibiotics
  • Functional symptoms — normal CRP and calprotectin; but small-bowel disease can hide

10The maintenance ladderNG129 · maintenance drugs are started and changed by the IBD team

Step 1 · thiopurineAzathioprine or mercaptopurine alone, if used with the steroid to induce remission. TPMT first. Some patients choose no maintenance: agree a follow-up plan.
Step 2 · methotrexateIf it was needed to induce remission, or a thiopurine is not tolerated or contraindicated. Once weekly.
Step 3 · advanced therapyAnti-TNF and other biologics or small molecules under the relevant NICE technology appraisal. Specialist only.
Step 4 · surgeryResection or stricturoplasty: not curative. After ileocolonic resection consider azathioprine + up to 3 months' metronidazole (NG129).

12Annual review checklistevery patient, every year

  • Symptoms, steroid courses, perianal symptoms, weight and BMI
  • Smoking status and cessation offer
  • FBC, ferritin, B12 (ileal disease or resection), vitamin D, U&E, LFTs, CRP
  • Shared-care monitoring bloods up to date and actioned (QS81)
  • Surveillance if colitis in >1 segment: first colonoscopy ~10 years after onset (CG118; BSG 2025 says ~8); PSC → from PSC diagnosis, then annually
  • Bone: steroid exposure, fracture risk; vaccines (Green Book)
  • Mood, fatigue, work; contraception and pregnancy plans
  • Kidney stones, stoma or post-surgery issues; IBD nurse contact

14Special groups and never-eventsMHRA · NG129 · BSG 2025

  • Pregnancy: plan it in remission with the IBD team; don't stop effective maintenance without advice; methotrexate is contraindicated — follow the SPC for the stopping interval.
  • Methotrexate: once weekly, day written on the prescription and pack (MHRA).
  • Never: steroids with a suspected abscess or obstruction · steroids as maintenance · a thiopurine without TPMT · steroids for a fibrotic stricture or perianal fistula · attribute new weight loss or anaemia to "just a flare".
  • Iron and B12: oral iron once daily or on alternate days; IV iron if active disease, intolerance or poor absorption. Terminal ileal resection → B12 by injection.
  • After resection: smoking cessation above all; make sure the post-operative colonoscopy is booked.
