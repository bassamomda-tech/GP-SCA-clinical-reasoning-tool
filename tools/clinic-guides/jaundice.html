Jaundice and raised bilirubin CLINIC GUIDE

Pale stools, dark urine and itch = obstructive picture → urgent ultrasound and HPB route. A palpable, non-tender gallbladder with jaundice raises concern for malignant obstruction.

1Confirm it is realexamination · laboratory

Look at the scleraIn natural light. Yellow skin with white sclera = carotenaemia, not jaundice
FractionateAsk the laboratory for conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin
Haemolysed sampleCan distort bilirubin and AST: repeat if the picture does not fit
Fasting, illnessRaise unconjugated bilirubin in Gilbert's — repeat when well
Raised INRCheck for warfarin, vitamin K deficiency, malabsorption before calling it liver failure

Visible jaundice is never just watched: send the first-line bloods the same day. Isolated mildly raised bilirubin with otherwise normal tests can be repeated once, when well.

For suspected drug injury the R-ratio (ALT/ULN ÷ ALP/ULN) classifies: ≥5 hepatocellular, 2–5 mixed, ≤2 cholestatic.

5Work-up checklistBSG 2017 · NG12

  • Bilirubin, fractionated
  • ALT, AST, ALP, GGT
  • INR, albumin
  • FBC + platelets
  • U&E, glucose
  • CRP if infection possible
  • USS: cholestatic pattern
  • HBsAg, HCV antibody
  • Hep A IgM, hep E if hepatitic
  • AMA, ANA, SMA, immunoglobulins
  • Ferritin + TSAT
  • Film, retics, LDH, haptoglobin

Do not send CA 19-9 without imaging evidence of malignancy: false positives are common, especially with obstruction.

7Imaging and the routeNG12 · NG85 · BSG 2017

Cholestatic patternUltrasound first: dilated ducts, stones, mass. Urgency by sepsis, pain and speed of rise.
Septic or in painSuspected cholangitis → hospital the same day, not an outpatient scan.
USS normal, still cholestaticMRCP via the specialist for stones, strictures or PSC; AMA for PBC.
Suspected pancreatic cancerRefer at 40+ without waiting; pancreatic-protocol CT before any biliary stent (NG85).

8Follow-up and monitoringBSG 2017 · NG49 · NG50

  • Referral booked and the result of every test actioned
  • Stable, selected patients: LFTs + FBC at 1–2 weeks — never instead of referral
  • Drug-induced: LFTs falling after the drug is stopped
  • Not improving at 4 weeks → escalate
  • Falling bilirubin does not exclude cancer or obstruction
  • Cirrhosis: 6-monthly ultrasound ± AFP (NG50)
  • Gilbert's confirmed: no further tests
  • Return the same day: deeper yellow, fever, unable to eat or drink, bruising, swelling tummy; 999 for confusion or vomiting blood

9When and where to referNG12 · BSG 2017 · CG165

999Encephalopathy, hypoglycaemia, shock, GI bleed, septic cholangitis, unwell after overdose
Same dayCoagulopathy, renal impairment, fever, significant pain, rapidly rising bilirubin, alcoholic hepatitis, paracetamol overdose, pregnancy
Suspected cancerJaundice 40+; 60+ weight loss + qualifying symptom (CT); duct dilatation or mass on imaging
Urgent hepatologyCholestatic LFTs with normal USS (PBC, PSC); HBsAg or HCV RNA positive; autoimmune hepatitis supported by pattern and IgG
RoutineStable chronic viral hepatitis, haemochromatosis, stable PBC, persistent unexplained derangement
Keep in practiceConfirmed Gilbert's syndrome

No single bilirubin or INR value decides the tier: the whole picture, the trend and the cause do.

10Pitfalls and never-eventsNG12 · BSG 2017

  • Never let a normal ultrasound stop a suspected cancer referral at 40+.
  • Never treat suspected cholangitis as an outpatient ultrasound.
  • Do not label Gilbert's with abnormal ALT, ALP, GGT, albumin or INR.
  • Do not delay acetylcysteine for staggered paracetamol overdose.
  • Painless jaundice is more worrying, not less.
  • Conjugated bilirubin is not the same as obstruction: hepatitis raises it too.
  • Ask about herbal remedies, supplements and recent antibiotics.
  • A raised INR is not automatically liver failure: check anticoagulants and vitamin K first.
  • Pregnancy: jaundice is never routine — same-day obstetric assessment (cholestasis, HELLP, acute fatty liver).
  • Do not start rifampicin for itch without specialist advice (liver toxicity, interactions).

11Advice and the conversationNG50 · Green Book

  • No alcohol until the cause is known; complete abstinence in alcohol-related disease or cirrhosis
  • Hepatitis A and B vaccines for chronic liver disease (Green Book)
  • Avoid NSAIDs and sedatives in significant liver disease; tailor the paracetamol dose
  • Itch: cool skin, emollients; colestyramine if cholestatic

"The yellow colour means a substance called bilirubin is building up. Sometimes that's harmless, but it can mean a blockage or inflammation in the liver, so I want to find the cause quickly. I'm arranging blood tests and a scan, and a fast-track hospital referral so nothing is missed."
