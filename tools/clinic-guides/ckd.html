Page 1 · Confirm, stage and start low eGFR or raised ACR → chronic? → G×A stage → kidney-protecting drugs

!Act todayNICE NG148 · NG203 · NG12 · UKKA (K⁺)

  • Possible AKI: creatinine up ≥26 µmol/L in 48 h, or ≥1.5× baseline within 7 days, or urine <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 h → same-day assessment of volume, sepsis, obstruction and drugs; withhold metformin, NSAIDs and other nephrotoxins.
  • K⁺ ≥6.5 mmol/L or ECG changes → emergency admission. K⁺ 6.0–6.4 → same-day repeat and ECG.
  • Uraemic symptoms (vomiting, confusion, pericardial rub), pulmonary oedema, anuria or retention → same-day hospital assessment (999 by severity).
  • Visible haematuria aged 45+ unexplained → suspected urological cancer referral (NG12) before the CKD work-up. New eGFR <15 → urgent renal discussion.

1Confirm it is chronicNG203 §1.1

  • CKD = abnormal kidney structure or function for >3 months: eGFR <60 on two tests ≥90 days apart, or persistent markers (ACR ≥3, haematuria, structural or inherited disease).
  • New low eGFR → repeat within 2 weeks to exclude AKI. One eGFR is never a diagnosis.
  • ACR on an early-morning sample; ACR 3–70 → confirm on a repeat; ≥70 needs no repeat. Avoid testing during UTI or acute illness.
  • Dip for blood, send for ACR — protein strips only if they report ACR.
  • eGFR misleads at extremes of muscle mass → consider cystatin C. Avoid a meat meal before the test.

3Work-up — find the causeNG203 §1.1–1.3

  • U&E, eGFR, ACR
  • Urine dip for haematuria
  • HbA1c, lipids, FBC
  • BP; review every drug
  • KFRE (4-variable) once staged
  • Ca, phosphate, PTH, vit D in G4–5

Renal ultrasound if: accelerated progression · visible or persistent non-visible haematuria · obstructive symptoms · family history of PKD and aged over 20 · eGFR <30 · biopsy planned.

Over 60 with anaemia or high calcium → think myeloma. Active sediment (blood + protein) → urgent renal advice.

Do not routinely start if K⁺ >5.0. Stop the ACEi/ARB if K⁺ ≥6.0 once other K⁺-raising drugs are stopped. Never ACEi + ARB.

Dapagliflozin 10 mg OD (or empagliflozin 10 mg): expect a small early eGFR dip — don't stop. Do not start if eGFR <20. Give sick-day rules (box 14).

ACEi or ARB; ACEi cough → ARB. Resistant hypertension → spironolactone only with K⁺ and eGFR checked before and soon after; avoid if K⁺ is high or kidney function is poor.

9eGFR falling? Check before blaming CKDNG203

  • AKI on CKD — dehydration, sepsis, contrast, a new drug
  • Obstruction — LUTS, palpable bladder → bladder scan, ultrasound
  • Nephrotoxins — NSAIDs (incl. OTC), lithium, PPI; trimethoprim raises creatinine
  • Real and sustained? — repeat before acting
  • Accelerated = sustained fall ≥25% with a category change in 12 months, or ≥15 mL/min/1.73 m² a year → refer

11Annual review checklistmore often by grid cell (box 7)

  • eGFR + ACR — plot the trajectory
  • KFRE 5-year risk recalculated
  • BP at target for the ACR band
  • ACEi/ARB at highest tolerated dose? SGLT2i considered? Statin prescribed?
  • Medication review: nephrotoxins, renal dose gates, OTC NSAIDs
  • HbA1c if diabetic; Hb from G3b (≤110 g/L → investigate)
  • Sick-day rules given in writing
  • Vaccines; smoking; pregnancy plans on ACEi/ARB

14AKI risk, sick days, never-eventsNG148 · NG203 · NG133

  • Sick days (vomiting, diarrhoea, fever with poor intake): pause metformin and the SGLT2i; review ACEi/ARB and diuretics by BP, volume and kidney function. Restart when eating and drinking normally; check U&E if the illness was significant.
  • Higher AKI risk: eGFR <60, age, diabetes, heart failure, sepsis, contrast, nephrotoxins → check U&E when unwell.
  • Pregnancy: ACEi/ARB, SGLT2i and finerenone are not for pregnancy — discuss contraception and plans first.
  • Never: diagnose CKD on one eGFR · combine ACEi + ARB · stop an ACEi/ARB for the expected dip · refer on eGFR alone without KFRE and ACR.
