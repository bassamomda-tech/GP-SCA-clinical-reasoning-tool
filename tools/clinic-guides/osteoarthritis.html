Osteoarthritis CLINIC GUIDE

For the new diagnosis and the review. Adults with hip, knee, hand and other peripheral joint osteoarthritis. Not for the hot swollen joint, inflammatory arthritis (NICE NG100), spinal pain or care after joint replacement.

!Act today — when a painful joint is not osteoarthritisNG100 · NG12

  • Hot, swollen joint (with or without fever; prosthetic joint highest risk) → same-day acute assessment for septic arthritis. Do not inject until infection is excluded. Sudden severe pain after minor trauma → same-day imaging for fracture.
  • Inflammatory pattern (soft, boggy MCP or wrist swelling, prolonged stiffness) → urgent rheumatology if small joints of hands or feet, more than one joint, or a delay of 3 months or more before seeking advice, even with normal CRP or RF.
  • Aged 60+ with persistent bone pain (especially back pain) or unexplained fracture → myeloma bloods (NG12). X-ray suggests bone sarcoma → suspected cancer pathway. Night pain with weight loss, a mass or previous cancer → image, do not label as "wear and tear".

1Diagnose clinically — no X-ray neededNG226 1.1.1–1.1.2

Diagnose osteoarthritis without investigations if the person is:

  • aged 45 or over, and
  • has activity-related joint pain, and
  • has no morning stiffness, or stiffness lasting no longer than 30 minutes.

Image only for atypical features or a suspected alternative or additional diagnosis. Do not image routinely for follow-up or to guide non-surgical care: X-ray change correlates poorly with pain and function.

5Core treatment — offer to everyoneNG226 §1.2–1.3

  • Information tailored to the person: OA is common, does not inevitably get worse, and movement does not wear the joint out.
  • Therapeutic exercise, tailored: local strengthening plus aerobic fitness; consider supervised sessions. Warn pain may rise at first.
  • Weight loss if overweight or obese: any loss helps; 10% of body weight is likely to help more than 5%.
  • Consider walking aids for lower-limb OA (stick in the opposite hand).
  • Manual therapy (hip or knee): only alongside exercise.
  • Insoles, braces, splints: only if unstable or abnormal loading.

6First drugs — to support exerciseNG226 §1.4

KneeOffer a topical NSAID
Hand, othersConsider a topical NSAID
Topical failsConsider oral NSAID + PPI
BridgeIA steroid: relief 2–10 weeks

Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time. Hip: topical NSAIDs are less likely to reach a deep joint.

7Tailor it — by joint and by personNG226 · MHRA

SituationWhat changes
HandTopical NSAID is a "consider", not an "offer". Hand therapy: joint protection, exercises, thumb-base splint if the base of the thumb is unstable. Inflammatory-looking hand → NG100 pathway.
HipTopical NSAIDs less likely to help a deep joint; oral may be needed earlier after risk assessment. Stick in the opposite hand. Remember hip pain presenting at the knee.
Older, CKD, cardiovascular diseaseStay topical where possible. Oral: lowest dose, shortest time, PPI, check U&E and BP; no systemic diclofenac in established CVD; avoid high-dose ibuprofen.
DiabetesWarn about a temporary rise in blood glucose after a steroid injection; agree a monitoring plan.
WorkAsk how the joint affects work; workplace adjustments, "may be fit" advice, occupational health.

9Targets — judge success by functionNG226 · QS87

MeasureWhat good looks like
FunctionThe agreed goal met: walking distance, stairs, grip, sleep, work
ExerciseRegular, progressing, sustained
WeightMoving towards 10% loss if overweight
DrugsLowest dose, shortest time; nothing from the do-not-offer list

Not a pain score and not the X-ray.

10Before you step upNG226

  • Still OA? New heat or swelling, night pain with weight loss → reassess
  • Core treatment in place? Most "failed" OA never had a structured exercise programme
  • Drug order right? Topical before oral; oral with a PPI
  • Mood, sleep, fear of movement addressed?
  • Risks changed? BP, eGFR, new anticoagulant or antiplatelet

11The drug ladder — every step supports exerciseNG226 §1.4 · MHRA DSU Jun 2013, Jun 2015

Step 1 · topical NSAIDOffer for the knee; consider for the hand and other superficial joints. Product per SPC and local formulary.
Step 2 · oral NSAIDIf topical is ineffective or unsuitable. Lowest effective dose, shortest time, with a PPI, after weighing GI, renal, liver and CV risk.
Step 3 · IA steroidIf other drugs are ineffective or unsuitable, or to support exercise. Explain relief is short-term: 2–10 weeks.
Step 4 · surgical opinionSymptoms still substantially affect quality of life and non-surgical care is ineffective or unsuitable → consider referral (box 16).

13NSAID safety and never-eventsNG226 · MHRA

  • Triple whammy: NSAID + ACEi/ARB + diuretic → AKI risk; avoid where possible, otherwise check U&E and BP.
  • Older people, CKD, CVD: stay topical where possible.
  • Pregnancy: seek advice before any NSAID.
  • Already on a regular opioid for OA: review the benefit to function; if none, agree a slow taper (MHRA, Sep 2020: dependence even at therapeutic doses).
  • Never: X-ray to diagnose typical OA · tell someone their knee is "bone on bone" · inject a possibly infected joint · exclude from surgical referral because of BMI or smoking.

14Review checklistQS87 · agree follow-up

  • Function and quality of life against last time's goal
  • Exercise: being done, at the right level? Re-refer if not
  • Weight change; offer weight-management support
  • Oral NSAID: BP, renal function, GI symptoms, PPI, still needed?
  • Any opioid or other drug NG226 advises against → plan a taper
  • Mood and sleep; work and caring
  • Red flags rescreened; new joints involved?
  • Referral: symptoms still substantially affect quality of life?

15Safety-netsay it, write it

  • Joint becomes hot, red and swollen, or you feel feverish → same day.
  • On an anti-inflammatory: black stools, vomiting blood, indigestion, ankle swelling or passing much less urine → stop it and contact us.
  • After an injection: worsening pain with fever or redness → same day.
