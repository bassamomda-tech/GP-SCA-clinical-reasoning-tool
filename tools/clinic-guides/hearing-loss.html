Hearing loss in adults CLINIC GUIDE

For the adult presenting with hearing loss. Adults 18+. Not for children (all suspected hearing loss → paediatric audiology or ENT) or tinnitus alone (NICE NG155).

1Focused historyNG98 · questions that change the plan

  • Onset: over 3 days or less (sudden) · over weeks (rapid) · over years (gradual). When did it start — within 30 days?
  • One ear or both? Unilateral or asymmetric loss needs specialist referral (NG98).
  • Fluctuating, not linked to colds → Ménière's or other inner-ear disease.
  • Tinnitus: one-sided, pulsatile, changed or distressing? Vertigo: recurrent or not resolved?
  • Ear pain, discharge, blood; immunosuppression or diabetes (necrotising otitis externa).
  • Noise at work or leisure; ototoxic drugs (aminoglycosides, cisplatin, high-dose loop diuretics); head injury; family history.
  • Impact: noisy places, phone, TV; isolation, mood, memory concerns.

4Investigations checklistNG98 1.1 · 1.3

  • Audiological assessment for hearing loss not explained by wax or acute ear infection
  • MRI internal auditory meati (requested by ENT or specialist audiology): consider if sensorineural asymmetry ≥15 dB at any 2 adjacent frequencies; offer if localising signs, whatever the thresholds
  • Dementia or MCI, diagnosed or suspected → consider audiology referral; if diagnosed and no hearing loss found, consider reassessing every 2 years
  • No routine bloods for wax, typical age-related loss or uncomplicated conductive loss

5Tinnitus with the hearing loss — which route?NICE NG155 · NG98 1.1

PulsatileIn time with the pulse or whooshing → refer for specialist assessment, including imaging. Listen for a bruit; check BP and fundi.
One-sided or changedUnilateral, or significantly changed in nature → refer to ENT, audiovestibular medicine or specialist audiology.
DistressingDistress affecting mental wellbeing despite initial support → refer to be seen within 2 weeks. Ask about mood and suicidal thoughts; act on risk the same day.
Bilateral, settledWith age-related loss → audiology; hearing aids often reduce it. Explain, reassure, give written information.

7Hearing aids and rehabilitationNG98 · QS185

  • Refer to audiology: offer hearing aids when hearing loss affects the ability to communicate and hear — no ENT referral needed first for typical bilateral loss.
  • Face-to-face follow-up 6–12 weeks after fitting: concerns, fit, insertion, care.
  • Many aids end up in a drawer: ask at reviews whether they are worn and helping; re-refer if hearing or benefit changes.

8Living well with hearing lossself-management

  • Communication: face the person, good light, less background noise; tell family to get attention before speaking.
  • Assistive devices: amplified phones, TV listeners, vibrating or flashing alerts; ask about a social care assessment.
  • Protect remaining hearing: limit loud noise, use hearing protection; no cotton buds in the canal.
  • Tinnitus: avoid silence; CBT-based support for distress (NG155). Betahistine is not a tinnitus treatment.

9Safety-net and follow-up checklistrecord it in the notes

  • Same day: sudden hearing loss, new facial weakness, mastoid swelling, severe ear pain with fever
  • 999: neck stiffness, confusion or new neurological symptoms with ear disease
  • Referral clock chosen from onset date (24 h / 2 weeks / routine) and documented
  • Wax: softener plan; second attempt booked; refer if it fails twice
  • Effusion: review; refer if not settling or one-sided
  • Hearing aid fitted → follow-up 6–12 weeks after fitting
  • Memory concerns or dementia → hearing assessed
  • Driving: check DVLA guidance if disabling vertigo, or for a bus or lorry licence

11Special groups — check before you decideNG98 · CKS

Dementia or MCIConsider an audiology assessment; in diagnosed dementia or MCI with no hearing loss, consider reassessing every 2 years (NG98). Uncorrected hearing loss worsens communication and isolation.
Diabetes or immunosuppressionSevere ear pain, granulation tissue or a cranial nerve palsy → urgent ENT (necrotising otitis externa). Immunocompromised with otalgia and otorrhoea not settling within 72 hours → ENT within 24 hours.
Ototoxic treatmentAminoglycosides, cisplatin: new hearing loss, tinnitus or imbalance → tell the prescribing team and refer to audiology.
Noise at workOccupational health and hearing protection; record the exposure history.

12Never-eventsNG98

  • Treat sudden one-sided hearing loss as "wax" or "glue ear" without looking: a normal drum means sensorineural until proven otherwise.
  • Wait for audiometry, or give steroids, instead of referring on the 24-hour clock.
  • Syringe ears manually, or irrigate a perforated drum or a grommet.
  • Dismiss a one-sided effusion in an adult with no cold.
  • Label one-sided or asymmetric loss as "age-related".
  • Forget hearing in someone with memory concerns.

13Say it like thiscommunication tips for everyone

"Hearing that goes suddenly is treated as urgent, even if the ear feels only blocked — I'm going to arrange for you to be seen by the ear team within 24 hours."

Face the person, cut background noise, check understanding. Offer written information and support about hearing aids and assistive devices.
