1Focused history — the questions that discriminateNG59 · GIRFT

  • Leg-dominant pain radiating below the knee in a dermatomal pattern, worse on coughing or sneezing → radicular.
  • Ask the cauda equina questions specifically: sensation when wiping · can you feel your bladder filling · hesitancy, poor stream, leaking · bowel control · genital or sexual sensation. Not just "any bladder problems?"
  • Both legs, walking distance limited, eased by sitting or bending forward → spinal stenosis.
  • Cancer history, weight loss, fever, night pain, steroids, IV drug use.
  • Psychosocial: fear of movement, catastrophising, low mood, work and sick leave.

3Investigations checklistNG59 · NG12

  • No routine imaging in primary care; specialist imaging only if it will change management
  • Neurology documented: power, reflexes, sensation, SLR
  • Myeloma bloods if 60+ with persistent bone pain
  • NSAID safety: eGFR, BP, GI history, anticoagulants
  • STarT Back score at first contact (box 5)

5Risk-stratify with STarT Back — at first contact for each new episodeNG59 §1.1 ("think about") · Keele STarT Back tool

9-item scoreRisk groupWhat it means for the plan
Total 0–3LowReassurance, advice, stay active, review
Total 4 or more, psychosocial subscale (items 5–9) 3 or lessMediumPhysiotherapy and exercise
Psychosocial subscale 4 or moreHighPhysiotherapy with closer follow-up; tackle fear-avoidance and work barriers; treat low mood or anxiety in its own right

The score informs shared decisions; it does not replace clinical assessment. The tool was built for back pain with or without sciatica; severe radicular pain or a neurological deficit still follows boxes 1–2. Since July 2026 NG59 no longer recommends psychological or combined physical and psychological programmes (withdrawn: partly based on retracted evidence).

7Manage — what to offerNG59 §1.2–1.3

Self-managementTailored advice: stay active, continue normal activities as pain allows. No bed rest. Realistic prognosis: most improve over time.
ExerciseConsider a group exercise programme (biomechanical, aerobic, mind-body or a mix). Manual therapy only as part of a package with exercise.
DrugNo NICE recommendation for or against an NSAID in sciatica: be aware of harms and limited evidence of benefit. If used: lowest dose, shortest time, gastroprotection.
Acute and severeNot controlled → consider referral for an epidural injection of local anaesthetic and steroid. Not for chronic sciatica.

8Prescribing the NSAID safelyNG59 · SPC

BeforeAvoid or seek adviceCounsel
eGFR, BP, heart failure, GI history, age, pregnancy, anticoagulants, antiplatelets, steroids, SSRIsMajor renal, cardiovascular or GI risk; on an anticoagulant; ACEi/ARB + diuretic ("triple whammy"). A PPI does not remove renal or CV risk.State the maximum dose and course length; take with food; stop and seek help for black stools, vomiting blood or much less urine

9Do not offer for sciaticaNG59 · NG215

Gabapentin, pregabalin, other antiepilepticsNo overall benefit and risk of harm. The neuropathic pain ladder is not the sciatica pathway.
BenzodiazepinesIncluding diazepam as a "muscle relaxant"
Oral corticosteroidsNo benefit for pain or function
Opioids for chronic sciaticaAlready taking? Explain risks; shared decision on stopping
Passive treatmentsBelts, corsets, orthotics, traction, TENS, ultrasound, acupuncture
Routine imagingOnly if the result will change management

Already on a gabapentinoid or benzodiazepine for sciatica → discuss risks and agree a gradual, shared taper (NG215). Gabapentin and pregabalin are Class C controlled drugs.

10Safety-net and follow-up checklistevery consultation

  • Cauda equina script spoken and written; record what was said and understood
  • Neurological baseline recorded to compare against
  • Specific review date agreed, not "come back if it doesn't settle"
  • NSAID: maximum dose, course length, gastroprotection discussed
  • Physiotherapy or group exercise referral made
  • Fit note supports a graded return or modified duties
  • Driving: not if pain, weakness or sedating drugs impair control
  • At review: trajectory, neurology vs baseline, red flags rescreened, alternative diagnoses

12Persistent sciaticaNG59 · NG193 · NG215

  • Reassess the diagnosis: stenosis, vascular claudication, hip, non-radicular pain.
  • Persistent radicular pain → specialist spinal or MSK assessment, not another scan from primary care.
  • Chronic primary pain features → NG193 options (supervised exercise, ACT or CBT).
  • Review analgesia against function; withdraw gabapentinoids and opioids gradually (NG215).
  • Treat co-existing depression or anxiety in its own right (NHS Talking Therapies).
  • Keep the cauda equina safety-net going at every contact.

13Never-eventsNG59 · GIRFT

  • Never book a routine MRI or a GP review for a cauda equina symptom.
  • Never skip the cauda equina safety-net because the pain is improving: the deficit can be painless.
  • Never start pregabalin or gabapentin "for nerve pain" in sciatica (NG59: do not offer).
  • Never advise bed rest, or prescribe diazepam for spasm.
  • Never refuse a scan without explaining why: incidental degenerative change is common.

14Say it like thisprognosis and safety-net

"Most sciatica improves over time without an operation, although it can be slow and the pain can stay bad for several weeks. Keep as active as the pain allows. If you get numbness between your legs or around your back passage, can't pass urine, or lose control of your bladder or bowels, go to A&E straight away — don't ring us first."
