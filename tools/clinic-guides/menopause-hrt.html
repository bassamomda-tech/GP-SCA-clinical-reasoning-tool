Menopause and HRT CLINIC GUIDE

For the menopause consultation and the annual HRT review. Women and people with menopausal symptoms; POI under 40 with specialist input. Not for HRT after breast cancer (specialist), gender-affirming hormones or children.

!Act first — never start HRT on top of theseNICE NG12 · NG23

  • Unexplained postmenopausal bleeding not attributable to HRT: aged 55+ → suspected cancer pathway; under 55 → consider it. Assess before any systemic HRT.
  • Breast lump aged 30+ (or one-sided nipple change aged 50+) → suspected cancer pathway before HRT.
  • Do not start: current or past breast cancer, current VTE or recent arterial thrombosis, active liver disease, undiagnosed bleeding, untreated hyperplasia, pregnancy → specialist or non-hormonal route.
  • Atypical features — severe unilateral headache, focal neurology, bone pain, weight loss → investigate; do not attribute to the menopause.
  • On HRT: leg pain with swelling, chest pain with breathlessness, stroke symptoms → stop HRT, 999.

1Diagnose clinicallyNICE NG23

AgeHow
Over 45Clinical, no FSH. Perimenopause = vasomotor symptoms + irregular periods. Menopause = no period for 12 months (not on hormonal contraception), or symptoms alone without a uterus
40–45Consider FSH if symptoms and a change in the menstrual cycle
Under 40Suspected POI — box 2

FSH is unreliable on combined hormonal contraception or high-dose progestogen. Average UK age at menopause is 51.

Ask about vasomotor, mood, sleep, joint, urogenital and sexual symptoms; score severity so you can judge the response at 3 months.

2Premature ovarian insufficiencyNICE NG23

  • Under 40 with symptoms and absent or infrequent periods → FSH on 2 samples 4–6 weeks apart.
  • Seek specialist input. Offer HRT or a combined hormonal contraceptive until at least the average age of natural menopause, unless contraindicated — for bone and cardiovascular health, not just symptoms.
  • HRT is not contraception; a combined hormonal contraceptive also gives contraception.

3Work-up before prescribingtargeted, not routine

  • BP, BMI
  • Bleeding pattern, LMP
  • Breast symptoms; screening up to date
  • Personal or family VTE, breast cancer
  • Migraine, liver disease, CVD
  • Uterus? (subtotal, ablation)
  • Contraception needs
  • TFTs only if features

Migraine (with or without aura) contraindicates the combined pill, not HRT — prefer transdermal. Risk factors change the route; contraindications change the plan.

Do not order routinely: FSH over 45, mammogram outside screening, coagulation screen, pelvic ultrasound.

4Choose the regimen — uterus firstNG23 · BMS

No uterusOestrogen alone (total hysterectomy)
Uterus, periPeriod in last 12 months → sequential combined
Uterus, post12 months' amenorrhoea → continuous combined
Vaginal onlyVaginal oestrogen, any age

Subtotal hysterectomy, endometrial ablation or endometriosis → may still need a progestogen; seek advice. Over 45 on sequential → consider continuous combined after 5 years or by 54, whichever first (BMS).

5Route — oral or transdermalNICE NG23 · BMS

Consider transdermal rather than oral oestrogen if VTE risk is increased, including BMI >30. Oral oestrogen raises VTE and slightly raises stroke risk; transdermal at standard doses carries little or no excess.

FormUsual start
Patch (Evorel, Estradot)25–50 micrograms twice weekly
Gel / sprayOestrogel 2 pumps ≈ Sandrena 1 mg ≈ Lenzetto 3 sprays ≈ 50 patch (BMS)

Start low, review at 3 months, titrate; most women need about a 50-microgram patch or equivalent.

6Protect the endometriumNG23 · SPC · BMS

Uterus + systemic oestrogen → always a progestogen: at least 12 days per cycle (sequential) or daily (continuous).

ProgestogenSequentialContinuous

Higher oestrogen dose → match the progestogen (specialist advice). Lower-dose LNG-IUS devices are not enough.

11Unscheduled bleeding on HRTNG23 (Apr 2026) · BMS

Common: in the first 6 months of systemic HRT, or within 3 months of a dose or product change → check adherence, regimen, interactions, cervical screening; examine; review.

Assess promptly (BMS pathway: TVUSS, gynaecology): bleeding starting outside those windows, heavy, prolonged or persistent bleeding, bleeding after established amenorrhoea on continuous combined, or endometrial cancer risk factors.

Bleeding after stopping HRT = unexplained PMB → NG12 (red box).

13Annual review checklistevery woman, every year

  • Symptom control; dose, route and regimen still right for her stage
  • Bleeding pattern — any unscheduled bleeding (box 11)
  • BP and weight; smoking, alcohol
  • Breast symptoms; breast screening up to date
  • New VTE, stroke or CVD history; family history; migraine
  • Contraception still needed? LNG-IUS change date recorded
  • Vaginal and urinary symptoms; sexual function
  • Benefit–risk discussed and documented; continue, change or taper

14HRT declined or unsuitableNICE NG23 · TA1143

  • CBT for vasomotor symptoms, sleep and low mood.
  • Do not routinely offer SSRIs, SNRIs or clonidine first line for flushes alone; avoid paroxetine and fluoxetine with tamoxifen.
  • Urogenital symptoms: moisturisers and lubricants; vaginal oestrogen if they persist.
  • Fezolinetant: an option for moderate to severe flushes and night sweats when HRT is unsuitable (NICE TA1143, Mar 2026).

15ReferNG23 · protocol

  • Menopause specialist: POI, failure after an optimised trial, complex history, breast cancer history wanting HRT.
  • Haematology / specialist: previous VTE or thrombophilia — transdermal may still be possible.
  • Gynaecology: unscheduled bleeding meeting the BMS criteria.

16Special groups and never-eventsCoSRH · MHRA · NG23

  • HRT is not contraception. Contraception until 55, or stop 2 years after the last period if under 50, 1 year if 50 or over (non-hormonal methods; CoSRH).
  • Breast cancer history: no routine systemic HRT; vaginal oestrogen only after discussion (on an aromatase inhibitor → breast team first).
  • Never: unopposed systemic oestrogen with a uterus · start HRT over unexplained bleeding · FSH to diagnose over 45 · compounded "bioidentical" hormones · stop at 5 years without a clinical reason.
