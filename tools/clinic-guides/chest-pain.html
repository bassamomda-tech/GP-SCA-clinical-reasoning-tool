Chest pain in adults CLINIC GUIDE

For undifferentiated chest pain in adults in general practice. Not for children, pregnancy or post-partum, major trauma, active cancer treatment or sickle cell disease (separate pathways).

!Red flags — sort by urgencyNICE CG95 · NG185 · NG158 · NG12 · ESC 2024

999 nowPossible cardiac pain now or recurring, whatever the ECG · sudden, severe, tearing pain to the back, with syncope, neuro deficit, pulse deficit or arm-to-arm BP difference (aortic syndrome) · suspected PE with collapse, hypotension or severe hypoxia · tension pneumothorax · tamponade · pain after vomiting with surgical emphysema · pulmonary oedema · shock.
Same-day hospitalPain-free, last episode <12 h with a normal ECG · last episode 12–72 h · rest or crescendo pain · stable suspected PE (Wells >4, or positive D-dimer) · first episode of pericarditis · pneumonia with CRB65 ≥2 or sepsis · pneumothorax.
Suspected cancer (NG12)Haemoptysis aged 40+ → lung pathway. CXR within 2 weeks at 40+ with 2 or more unexplained of cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, weight loss, appetite loss (1 or more if ever smoked). Dysphagia at any age → oesophageal pathway.

While the ambulance comes (CG95): aspirin 300 mg chewed unless clearly allergic — withhold if dissection is the leading thought; GTN unless hypotensive · oxygen only if SpO₂ <94% (target 94–98%; 88–92% if at risk of hypercapnia) · a 12-lead ECG must not delay transfer. Never drive them yourself.

2Focused history — the discriminating questionsCG95

CharacterPressure, heaviness, tightness, burning (cardiac) vs sharp, stabbing, positional
OnsetBuilds over minutes (ischaemia) · maximal at once (dissection, PE, pneumothorax)
Trigger, reliefExertion, cold, emotion, eased by rest · breathing in · lying flat · meals · movement
With itSweating, nausea, breathlessness, syncope · haemoptysis, leg swelling · fever, cough
BackgroundAge, smoking, diabetes, BP, lipids, family history, CKD · VTE risk · cocaine (ask under-50s)

Women, older people and people with diabetes often present with breathlessness, epigastric pain or "indigestion" alone.

3Suspected PE — two-level WellsNICE NG158

Wells >4 (likely)Immediate CTPA via the acute route; interim anticoagulation if imaging is delayed. No D-dimer.
Wells ≤4 (unlikely)D-dimer; interim anticoagulation if the result will take over 4 h; positive → imaging. Consider an age-adjusted threshold over 50.
Pregnant or post-partumWells and D-dimer not validated → acute/maternity pathway

6Investigations checklistorder what changes management

  • 12-lead ECG — but never ahead of a time-critical transfer
  • Compare with an old ECG
  • CXR if pneumonia, effusion, pneumothorax, cancer
  • FBC (anaemia), U&E, HbA1c, lipids
  • TFTs if symptoms
  • NT-proBNP only if heart failure suspected (NG106)

Not in primary care: a single GP troponin as a rule-out · exercise ECG to diagnose or exclude angina (CG95) · CTCA (via RACPC). A normal ECG does not exclude ACS.

7Stable chest pain — the CG95 (2016) pathwayonly once ACS has been excluded · NICE CG95 · CG126

1 · Classify the painConstricting discomfort · brought on by exertion · relieved by rest or GTN within about 5 min. 3 = typical, 2 = atypical angina, ≤1 = non-anginal.
2 · CTCA first-line64-slice (or above) CT coronary angiography for typical or atypical angina, or non-anginal pain with ST-T changes or Q waves on the resting ECG. Arranged via the local RACPC.
3 · ThenNon-invasive functional imaging if CTCA shows disease of uncertain significance or is non-diagnostic; invasive angiography if needed.
Meanwhile in primary careGTN spray 1–2 sprays under the tongue; repeat after 5 min; 999 if not eased 5 min after the second dose. Consider aspirin 75 mg (bleeding risk). Beta-blocker or CCB first-line (CG126).

Non-anginal pain with a normal ECG → look for a non-cardiac cause. CTCA is not an acute ACS rule-out test. Never GTN within 24 h of sildenafil or vardenafil (48 h tadalafil).

8Non-cardiac causes — first-lineCG184 · CG113 · SPC

GORDFull-dose PPI for 4 or 8 weeks, then lowest effective dose; dysphagia, or 55+ with weight loss → NG12 pathway
Costochondritis, strainReassure; paracetamol or topical NSAID first; oral NSAID lowest dose, shortest time, check CV, renal, GI risk
PericarditisSame-day assessment first; NSAID and colchicine per the acute or cardiology plan
Panic or anxietyOnly once serious causes are considered: NHS Talking Therapies, CBT (CG113)
PneumoniaCRB65 with judgement to decide place of care (NG250)
ShinglesAnalgesia; antiviral per NICE CKS shingles

10Safety-net and follow-up checklistrecord it in the notes

  • 999 if pain at rest, lasting, with sweating, breathlessness or collapse — don't wait to see if GTN works
  • Same day if pain now comes at rest, GTN stops working, new leg swelling, fever, black stools
  • Told: a normal ECG or tenderness does not exclude a serious cause
  • Named clinician owns ECG, bloods, CXR and referral results
  • Awaiting RACPC: date chased at 2 weeks if none arrived
  • PPI trial reviewed at 4 weeks; MSK pain at 2 weeks if not settling
  • QRISK3 and lifestyle (smoking, activity) once the acute issue is settled

11Never-eventsCG95 · NG185 · NG158

  • Never delay a 999 transfer for an ECG, bloods, D-dimer or imaging.
  • Never let a normal ECG, a normal SpO₂, chest-wall tenderness or relief after GTN reassure you on their own.
  • Never give aspirin or anticoagulation when dissection is the leading diagnosis.
  • Never use D-dimer when PE is likely (Wells >4).
  • Never label anxiety at a first presentation without observations and an ECG decision.
  • Never a single GP troponin as a rule-out, or an exercise ECG to exclude angina.
