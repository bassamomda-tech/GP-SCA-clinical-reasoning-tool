Page 1 · Diagnose and decide raised reading → confirm → work-up → who to treat → lifestyle

!Act todayNICE NG136 §1.5 · NG133

  • Clinic ≥180 systolic or ≥120 diastolic with retinal haemorrhage or papilloedema, new confusion, chest pain, signs of heart failure or AKI → same-day specialist assessment.
  • Suspected phaeochromocytoma (labile or postural hypotension, headache, palpitations, pallor, sweating, abdominal pain) → same-day specialist assessment.
  • Tearing chest or interscapular pain with an arm-to-arm BP difference → 999 (possible dissection). New hypertension in pregnancy → urgent maternity assessment; ≥160/110 needs immediate hospital treatment.
  • Severe BP without same-day features → look for target-organ damage now: damage present → consider treating at once, without waiting for ABPM/HBPM; none → repeat clinic BP within 7 days.

1Measure properlyNG136 §1.1

  • Validated upper-arm device, correct cuff size; seated 5 min, back supported, arm at heart level; no caffeine or smoking for 30 min.
  • Both arms once: difference >15 mmHg → repeat; if it persists, use the higher arm from now on.
  • First reading ≥140/90 → take a second (a third if very different); record the lower of the last two.
  • Irregular pulse → automated devices may be unreliable: palpate, measure manually, ECG.
  • Aged 80+, type 2 diabetes or postural symptoms → lying (preferred) or seated, then standing for at least 1 min: drop ≥20 systolic or ≥10 diastolic is significant.

2Confirm out of officeNG136 §1.2

Clinic 140/90–179/119 → ABPM: ≥2 readings/hour while awake, use the average of ≥14.

ABPM unsuitable or declined → HBPM: 2 seated readings 1 min apart, twice daily, 4–7 days; discard day 1, average the rest.

Diagnosis = clinic ≥140/90 and ABPM daytime / HBPM average ≥135/85. Start the work-up (box 3) while waiting.

3Work-up — every new diagnosisNG136 §1.3

  • Urine ACR + dipstick haematuria
  • HbA1c, U&E, creatinine, eGFR
  • Total and HDL cholesterol
  • 12-lead ECG (LVH, old MI, AF)
  • Fundi
  • QRISK3 after the tests, on clinic BP

Selectively: aldosterone:renin ratio if <40, low K⁺ or resistant · TFTs if symptoms · renal US if U&E or urine abnormal · echo if murmur, LVH or ?coarctation.

Haematuria is not "part of the BP work-up": visible haematuria aged 45+ without UTI, or non-visible aged 60+ with dysuria or raised WCC → suspected cancer pathway (NG12).

8First drugNG136 §1.4 · NG203 §1.6

Type 2 diabetesany age → A (ACEi or ARB)
Under 55no T2DM → A
55 or overno T2DM → C (CCB)
Black African / African–Caribbeanno T2DM → C

T2DM + Black African / African–Caribbean origin → consider an ARB rather than an ACEi.

CKD: diabetes + ACR >3, or hypertension + ACR >30, or any ACR ≥70 mg/mmol → A titrated to the highest tolerated licensed dose (ACR ≥70 without diabetes → also refer). SGLT2 inhibitor per NICE TA / local CKD pathway.

Usual starts: ramipril 2.5 mg (1.25 mg if on a diuretic) → up to 10 mg OD · amlodipine 5 → 10 mg OD. ACEi cough → ARB. Never ACEi + ARB.

10The A → C → D ladderA = ACEi/ARB · C = CCB · D = thiazide-like diuretic · NG136 §1.4

Step 1 · one drugA (T2DM, or under 55) or C (55+, or Black African / African–Caribbean, no T2DM).
Step 2 · two drugsOn A → add C or D. On C → add A (ARB preferred in Black African / African–Caribbean origin) or D. Consider a fixed-dose combination.
Step 3 · A + C + DD = indapamide 2.5 mg OD (or MR 1.5 mg). Stable on bendroflumethiazide → can continue. A diuretic already taken counts as D.
Step 4 · resistantRe-confirm with ABPM/HBPM, check posture and adherence. K⁺ ≤4.5 → consider spironolactone 25 mg OD. K⁺ >4.5 → alpha- or beta-blocker. Still uncontrolled on 4 → specialist.

11Before every step-upNG136 §1.4

  • Adherence — ask openly; check repeat-prescription record; once-daily or combination tablet
  • Measurement — cuff, both arms; re-confirm with ABPM/HBPM if white-coat likely
  • BP-raising drugs — NSAIDs, steroids, COCP, venlafaxine, decongestants, liquorice, stimulants, alcohol
  • Lifestyle drift — salt, weight, alcohol, activity: agree one target
  • Doses — is every drug at its maximum tolerated dose?
  • Secondary cause — <40, abrupt onset, low K⁺, resistant (box 5)

12Side effects — fix the symptom, keep the classSPC · NG136

Dry cough (ACEi)Switch to an ARB; don't let them just stop
Angioedema (ACEi)Stop permanently; 999 if airway; ARB only on specialist advice
Ankle oedema (amlodipine)Lower dose, stop, or switch class; adding A may help
Gout (thiazide)Treat flare; consider swapping D; losartan is mildly uricosuric
Na⁺ <130 / K⁺ <3.5 (thiazide)Act on symptoms and trend; usually change class
Erectile dysfunctionAsk — a silent cause of non-adherence; PDE5i never with nitrates

13Annual review checklistevery patient, every year

  • Clinic BP + home readings; standing BP if 80+, T2DM or dizzy
  • Adherence and side effects (cough, ankles, gout, erectile dysfunction, dizziness)
  • U&E/eGFR, urine ACR, HbA1c, lipids
  • QRISK3 and statin conversation if not already treated (NG238)
  • Lifestyle: one agreed change, smoking status, alcohol
  • Women who could become pregnant: plans and contraception (no ACEi/ARB in pregnancy)
  • Frailty, falls, postural symptoms → relax target, review the whole drug list
  • Record target, current step and next action in the notes

14Special groups and never-eventsMHRA DSU · NG133 · NG106

  • Planning pregnancy: switch ACEi/ARB (and review thiazides) before conception; stop as soon as pregnancy is confirmed. In pregnancy: labetalol → nifedipine → methyldopa, aim ~135/85. Postpartum: enalapril (monitor U&E, K⁺).
  • Known HFrEF: not this ladder — prognostic therapy on the heart-failure pathway (ACEi/ARNI/ARB, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2i). Avoid verapamil and diltiazem.
  • Never: label hypertension on one clinic reading · combine ACEi + ARB · add a drug for one that isn't being taken · judge a home diary against the clinic target.
  • AF found on pulse or ECG → manage on the AF pathway (stroke risk, rate).
