Depression in adults CLINIC GUIDE

For the new episode and the medication and relapse review. Adults 18+. Not for under-18s (CAMHS), bipolar disorder (NICE CG185), the perinatal period (CG192) or psychotic depression.

Page 1 · Assess and start risk first → clinical diagnosis → shared choice of treatment

!Act todayNG222 · NG225 · CG185

  • Immediate danger or any overdose → 999 / A&E. Active plan or intent, unable to keep safe, psychotic features, stupor or severe self-neglect → same-day crisis or mental health assessment.
  • Postpartum confusion, hallucinations or rapidly changing mood → immediate specialist assessment, within 4 hours (possible postpartum psychosis, CG192).
  • Past mania or hypomania → do not start an antidepressant alone; seek psychiatric advice. A new "high" on an antidepressant is a bipolar signal, not a good response.
  • Agitation, sweating, tremor, clonus and fever after a dose rise or a new serotonergic drug → serotonin syndrome: stop and assess the same day.

1Diagnose clinically, measure with PHQ-9NG222

Core: persistent low mood and/or loss of interest or pleasure, most days for at least 2 weeks, plus sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, guilt or worthlessness, psychomotor change, thoughts of death — and an effect on function.

PHQ-9 measures severity and change; it is not the diagnosis. Use it with clinical judgement, duration, function and history:

NG222 groupUsually
Less severe (subthreshold and mild)PHQ-9 <16
More severe (moderate and severe)PHQ-9 ≥16

Record the score and date at every contact. Score anxiety alongside (GAD-7). Grief and adjustment reactions are different problems with different help.

2Suicide and self-harm — ask, don't scoreNG225 · NG222

  • Ask directly about thoughts, plans, intent, means, past self-harm, alcohol and drugs, and what keeps them safe. Asking does not plant the idea.
  • Do not use risk assessment tools or scales to predict suicide or repeat self-harm, and do not use them or a low / medium / high label to decide who is offered treatment or discharged (NG225).
  • Assess needs instead; agree a safety plan; reduce access to means; smaller prescription quantities; involve family or friends with consent.
  • After self-harm: check physical harm; prioritise mental health referral if distress is rising, high or sustained, self-harm or suicidal intent is increasing, they ask for it, or you are concerned.
  • Crisis contacts saved in their phone: NHS 111 option 2 (England), Samaritans 116 123, 999 in an emergency.

3Before the first prescriptionNG222 · MHRA · SPC

  • Mania screen done and recorded
  • Previous antidepressants: what helped, what didn't, why stopped
  • Interactions: NSAIDs, aspirin, anticoagulants (bleeding); tramadol, triptans, St John's wort (serotonin); QT drugs (citalopram/escitalopram contraindicated)
  • Pregnant, planning pregnancy or breastfeeding?
  • Aged 18–25 or at increased suicide risk → 1-week review booked
  • Overdose risk → small quantities, a drug safer in overdose

4Work-up — by history, not a panelNG222 · NG12

  • Past mania or hypomania; family history
  • Alcohol (AUDIT-C), drugs
  • Drugs that lower mood
  • Debt, housing, abuse, loneliness
  • TFTs, FBC, B12/folate, HbA1c if indicated
  • Sodium if older or on a diuretic

Citalopram, escitalopram: not with long QT or other QT-prolonging drugs; consider ECG in cardiac disease; correct low K⁺ or Mg²⁺ first. Unexplained weight loss or iron-deficiency anaemia → investigate on its own merits (NG12), not as "part of the depression".

Less severe: do not routinely offer an antidepressant first-line; only if it is the person's informed preference. Refer or signpost to NHS Talking Therapies (self-referral possible); chase it at review.

6Starting an SSRING222 · MHRA · SPC

  • Usually an SSRI, chosen with the person: sertraline 50 mg OD is a common start; mirtazapine if sleep and appetite dominate.
  • Explain: benefit builds over weeks; early nausea or jitteriness usually settles; not addictive, but never stop suddenly.
  • Review, usually within 2 weeks; 1 week if aged 18–25 or at increased suicide risk — after starting and after a dose increase. Then regularly.
  • Give an interim contact route and the crisis numbers; prescribe smaller quantities if there is any risk.
  • More severe depression: NG222 lists individual CBT plus an antidepressant first — refer for therapy at the same visit.

Page 2 · Review and continue the medication review: response → optimise or switch → continue → stop properly

8TargetsNG222

  • Remission: back to usual function, with the PHQ-9 trend to support it — not a score alone.
  • Then continue the antidepressant for at least 6 months after remission.
  • Longer for recurrent depression, residual symptoms or high relapse risk — review at least every 6 months.

9Not improving? Check before you switchNG222

  • Adequate trial — therapeutic dose, actually taken, long enough
  • Alcohol and drugs — AUDIT-C again
  • Diagnosis — bipolar II, physical illness, ADHD, trauma, perimenopause
  • Therapy — referral made and started?
  • Life drivers — abuse, debt, housing, caring
  • Side effects driving non-adherence?

10Annual review checklist6-monthly if continuing

  • PHQ-9 and function; GAD-7 if anxious
  • Suicide and self-harm asked again — risk changes
  • Adherence; sexual side effects asked directly; weight; sleep
  • Is it still needed? Shared decision to continue or taper
  • Sodium if older or on a diuretic; BP on venlafaxine or duloxetine
  • Interactions: NSAIDs, anticoagulants, tramadol, triptans, St John's wort
  • Pregnancy plans and contraception
  • Alcohol and drugs (AUDIT-C)
  • Relapse plan: early-warning signs; group CBT or MBCT if stopping
  • Record PHQ-9, the plan and the next review date

11Optimise → switch → combineNG222 further-line treatment

Step 1 · reviewUsually within 2 weeks (1 week if 18–25 or at risk). Side effects, risk, adherence, early response.
Step 2 · optimisePartial response → increase the dose within the licence, if tolerated. Add psychological therapy if not already.
Step 3 · switchNo useful response after an adequate trial → another SSRI or a different class (e.g. mirtazapine, an SNRI). Check a switching guide for the exact pair.
Step 4 · specialistCombination or augmentation (second antidepressant, antipsychotic, lithium) → seek specialist advice or refer.

12Stopping — taper, don't stopNG222

  • Reduce in stages, each a proportion of the previous dose, smaller steps as the dose gets lower; liquid forms help at the end.
  • Withdrawal: dizziness, "electric shock" sensations, nausea, anxiety, poor sleep. Often mild, but can be severe or last weeks to months.
  • Severe symptoms → consider going back to the previous dose, then reduce more slowly in smaller steps.
  • Old mood symptoms returning later = relapse, not withdrawal.

13Side effectsSPC · MHRA

NauseaTake with food; usually settles early on
Sexual dysfunctionAsk — often hidden; reduce dose or switch
Agitation, activationEarly weeks: review sooner, ask about suicidal thoughts
HyponatraemiaConfusion, falls in older people → check sodium
BleedingHigher with NSAIDs or anticoagulants; small rise in postpartum haemorrhage if used in the month before delivery (MHRA)

14When to referNG222 · NG225 · CG192

  • Same day: active suicidal plan or intent, psychosis, severe self-neglect.
  • Specialist mental health: significant risk; suspected bipolar disorder; more severe depression not responding after optimising and switching; combination or augmentation needed.
  • Perinatal team: severe depression or other severe mental illness, now or in the past, in pregnancy or the first year after birth.
  • Under 18 → CAMHS. Rising distress or escalating self-harm → priority mental health referral (NG225).

16Special groups and never-eventsNG222 · NG225 · CG192 · CG185 · MHRA

  • Aged 18–25: higher risk of suicidal thoughts early in treatment — 1-week review and a clear "contact us" plan.
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: don't stop an effective antidepressant abruptly; decide with perinatal mental health advice (CG192).
  • Older adults: sodium, falls, interactions; citalopram max 20 mg, escitalopram max 10 mg over 65.
  • Long-term physical illness: depression is common and treatable — treat it with the same care, and check interactions with their other medicines.
  • Never: use a risk scale to predict suicide · start an antidepressant alone after past mania · stop an antidepressant suddenly · exceed the citalopram or escitalopram caps · leave a review unbooked after starting.
