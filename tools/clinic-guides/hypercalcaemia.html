Hypercalcaemia: the raised calcium CLINIC GUIDE

Albumin-adjusted calcium above 2.6 mmol/L in adults 18+. Not for children (paediatric or endocrine advice), pregnancy (specialist MDT) or the acute hospital treatment itself.

Severity is never read off the number alone: a modest calcium in a sick patient is still an emergency. Never delay urgent assessment for a repeat sample.

1Confirm it is realNG132 · use local laboratory ranges

  • Use the laboratory-reported albumin-adjusted calcium, not a hand-applied formula: methods differ between laboratories. Normal is about 2.2–2.6 mmol/L.
  • Repeat at least once if ≥2.6 mmol/L, or ≥2.5 with features of PHPT. Timing follows the level and symptoms; act on the first result if high or symptomatic.
  • Spurious or transient rise: prolonged tourniquet or venous stasis, dehydration, a sample taken during acute illness. Compare with previous results.
  • Do not request ionised calcium routinely for PHPT (NG132); discuss with the laboratory only if adjustment is unreliable (critical illness, severe low albumin).
  • Measure PTH once the raised calcium is confirmed: random, non-fasting, with a calcium on the same sample. Do not defer it for months.

5Work-up checklistNG132 · NG12

  • Adjusted Ca + albumin: confirm
  • Intact PTH + same-sample Ca
  • U&E, eGFR: AKI, CKD
  • Phosphate: low in PHPT
  • Magnesium, ALP, LFTs
  • FBC, ESR or plasma viscosity
  • 25-OH vitamin D: deficiency masks PHPT
  • TSH
  • Electrophoresis + free light chains if myeloma fits
  • ECG if ≥3.0, rising or symptomatic
  • CXR if cancer or sarcoid suspected
  • Urine calcium if FHH considered

Low phosphate with a non-suppressed PTH supports PTH-mediated disease; a raised phosphate or ALP points elsewhere but is not diagnostic.

Not routine in primary care: PTHrP, 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, ACE, ionised calcium, sestamibi or neck imaging (specialist-directed). ECG: short QT or arrhythmia supports severity; no fixed QTc cut-off.

8Self-care in stable mild diseaseNG132

  • Hydration: drink normally and avoid dehydration, within cardiac and renal limits; illness with vomiting → seek advice early.
  • Keep normal dietary calcium: cutting out dairy raises PTH. No unprescribed calcium or vitamin D.
  • Stay active; avoid immobility and excess alcohol. Weight-bearing exercise for bone health.
  • Stone-former: fluids within limits, less salt, moderate animal protein; recurrent stones → urology.

"Your calcium is a little high. The commonest reason is a small, non-cancerous overactive gland in the neck, but we need a second blood test to check the hormone that controls calcium. Drink normally, do not cut out dairy, and call 999 if you become confused or drowsy, or get seen the same day if very thirsty or being sick."

9Follow-up and monitoringNG132

  • Mild PHPT: adjusted Ca + eGFR annually; decide each year: survey or refer
  • DXA (spine, hip, distal radius) at diagnosis and every 2–3 years
  • Renal ultrasound at diagnosis, then only if a stone is suspected
  • Drug stopped → recheck Ca; not normal → investigate for PHPT
  • After discharge: Ca, U&E, drugs reconciled, specialist follow-up confirmed
  • After parathyroidectomy: surgical team's plan for low calcium ("hungry bone") recorded
  • Same day if a routine Ca returns ≥3.0, or symptoms at any level
  • Not NG132 recalls: routine annual 24-hour urine Ca, routine 2–3-yearly renal scans

11Pitfalls and never-eventsNG132

  • Never stop lithium unilaterally · never start community IV fluids unmonitored · never use a loop diuretic to lower calcium.
  • Never refer every suppressed PTH straight to a cancer pathway, or miss myeloma in hypercalcaemia with back pain or anaemia.
  • Do not use bisphosphonates to lower calcium long term in PHPT (NG132) · do not restrict dietary calcium · no blanket "drink 2–3 L a day" in heart failure, CKD or frailty.
  • Low vitamin D can mask PHPT: recheck calcium after correcting it.
  • A single low urine calcium ratio does not diagnose FHH or rule out surgery · do not order ACE or PTHrP to "screen".

12Safety-net and special groupsSociety for Endocrinology · NG132

  • 999: confusion, drowsiness or collapse · seizure · palpitations or chest pain · vomiting and unable to keep fluids down · back pain with leg weakness. Same day: worsening weakness or lethargy, unable to eat or drink for more than a day, severe bone pain, much less urine.
  • Pregnancy: specialist (endocrine and obstetric) advice; do not start cinacalcet or bisphosphonates. Offer parathyroid surgery to women with PHPT who are considering pregnancy (NG132). Children: paediatric or endocrine advice; adult thresholds do not apply.
  • Digoxin: hypercalcaemia potentiates toxicity. CKD: tertiary HPT is usually on the renal pathway. Known cancer: calcium and any antiresorptive follow the oncology plan.
