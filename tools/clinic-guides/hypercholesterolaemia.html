Lipids and hypercholesterolaemia CLINIC GUIDE

For the new raised cholesterol, statin start and the annual review, including spotting familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH). Adults 18+. Not for children, pregnancy or acute coronary syndrome.
NICE NG238 (2023) · CG71 (FH) · TA385 · TA694 · TA733 · TA393/394
Reviewed Oct 2026 · next review Apr 2027

Page 1 · Assess and start raised lipids → frame the patient → right statin, right dose

!Act todayNICE NG238 · CG71

  • Acute, ongoing or recent chest pain → 999 as possible ACS, whatever the cholesterol shows.
  • Triglycerides >20 mmol/L not explained by alcohol or poor glycaemic control → urgent specialist review. Very high triglycerides with abdominal pain or vomiting → same-day assessment (pancreatitis).
  • Suspected homozygous FH: LDL-C >13 mmol/L in an adult or >11 in a child → specialist FH centre without delay; do not wait to apply Simon Broome or DLCN criteria.
  • Heavy proteinuria, oedema and low albumin (nephrotic syndrome) → urgent medical assessment; do not just treat the cholesterol.

3Work-up before a statinNG238

  • Full lipid profile: TC, HDL, non-HDL, triglycerides
  • ALT or AST baseline
  • HbA1c
  • U&E, eGFR; urine ACR if CKD
  • TSH if symptoms or signs of hypothyroidism
  • QRISK3 (primary prevention only)
  • Alcohol history and drug review
  • CK only if unexplained muscle pain

Treat secondary causes first: hypothyroidism, diabetes, nephrotic syndrome, CKD, liver disease, alcohol excess, drugs (steroids, ciclosporin, retinoids, some antipsychotics and antiretrovirals) — without delaying a statin in established CVD.

Triglycerides 10–20 mmol/L → repeat fasting (after 5 days, within 2 weeks) and look for secondary causes; still >10 → specialist advice. 4.5–9.9 → QRISK3 may underestimate risk; non-HDL >7.5 → specialist advice.

4Is this FH?CG71 · NG238

Suspect FH: total cholesterol (TC) >7.5 mmol/L, especially with CHD before 60 in the patient or a first-degree relative, or tendon xanthomata. TC >9.0 or non-HDL >7.5 → specialist assessment even with no family history.

Search the records: under 30 with TC >7.5; aged 30+ with TC >9.0.

Simon BroomeCriteria (adult TC >7.5 or LDL-C >4.9 mmol/L, plus)
DefiniteTendon xanthomata in patient or 1st/2nd-degree relative, or a DNA-confirmed mutation
PossibleMI in a 1st-degree relative <60 or 2nd-degree relative <50, or TC >7.5 in an adult 1st/2nd-degree relative

Possible or definite Simon Broome, or DLCN score >5 → refer to the specialist FH service for DNA testing and cascade testing of 1st-, 2nd- and, where possible, 3rd-degree relatives.

6Start safelyNG238 · SPC

Transaminases raised but <3× ULN → not a reason to withhold a statin.

Baseline CK >5× ULN → re-measure after 7 days; still >5× → do not start, find the cause. Raised but <5× → start at a lower dose.

Interactions: simvastatin with amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil, macrolides or azole antifungals; check every new prescription.

Women who could become pregnant: statins are contraindicated in pregnancy — discuss contraception (box 15).

Recheck a full lipid profile and transaminases 2–3 months after starting or changing treatment.

9Before every step-upNG238

  • Adherence — taken daily? Myalgia fears? Repeat-prescription record
  • Lifestyle — diet, alcohol, weight: agree one change
  • Secondary cause — TSH, HbA1c, eGFR, urine, alcohol, new drugs
  • Dose — is the statin at the maximum tolerated dose?
  • Is it FH? — very high baseline or family history (box 4)

10The lipid ladderNG238 · TA385 · TA694 · TA733 · TA393/394

Step 1 · statinDose by group (box 5). Atorvastatin 20 → 40 → 80 mg to the highest tolerated dose if not at target.
Step 2 · switch or reduceNot tolerated → stop, rechallenge when symptoms settle, lower dose or a different statin. Treat at the maximum tolerated dose.
Step 3 · ezetimibe10 mg OD added to the maximum tolerated statin when not at target (can be considered even at target in secondary prevention). Alone if statins can't be taken.
Step 4 · NICE TA optionsBempedoic acid + ezetimibe or inclisiran, only within the TA criteria below; PCSK9 inhibitors via the lipid clinic.

11Side effects — adjust, don't abandonNG238 · SPC

Muscle achesCheck CK only if symptomatic; <4× ULN → other causes; continue if tolerable, or pause and rechallenge/switch; 4–10× ULN or intolerable → stop 4–6 weeks, then rechallenge; ≥10× → same day
Severe pain, weakness, dark urineStop; same-day CK and renal function (rhabdomyolysis)
ALT/AST ≥3× ULNRecheck; stop if persistently ≥3×. <3× → continue
Blood glucoseSmall rise in diabetes risk; benefit outweighs it — monitor, don't withhold
Clarithromycin, erythromycin, itraconazole, ketoconazoleSimvastatin contraindicated — pause it for the course; others per SPC

12Annual review checklistevery patient on lipid-lowering

  • Medicines review: adherence, side effects, lifestyle
  • Consider a non-fasting non-HDL to inform the discussion
  • BP, HbA1c, eGFR, smoking — whole CVD risk, not just lipids
  • Still at target? If not → box 9, then box 10
  • FH: structured review at least yearly; has cascade testing happened?
  • Women who could become pregnant: plans and contraception
  • Not on a statin and QRISK3 <10%: reassess risk periodically

15Special groups and never-eventsCG71 · NG238

  • Pregnancy: stop statins and other systemic lipid-lowering drugs 3 months before trying to conceive; conceives on a statin → stop at once and refer urgently for fetal assessment.
  • FH: diagnosing one person means testing the family — check the cascade at every review.
  • Never: run QRISK3 in FH or established CVD · stop a statin for a mild ALT rise · leave a statin-intolerant high-risk patient on nothing · ignore chest pain because lipids are "fine".
