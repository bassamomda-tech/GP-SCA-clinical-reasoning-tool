Raised PSA CLINIC GUIDE

Men and people with a prostate aged 18+ with a raised or rising PSA. Not for men under active surveillance or on ADT (follow the urology or oncology plan), or acute retention and sepsis (emergency pathways).

1Confirm it is realPCRMP · GIRFT 2024 · SPC

Ejaculation, vigorous exerciseAvoid for 48 h before the test (cycling included)
UTI or prostatitisDo not test while active; MSU; repeat once settled, not within 6 weeks of infection
Biopsy, catheter, cystoscopy, retentionRaise PSA: interpret with caution; biopsy: wait 6 weeks
5-ARI (finasteride, dutasteride)Roughly halves PSA after 6 months; a confirmed rise from the lowest on-treatment value needs assessment
Lab and timingSame laboratory for trends; take blood before DRE where possible

Do not repeat a raised PSA routinely before referral unless a probable transient cause exists (GIRFT 2024). A DRE is not needed before referral.

4Work-up checklistNG12 · NG131 · CG97

  • Symptoms: LUTS, ED, haematuria
  • Urine dip ± MSU
  • DRE if trained and appropriate
  • Previous PSA values
  • 5-ARI or prostate surgery
  • Family history, BRCA2
  • Ethnicity: Black ancestry
  • Bone pain, weight loss
  • Bladder palpation if retention
  • Lower-limb neurology if back pain
  • IPSS for LUTS
  • Comorbidity, life expectancy

5Men without symptomsPCRMP · UK NSC · GIRFT

  • No national PSA screening programme (UK NSC). Offer a balanced discussion: false positives, overdiagnosis, MRI or biopsy may follow, a normal result does not exclude cancer.
  • A man aged 50+ who still wants a test after counselling may have it (PCRMP).
  • Higher risk: Black men, strong family history, known BRCA2 variant: discuss testing earlier; follow clinical genetics advice.
  • Do not arrange BRCA testing just because of one high PSA.

6What to put in the referralNG12 · GIRFT 2024

  • PSA value, date, earlier values
  • Symptoms; IPSS
  • DRE finding, if done
  • 5-ARI or prostate surgery
  • Family history, ethnicity, BRCA2
  • Comorbidity, preferences discussed
  • Bone pain, weight loss
  • Anticoagulants (for biopsy)

8Self-care and supportPCRMP · NG131

  • Before a repeat test: no ejaculation or vigorous exercise, including cycling, for 48 h.
  • Unable to pass urine, fever with shivering, or new back pain → contact us the same day.
  • Prostate Cancer UK: decision aid and specialist nurses. Regular exercise helps during and after treatment.

9Treatment side effects — GP roleNG131 · specialist plan

  • Hot flushes on ADT: medroxyprogesterone 20 mg daily, initially for 10 weeks (NG131); cyproterone if ineffective.
  • Bone: assess fracture risk at ADT start; no bisphosphonate solely to prevent osteoporosis (NG131).
  • Metabolic: weight, BP, HbA1c, lipids; exercise.
  • ED after treatment: PDE5 inhibitors on the NHS; specialist nurse or psychosexual support.
  • ADT itself is specialist-initiated; shared care only by formal agreement.

10Follow-up and monitoring checklistNG131 · CG97 · NG12

  • Referral accepted and appointment received; outcome reviewed
  • Discharged after low-risk MRI or biopsy: written PSA re-referral threshold and interval on file
  • After radical treatment: PSA no earlier than 6 weeks, at least 6-monthly for 2 years, then at least yearly (NG131)
  • Active surveillance: follow the urology protocol
  • On a 5-ARI: compare with the lowest on-treatment value
  • Below threshold: reassess if symptoms persist or progress
  • Safety-net: back pain with leg weakness or bladder change → 999; unable to pass urine → same day

12After referral — what happens nextNG131 · GIRFT 2024

ReferralSuspected cancer pathway; 28-day Faster Diagnosis Standard. Fit men usually go straight to MRI.
MRI firstMultiparametric MRI before any biopsy (NG131); a low-risk MRI may avoid biopsy.
Biopsy if suspiciousTargeted biopsy guided by the MRI; risk group set by urology.
OutcomeDischarge with a written PSA plan, active surveillance, or radical treatment after shared decision.

13Pitfalls and never-eventsNG12 · NG131 · NG234

  • Never test during active UTI or prostatitis · never let a normal DRE or normal PSA override a malignant-feeling prostate or symptoms.
  • Never forget 5-ARI halving · never use PSA velocity, density or free-to-total ratio as primary-care referral rules.
  • Never start ADT or anti-androgen flare cover in primary care · do not order a bone scan just because PSA is above 10.
  • Never lose track of a suspected cancer referral: confirm the appointment and the outcome · never arrange genetic testing on one high PSA alone.

14Say it like thisraised PSA

"PSA is a protein the prostate makes. It goes up with a larger prostate, infection and some cancers, so a raised result doesn't mean you have cancer. The next step is usually an MRI scan through the hospital team, which tells us much more."
