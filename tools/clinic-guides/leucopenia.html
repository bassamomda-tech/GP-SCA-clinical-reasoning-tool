Leucopenia: the low white cell count CLINIC GUIDE

A low white cell or neutrophil count in adults 18+. Not for children, or for counts expected during chemotherapy (follow the acute oncology plan). Low lymphocytes alone: see the lymphopenia guide.

Fever can be absent in neutropenic or steroid-treated patients, and CRP can be normal despite serious infection. A stable known chronic neutropenia goes through the local haematology route, not 999.

1Confirm it is realthe ANC is the number that matters

  • Look at the differential: a low WCC may be low neutrophils, low lymphocytes or both. ANC = WCC × neutrophil %.
  • Transient: viral infections (flu, EBV, CMV) commonly lower the count; repeat once recovered.
  • Artefact: delayed or old samples and laboratory flags — repeat and ask for a blood film if it does not fit.
  • Trend beats a single value: a falling ANC matters more than a stable low one.
  • Duffy-null-associated (benign ethnic) neutropenia: well, stable, isolated, normal Hb and platelets, normal film, no recurrent infection. Never diagnose it from ethnicity alone.

5Work-up checklisttarget to the pattern

  • Repeat FBC + differential, trend
  • Blood film: dysplasia, blasts
  • Medicine review, including OTC
  • U&E, LFTs; CRP if indicated
  • B12 and folate, esp. macrocytosis
  • HIV test when appropriate
  • ANA, RF, anti-dsDNA only if suggested
  • Immunoglobulins if recurrent infection
  • Pancytopenia: retics, LDH, haptoglobin, TFTs
  • EBV/CMV only in a compatible illness

No bone marrow and no G-CSF from primary care. Never delay transfer or referral to "complete investigations" when the ANC is <0.5 or there is pancytopenia.

6Management by the commonest causesCG151 · NG239 · SPC · Green Book

CauseFirst lineCheck and continue
Suspected neutropenic sepsisImmediate transfer; empirical antibiotics are given at once in hospital. Do not give oral antibiotics and send home, and do not wait for the ANCShocked or deteriorating: sepsis pathway while arranging 999
Drug-inducedWithhold the likely drug where clinically safe; urgent FBC. Carbimazole: stop immediately. Clozapine: the brand monitoring service (CPMS, DMS or ZTAS: WBC and neutrophils, green/amber/red; not EMA/US ANC-only thresholds) and the mental health team decide — do not stop it alone in a stable patient. 7Living with a low neutrophil countproportionate advice · Green Book

Hands and foodHand washing is the single most useful step. Normal diet with ordinary safe food handling; no "neutropenic diet" for stable mild neutropenia.
People and petsNormal social contact; avoid obvious infections such as chickenpox where practical. Healthy pets and gardening are fine with gloves and hand washing.
MouthBrush twice daily with a soft brush. Keep routine dental care; dental work during profound or treatment-related neutropenia follows specialist advice.
VaccinesAnnual flu and pneumococcal (Green Book chapter 25). Live vaccines depend on immunosuppression, not the ANC alone.

8Follow-up and monitoringlocal haematology advice

  • Drug-induced: repeat FBC at the interval agreed with the specialist team; the count should be recovering
  • Post-viral: repeat once recovered; target back within the laboratory range, then stop
  • Possible Duffy-null: repeat counts to show the ANC is stable and isolated with a normal film; then reassure
  • Chronic idiopathic neutropenia: follow the haematology plan
  • Re-refer: ANC falling despite stopping the drug, new Hb or platelet drop, recurrent significant infections
  • Watch the trend: a falling ANC over months suggests marrow or autoimmune disease even if each value is "mild"
  • Recurrent significant infections at an acceptable count → immunoglobulins and haematology review
  • Diagnosis and plan in the problem list

9Safety-nettell the patient

Immediately (999 if confused, breathless or collapsing): fever, shivers or feeling suddenly unwell — especially on cancer treatment (call the acute oncology line). Same day: sore throat, mouth ulcers, cough, burning urine, diarrhoea, tummy pain, new bruising or bleeding.

10When and where to referCG151 · NG12 · local haematology pathway

ImmediateSuspected neutropenic sepsis; any low count with fever, sepsis or deterioration; blasts or a film reported as acute leukaemia.
Same-day haematologyNew or falling ANC <0.5 (afebrile); new pancytopenia, dysplasia or rapidly falling counts; suspected drug agranulocytosis.
Urgent haematologyPersistent ANC 0.5–1.0 · persistent unexplained low WCC · abnormal film · neutropenia with spleen, nodes, recurrent infection or B symptoms · mild stable pancytopenia.
Routine or primary careRoutine: persistent ANC 1.0–1.5 not explained by Duffy-null. Primary care: stable mild isolated, recovering post-viral, confirmed Duffy-null.

Infectious diseases or HIV team: every person with HIV and a cytopenia; suspected TB. Recurrent infections are judged by severity, site, unusual organisms and hospital treatment.

11Pitfalls and never-eventsCG151 · SPC

  • Never manage suspected neutropenic sepsis with oral antibiotics in the community or wait for the count.
  • Never rely on fever: it may be absent on steroids or with neutropenia.
  • Never label Duffy-null-associated neutropenia on ethnicity alone, or when there are other cytopenias.
  • Never give folic acid alone before B12 deficiency is excluded or treated.
  • Never stop clozapine on your own in a stable patient — contact the monitoring service and mental health team today.
  • Carbimazole users should know to stop it and get an urgent FBC for sore throat, mouth ulcers or fever.
  • A neutropenic patient may have pneumonia or sepsis with few signs and a normal CRP.
  • A stable chronic ANC of 0.8 in a well patient is not the same as a new falling 0.8 with fever.

12Say it like thisplain English

"Your neutrophils, the white cells that fight bacteria, are lower than usual. Often that's after a virus, a medicine or just how your body is made. I'll repeat it, but if you get a fever, shivers or feel suddenly very unwell, don't wait — get seen straight away."
