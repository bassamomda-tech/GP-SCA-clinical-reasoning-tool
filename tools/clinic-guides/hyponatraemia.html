Hyponatraemia: the low sodium CLINIC GUIDE

Interpreting a serum sodium below 135 mmol/L in adults 18+. Not for pregnancy or the peripartum period (obstetric guidance, NICE NG235), children, or hospital correction with hypertonic saline.

1Confirm it is realrepeat urgently if unexpected

  • Repeat U&E the same day if the result is new, unexpected, below 130 mmol/L or there are symptoms. Send the urine tests at the same time.
  • Sample from a drip arm: hypotonic or dextrose fluid dilutes the sample. Resample from the other arm.
  • Pseudohyponatraemia: very high triglycerides or a paraprotein (myeloma) with indirect electrode methods; measured osmolality is normal. No treatment needed.
  • Hyperglycaemia (or mannitol): water shifts out of cells, so osmolality is high. Treat the glucose; do not apply a single fixed correction factor.
  • Only hypotonic hyponatraemia (osmolality <275 mOsm/kg) is true water excess. Normal serum osmolality is 275–295 mOsm/kg.
  • Duration unknown? Treat it as chronic: the safer correction rate applies.

Remote or out-of-hours result: volume status cannot be judged by phone, so Na⁺ below 130 mmol/L needs a face-to-face examination.

3Classify in four stepsEuropean guideline 2014 · SfE 2022

1 · Serum osmolality<275 true hypotonic · 275–295 pseudo · >295 glucose or mannitol.
2 · Urine osmolality≤100: water excess or low solute. >100: vasopressin is active, go to step 3.
3 · Urine sodium≤30: low effective volume (losses, heart failure, cirrhosis). >30: SIAD, diuretic, adrenal, renal salt loss.
4 · Examine and review drugsVolume status refines, not replaces, the urine tests. Unsure → say so and ask.

Urine Na⁺ is unreliable on diuretics, in CKD or after saline. Never label SIAD without TFTs, cortisol, urine tests and a drug review.

5Work-up checklistbloods and urine together

  • U&E: K⁺, urea, creatinine
  • Measured serum osmolality
  • Spot urine osmolality: first discriminator
  • Spot urine Na⁺: before any IV fluid
  • Glucose: excludes hyperglycaemia
  • TFTs: hypothyroidism mimics SIAD
  • 8–9 am cortisol if adrenal cause possible
  • LFTs, albumin: liver, nephrotic
  • Lipids, protein electrophoresis if pseudo
  • Every medicine, alcohol and fluid intake
  • Lying and standing BP, JVP, oedema
  • Chest X-ray if SIAD has no clear cause

Cortisol: interpret with the local assay and NG243; do not delay treatment for it. ADH (vasopressin) levels are not useful in primary care.

6Management of the commonest causesSfE 2022 · European guideline 2014 · NICE CKS · NG243

CauseFirst lineCheck and continue
Drug-inducedStop the thiazide, SSRI, carbamazepine or other likely drug if safe; review unnecessary PPIsRecheck Na⁺ promptly after stopping. Persists → investigate as SIAD. Never restart a thiazide without sodium monitoring
Hypovolaemic (D&V, diuretic)Oral rehydration if stable and drinking; stop the thiazide. Shocked → 999. Never fluid-restrictOnce volume is restored, a brisk water diuresis can raise Na⁺ fast: recheck closely
SIADRemove the cause. Individualised fluid restriction counting every drink (tea, soup, juice); no generic fixed volume without specialist input. Keep normal dietary salt and soluteReview the sodium and adherence early. Urine:plasma osmolality ratio >1 → restriction alone unlikely to work: specialist advice
HypothyroidismTreat overt hypothyroidism on the thyroid pathway (check TSH and free T4)Recheck Na⁺. Mild hypothyroidism rarely explains significant hyponatraemia: keep looking
Adrenal insufficiencyCrisis → hydrocortisone and 999. Suspected, not in crisis → same-day endocrinology (NG243)Sick-day rules, steroid card; recheck U&E as advised
Heart failure, cirrhosisTreat the condition with the specialist team; no reflex IV salineDiuretic plan per specialist; daily weights
Low solute, beerCut alcohol, restore nutritionHigh risk of over-rapid correction
Acute: exercise, MDMA, postoperativeAny symptom → 999; no hypotonic fluids while waitingHospital correction; sodium checked on recovery

Correction limits (hospital, maximum not target): rise no more than 10 mmol/L in the first 24 h, then 8 mmol/L in each further 24 h (SfE 2022); slower still with alcohol dependence, malnutrition, liver disease or hypokalaemia. Over-correction risks irreversible osmotic demyelination. Specialist or hospital only: hypertonic saline, oral urea, tolvaptan; demeclocycline is not advised (European guideline).

7Follow-up and monitoring checklistNICE CKS · SfE 2022

  • Recheck Na⁺ promptly after stopping a drug, changing fluids or starting treatment
  • Na⁺ 125–129 not admitted: early repeat agreed with the specialist
  • Fluid restriction reviewed early: sodium, adherence, thirst
  • After discharge: recheck at the interval named in the discharge plan; ask for one if missing
  • Target: stable Na⁺ ≥130 with the cause found; normalising is not always needed in chronic mild cases
  • Widen intervals once Na⁺ is stable above 130; review the cause yearly in chronic SIAD
  • Re-refer if Na⁺ falls below 125 again or new neurology appears
  • Remote result below 130 → face-to-face examination, not a phone plan
  • Cause and culprit drug recorded; future prescribers warned

9Special situationsSfE 2022 · NICE CKS · NG243

  • Older people who fall: check sodium; Na⁺ <130 with falls → falls service as well as the cause.
  • MDMA or endurance exercise: acute, can be severe in the young; any symptom → 999.
  • On long-term steroids: never stop abruptly; low Na⁺ with vomiting or low BP is adrenal crisis until proven otherwise.
  • Heart failure on ACE inhibitor or ARB: low sodium is a marker of severity; seek specialist advice before changing diuretics.

10Pitfalls and never-eventsSfE 2022 · European guideline

  • Never reassure because a patient seems well below 125 mmol/L, or call a number "mild" when there are symptoms or a fast fall.
  • Never fluid-restrict a dry patient, or give saline for SIAD or to an oedematous patient.
  • Low Na⁺ plus raised K⁺, each looking mild: think adrenal crisis.
  • Never chase normal sodium quickly: a fast rise is more dangerous than the low level.
  • Mild chronic hyponatraemia still causes falls, unsteadiness and poor attention.
  • Never start urea, tolvaptan or demeclocycline in primary care, or give hypertonic saline outside hospital.
  • Never accept "SIADH" on a discharge letter without the cause and the plan; ask for them.

11Safety-net and self-care — say it, then document itSfE 2022 · NICE CKS

Call 999A fit, collapse or fainting, unusual drowsiness, new or worse confusion, severe headache, vomiting or breathing difficulty.
GP or 111 todayFeeling sick, dizzy on standing, unsteady or a new fall, cramps, very thirsty with little urine, cannot keep up with fluids.
Fluid limitEvery drink counts, including tea, soup and fruit. Use a measured jug and write it down. Attend the repeat blood test even if well.
Prevent the next oneEndurance exercise: drink to thirst, not to a schedule. Tell every new prescriber about the low sodium.

12Say it like thisplain English

"The sodium, or salt, level in your blood is lower than it should be. This is usually not about how much salt you eat: more often a medicine or your body holding on to too much water is diluting it. We'll find the cause, fix it gently rather than quickly, and recheck your blood. If you become confused, very drowsy, have a fit or keep vomiting, call 999."
