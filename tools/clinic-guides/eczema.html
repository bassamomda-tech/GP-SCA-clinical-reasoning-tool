Atopic eczema CLINIC GUIDE

For children and adults in primary care. NICE CG57 applies to children under 12; adolescents and adults follow NICE CKS. Not for contact, seborrhoeic or venous eczema, or chronic hand eczema.

Page 1 · Assess and start dangerous presentations → diagnose and grade → emollient base → steroid by site

!Act todayNICE CG57 · QS44 statement 7 · NG190 · NG12

  • Eczema herpeticum — painful, rapidly worsening eczema with clustered, uniform, punched-out erosions or blisters, often fever → systemic aciclovir now and same-day dermatology advice. Around the eyes → same-day ophthalmology too. Don't wait for a swab.
  • Systemically unwell with spreading infection, suspected sepsis or severe cellulitis → same-day assessment or admission. Erythroderma (most of the skin red) → same day.
  • Nipple: aged 50+ with discharge, retraction or other change of concern in one nipple only → suspected cancer pathway referral (NG12). Persistent one-sided nipple "eczema" at any age → think Paget's.

1Is it atopic eczema?CG57 §1.1 · CKS

Children under 12: an itchy skin condition plus 3 or more of:

  • Visible flexural dermatitis (cheeks or extensor surfaces if 18 months or under)
  • Personal history of flexural dermatitis (cheeks or extensors if 18 months or under)
  • Dry skin in the last 12 months
  • Asthma or allergic rhinitis (or atopic disease in a first-degree relative if under 4)
  • Onset before age 2 (not used under 4)

Skin of colour: may affect extensor surfaces; redness is harder to see — judge by itch, thickening and colour change.

Adults: clinical diagnosis. Consider contact dermatitis, psoriasis, seborrhoeic dermatitis, scabies, tinea, drug eruption and, for fixed atypical plaques, cutaneous T-cell lymphoma.

2First visit checklistCG57 · CKS

  • Sites mapped (they set potency)
  • Severity and impact recorded
  • Signs of infection or herpeticum
  • Triggers: soaps, heat, wool, pets, stress
  • Steroid fears asked about
  • Who applies it, and how much
  • Infant, moderate–severe: milk or egg reactions?
  • Smoker, oxygen, mobility: fire risk

5Emollients — the base of every stepCG57 · MHRA DSU Dec 2018

  • Leave-on, unperfumed, the one they'll use; daily, even when clear; used as the soap substitute.
  • Children: 250–500 g a week, plus a supply for nursery or school. Adults with widespread eczema often need more.
  • No routine emollient bath additives for children (CG57 2023 update: no added benefit).
  • Fire risk applies to all emollients, paraffin-based or not: residue on clothing and bedding fuels a fire. No smoking or naked flames; wash fabrics often. Don't stop an effective emollient for this.

8What control looks likeCG57 · QS44

GoalSign it is met
SkinClear or mild between flares
Sleep and lifeSleeping through; school, work, mood unaffected
FlaresTreated early with the right potency, settling quickly
UseEmollient issue matches the area; steroid used, not feared

9Before every step-upCG57 · CKS

  • Quantity — check issue dates: enough emollient for the area?
  • Technique — how much, how often, where; FTU shown
  • Steroid fear — stopped early? Separate short courses from long-term misuse
  • Potency for site — mild on thick limb skin won't work
  • Infection — weeping, crusting, punched-out lesions
  • Diagnosis — contact allergy, tinea, scabies?

10The stepped approachCG57 (children) · CKS (adults) · TA82 · TA534

Step 1 · every patientEmollient base, soap substitute, written plan. Step up or down with the severity.
Step 2 · flareSteroid matched to site and severity, once or twice daily until settled, then stop; emollient continues.
Step 3 · frequent flaresProactive: once controlled, steroid on usual flare sites 2 days a week (children, CG57; adults per CKS).
Step 4 · not controlledTopical calcineurin inhibitor, started by a clinician experienced in dermatology (TA82); then specialist: phototherapy, systemic drugs, biologics.

11Specialist treatmentsNICE TA534 · specialist only

Phototherapy and systemic immunosuppressants (e.g. ciclosporin, methotrexate) are started and monitored by dermatology.

Dupilumab (TA534): adults with moderate to severe disease after at least 1 systemic therapy (or unsuitable). Stop at 16 weeks without at least a 50% fall in EASI and a 4-point fall in DLQI. Never started in primary care.

12Infected eczemaNICE NG190

Not systemically unwellNo routine antibiotic; continue emollient and steroid
Localised, antibiotic judged neededFusidic acid 2% three times daily, 5–7 days
Widespread or unwellFlucloxacillin 500 mg four times daily, 5–7 days (adults); penicillin allergy: clarithromycin 250 mg twice daily (500 mg if severe)
SwabNot at first presentation; if recurrent or not improving

Avoid repeated or long topical antibiotic courses (resistance). Replace contaminated tubs.

13Side effects and fixesMHRA DSU Sep 2021 · CG57

Topical steroid withdrawalRedness, burning worse than before after stopping long-term moderate–potent use: lowest potency, shortest time; review repeats
Skin thinningRight potency, right site, shortest course; occlusion raises risk
TCI stingingUsually settles; warn in advance
Emollient stingsSwitch product (avoid aqueous cream as leave-on)

14Review checklistevery review · written plan updated

  • Severity and impact (sleep, school or work, mood)
  • Emollient: type liked, quantity issued vs area
  • Steroid: potency per site, courses used, any repeats running on
  • Frequent flares at the same sites → proactive plan
  • Infections since last review; any herpeticum
  • Withdrawal-type symptoms after stopping a steroid
  • Fire safety discussed; triggers and soap avoided
  • Children: growth if long-term potent or occluded steroids
  • Referral criteria met? (box 15)

15Refer and never-eventsCG57 · NG12 · NG190

  • Same day: herpeticum, erythroderma, systemically unwell infection.
  • Urgent (2 weeks), child under 12: severe eczema not responding to optimal topical treatment after 1 week; infected eczema where treatment has failed.
  • Routine: diagnosis uncertain; not controlled; facial eczema not responding; suspected contact allergy; major sleep or psychosocial impact; recurrent severe infections. Adults: by severity, impact and treatment failure.
  • Never: potent steroid on a baby's face · very potent in a child without advice · bath additives as treatment for children · steroid for a "tinea" rash.
