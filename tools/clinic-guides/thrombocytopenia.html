Low platelets in adults CLINIC GUIDE

Interpreting a platelet count below 150 × 10⁹/L in adults 18+. Not for children and young people (paediatric pathway), patients on chemotherapy (oncology hotline) or inpatient HIT management.

1Is it real?repeat FBC + film

Unexpected low count, well, no bleedingEDTA can make platelets clump (pseudothrombocytopenia): repeat FBC with a film; if clumps, send a citrate sample as the laboratory advises
Clotted or difficult sampleFalsely low: repeat
Recent viral illnessTransient falls are common: repeat once recovered
ComparePrevious counts: new, falling or long-standing and stable?

Assume artefact only in a well patient without bleeding, other cytopenias or film abnormalities. Never delay an urgent referral to exclude clumping.

4Work-up checklistsend and why

  • Repeat FBC + film: clumps, schistocytes, blasts
  • Clotting, fibrinogen if bleeding, liver disease or DIC possible
  • LFTs, albumin: liver disease
  • U&E; LDH, reticulocytes if haemolysis possible
  • HIV, hepatitis B and C if ITP suspected
  • B12, folate
  • ANA if features of SLE
  • Immunoglobulins if suspected ITP

Examine skin and mouth for petechiae and purpura, nodes, liver and spleen, BP and fundi if severe. No routine antiplatelet antibody tests; no marrow in primary care.

6Reading the result in four stepsBSH · NG24 · NG12

1 · Bleeding or unwell?Major bleeding, neurology, fever with a rash, schistocytes or pregnancy symptoms → 999.
2 · Real?Repeat FBC with a film; clumps → citrate sample. Never let this delay an urgent referral.
3 · How low, how fast?<20 or falling fast → same day · unexplained <50 → same-day discussion · <100 → discuss or refer.
4 · Isolated or not?Other cytopenias, nodes or spleen point away from ITP towards marrow, liver or lymphoma.

7Management by causeBSH 2023 · ITP consensus · NG24 · Green Book

CauseFirst lineWho leads, and what next
ITPObserve or treat is a haematology decision: corticosteroids or IVIg (IVIg when a fast rise is needed); later TPO receptor agonists, rituximab or splenectomyHaematology-led. GP: avoid NSAIDs and aspirin, manage bleeding emergencies, prescribe only under shared care. No routine prophylactic platelet transfusion
Suspected HITStop all heparin, including flushes; 4Ts score; non-heparin anticoagulant (e.g. argatroban, fondaparinux, a DOAC in selected patients) per specialistSame-day haematology. Platelet transfusion (NICE NG24): prophylaxis below 10 is not routine in ITP, HIT, TTP or chronic marrow failure; procedures usually need above 50, and above 100 for brain or posterior eye surgery — specialist decision.

8Self-care and the conversationBSH · NG24

Use paracetamol, not ibuprofen or aspirin, unless haematology advises · electric razor, soft toothbrush · tell your dentist · nosebleed: lean forward and pinch the soft part for 10–15 minutes · do not stop blood thinners without advice.

"Platelets are the tiny cells that help your blood clot. Yours are lower than normal. Often that's from a virus, a medicine or the immune system, and it can settle by itself, but we need to find the reason. Any bleeding that won't stop, a bad headache, or a rash with a temperature — call 999."

9Follow-up checklistlocal pathway · haematology plan

  • Repeat FBC + film per local pathway; plot the trend
  • Mild, stable, isolated: recheck; refer if persistent, falling or <100
  • Post-viral: should normalise; persistent → discuss
  • Drug stopped: count should rise; still falling → same day
  • Known ITP: FBC at intervals set by haematology; falling or bleeding → re-refer
  • Steroids: glucose, mood; bone protection on long courses
  • Splenectomy: vaccines up to date; standby antibiotics as advised
  • Before any procedure, agree a platelet threshold with haematology

11Pitfalls and never-eventsBSH 2023 · NG24

  • Never continue any heparin, including flushes, when HIT is suspected.
  • Never wait for the full pentad or ADAMTS13 to act on suspected TTP.
  • Never stop anticoagulants or antiplatelets without discussing the clot risk.
  • Do not call it ITP without a film and a search for secondary causes.
  • Low platelets alone in pregnancy is not HELLP — but check BP, urine and LFTs.
  • Splenomegaly argues against simple ITP.

12Special groupsBSH 2023 · NG24 · NG133

PregnancyMild, isolated, late and well: likely gestational. New, falling or moderate → obstetric and haematology assessment. Hypertension or symptoms → emergency.
On anticoagulantsDo not stop automatically: discuss the same day, weighing bleeding against clot risk (valves, recent VTE, AF).
Before proceduresDental work, injections, surgery: agree a platelet threshold with haematology; usually above 50 for invasive procedures (NG24).
Heavy periodsTranexamic acid or hormonal options with haematology advice; avoid tranexamic acid in TTP, HIT or DIC. Iron if anaemic.

13Who needs an FBC fastNICE NG12

Adults: very urgent FBCWithin 48 hours for unexplained bruising, bleeding or petechiae, pallor, persistent fatigue, fever, recurrent infection, generalised lymphadenopathy or hepatosplenomegaly.
Count says leukaemiaFBC or film reported as acute leukaemia → immediate specialist assessment (standard practice, not an NG12 criterion).
Children and young peopleUnder 25 with unexplained petechiae or hepatosplenomegaly → immediate specialist assessment.
Non-blanching rashWith fever or unwell, at any age → 999 (meningococcal disease, NG240).
