Page 1 · Diagnose and start raised HbA1c → right diagnosis → dual first-line

!Act todayMHRA DSU · NG12 · NG19

  • Vomiting, abdominal pain, deep sighing breathing, drowsiness → check blood ketones and glucose. On an SGLT2 inhibitor DKA can occur with near-normal glucose: stop the SGLT2i and arrange same-day hospital assessment. Profound dehydration, confusion, very high glucose (possible HHS) → 999.
  • Could this be type 1? Ketosis, rapid weight loss, low BMI, young, personal or family autoimmunity → same-day glucose, ketones and diabetes-team advice. Type 1 treated as type 2 can be fatal.
  • New diabetes with weight loss, aged 60+ → consider urgent direct-access CT abdomen for pancreatic cancer (NG12), not just metformin.
  • Active foot problem (ulcer, spreading infection, ischaemia, suspected Charcot) → MDT foot service within 1 working day; limb- or life-threatening → acute admission. Confused hypo on a sulfonylurea or insulin → 999.

1Confirm the diagnosisNG28 · WHO criteria

ResultMeaning · action
HbA1c ≥48 mmol/molDiabetes if two results ≥48, or one with osmotic symptoms. Asymptomatic → repeat before labelling
HbA1c 42–47Non-diabetic hyperglycaemia → annual HbA1c + NHS Diabetes Prevention Programme
Fasting glucose ≥7.0Diagnostic (venous plasma); confirm if asymptomatic
Random glucose ≥11.1Diagnostic only with symptoms; otherwise repeat fasting glucose or HbA1c

Do not rely on HbA1c with haemoglobinopathy, haemolysis, recent transfusion, pregnancy or suspected type 1 → use plasma glucose.

3Work-up at diagnosisbaseline = first annual review

  • HbA1c, U&E/eGFR
  • Urine ACR (early-morning)
  • Lipids, LFTs, FBC
  • QRISK3, BP, BMI, waist
  • Feet: monofilament, pulses, risk
  • Refer to NHS eye screening
  • Smoking, mood, erectile function
  • ECG, TSH only if indicated

eGFR before metformin is non-negotiable; it also sets the SGLT2 inhibitor choice (box 5).

5Dual first-line, safelyNG28 · MHRA DSU · SPC

Metformin MR500 mg OD with food, up weekly to 1–2 g/day
eGFR gates<45 → review dose; <30 → stop
Then SGLT2idapagliflozin or empagliflozin 10 mg OD once metformin tolerated
Before SGLT2iprevious DKA? volume depletion? keto diet? not while unwell

Give written sick-day rules with every SGLT2i (box 11). Explain genital hygiene.

Treat the risk too: ACEi/ARB for hypertension or ACR >3 mg/mmol; atorvastatin 20 mg if QRISK3 ≥10% (80 mg with established CVD, NG238). No aspirin for primary prevention.

Already stable on metformin alone? NG28 changes the default, not a mass switch — review whether an SGLT2i should now be added (CVD, CKD, rising HbA1c, obesity, early onset).

"Diabetes is mostly about protecting your heart, kidneys, eyes and feet. These two tablets do that as well as lowering your sugar — and losing some weight could even put it into remission."

Self-monitoring of glucose is not routine on diet, metformin or an SGLT2i — offer it on insulin, with hypos, on a sulfonylurea if driving, or when planning pregnancy.

8Above target? Before stepping upNG28

  • Adherence — tablets actually taken? GI effects (MR metformin helps)
  • Lifestyle drift — weight, diet, alcohol: agree one goal
  • Intercurrent — steroids, infection, antipsychotic, low mood
  • Right target? — frail or hypo-prone → relax it
  • Right diagnosis? — weight loss or rapid rise → think LADA, pancreas
  • Too low? — HbA1c well under target on a sulfonylurea or insulin, or frail → de-intensify, hypo-causing drugs first

9The glucose ladderNG28 2026 · add by phenotype, not HbA1c alone · never DPP-4i with a GLP-1 RA

Step 1 · dualMR metformin + SGLT2i (dapagliflozin or empagliflozin). ASCVD → add s/c semaglutide as initial triple therapy.
Step 2 · by phenotypeNo comorbidity → DPP-4i (SU or pioglitazone if unsuitable). Obesity, after ≥3 months of dual → consider GLP-1 RA or tirzepatide. ASCVD → SU, pioglitazone or insulin.
Step 3 · third agentPick another mechanism: DPP-4i, sulfonylurea, pioglitazone, GLP-1 RA. Run box 8 first every time.
Step 4 · insulinBasal insulin once or twice daily; consider basal + short-acting if HbA1c ≥75. Keep metformin and SGLT2i; stop or cut the sulfonylurea.

11Sick days, hypos, drivingMHRA DSU 2016 · 2020 · DVLA

  • Sick days (vomiting, diarrhoea, not eating or drinking): pause metformin, the SGLT2i, ACEi/ARB and diuretics; never stop insulin; test glucose often. Restart when eating and drinking normally. On an SGLT2i and unwell → blood ketones even if glucose is normal.
  • Hypo (<4 mmol/L): 15–20 g fast-acting carbohydrate, recheck after 10–15 min, then a longer-acting snack.
  • DVLA: insulin → tell the DVLA (Group 1, unless temporary under 3 months); Group 2 → also for a sulfonylurea. Test before driving and every 2 hours on long journeys; don't drive if <4 ("5 to drive"). More than one severe hypo while awake in 12 months → must notify.
  • Never: start an SGLT2i in someone unwell · leave a frail person on a hypo-causing drug to hit 48.

12Annual review checklistevery patient, every year

  • HbA1c (3–6-monthly until stable, then 6-monthly)
  • BP; lipids and QRISK3 / statin review
  • Urine ACR + eGFR — at least yearly; more often in CKD
  • BMI and smoking status
  • Foot check: monofilament, pulses, risk (low · moderate · high)
  • Eye screening attended (NHS DESP sets the interval)
  • Hypos and awareness; driving group and DVLA duties
  • Sick-day rules; mood; erectile function; flu and pneumococcal vaccines
  • Pregnancy plans and contraception; B12 if on long-term metformin with neuropathy or anaemia
