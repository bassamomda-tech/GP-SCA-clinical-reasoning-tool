1Confirm and classifyuse local laboratory ranges

Anaemia: Hb <130 g/L in men, <120 g/L in non-pregnant women (WHO). Read the MCV with the ferritin, reticulocytes and the rest of the count: mixed deficiency can give a normal MCV.

2Focused history — what discriminatesBSG 2021 · NG239

Where is the blood going?Heavy periods, melaena, rectal bleeding, haematuria, blood donation
Bowel and upper GI symptomsChange in habit, dysphagia, dyspepsia, weight loss
DrugsNSAIDs, aspirin, anticoagulants; PPIs and metformin (B12); methotrexate (folate)
Diet and absorptionVegan diet, alcohol, coeliac or IBD, gastric or bariatric surgery
Nerves and mindNumbness, unsteadiness, memory: B12; ask about nitrous oxide
Periods, pregnanciesHeavy menstrual bleeding is the commonest cause before the menopause
Family and heritageGI cancer, coeliac disease, thalassaemia

A normal MCV does not exclude iron deficiency: mixed iron and B12 or folate deficiency can cancel out. Check ferritin, B12 and folate when the picture does not fit.

6Management of the commonest causesBSG 2021 · NICE CKS · NG239 · SPC

CauseFirst lineCheck and continue
Iron deficiencyOne standard oral iron tablet once daily, e.g. ferrous sulfate 200 mg (65 mg elemental iron); alternate days if not tolerated (BSG 2021). IV iron if oral iron fails, is not tolerated or will not be absorbedRecheck Hb at 2–4 weeks; continue for 3 months after Hb normalises. Treat the cause (heavy periods, coeliac) and investigate it
B12 deficiencyHydroxocobalamin 1 mg IM three times a week for 2 weeks, then every 2–3 months. Neurological features: 1 mg on alternate days until no further improvement, then every 2 months (seek specialist advice)Review symptoms at about 3 months (1 month in pregnancy). Autoimmune gastritis or ileal resection → lifelong IM. Diet only → oral B12
Folate deficiencyFolic acid 5 mg daily, usually for 4 months — only once B12 is known to be normal or is being replacedFind the cause: diet, alcohol, malabsorption, drugs
Chronic disease, CKDTreat the underlying condition; iron only if deficiency is shownCKD anaemia → renal team (NG203)
Thalassaemia traitNo iron unless also iron deficientExplain; partner testing before pregnancy

7Iron deficiency — who needs GI investigationBSG 2021 · NG12 · HTG690 · NG20

Every adult with IDAFIT; ≥10 µg Hb/g → colorectal suspected cancer pathway. Coeliac serology (NG20).
Men, post-menopausal womenBidirectional endoscopy (OGD + colonoscopy) unless there is a clear cause — even if FIT is negative.
Pre-menopausal womenUsually menstrual: treat and recheck. Endoscopy if aged 50+, GI symptoms or family history of colorectal cancer.
Still unexplainedPositive coeliac serology → gastroenterology. After negative endoscopy → H. pylori test; specialist advice.

8Self-care and the conversationBSG 2021 · NG239

Take iron away from tea, coffee, calcium and antacids · black stools on iron are expected, but tarry, sticky stools with dizziness are not · vegans need a B12 supplement or fortified foods.

"The iron tablets will build you back up, but they don't tell us why you were low. Finding where the iron went is the important part, so the tests I'm arranging matter even when you feel better."

9Iron treatment problems — and the fixBSG 2021 · SPC · MHRA

Nausea, constipationAlternate days, a different salt, or take with food (absorbs less)
No Hb riseAdherence, tea with meals, ongoing loss, coeliac, wrong diagnosis
IV ironOnly where anaphylaxis can be managed; observe afterwards
Ferric carboxymaltoseCheck phosphate if repeated or high doses or risk factors (MHRA)

10Safety-net and follow-up checklistBSG 2021 · NICE CKS · NG239

  • Hb at 2–4 weeks on iron: no rise → adherence, ongoing loss, malabsorption, wrong diagnosis
  • FIT returned and actioned; unreturned kit chased
  • GI investigation and coeliac result reviewed
  • Iron for 3 months after Hb normalises; ferritin at the end
  • B12: symptom review at about 3 months; injections booked
  • Recurrent IDA after treatment → investigate again
  • Return the same day: breathless, chest pain, faint, black tarry stools, new numbness or weakness
  • Cause recorded in the problem list

12Special groups and never-eventsBSG 2021 · NG12 · NG239

  • Pregnancy: different Hb thresholds — use the maternity pathway. IBD or bariatric surgery: IV iron is often preferred (specialist pathway). On anticoagulants: IDA is still investigated, not attributed to the drug.
  • Never give folic acid alone before B12 deficiency is excluded or treated · never treat IDA in a man or post-menopausal woman without investigating the cause · never treat a negative FIT as an all-clear when concern persists.
  • Never give iron for thalassaemia trait without proven iron deficiency · never delay B12 treatment when there are neurological features · never transfuse, or withhold transfusion, on the Hb number alone.
