Atrial fibrillation CLINIC GUIDE

For the new diagnosis and the annual review. Adults 18+ with AF or atrial flutter. Not for haemodynamically unstable AF, mechanical valves or moderate–severe mitral stenosis (warfarin), pregnancy or children.

Page 1 · Diagnose and start irregular pulse → ECG → stroke risk → DOAC → rate control

!Act todayNICE NG196 · protocol

  • Fast AF with chest pain, syncope, hypotension or severe breathlessness → 999. Any new focal neurology → stroke pathway, 999, even if it has resolved.
  • Suspected pre-excitation (young, very fast, delta wave) → no beta-blocker, verapamil, diltiazem or digoxin; urgent cardiology.
  • Reliably <48 h onset in a well patient → same-day assessment for rate or rhythm control. Onset uncertain → treat as longer-standing AF.
  • Thyrotoxicosis, sepsis, anaemia or AF with decompensated heart failure → treat the driver urgently as well as the rhythm.

1Confirm and classifyNICE NG196

  • Irregular pulse (with or without symptoms) → 12-lead ECG: irregularly irregular, no P waves. A pulse or smartwatch alone never makes the diagnosis.
  • Suspected paroxysmal AF not seen on ECG → ambulatory ECG (24-h tape or event recorder, by episode frequency).
TypePattern
ParoxysmalSelf-terminating, episodes <7 days
Persistent>7 days, or needs cardioversion to stop
PermanentAccepted; no further rhythm attempts

The type guides rhythm decisions only. Paroxysmal AF is anticoagulated exactly like permanent AF.

2Work-up — every new diagnosishunt the driver

  • U&E + CrCl (Cockcroft-Gault)
  • Weight (for CrCl and dosing)
  • FBC, LFTs
  • TFTs, HbA1c
  • BP; alcohol history
  • OSA screen (STOP-BANG)

Echo if it would change management: murmur, suspected structural or LV disease, or rhythm control planned (mitral stenosis or a mechanical valve means warfarin, not a DOAC).

4Bleeding risk — ORBITNICE NG196

NG196: consider ORBIT (more accurate than HAS-BLED): low haemoglobin or anaemia history, age >74, bleeding history, renal impairment, antiplatelet use.

A high score means fix the modifiables and review more often — it is not a reason to withhold. BP to target · stop NSAIDs and unneeded antiplatelets · cut alcohol · treat anaemia and find its cause.

Do not withhold anticoagulation solely because of age or risk of falls. Contraindicated or not tolerated → specialist (left atrial appendage occlusion).

5Anticoagulate — a DOAC firstNG196 · dose per SPC

Offer a DOAC; warfarin if DOACs are contraindicated or not tolerated. Never aspirin alone for stroke prevention. Dose by CrCl (Cockcroft-Gault), not eGFR.

9Before stepping upcheck these first

  • Adherence — DOAC taken every day? Missed doses are unprotected days
  • Dose — rate drug at maximum tolerated dose before adding another
  • Driver — thyroid, alcohol, OSA, anaemia, infection back?
  • Right DOAC dose — CrCl recalculated; criteria met in both directions
  • Target realistic? — a modestly raised rate in a symptom-free patient is acceptable
  • Right strategy — symptomatic despite good rate control → rhythm referral, not a third rate drug

12Annual review checklistevery patient, every year

  • Re-score CHA₂DS₂-VASc (birthdays, new diagnoses) — esp. if not anticoagulated
  • Weight, U&E, CrCl → is the DOAC dose still right?
  • FBC, LFTs; falling Hb = find the bleed
  • Bleeding since last review; modifiable bleeding risks
  • Adherence, missed-dose pattern, side effects
  • Interactions: NSAIDs, OTC and herbal, new prescriptions
  • Antiplatelet still indicated? Stent stop dates passed?
  • Pulse, BP, symptoms; alcohol, weight, OSA
  • Alert card carried; record score, dose, CrCl, next action

More often if CrCl is falling, the patient is frail, or after acute illness or AKI (SPC, local DOAC protocol).

13When to referNG196 · NG12

  • Same day: reliably new onset (<48 h), poorly tolerated fast AF, AF with decompensated heart failure.
  • Rhythm control: still symptomatic on rate control, AF-driven heart failure, reversible cause, new onset, flutter suitable for ablation.
  • Anticoagulation impossible → specialist; left atrial appendage occlusion.
  • NG12: unexplained visible haematuria aged 45+, or iron-deficiency anaemia on a DOAC → investigate; never "just the DOAC".
  • Specialist: antiphospholipid syndrome (DOAC not recommended, MHRA), CrCl <15. Unexplained murmur → echo.

14Special groups and never-eventsNG196 · SPC

  • Mechanical valve or moderate–severe mitral stenosis → warfarin, not a DOAC. Pregnancy → no DOAC; specialist.
  • After ACS or a stent: combined antithrombotics are cardiology-directed and time-limited — know the stop dates.
  • HFrEF: beta-blocker, not verapamil or diltiazem. Pre-excitation: no AV-nodal blocker.
  • Never: stop anticoagulation because the rate is controlled or after "successful" ablation or cardioversion · dose on eGFR · give aspirin "for the AF" · withhold for age or falls alone · under-dose "to be kind".

15Safety-net — what they must knowevery review

  • Stroke (FAST): face, arm, speech → 999, even if it passes off in minutes.
  • Bleeding: black stools, vomiting blood, blood in urine, a bleed that won't stop, or any head injury → A&E; say "I'm on a blood thinner".
  • Racing heart with chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness → 999.
  • Never stop the blood thinner because you feel well; check before buying ibuprofen or herbal remedies.
