1Is it real? Confirm before you label itNG145 · MHRA 2023 · SPS

BiotinHair, skin and nail supplements can make TSH and FT4 falsely high or low, depending on the assay. Ask every time; stop, tell the lab, repeat.
Acute illness, drugsNon-thyroidal illness, steroids and dopamine lower TSH; recovery can raise it. Avoid testing when acutely ill; repeat once well.
Timing, adherenceLevothyroxine just before the test raises FT4; tablets taken only before blood tests give high TSH with normal FT4. TSH lags a dose change by 6–8 weeks.
Discordant patternResults that do not fit the patient (heterophile antibodies, assay error) → ask the lab to recheck on another platform before acting.

Subclinical results always need a repeat: TSH with FT4 about 3 months later (many mild rises settle). Pregnancy: only the trimester-specific range applies.

4Work-up checklistNG145 · SPS

  • Repeat TFTs if subclinical
  • FT3 whenever TSH is low
  • TRAb in hyperthyroidism
  • TPO antibodies once, raised TSH
  • Biotin and drug history
  • Pregnancy status and plans
  • ECG if palpitations or AF
  • FBC, LFTs before any antithyroid drug
  • 9 am cortisol if central or Addison's
  • Ultrasound only for a lump

TRAb negative → think thyroiditis or toxic nodular disease. Do not repeat TPO antibodies or scan a normal neck. On amiodarone, read with previous TFTs: a raised FT4 alone can be the drug effect.

6Follow-up and monitoring checklistNG145 · SPS

  • Subclinical result: repeat TSH + FT4 at about 3 months
  • Levothyroxine: TSH every 3 months until 2 similar results in range, then yearly
  • Untreated subclinical hypo: TSH + FT4 yearly if TPO positive or previous surgery or radioiodine; otherwise every 2–3 years
  • Untreated subclinical hyper: TSH every 6 months
  • Antithyroid drug: TSH, FT4, FT3 every 6 weeks until TSH in range, then TSH every 3 months
  • After stopping (Graves'): TSH within 8 weeks, then every 3 months for a year, then yearly
  • After radioiodine: TSH, FT4, FT3 every 6 weeks for 6 months; hypothyroidism is expected
  • Amiodarone, lithium: TFTs 6-monthly (SPS)
  • Thyroiditis: recheck TFTs to catch the hypothyroid phase

9Pitfalls and never-eventsNG145 · MHRA · SPC

  • Never diagnose subclinical disease on one result, or test during acute illness unless thyroid disease is suspected.
  • Never titrate central hypothyroidism on TSH, or give levothyroxine before cortisol when Addison's is possible.
  • Never give carbimazole for thyroiditis, or skip the written sore-throat warning.
  • Never act on a discordant result before asking about biotin and timing.
  • Never raise levothyroxine to suppress TSH for persistent tiredness: check anaemia, B12, coeliac disease, sleep and mood.
  • Do not stop amiodarone without cardiology; a suppressed TSH after thyroid cancer may be the oncology target.

10Reassure and say it like thisNG145 · plain English

  • A mildly abnormal TSH in acute illness that settles on recovery needs no treatment.
  • Positive TPO antibodies with a normal TSH are not a reason to treat.
  • Untreated mild subclinical disease: most people stay well; the plan is monitoring, not tablets.

"Your thyroid test is a little outside the normal range, but one result can be thrown off by a recent illness, some supplements or timing. Before we label anything, we'll repeat it in about three months. If it's still off, we'll decide together whether treatment would actually help you."
