A normal ECG does not exclude severe hyperkalaemia. A critical result must never sit in an inbox or a routine repeat queue overnight: name who acts out of hours.

1Confirm it is real — only if no red flagUKKA 2023

  • Haemolysis: check the lab comment; difficult draw, small needle.
  • Delayed processing or a refrigerated sample: K⁺ leaks from red cells. Common with late collections and rural transport.
  • Fist clenching, prolonged tourniquet: falsely high.
  • EDTA contamination (wrong tube order): very high K⁺ with a very low calcium.
  • High platelets or white cells: ask for a plasma (lithium heparin) K⁺.
  • Repeat with free-flow venepuncture, no fist clenching, prompt processing, marked "urgent".

Never presume pseudohyperkalaemia at ≥6.5, or with symptoms, ECG changes, AKI or a rising trend. A normal haemolysed repeat after a genuine high result does not reassure.

4Work-up checklistconditional, not a panel

  • Repeat U&E, clean sample
  • eGFR trend: AKI?
  • 12-lead ECG at ≥6.0
  • Bicarbonate, glucose
  • FBC: platelets, white cells
  • Full drug list, OTC and salt
  • Postural BP, volume status
  • Bladder: retention?
  • Sodium: low → adrenal?
  • Digoxin level if toxicity possible

Only with a trigger: CK, urate and phosphate (muscle injury, tumour lysis) via acute services; aldosterone and renin are specialist tests. ECG: compare with old tracings; a broad QRS may be bundle branch block or pacing, but do not let that reassure.

5Reading the ECGUKKA 2023

EarlyTall, narrow, peaked T waves (V2–V4)
LaterLong PR, flat or absent P, broadening QRS
Pre-arrestSine wave, bradycardia, junctional rhythm
Any of theseEmergency transfer, whatever the number

6If you transfer — hand overUKKA 2023

  • The K⁺, its trend and timing, and whether the sample was haemolysed.
  • eGFR and creatinine against baseline; urine output; dialysis status.
  • The ECG if done; every drug and which ones you withheld.
  • Suspected cause: AKI, muscle breakdown, tumour lysis, adrenal, digoxin.

7Safe to manage in primary care only if — every box ticked and documentedUKKA 2023

  • K⁺ 5.5–5.9, well, no AKI, no Act-today feature
  • ECG normal where indicated; sample quality checked
  • Sodium, bicarbonate and glucose reviewed
  • Contributors stopped; cause acted on
  • Repeat within 3 days booked; named reviewer
  • Written escalation threshold, safety-net, sick-day plan

9Sick-day plan — personalised, in writingUKKA · NG203

Name which medicines to pause (ACE inhibitor, ARB, MRA, diuretics, metformin, SGLT2 inhibitor) during significant vomiting, diarrhoea or not drinking, and when to restart. Check U&E after the illness before restarting after AKI or high K⁺. Avoid NSAIDs altogether rather than pausing them.

10Follow-up and monitoring checklistUKKA 2023 · NG203 · MHRA

  • 5.5–5.9: repeat within 3 days, named reviewer, written escalation threshold
  • 6.0–6.4, well: renal/medical team discussion, repeat within 1 day; still ≥6.0 → admit
  • 5.1–5.4 on RAAS: recheck in 1–4 weeks
  • Any RAAS dose change: K⁺ and eGFR at 1–2 weeks (NG203)
  • New trimethoprim, NSAID or second RAAS drug: K⁺ at 1 week
  • After discharge: K⁺ and eGFR within days, at the interval in the plan; check what was stopped and plan reintroduction
  • Target: K⁺ back in the reference range while keeping the prognostic drugs where possible
  • Binder: monitoring as the initiating service sets; magnesium on patiromer
  • Monitoring register and recalls for overdue bloods

12What the hospital will do — hospital only, so you can explain it and check the discharge planUKKA 2023

IV calciumProtects the heart for a while; does not lower potassium. Not for routine primary care.
Insulin–glucose, salbutamolMove potassium into cells. Glucose is monitored for hours afterwards because of late hypoglycaemia.
ReboundThese shift potassium rather than remove it: it rises again unless the cause is fixed or the potassium is removed.
RemovalDialysis via the renal team for refractory cases. Calcium resonium is no longer part of acute treatment.

13Pitfalls and never-eventsUKKA 2023

  • Never repeat a K⁺ ≥6.5 instead of transferring, or call it "probably haemolysis" without asking the lab.
  • Never reassure because the patient feels well or the ECG is normal.
  • "Chronic and stable" without comparing the trend.
  • Blaming diet with a normal eGFR and no drugs, or with a low sodium or bicarbonate beside it.
  • Never stop a heart failure drug for good without a reintroduction plan.
  • Never start a binder instead of emergency transfer.
  • Never leave a critical result in an inbox or a routine repeat queue overnight.

14Safety-net — say it, then document itUKKA 2023

Call 999Collapse or blackout; chest pain; a racing or very slow heartbeat with feeling faint; legs or arms going weak.
Practice or 111 todayPalpitations, feeling faint, new numbness or weakness, cannot keep fluids down, passing much less urine.
AvoidSalt substitutes (LoSalt and similar); ibuprofen and other anti-inflammatories without checking first.
Keep the appointmentCome for the repeat blood test even if you feel well; bring a list of every medicine.

15Say it like thisplain English

"Your potassium is higher than it should be. Potassium helps your heart beat in rhythm, so we take a high level seriously even when you feel well. One of your medicines and your kidneys are the likely reasons. We'll adjust things and recheck within a few days. If you feel faint, your heart races or your legs go weak, call 999."
