Allergic rhinitis CLINIC GUIDE

For the first consultation and the seasonal review. Adults and young people 12+. Not for chronic rhinosinusitis with polyps, non-allergic rhinitis or children under 12 (doses differ).

Page 1 · Assess and start is it allergic rhinitis → how bad → the right first treatment, taught properly

!Not simple hay fever — act todayBSACI 2017 · NICE CKS

  • One-sided blockage or discharge, blood-stained discharge or epistaxis, facial pain or swelling, a visible mass, proptosis, cranial nerve signs or a neck lump → urgent ENT via the local head-and-neck suspected-cancer pathway. A normal anterior examination does not exclude sinonasal disease.
  • Severe crusting or septal perforation → think granulomatosis with polyangiitis, sarcoidosis, cocaine use; investigate, do not escalate antihistamines.
  • Oral itch with raw fruit that spreads to throat tightness, breathing difficulty, vomiting or collapse → treat as anaphylaxis (999, IM adrenaline), then refer to allergy.
  • Wheeze, night cough or a severe asthma attack → treat the asthma first (BTS/SIGN); 999 if life-threatening.

1Confirm it — the history carries itBSACI 2017

Two or more of nasal blockage, running (front or back), sneezing or itch, for an hour or more on most days, on both sides, linked to a trigger.

3Work-up — every new patientBSACI 2017

  • Both nostrils: anterior rhinoscopy (polyps, septum, crusting)
  • Chest: wheeze, cough, night waking, reliever use
  • Eyes: itchy, watery (add drops)
  • Oral itch with raw fruit or nuts
  • OTC decongestant use and duration
  • Drugs: beta-blockers, ACEi, NSAIDs, oral contraceptives
  • Occupation and pets in the home
  • Pregnancy, driving, exams

Skin prick or specific IgE only when the trigger is unclear, avoidance depends on it, or immunotherapy is being considered. A positive test without matching symptoms is sensitisation, not allergy.

4First treatment — match it to the gradeBSACI 2017 · NICE CKS · SPC

Mild or intermittentNon-sedating oral antihistamine: cetirizine or loratadine 10 mg OD; fexofenadine 120 mg OD (seasonal). Or an intranasal antihistamine.
Moderate–severe or persistentRegular intranasal steroid — first choice, best for blockage. Every day, not as needed.
Mometasone 50 micrograms/spray2 sprays each nostril OD; 1 each side once controlled
Fluticasone propionate 50 micrograms/spray2 sprays each nostril OD
Pollen seasonStart about 2 weeks before the expected season; effect builds over days

Avoid sedating antihistamines (chlorphenamine, promethazine): they impair alertness, memory and driving.

5Teach the spray — watch them do itBSACI SOP 2023

1 · ClearBlow gently or use saline first; shake the bottle.
2 · HeadSlightly forward, not tilted back.
3 · AimOpposite hand; nozzle just inside, aimed outwards, away from the septum.
4 · SniffGently — not hard into the throat.

Aiming at the septum causes crusting and nosebleeds and wastes the dose. Most "failed" sprays are technique or intermittent use.

6Self-management — for every patientBSACI 2017

PollenWindows shut on high-count days; wraparound glasses; shower and change after being out
House dust miteCombined bedroom measures: barrier covers, hot-wash bedding, less clutter
PetsOut of the bedroom; removal is the only reliable step, and takes months
Saline douchingUseful add-on, including in pregnancy; douche before the steroid; sterile or boiled-and-cooled water
SmokeTobacco smoke and fumes worsen nasal reactivity

"The steroid spray is the most effective treatment, but only if it goes in at the right angle every day — let me show you, then you show me."

7One airway — look for the asthmaBSACI 2017 · NICE NG245 · ARIA (international)

Ask everyoneWheeze, cough, night waking, exercise limitation, reliever use.
Why it mattersUncontrolled rhinitis can worsen asthma control; treating the nose does not treat the chest.
Suspected asthmaObjective tests and treatment per NG245 — not a rhinitis add-on.
MontelukastNot a rhinitis drug on its own: UK licence covers seasonal AR only in people who need it for asthma.

Page 2 · Review and step up review at 2–4 weeks: technique → adherence → exposure → step up → refer

8What "controlled" looks likeBSACI 2017

  • Sleep, work or school, and leisure no longer affected; symptoms not troublesome.
  • No rescue oral steroid courses; no OTC decongestant use.
  • Any coexisting asthma controlled — uncontrolled rhinitis can worsen it.
  • Controlled → continue through the season, then stop; perennial → step down to the lowest effective dose.

9Before every step-upBSACI 2017

  • Technique — watch it: septum, snorting into the throat, nosebleeds
  • Adherence — used only on bad days, or stopped after a week?
  • Exposure — pet in the bedroom, damp home, new job, pollen on clothes
  • Other drugs — regular decongestant spray, beta-blocker, ACEi, NSAID
  • Diagnosis — unilateral, polyps, loss of smell, crusting → ENT

10Review checklist2–4 weeks, then each season

  • Symptom control and impact on sleep, work, school
  • Spray technique demonstrated by the patient
  • Daily use; started pre-season for pollen allergy
  • Chest: wheeze, cough, night symptoms → objective asthma tests (NG245)
  • Eyes: persistent symptoms → add drops
  • OTC decongestant: stop if used beyond 5 days
  • Montelukast: mood and sleep asked, counselling recorded
  • Plan next season: start date about 2 weeks before

11When to referBSACI 2017 · BSACI 2011

Allergy clinic: immunotherapyClear IgE-mediated disease, matching sensitisation to a dominant allergen, moderate–severe symptoms despite correct, adherent optimal treatment
Allergy clinic: otherDiagnostic doubt; occupational rhinitis; pollen food syndrome with systemic reactions; difficult asthma
ENTRed flags (urgent); polyps or structural obstruction not responding

Immunotherapy is specialist-initiated and lasts years; NICE TA1045 (Mar 2025) covers the house dust mite tablet, ages 12–65.

13Side effects — fix, don't abandonSPC · MHRA

Nosebleeds, crustingRe-check the angle; alternate nostrils; persisting → look for septal perforation
DrowsinessSwitch to a non-sedating agent; warn drivers
Rebound blockageDecongestant overuse: stop it, steroid spray, taper
MontelukastSleep disturbance, nightmares, anxiety, depression, suicidal thoughts → stop and seek help (MHRA, Apr 2024)
Long, high-dose steroidCare in glaucoma, cataract and with inhaled or skin steroids

14Special groups and never-eventsBSACI 2017 · MHRA · UKTIS

  • Never a depot steroid injection (for example triamcinolone): adverse risk–benefit, cannot be withdrawn, fat atrophy; not even "before the season".
  • Never start montelukast for rhinitis alone (not licensed without asthma). If used for asthma: ask about mood and sleep first, counsel and document (MHRA 2019, 2024).
  • Pregnancy: saline and avoidance first; loratadine or cetirizine preferred; intranasal steroid at licensed dose if needed; no decongestants.
  • Students and drivers: non-sedating only; start the spray pre-season before exams.
  • Older adults: avoid sedating and anticholinergic antihistamines (falls, confusion).
