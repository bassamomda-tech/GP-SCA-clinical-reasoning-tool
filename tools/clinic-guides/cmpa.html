Cow's milk protein allergy in infants CLINIC GUIDE

For suspected CMPA in infants and children under 2, breastfed or formula-fed. Not for food allergy in older children, lactose intolerance or the treatment of anaphylaxis itself (Resuscitation Council UK).

!Act todayResuscitation Council UK · NICE CG116 · iMAP/UK

  • Anaphylaxis after milk (airway swelling, wheeze, stridor, collapse) → IM adrenaline now: under 6 months 100–150 micrograms, 6 months–6 years 150 micrograms; 999; repeat after 5 min if no better. Don't wait for antihistamine or steroid.
  • Acute FPIES: repetitive, profuse vomiting typically 1–4 hours after milk, with pallor, lethargy or floppiness, no rash or wheeze → emergency assessment (often mistaken for sepsis or gastroenteritis).
  • Same day: dehydration, can't feed or persistent vomiting, haematemesis, melaena or heavy rectal bleeding, faltering growth in an unwell child. Paediatric referral: blood in stools persisting despite a complete milk-free diet (look for another cause).

1Allergy-focused history and examCG116 §1.1

  • Symptoms by system, timing, reproducibility
  • Feeding: breast, formula type, volume
  • Maternal diet if breastfed
  • Foods already avoided and why
  • Eczema, asthma, rhinitis; family atopy
  • Previous reactions; response to past exclusion
  • Weight, length, head plotted on UK-WHO chart
  • Eczema extent and infection; hydration

Look in the nappy (or a photo): "blood" may be food dye, urates or a fissure.

Timing is a guide, not a cut-off: read it with the whole history.

3Tests — order and refuseCG116 §1.1 · BSACI

IgE suspected → skin prick and/or specific IgE to milk only, by a competent clinician with anaphylaxis cover. Sensitisation ≠ allergy.

Non-IgE → no test diagnoses it: eliminate cow's milk protein, normally 2–6 weeks, then reintroduce (CG116 1.1.11). Symptoms back = confirmed.

Never: vega test, applied kinesiology, hair analysis (CG116); IgG "food intolerance" tests (BSACI). No patch tests or routine bloods.

4Avoid over-diagnosisiMAP/UK 2019 · CG116

  • Colic, possetting, eczema and odd stools are common in well babies: one symptom in a thriving infant is not CMPA.
  • Improvement off milk alone proves nothing: symptoms fluctuate. Reintroduction is the diagnosis.
  • No better after a complete 2–6-week trial → stop the diet and rethink (reflux, constipation, infection, fissure).
  • Eczema: treat the skin properly first; milk exclusion is not an eczema treatment.
  • Every exclusion has a stop or reintroduction date written down.
  • Isolated proctocolitis in a thriving baby: reassure, maternal exclusion trial; no colonoscopy.

5Who goes straight to a specialistCG116 §1.1

  • Allergy: suspected IgE or mixed CMPA, FPIES, one or more systemic reactions, multiple food allergy.
  • Paediatrics: faltering growth with GI symptoms; severe delayed reactions; significant eczema with suspected multiple food allergy.
  • Start the safe feed while waiting; don't leave the baby on standard formula.

6First-line feedCKS · iMAP/UK · CG116

BreastfedKeep breastfeeding. Maternal exclusion of all cow's milk protein for the trial, with dietetic support and a calcium and vitamin D supplement for the mother.
Formula · non-IgEMild to moderate → extensively hydrolysed formula (eHF) for the trial. Bitter taste: give transition advice before switching.
Amino-acid formulaSevere or IgE reactions with anaphylaxis risk, FPIES, significant faltering growth, multiple food allergy, or eHF failed — with paediatric or dietetic advice.
Not treatmentsSoya under 6 months · "lactose-free" formula · goat's or sheep's milk · plant drinks as the main milk under 1 year.

IgE allergy: if an adrenaline auto-injector is prescribed, give two and train parents (MHRA 2023), with a written plan.

Vitamin D for baby: breastfed → 8.5–10 micrograms a day; formula-fed on more than 500 mL a day → none extra (check the eHF/AAF content).

11Before escalating eHF → AAFCKS · iMAP/UK

  • Complete exclusion? Hidden milk in the mother's diet, weaning foods or a top-up formula
  • Long enough? 2–6 weeks, not 2–6 days
  • Taken properly? Volume, preparation, taste refusal
  • Right diagnosis? Reflux, constipation, infection, fissure, another food
  • Growth faltering → AAF with paediatric and dietetic advice

13Follow-up checklistevery review until back on milk

  • Weight, length, head plotted and compared with the last
  • Symptom response; date the trial ends and reintroduction starts
  • Formula: accepted, volume right for age and weight, prescription updated
  • Breastfeeding mother: diet sustainable, calcium + vitamin D taken
  • Baby's vitamin D as UK advice (not duplicating formula)
  • Weaning milk-free and varied; label reading; dietitian seen
  • IgE: two auto-injectors in date, plan with nursery
  • Next step agreed: ladder, supervised challenge or tolerance review

14Refer and never-eventsCG116 §1.1 · iMAP/UK · MHRA

  • Allergy service: any IgE-mediated or mixed CMPA, FPIES, previous systemic reaction, multiple food allergies, severe eczema with suspected food allergy, any supervised challenge.
  • Paediatrics / dietitian: faltering growth with GI symptoms, no response to a correct single-allergen elimination, persisting parental concern, complex exclusions. Dietitian for every family where possible.
  • Paediatric gastroenterology: visible blood in stools persisting despite a complete milk-free diet.
  • Never: diagnose on an IgG or high-street test · leave a child milk-free with no reintroduction plan · home ladder for IgE, mixed or FPIES · soya under 6 months · "lactose-free" for CMPA · AAF first without an indication.
