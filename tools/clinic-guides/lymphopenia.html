Lymphopenia: the low lymphocyte count CLINIC GUIDE

A low absolute lymphocyte count in adults 18+. Not for children or infants (age-specific ranges; suspected SCID → paediatric immunology), or people under active HIV, transplant or chemotherapy care (follow that team's plan).

In confirmed HIV, CD4 <200 cells/mm³ is severe immunosuppression; a low total lymphocyte count does not tell you the CD4 count, and CD4 <200 without HIV is not AIDS.

1Confirm it is realrepeat unless unwell

  • Absolute count, read with neutrophils, Hb, platelets, the film and previous FBCs: new, persistent or progressive?
  • Transient causes: acute viral illness (COVID-19, flu, EBV, CMV), sepsis, recent steroids, physiological stress. These usually recover; confirm with a repeat.
  • Repeat FBC + differential at about 4–8 weeks, or sooner by severity — unless the patient is unwell or there are features of immunodeficiency, malignancy or marrow disease.
  • Steroids lower the measured count by redistribution; that alone does not mean clinically important T-cell deficiency.

7Living with a weak immune systemproportionate advice · Green Book

Food and handsWash hands with soap before meals and after exposures. Avoid raw meat, raw eggs and unpasteurised dairy; avoid cat litter in severe T-cell deficiency.
TravelTravel health review before going abroad: live travel vaccines need specialist assessment; malaria prophylaxis and bite avoidance; food safety.
PregnancyHIV: continue ART throughout. CVID: replacement continues. DMARDs: check safety with the prescriber before conception.
Mind and supportNHS Talking Therapies; address HIV stigma openly. Support: Terrence Higgins Trust, aidsmap, UKPIN. CVID: carry a letter for emergencies.

8Follow-up and monitoringlocal pathways

  • Transient: FBC at 4–8 weeks after the illness or steroid course; target back within the laboratory range, then stop
  • Not normalised: film, HIV test, targeted tests by history
  • Persistent unexplained severe lymphopenia → haematology or immunology within a week
  • On a DMARD: falling trend → discuss with rheumatology
  • HIV with CD4 falling below 200 → HIV service for prophylaxis and ART review
  • Steroid card and sick-day rules given and recorded
  • Vaccines reviewed against Green Book chapter 6; annual inactivated flu, COVID-19 and pneumococcal (chapter 25)
  • Recurrent oral thrush → HIV test, diabetes check, inhaler technique; oral medicine if it keeps recurring

10Pitfalls and never-eventsGreen Book · BHIVA · UKHSA

  • Never start PCP, antifungal or herpes prophylaxis on the lymphocyte count alone.
  • Never decide on live vaccines from the count: check Green Book chapter 6 and ask the specialist. Inactivated vaccines (flu, COVID-19, pneumococcal) are safe; Shingrix (two doses) where indicated.
  • Never stop steroids abruptly or a DMARD just because the count is below range.
  • Never skip the HIV test because the patient "is not at risk".
  • Non-immune and immunosuppressed after a chickenpox or shingles exposure → same-day advice; post-exposure prophylaxis is oral aciclovir or valaciclovir (UKHSA).
  • Oral thrush: a single 150 mg fluconazole dose is the vaginal regimen, not the oral one.

11Safety-nettell the patient

Same day or 999: fever with shivers, breathlessness, a new cough, confusion, severe headache, or feeling suddenly very unwell. Soon: mouth or throat thrush, recurring chest or sinus infections, night sweats, weight loss, new lumps. On long-term steroids: follow your sick-day rules.

12Say it like thisplain English

"Your lymphocytes, one kind of white cell, are a bit low. That's very common after a virus or with steroid tablets, and it usually comes back up. I'll repeat the test, and I recommend an HIV test as a routine part of checking — we offer it to everyone with this result."
