Breathlessness in adults CLINIC GUIDE

For new or worsening breathlessness in adults in general practice. Not for children, pregnancy or post-partum (separate VTE pathway), major trauma or palliative breathlessness.

!Red flags — sort by urgencyBTS/SIGN 158 · NG158 · NG12 · BTS oxygen 2017

999 nowSpO₂ below the person's target or falling, cyanosis, exhaustion, can't finish sentences, drowsy or confused · shock · life-threatening asthma (PEF <33%, SpO₂ <92%, silent chest, poor effort) · suspected ACS or malignant arrhythmia · tension pneumothorax · anaphylaxis (IM adrenaline) · stridor · pulmonary oedema · PE with collapse or hypotension.
Same-day hospitalNew breathlessness at rest · stable suspected PE (Wells >4, or positive D-dimer) · acute severe asthma (PEF 33–50%, RR ≥25, pulse ≥110) — treat; admit if features persist · pneumonia with CRB65 ≥2 · pneumothorax · acute heart failure · SVC obstruction.
Suspected cancer (NG12)Haemoptysis aged 40+ → lung pathway. CXR within 2 weeks at 40+ with 2 or more unexplained of cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, weight loss, appetite loss (1 or more if ever smoked). CXR suggesting cancer → pathway.

Oxygen treats hypoxaemia, not breathlessness: target 94–98%, or 88–92% if at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure (BTS 2017). A normal SpO₂ does not exclude PE, and NEWS2 informs, but does not replace, judgement.

1Focused history — speed of onset firstNICE CKS

OnsetThink
MinutesPE, pneumothorax, anaphylaxis, pulmonary oedema, arrhythmia
Hours–daysPneumonia, asthma or COPD exacerbation, acute heart failure
Weeks–monthsCOPD, heart failure, anaemia, effusion, cancer, ILD, deconditioning

Then ask: wheeze and night symptoms · orthopnoea, PND, ankle swelling · pleuritic pain, haemoptysis, leg swelling, immobility or surgery · palpitations · fever, sputum · smoking pack-years · asbestos, birds, farming, work exposures · drugs (beta-blockers, NSAIDs, amiodarone, methotrexate).

Grade it with the MRC dyspnoea scale (1 = strenuous exercise only … 5 = too breathless to leave the house) and record the baseline.

4First tests — checklisteach test answers a question

  • SpO₂ and full observations
  • ECG — any unexplained new breathlessness
  • FBC (anaemia, polycythaemia)
  • NT-proBNP if HF suspected
  • Spirometry (post-bronchodilator)
  • CXR — targeted, not for everyone
  • U&E, eGFR, LFTs, TFTs if HF suspected
  • FeNO or eosinophils if asthma suspected

Not: spirometry during an exacerbation · D-dimer when Wells >4 · echo without an NT-proBNP or clinical indication · a CXR for an uncomplicated asthma attack.

6Management by the commonest causes — first-linediagnose first; full regimens live in each condition's protocol

CauseFirst-lineKey point
Asthma (NG245)Low-dose ICS/formoterol as needed (AIR) → low-dose MART → moderate-dose MARTNever SABA alone. Check technique and adherence before each step; written action plan for all
COPD (NG115)SABA or SAMA as needed → LABA + LAMA (no asthmatic features) or LABA + ICS (asthmatic features)Confirm with spirometry first; stop smoking; pulmonary rehab
Heart failure (NG106)Loop diuretic only for fluid overload; disease-modifying drugs after echo and phenotypeAcute pulmonary oedema is a 999, not a clinic
Pneumonia (NG250)Low severity: amoxicillin 500 mg TDS for 5 daysCRB65 and judgement decide place of care; review if not better at 48–72 h
PE (NG158)Apixaban or rivaroxabanAt least 3 months; baseline FBC, renal and liver function, clotting — do not delay for results
Iron deficiencyOral iron, one tablet daily or on alternate daysFind the cause: never assume it; continue until stores replenished
Asthma attack (BTS/SIGN)Salbutamol via spacer or nebulised · prednisolone 40–50 mg daily for at least 5 days · oxygen to 94–98% · no routine antibiotic · life-threatening, or severe features persisting after treatment → admit.
COPD exacerbation (NG115)More short-acting bronchodilator · prednisolone 30 mg daily for 5 days · antibiotic only if sputum purulence or other bacterial features (NG114) · oxygen to 88–92% if hypercapnia risk.

9Never-eventsNG245 · NG158 · BTS 2017

  • Never SABA-only treatment for asthma.
  • Never wait for PEF, a full response or a score before transferring a sick asthmatic.
  • Never D-dimer when PE is likely; never let a normal CXR or SpO₂ exclude PE.
  • Never withhold oxygen from a hypoxic COPD patient — titrate it to target.
  • Never a diuretic for breathlessness of uncertain cause.
  • Never label anxiety or deconditioning before organic causes are excluded.

8Safety-net and follow-up checklistrecord it in the notes

  • 999 if suddenly worse at rest, can't finish a sentence, blue lips, chest pain, collapse, stridor
  • Same day if not improving at 48 h, fever returns, new ankle swelling, haemoptysis
  • Acute infection or exacerbation: review at 48–72 h if not improving
  • Asthma attack treated in hospital or out of hours: review within 2 working days
  • New ACEi, ARB, ARNI or MRA in HF: U&E 1–2 weeks after starting and each dose rise
  • Suspected cancer referral acknowledged; echo result chased
  • Asthma: personalised action plan · COPD: self-management plan
  • Named clinician owns every result and referral

10Lifestyle — treatment, not an afterthoughtNG115 · Green Book

SmokingNHS Stop Smoking Service — the biggest single change for COPD, asthma and lung cancer
Pulmonary rehabCOPD with MRC grade 3 or above, and after admission for an exacerbation
VaccinesInfluenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, RSV per the current Green Book
Weight, activityWeight management; graded exercise for deconditioning
BreathingPhysiotherapy-led retraining for breathing pattern disorder

"We'll find out why you're breathless rather than just treating the symptom. If it suddenly gets worse at rest, you can't finish a sentence or your lips look blue, call 999."
