TierResult or featureAction

A normal ECG does not exclude important hypokalaemia. Severe alkalosis, rapid-onset hypertension, proximal weakness and hyperglycaemia in a smoker: think ectopic ACTH → same-day endocrine advice.

1Confirm it is realrepeat with magnesium

  • Very high white cell count (leukaemia): cells take up K⁺ in a delayed or warm sample → falsely low. Ask for prompt processing.
  • Drip arm: sample above dextrose or insulin infusion. Resample from the other arm.
  • Recent salbutamol nebs or insulin: a real but transient shift into cells.
  • Repeat U&E with magnesium, bicarbonate and glucose when the result is unexpected or does not fit; do not delay action at <2.5 or with ECG change.

3Classify the mechanismbicarbonate, BP and drugs first

Renal lossDiuretics (commonest). With hypertension and alkalosis: primary aldosteronism, Cushing's. Acidosis: renal tubular acidosis. Young, normotensive, low Mg: Bartter or Gitelman.
Gut lossVomiting (alkalosis, kidney loses K⁺), diarrhoea, laxatives, high-output stoma. Treat the cause.
Shift into cellsInsulin, salbutamol, alkalosis, refeeding, thyrotoxic periodic paralysis. Replace cautiously: rebound high K⁺.
Low intakeRarely alone: "tea and toast" with a diuretic, alcohol, eating disorders. Low magnesium keeps K⁺ low.

5Work-up checklistproportionate to the picture

  • Repeat U&E, eGFR
  • Magnesium: every time
  • Bicarbonate; gas if unwell
  • Glucose
  • ECG: symptoms, moderate, digoxin
  • BP, lying and standing
  • Power, reflexes, bowel sounds
  • Full drug list, laxatives, liquorice
  • TFTs if periodic weakness
  • CK if muscle pain
  • ARR if hypertensive
  • Urine K⁺ only if cause unclear

Well, stable 3.0–3.4 with an obvious diuretic cause: repeat U&E with bicarbonate and magnesium and review the diuretic before wider tests. Aldosterone:renin ratio: correct the potassium first and follow the laboratory's advice on interfering drugs. Never use a 9 am cortisol to screen for Cushing's.

7Safe in primary care only if — every boxSPS 2024

  • K⁺ 3.0–3.4, or 2.5–2.9 meeting the oral criteria; no Act-today feature
  • ECG done where indicated and normal
  • Magnesium, bicarbonate, BP checked; cause acted on
  • Recheck booked; safety-net given and recorded

8Management of the commonest causesSPS 2024 · Sando-K SPC · NG136 · NG69

CauseFirst lineCheck and continue
Replacement regimen
oral potassium chloride		Sando-K effervescent: 12 mmol K⁺ per tablet, dissolved in water, with or after food. Mild (3.0–3.4): 24 mmol (2 tablets) two or three times a day. Modified-release potassium chloride tablets are less suitable: more GI irritation (SPS). Hospital only: IV potassium, premixed and glucose-free, by infusion pump, never as a bolus. Potassium-containing salt substitutes are not a treatment for hypokalaemia.

9Safety-net and self-care — say it, then document itSPS 2024

Call 999Chest pain, fainting, palpitations with feeling faint or breathless, cannot walk, breathing difficulty, dark urine with severe muscle pain.
Practice todayCramps or weakness worse despite the tablets, palpitations, vomiting or diarrhoea stopping you taking them.
Taking Sando-KDissolve fully in water; take with or after food; do not chew. Tell us about side effects.
AvoidLiquorice in quantity; laxatives you do not need. Normal balanced diet with fruit and vegetables.

10Follow-up and monitoring checklistSPS 2024 · NG136

  • Recheck U&E and magnesium soon after starting replacement or changing the diuretic; earlier for moderate
  • Target: K⁺ back within the reference range
  • Stop potassium once in range and the cause is fixed; continue only for ongoing losses
  • Recheck after stopping or after any new ACE inhibitor, ARB or potassium-sparing drug
  • Over-replacement risk: CKD, ACE inhibitor, ARB, MRA, amiloride
  • Long-term diuretic: U&E after each dose change and in illness, then at individualised intervals
  • Recurrent without a cause, or with hypertension → investigate
  • Cause recorded in the problem list

12Special situationsSPS 2024 · NG69 · CG32

  • On digoxin or anti-arrhythmics: low K⁺ raises the arrhythmia risk even at a usual digoxin level; ECG, and a lower threshold for same-day assessment.
  • Heart failure on a loop diuretic: balance fluid against potassium; adding or raising an ACE inhibitor, ARB or MRA changes the potassium, so recheck after every change.
  • Refeeding (very low weight, eating disorder, alcohol, prolonged poor intake): potassium, phosphate and magnesium can fall fast once feeding restarts (CG32).
  • Thyrotoxic or familial periodic paralysis: the low K⁺ is a shift, so replace cautiously and watch for rebound hyperkalaemia; treat the thyroid.

13Pitfalls and never-eventsSPS 2024 · NG136

  • Never give IV potassium in primary care.
  • Never reassure because the patient feels well or the ECG is normal.
  • "Refractory" before the magnesium is checked and corrected.
  • "It's the diuretic" without the BP, bicarbonate and magnesium.
  • A diuretic explanation in a young normotensive patient: ask about purging.
  • Leaving potassium running indefinitely; adding an ACE inhibitor on top.

14Say it like thisplain English

"Your potassium is a little low. Potassium keeps your muscles and heart working properly. Your water tablet is the most likely reason. These dissolving tablets will top it up, and we'll recheck the blood soon. If you get palpitations with feeling faint, chest pain or your legs go weak, call 999."
