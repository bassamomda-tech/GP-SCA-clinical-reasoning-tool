Abnormal bone profile CLINIC GUIDE

Calcium, phosphate, ALP and PTH read together, in adults 18+. Not for children (ALP is physiologically high while growing), pregnancy (placental ALP) or dialysis (renal team).

1Confirm it is realNG132 · local laboratory

  • Use the laboratory's adjusted calcium; do not re-apply a formula to a value already adjusted. Typical range 2.20–2.60 mmol/L; phosphate 0.8–1.5 mmol/L.
  • EDTA contamination: very low Ca with very high K (and often low ALP) → repeat on a fresh sample.
  • Haemolysis or delayed separation can falsely raise phosphate and potassium.
  • Biotin supplements interfere with some PTH assays: ask, and tell the laboratory.
  • Raised ALP without disease: healing fracture, pregnancy, recent surgery. Repeat and trend; check GGT.
  • Repeat Ca at least once if ≥2.6 (or ≥2.5 with features of PHPT). PTH once confirmed on two occasions, with a same-sample calcium. Never delay transfer of an unwell patient to repeat.

GGT is the quickest bone-or-liver test: raised GGT → liver ALP; normal GGT and LFTs → bone ALP. Bone-specific ALP if the pattern is unclear.

5Work-up checklistNG132 · NG12

  • Adjusted Ca, albumin, phosphate
  • ALP + GGT, LFTs
  • PTH with same-sample Ca
  • 25-OH vitamin D
  • U&E, eGFR; magnesium
  • FBC, ESR or PV
  • SPE + free light chains if myeloma possible
  • X-ray of painful bones
  • CXR if lymphoma or sarcoid suspected
  • Urine Ca if FHH possible

Specialist-directed only: PTHrP, 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, parathyroid imaging. ACE is not a sarcoid screen; PSA only via the symptom-led prostate pathway.

7Isolated raised ALP — step by stepbone or liver?

1 · Repeat and trendAsk about recent fracture or surgery, pregnancy, bone pain, drugs, weight loss. Check GGT and LFTs.
2 · GGT raisedLiver source: follow the abnormal LFT pathway first.
3 · GGT normalBone source: Ca, phosphate, 25-OH D, PTH, eGFR; bone-specific ALP if unclear. X-ray painful bones.
4 · Still unexplainedPersistent or marked, or bone pain → metabolic bone services. Pain with weight loss → NG12.

8Self-care and the conversationNHS · ROS

Calcium: 700 mg a day from food for adults. Vitamin D: 10 micrograms (400 IU) daily in autumn and winter; all year if rarely outdoors. Weight-bearing exercise, no smoking, alcohol within 14 units a week.

"Your bone blood test is a bit out of balance. It is most often a simple vitamin D shortage or a small overactive gland, but I want to check a couple more things, including a hormone level, so we treat the right cause."

9Follow-up and monitoringNG132 · ROS · specialist plan

  • PHPT surveillance: adjusted Ca + eGFR at least annually; DXA every 2–3 years; renal ultrasound if a stone is suspected
  • Refer when Ca reaches ≥2.85, stones, osteoporosis, fracture or symptoms
  • After vitamin D loading: calcium; PTH still high once replete → consider coexisting PHPT
  • Paget's: ALP and symptom response as the specialist advises; hearing loss → audiology
  • Raised ALP: repeat and trend; still raised and non-hepatic → bone work-up
  • Denosumab: dose never overdue; Ca before each dose

11Pitfalls and never-eventsNG132 · SPC

  • Never diagnose Paget's on ALP alone, or treat it to normalise ALP · never diagnose normocalcaemic PHPT from one raised PTH.
  • Never use a loop diuretic to lower calcium, or restrict dietary calcium in PHPT · do not order PTHrP or ACE to screen.
  • Check renal function, calcium and vitamin D before any bisphosphonate or denosumab; alendronic acid is not recommended if CrCl <35 mL/min (SPC).
  • Age alone does not justify GP-only PHPT surveillance: agree it with endocrinology.
  • A low urine Ca ratio does not prove FHH · parathyroid imaging localises, it does not diagnose.

12Safety-net and special groupstell the patient

999: confusion or drowsiness, a fit or collapse, chest pain or irregular heartbeat, tight throat or noisy breathing with tingling or cramps, back pain with new leg weakness, numbness or loss of bladder or bowel control. Same day: vomiting or unable to keep fluids down, severe thirst or passing much more urine, new tingling round the mouth or fingers, cramps, new bone pain or a fracture. On a bisphosphonate or denosumab: report thigh, groin or hip pain and jaw or mouth symptoms. If calcium is high, take no calcium or vitamin D supplements unless agreed.

  • Pregnancy: placental ALP rises normally; do not chase it. Young adults still growing: bone ALP can be physiologically high.
  • On oncology bone treatment (zoledronic acid or denosumab 120 mg monthly for metastases, distinct from 60 mg six-monthly for osteoporosis): oncology-led; dental review, calcium and vitamin D, renal function.
  • CKD G4–G5 or dialysis: interpret calcium, phosphate and PTH trends with the renal team; no universal PTH target outside dialysis.
