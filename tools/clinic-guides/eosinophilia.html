The count does not predict organ damage: 2.0 × 10⁹/L with cardiac or neurological involvement is more urgent than a well patient at 8.0. Organ damage → urgent assessment without waiting for duration criteria.

1Confirm it is realBSH 2017

RepeatAn unexpected result: repeat FBC with differential and a blood film before further tests
FilmConfirms the count and morphology; excludes blasts
Previous countsLong-standing, stable, mild = usually atopy
Drug timelineAnything started in the previous 8 weeks, including OTC NSAIDs and herbal products
SteroidsLower the count and can hide the cause — and are dangerous in undiagnosed Strongyloides

Hypereosinophilia = ≥1.5 × 10⁹/L on two occasions, usually at least 4 weeks apart. Persistent ≥1.5 needs investigation (BSH).

At any count, an abnormal film, blasts, cytopenias, splenomegaly or constitutional symptoms → urgent haematology advice. Repeat testing is guided by the count, persistence and symptoms — not automatically.

3Focused history — what discriminatesBSH 2017 · BIA 2024

Where have you lived?Country of birth, migration, travel, barefoot soil or freshwater contact — even decades ago.
What are you taking?New prescription, OTC NSAID, herbal or supplement; match the drug timeline to the count.
Allergy historyAsthma (especially new or difficult), eczema, rhinitis, nasal polyps, food allergy.
Organ symptomsChest pain, breathlessness, numbness, rash, diarrhoea, weight loss, sweats, nodes.

5Work-up checklistBSH initial tests · targeted extras

  • Repeat FBC + film
  • U&E, eGFR, LFTs
  • Bone profile, LDH
  • ESR and/or CRP
  • Vitamin B12 (raised in clonal disease)
  • Medication and travel review
  • Strongyloides IgG if exposure
  • Urine dip; CXR if chest symptoms
  • ANCA only if EGPA suspected
  • ECG, troponin, echo if persistent ≥1.5 or cardiac symptoms

Stool microscopy has low sensitivity for Strongyloides — serology is the key test. Bone marrow and molecular tests are haematology-led.

6Management by the commonest causesBSH 2017 · BIA 2024 · MHRA · SPC

CauseFirst lineCheck and continue
AtopyOptimise treatment: inhaled steroid for asthma, intranasal steroid for rhinitis, non-sedating antihistamine, emollients and topical steroid for eczemaThe count may stay up despite good control; persistent or rising → look for another cause
Drug-inducedStop the suspected drug if clinically safe; specialist advice if essential. Document the reaction; Yellow CardRecheck FBC after stopping; not settling → other cause. Never rechallenge after DRESS, SJS/TEN, hepatitis or nephritis
StrongyloidesIvermectin, regimen per tropical medicine or local protocol. Immunosuppressed or needs immunosuppression → discuss with ID/tropical medicine urgentlyFollow with the eosinophil count, not serology (stays positive for months)
Other helminthsTreat per microbiology or tropical medicine advice, guided by exposure and serologyRecheck FBC at about 3 months: should normalise

7Before any immunosuppression — Strongyloides firstBIA 2024 · UKHSA migrant health guide

WhoBorn in, or lived in, an endemic area (South and South-East Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, South America, parts of southern Europe). Infection can persist for decades.
WhenBefore high-dose or prolonged steroids, rituximab, anti-TNF and other immunosuppressants — whatever the eosinophil count.
TestStrongyloides IgG serology. Positive → ID/tropical medicine and treat before immunosuppression starts.
Can't wait?Empirical treatment only on specialist advice, when urgent immunosuppression cannot wait or testing is unavailable.

Steroids in undiagnosed Strongyloides can trigger hyperinfection with Gram-negative sepsis, which is often fatal.

8Advice and the conversationplain English

  • Travel: wear shoes on soil and sand, safe water and food, avoid freshwater swimming in endemic areas
  • Pets: regular worming; wash hands after soil or pet contact
  • Report rash or fever after starting a new medicine

"Eosinophils are a type of white blood cell that go up with allergies, some medicines and certain infections picked up abroad. Yours are a little high. We'll look at your medicines and travel, repeat the test, and check for a common hidden worm infection before any steroid treatment."

9DRESS — recognise and actMHRA · BSH

Drug started usually 2–8 weeks before · fever · widespread rash or facial swelling · eosinophilia or atypical lymphocytes · lymphadenopathy · liver, kidney, lung or heart involvement.

Act: stop the culprit, same-day hospital or dermatology; steroids are specialist-led. Record as a severe allergy; never rechallenge. Common culprits: allopurinol, carbamazepine, lamotrigine, sulfonamides, vancomycin, abacavir. MHRA: HLA-B*15:02 testing before carbamazepine in Han Chinese or Thai ancestry.

10Follow-up and monitoringBSH 2017 · BIA 2024

  • Recheck after each intervention: drug stopped, parasite treated, atopy optimised
  • After parasite treatment: FBC at about 3 months
  • Persistent eosinophilia → ID or haematology review
  • Atopy: no routine annual FBC for a past mild rise
  • Drug reaction recorded as an allergy
  • Return the same day: chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, new numbness or weakness, rash with fever

12Pitfalls and never-eventsBSH · BIA · MHRA

  • Never start steroids in an at-risk patient without thinking Strongyloides.
  • Never use a negative ANCA to exclude EGPA.
  • Do not grade urgency by the count alone.
  • Do not blame atopy for a rising or persistent count.
  • Allopurinol + rash + fever = DRESS until proved otherwise.
  • Steroids lower the count and can mask the cause.
  • Negative stool tests do not exclude Strongyloides.
