Dyspepsia and reflux in adults CLINIC GUIDE

For adults 18+ with indigestion, epigastric pain or heartburn and reflux. Not for children, pregnancy-specific care, active GI bleeding (emergency) or known Barrett's surveillance (NG231).

4Investigations checklistCG184 · QS96

  • H. pylori: urea breath test or stool antigen
  • PPI stopped 2 weeks before the test
  • No antibiotics for 4 weeks before
  • FBC if pallor, anaemia or 55+
  • Coeliac serology if indicated
  • LFTs if biliary; ECG if cardiac

Do not: request OGD for typical reflux without alarm features · test for H. pylori while on a PPI · use office-based serology. Uninvestigated "reflux-like" symptoms are managed as uninvestigated dyspepsia (CG184).

5Make the call before you prescribeCG184 · NG12

Alarm featureRefer first (red box). A PPI is fine only if it does not delay the referral; tell the endoscopy team.
Typical refluxClinical diagnosis of GORD: no endoscopy needed. Full-dose PPI for 4 or 8 weeks (page 2).
Uninvestigated dyspepsiaTest-and-treat H. pylori or a 4-week PPI first, then the other (page 2).
Ulcer-type + NSAIDStop or switch the NSAID; test for H. pylori; gastroprotection if it must continue.

Record the alarm-feature screen in the notes: it is the safety-net you will rely on if symptoms change. Set the step-down goal at the first prescription.

6Uninvestigated dyspepsia — the CG184 sequenceeither test-and-treat or a PPI first, then the other

Step 1 · causesReview drugs (stop NSAIDs where possible); lifestyle; antacid or alginate for mild symptoms.
Step 2 · first strategyH. pylori test-and-treat, or full-dose PPI for 4 weeks. Still symptomatic → offer the other.
Step 3 · step downSettled → lowest effective dose or as-needed use. Annual review for anyone on long-term treatment.
Step 4 · not settlingRecheck alarm features and the diagnosis; 55+ and treatment-resistant → consider OGD (NG12); otherwise gastroenterology.

10Safety-net and follow-up checklistCG184 · NG12

  • Alarm features asked and documented (dysphagia, weight, bleeding, vomiting)
  • Review at the end of the 4- or 8-week PPI course
  • Step-down plan agreed at the first prescription
  • H. pylori result actioned; retest booked only if indicated
  • Return at once: swallowing trouble, vomiting blood, black stools, weight loss
  • Annual review of any long-term PPI: indication, dose, as-needed use
  • New alarm feature at any point → re-triage against NG12
  • Written information given (NHS, Guts UK)

11Lifestyle — offer to everyoneCG184

Lose weight if overweight · stop smoking · cut alcohol and trigger foods · smaller meals, none late at night · raise the head of the bed for night symptoms.

"This is a course, not a forever tablet. Once you're settled we'll step it down to the lowest dose that works — and if you ever have trouble swallowing, vomit blood or lose weight, come back straight away."

12Special groups and never-eventsNG12 · CG184 · NG231 · QS96

  • Pregnancy: lifestyle and an alginate first; omeprazole if acid suppression is needed. Barrett's: keep the PPI and the NG231 surveillance plan; don't deprescribe without the specialist plan.
  • Never put new dysphagia down to reflux or answer it with a higher PPI dose · never let a PPI trial delay an NG12 referral · never test for H. pylori within 2 weeks of a PPI.
  • Never call exertional or sweaty "indigestion" reflux without considering ACS · never leave someone on full-dose PPI for years with no review · never use domperidone or metoclopramide as dyspepsia treatment (MHRA restrictions).
