1Is it real? Confirm before you actNG238

Fasting or notNon-fasting lipids are fine for routine CVD risk. Repeat fasting when TG is ≥4.5 mmol/L, the result is unexpected, or chylomicronaemia is suspected.
10–20 mmol/LRepeat fasting after at least 5 days but within 2 weeks, and review secondary causes meanwhile.
Recent triggersA binge, a heavy fatty meal, new steroids or a diabetes flare can raise a single result. Ask before you label.
LDL unreliableCalculated LDL is unreliable when TG is above 4.5 mmol/L: use non-HDL cholesterol (total minus HDL) or a direct LDL. Milky plasma can disrupt other tests.

5Work-up checklistNG238

  • Fasting lipids + non-HDL
  • HbA1c, glucose
  • TSH
  • LFTs + GGT
  • U&E/eGFR
  • Urine dipstick or ACR
  • BMI, waist, BP
  • QRISK3 (may underestimate)
  • Alcohol units (AUDIT-C)
  • Drug review

Look for eruptive xanthomata (buttocks, elbows) and, if TG is very high, lipaemia retinalis. Ketones only if unwell or hyperglycaemic, not because of a TG number.

7Lifestyle that lowers triglyceridesNG238 · UK CMO

Sugar firstSugary drinks, fruit juice and refined carbohydrate drive TG as much as fat. Swap to water or unsweetened drinks.
AlcoholReduce substantially; stop completely while TG is severely raised or after pancreatitis. Record units every review.
WeightWaist loss matters most; effect varies with diabetes control and genetics. Offer weight-management services.
ActivityAbout 150 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity, spread through the week; the effect fades within days of stopping.

8Follow-up and monitoring checklistNG238 · SPCs

  • 10–20 mmol/L: fasting repeat 5 days–2 weeks later; still >10 → specialist
  • Repeat lipids after the cause is treated (fasting if TG markedly raised)
  • No universal TG target: in severe TG the aim is less pancreatitis risk; use specialist-agreed targets
  • Manage LDL and non-HDL by CVD-risk category
  • Fenofibrate: renal function and LFTs per SPC; reassess if eGFR falls
  • Icosapent ethyl: AF symptoms, bleeding risk
  • After TG pancreatitis: alcohol abstinence; specialist target
  • Planning pregnancy: stop statins when pregnancy is possible
  • Alcohol units recorded at every review

10Special groupsNG238 · NG104 · HST13

  • Pregnancy: TG rises normally; with pre-existing high TG it can reach pancreatitis levels → obstetric and lipid co-management. Avoid fibrates unless a specialist advises; stop statins when pregnancy is possible and do not restart until breastfeeding ends.
  • Diabetes: a very high TG may be the first sign of new or uncontrolled diabetes; control glucose before adding lipid drugs.
  • Chylomicronaemia: very-low-fat diets only under a specialist dietitian (risk of essential fatty-acid and fat-soluble vitamin deficiency).
  • After pancreatitis: steatorrhoea → think exocrine insufficiency; check faecal elastase and involve gastroenterology.

11Pitfalls and never-eventsNG238 · NG104 · MHRA

  • Never wait for a fasting repeat when there is abdominal pain or vomiting.
  • Never trust calculated LDL when TG is above 4.5 mmol/L.
  • Never start a fibrate just to lower CVD risk, or combine gemfibrozil with a statin.
  • Never treat over-the-counter fish oil as icosapent ethyl.
  • Do not miss new diabetes, hypothyroidism or nephrotic syndrome behind a high TG.

12Say it like thisplain English

"Your triglycerides — a type of fat in the blood — are very high, and at this level they can inflame the pancreas. Cutting out alcohol and sugary drinks brings them down quickly. If you get severe tummy pain going through to your back, or vomiting, go straight to A&E."
