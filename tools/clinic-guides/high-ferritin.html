1Confirm it is realBSH 2018

  • Check CRP with every ferritin: ferritin is an acute-phase protein. A high ferritin with a high CRP says nothing about iron stores.
  • Recheck when the illness has settled (CRP normal) before investigating further.
  • Recent iron tablets, IV iron or transfusion raise ferritin; recent oral iron can also raise TSAT.
  • Alcohol is often the only cause: ask (AUDIT-C) and look at GGT.
  • TSAT: a fasting sample is not routinely needed, but repeat a borderline or unexpected result before acting on it.
  • Do not request HFE genotyping without a TSAT: the genotype alone does not make the diagnosis.

5Work-up checklistBSH 2018

  • Ferritin + CRP together
  • TSAT (serum iron + transferrin)
  • LFTs + GGT; alcohol history
  • FBC; film if cytopenias
  • U&E/eGFR
  • HbA1c, lipids, BMI
  • Hepatitis B, C if LFTs abnormal
  • HFE genotype if TSAT raised

Liver ultrasound if LFTs abnormal, hepatomegaly or suspected liver disease. MRI liver iron is a specialist test.

8Living with haemochromatosisBSH 2018 · EASL 2022

  • No iron supplements or iron-containing multivitamins; avoid high-dose vitamin C supplements. Diet alone does not replace venesection.
  • Alcohol: keep low; avoid if there is liver disease.
  • Raw oysters and shellfish: avoid with significant iron overload or liver disease (severe Vibrio infection).
  • Some patients can give blood through NHS Blood and Transplant if they meet donor criteria.

9Follow-up checklistBSH 2018 · EASL · NG50

  • Inflammatory rise: ferritin + CRP rechecked once well
  • Alcohol or metabolic: LFTs and ferritin rechecked after change
  • Haemochromatosis: venesection plan and targets recorded from the specialist letter
  • Cirrhosis: liver ultrasound every 6 months for HCC (NG50), ± AFP per hepatology
  • HbA1c, lipids and joints reviewed in haemochromatosis
  • Adult first-degree relatives of a C282Y homozygote offered HFE genotype, ferritin and TSAT
  • Return the same day: fever with bruising or bleeding, jaundice, confusion, abdominal swelling

11Pitfalls and never-eventsBSH 2018 · EASL

  • Never interpret ferritin without a CRP.
  • Never diagnose haemochromatosis from a genotype alone, or from ferritin alone.
  • Never venesect for inflammatory or metabolic hyperferritinaemia.
  • Never diagnose HLH from ferritin alone — but never sit on fever, cytopenias and a very high ferritin.
  • Ancestry informs the odds; it is not a reason to withhold testing.
  • Do not start HCC surveillance just because ferritin is high or the patient is C282Y homozygous: it is for cirrhosis (or advanced fibrosis on hepatology advice).
  • Do not let a normal TSAT reassure you about a ferritin ≥1,000 µg/L.

12Special groups and familiesBSH 2018 · EASL 2022

  • Family cascade: siblings of a C282Y homozygote have a 1 in 4 chance of being homozygous — offer adult first-degree relatives HFE genotype, ferritin and TSAT. Avoid routine testing of children unless clinically indicated or advised by genetics.
  • Women before the menopause: periods protect, so ferritin often rises after the menopause. Pregnancy: venesection can usually pause; specialist plan if overload is severe.
  • Transfusion-dependent anaemias (thalassaemia, sickle cell, MDS): iron overload is managed by haematology with MRI liver and cardiac iron; cardiac symptoms need urgent specialist review.

13Say it like thisplain English

"Ferritin is a protein that stores iron, but it also goes up when the body is inflamed, with alcohol or with a fatty liver. Most raised results are not iron overload. One more test, the iron saturation, tells us whether you are actually holding too much iron."
