Do not use CRP to rule sepsis in or out: serious infection can occur with a modest or initially normal CRP. Decide on observations, NEWS2 and the patient.

1Confirm it is reallaboratory · timing

TimingCRP rises within hours and falls fast; ESR rises and falls over days to weeks — a lagging ESR after recovery is expected
ESR raised byAge, female sex, anaemia, renal disease, pregnancy, paraprotein
ESR lowered byPolycythaemia
CRP raised byObesity, smoking, hard exercise in the previous 48 hours, recent surgery or trauma
Masked bySteroids and tocilizumab suppress CRP (and fever) — assess infection clinically

Mild, unexplained rise in a well patient: repeat in 4–6 weeks with a clinical review — not a cascade of tests, and not endless rechecks without examining the patient. Use your laboratory's ESR range for age and sex.

A rising trend matters more than one value: a CRP that keeps climbing over months needs more thought than a stable mild rise over years.

4Work-up checklisttargeted, not scattergun

  • FBC + film
  • U&E, LFTs, calcium
  • Urine dipstick + ACR
  • SPEP + serum FLC if myeloma plausible
  • CXR if chest, nodes or weight loss
  • RF + anti-CCP if synovitis
  • ANA, ANCA only with features
  • Blood cultures before antibiotics if septic
  • CK, TSH if girdle symptoms
  • LDH if nodes or sweats

Myeloma (NG12): aged 60+ with persistent bone or back pain or unexplained fracture → FBC, calcium, ESR or plasma viscosity, SPEP + serum free light chains (urine Bence-Jones if FLC unavailable).

6Suspected GCA — the same-day sequenceBSR 2020 · local fast-track pathway

1 · RecogniseAged 50+: new headache, scalp tenderness, jaw or tongue claudication, visual symptoms, PMR symptoms. Markers can be modest or rarely normal.
2 · Treat nowPrednisolone 40–60 mg today. Visual loss: IV methylprednisolone 0.5–1 g up to 3 days, or 60 mg oral if IV not immediately available.
3 · Refer todayGCA fast-track for ultrasound or biopsy, ideally soon after steroid starts — never delay treatment for it.
4 · ProtectFracture-risk assessment and bone protection, GI risk, glucose and BP; steroid card.

7Follow-up and monitoringBSR · SPS · NG250

  • Mild unexplained rise: recheck in 4–6 weeks with a clinical review
  • Still raised: FBC + film, renal, liver, calcium, urine, targeted tests
  • Treated infection: judge on the patient; CRP trend supports only
  • PMR/GCA: symptoms with ESR/CRP during the taper
  • Flare = symptoms return; never raise steroid on markers alone
  • PMR on treatment beyond 2 years → review diagnosis, refer
  • Long-term steroid: BP, weight, HbA1c, fracture risk reviewed (SPS)
  • Plan and reasoning documented
  • Return the same day: new vision change, headache or jaw pain; fever with back pain; 999 if very unwell

9Pitfalls and never-eventsBSR · NG253 · NG12

  • Never wait for ESR/CRP before starting steroid in suspected GCA.
  • Never diagnose or exclude sepsis on CRP.
  • Normal markers do not exclude GCA, PMR or serious infection.
  • On steroids or tocilizumab, CRP and fever may stay low despite infection.
  • PMR not responding to 15 mg → rethink the diagnosis.
  • High ESR with normal CRP in an older patient: think myeloma.
  • SLE flares and active IBD can have a normal CRP.
  • A positive ANCA or ANA alone does not make the diagnosis.
  • Procalcitonin is not a routine primary-care test.

10Advice and the conversationplain English

  • Avoid hard exercise for 48 hours before a repeat test
  • On steroids: carry the steroid card; never stop suddenly
  • Weight loss, stopping smoking and dental care lower a low-grade CRP

"This test shows there is some inflammation in your body, but it doesn't tell us where or why. It can be as simple as a recent infection or extra weight. I'll examine you and do a few targeted tests rather than lots of random ones, and recheck it in a few weeks."
