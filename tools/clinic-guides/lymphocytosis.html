Lymphocytosis: the high lymphocyte count CLINIC GUIDE

A raised absolute lymphocyte count in adults 18+. Not for children (use age-specific ranges and the paediatric pathway), or known CLL or lymphoma under active haematology treatment.

No single lymphocyte count is an emergency threshold on its own: serious disease can occur at modest counts, and a well patient with a high, stable CLL count may not need same-day referral.

1Confirm it is realabsolute count, not percentage

  • Use the absolute count: a high lymphocyte % with low neutrophils is a relative rise, not lymphocytosis.
  • Look back: CLL is often visible on old FBCs for years.
  • Timing: during or just after a viral illness the rise is usually reactive.
  • Repeat after recovery, commonly at 4–6 weeks, to confirm it has settled.
  • Film if persistent, rising, or with cytopenias, nodes or spleen. Smear cells support CLL but are not diagnostic; diagnosis is by flow cytometry.

7Living with CLL, recovering from glandular feverBSH CLL · NICE CKS

Infections in CLLSame-day advice for fever over 38 °C, worsening cough or spreading skin infection; on treatment, use the haematology contact number.
Skin in CLLHigher skin cancer risk: daily high-SPF sunscreen, avoid peak sun, report new, bleeding or non-healing lesions.
Mind"Watch and wait" is the evidence-based treatment. Patient support: CLL Support and Lymphoma Action; NHS Talking Therapies for anxiety or low mood.
After glandular feverGradual return to work, study and exercise, guided by symptoms. Avoid alcohol while unwell or if LFTs are raised. Persistent fatigue → reassess.

9Follow-up and monitoringwritten plan where diagnosed

  • Reactive: repeat FBC after recovery, commonly 4–6 weeks; target back within the laboratory range, then stop
  • Not settled: film, previous counts, examine, HIV if indicated, flow per the local pathway
  • MBL: FBC at the frequency in the haematology letter
  • CLL: FBC per the plan; falling Hb or platelets, rapid rise, B symptoms, new nodes or spleen → haematology
  • CLL: vaccines reviewed; immunoglobulins known; skin checks and sun protection
  • Mood after a CLL diagnosis: offer support and NHS Talking Therapies
  • Glandular fever: contact-sport advice given and recorded
  • Cause, repeat result and plan recorded in the notes

11Pitfalls and never-eventsBSH · CKS

  • Never diagnose CLL from the total count or smear cells: it needs flow cytometry.
  • Never give amoxicillin in glandular fever, or code the rash as penicillin allergy.
  • Never give live vaccines in CLL without haematology advice.
  • CLL: falling Hb with raised bilirubin and LDH and a positive DAT is AIHA; falling platelets with bruising may be ITP — urgent haematology, even if the CLL is stable.
  • Mild stable lymphocytosis after splenectomy needs no action; a rising count does.
  • Routine spleen ultrasound is not needed after glandular fever; seek advice for athletes or a persisting spleen.
  • Persistent or severe fatigue after glandular fever: reassess for other diagnoses and low mood.
  • CLL and COVID-19: check interactions with CLL drugs before nirmatrelvir/ritonavir; use the NHS antiviral pathway.

12Safety-nettell the patient

999: breathing difficulty, cannot swallow saliva, sudden left-sided tummy or shoulder-tip pain, collapse. Same day: fever over 38 °C, worsening cough or breathlessness, spreading skin infection, new bruising or bleeding. Within a week: new lumps, drenching sweats, weight loss.

13Say it like thisplain English

"Your lymphocytes, one type of white cell, are raised. After a virus that's very common and settles. If it stays up we'll do a special test on the blood. If that shows a slow-growing condition like CLL, most people just need regular checks — watching it carefully is the right treatment, not doing nothing."
