Macrocytosis: the high MCV CLINIC GUIDE

Interpreting an MCV above the reference range (usually >100 fL) in adults 18+. Not for pregnancy (physiological rise; maternity pathway), children or patients under active haematology care.

1Confirm it is realrepeat if unexpected

  • Look back at previous FBCs: stable, long-standing and isolated, or new and rising?
  • Delayed or stored sample: red cells swell and the MCV rises. Repeat on a fresh sample if unexpected.
  • Cold agglutinins: very high MCV with raised MCHC and low red cell count, clumping on the film. Repeat on a sample kept at 37 °C; discuss with haematology.
  • Reticulocytosis (bleeding, haemolysis, recent treatment) raises the MCV: check reticulocytes.
  • B12 results mislead: recent injection or supplement; combined oral contraceptive lowers total B12 (use active B12); nitrous oxide gives functional deficiency with a normal B12 (use MMA or homocysteine). Anti-IF antibodies can falsely normalise B12 assays.
  • Pregnancy, smoking cause a mild physiological rise.

There is no single MCV that diagnoses MDS or mandates referral: a marked or rising MCV with anaemia, cytopenias or an abnormal film needs prompt review. Deficiency is not excluded by a normal Hb or MCV (NG239).

4Work-up checklistNG239 · BSH

  • Repeat FBC + film if unexpected
  • B12 and folate together
  • LFTs + GGT; alcohol history
  • TSH
  • Reticulocytes; U&E
  • Full drug review, incl. nitrous oxide
  • Anti-IF antibodies if B12 low
  • tTG IgA + total IgA if malabsorption

Haemolysis screen if reticulocytes high. Do not request anti-parietal cell antibodies routinely; a negative anti-IF does not exclude autoimmune gastritis.

7B12 deficiency — choose the route by the causeNG239 · SPC

Autoimmune gastritisIM hydroxocobalamin for life; also after total gastrectomy or terminal ileal resection. Oral is unreliable without intrinsic factor.
Diet (vegan, poor intake)Oral cyanocobalamin; fortified foods or a supplement long term. Stop when the cause is addressed and symptoms resolve.
Medicines (metformin, PPIs)Oral or IM by clinical judgement and preference; usually continue the medicine and supplement alongside.
Nitrous oxideStop exposure; IM hydroxocobalamin without waiting for results; same-day assessment if neurological symptoms.

8Follow-up and monitoring checklistNG239 · SPS

  • Cause found: treat, then recheck FBC to confirm the response
  • B12: initial review at about 3 months (earlier if severe) — symptoms, adherence, cause
  • Do not use B12 levels to monitor regular IM injections
  • Poor response → reconsider: iron deficiency, MDS, coeliac, wrong diagnosis
  • Folate: FBC rechecked; continue beyond 4 months if the cause persists
  • Alcohol: GGT and MCV trend as feedback
  • No cause after first and second line → haematology
  • Isolated, stable, no cause: periodic FBC in primary care
  • Return the same day: fever, mouth ulcers, bruising or bleeding, new numbness, weakness or unsteadiness

10Special groupsNG239 · SPS · BSH

  • On methotrexate: monitor FBC per the shared-care schedule and watch the MCV trend; folic acid weekly, never daily; acute rise with ulcers or cytopenias = toxicity.
  • Older adults: unexplained macrocytosis with any cytopenia raises MDS — refer rather than repeat. Follow the haematology plan for transfusion and shared care.
  • Coeliac disease, bariatric surgery: combined B12, folate and iron deficiency is common — check all three. Pregnancy: use active B12 first and the maternity pathway.
  • Autoimmune overlap: autoimmune gastritis and hypothyroidism often coexist — a high MCV in treated hypothyroidism still needs B12 checked. Recent B12 injection: the B12 level is meaningless; read the film and response instead.

11Pitfalls and never-eventsNG239 · BSH · SPS

  • Never give folic acid alone before B12 deficiency is excluded or treated: it can precipitate cord damage.
  • Never co-prescribe trimethoprim or co-trimoxazole with methotrexate where avoidable: marrow toxicity can be fatal.
  • Never delay B12 when there are neurological features.
  • Never stop injections in autoimmune gastritis because symptoms improve.
  • Do not blame alcohol without checking B12, folate and the film.
  • Ask about nitrous oxide in young adults with neuropathy.

12Say it like thisplain English

"Your red blood cells are a bit larger than usual. On its own that isn't harmful, but it's a clue. The commonest reasons are alcohol, low vitamin B12 or folate, the thyroid, the liver or some medicines. These blood tests will tell us which, and most of these causes are easy to put right."
