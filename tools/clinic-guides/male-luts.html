LUTS in men CLINIC GUIDE

Lower urinary tract symptoms: first assessment and drug review. Men and others with a prostate. Not for prostatitis, neurogenic bladder, incontinence after prostate surgery, or children.

Page 1 · Assess and start screen the emergencies and cancers → voiding or storage? → conservative first → drug by phenotype

!Act today — and the cancer routesCG97 · NG12

  • Acute retention (unable to void, painful bladder) → same-day catheterisation via the local pathway; offer an alpha-blocker before a trial without catheter. Saddle numbness, leg weakness or new incontinence → 999 / ED (cauda equina). Fever, rigors, loin pain → same day.
  • Chronic retention, often painless: residual over 1 litre or a palpable bladder → creatinine and upper-tract imaging, urgent urology; renal impairment or hydronephrosis → catheterise and discuss with urology urgently.
  • Prostate feels malignant on DRE → suspected cancer pathway, whatever the PSA. Aged 45+ with unexplained visible haematuria without UTI, or that persists or recurs after a treated UTI → bladder and renal pathway. Aged 60+, non-visible haematuria plus dysuria or raised white count → bladder pathway.

1Voiding or storage? Everything followsCG97

GroupSymptomsUsual driver
VoidingHesitancy, weak or intermittent stream, straining, incomplete emptyingOutflow obstruction, often benign prostatic enlargement
StorageUrgency, frequency, nocturia, urgency incontinenceOveractive bladder
Post-micturitionDribbling after leaving the toiletUrethral pooling

Often mixed and multifactorial. An alpha-blocker alone fails the man whose main problem is storage.

2Score it and chart itCG97 · QS45

  • IPSS: 7 questions scored 0–5 (total 0–35) plus a quality-of-life question scored 0–6. Mild 0–7 · moderate 8–19 · severe 20–35. Treat on bother and quality of life, not the band alone.
  • Frequency-volume chart for men with bothersome LUTS, day and night, especially with storage symptoms or nocturia.
  • Nocturnal polyuria = more than a third of the 24-hour urine output at night → look for heart failure, oedema, diabetes, sleep apnoea, evening fluids or diuretic timing. Not an alpha-blocker problem.

3Work-up — every man with LUTSCG97

  • History, medicines (incl. OTC)
  • IPSS + bother score
  • Frequency-volume chart
  • Abdomen: palpable bladder?
  • External genitalia; DRE
  • Urine dipstick: blood, glucose, protein, leucocytes, nitrites
  • PSA information and offer
  • Creatinine/eGFR only if renal impairment suspected

Not routine in primary care: flow rate, cystoscopy, imaging. Post-void residual only when retention, poor emptying or recurrent UTI is suspected, or before an antimuscarinic if emptying is a concern.

4PSA — an informed choiceNG12 · CG97 · PCRMP

With LUTS, erectile dysfunction or visible haematuria, consider a PSA and DRE (NG12). Give information, advice and time to decide.

Age40–4950–5960–6970–79
Symptomatic: PSA above (ng/mL)2.53.54.56.5

Above the threshold → consider a suspected cancer pathway referral, taking account of preference and comorbidity. Under 40 or 80+: clinical judgement.

Asymptomatic man aged 50–69 who chooses a test (PCRMP): referral value ≥3.0 ng/mL.

Before testing: no active UTI; no prostate biopsy in the last 6 weeks; no ejaculation or vigorous exercise in the last 48 hours. On a 5-ARI for about 6 months, PSA falls by about half.

6Conservative measures — first, and often enoughCG97

FluidsIrritantsStorageDribbleMedicinesContainment
Adequate, not excessive; less in the evening if nocturiaReduce caffeine, fizzy drinks and alcoholSupervised bladder trainingUrethral milking after voidingDiuretic timing; stop decongestants and anticholinergics where possiblePads or collecting devices while treatment takes effect

Mild or non-bothersome: explanation, reassurance and lifestyle advice, with a review date, rather than a prescription. Lifestyle advice and, if needed, containment products for storage symptoms.

Do not offer homeopathy, phytotherapy (for example saw palmetto) or acupuncture for LUTS.

"Most of these symptoms settle with simple changes and, if needed, a tablet that relaxes the outlet — that one works within days. If your prostate is enlarged we may add one that shrinks it, but that can take six months, so don't lose heart early."

7First drug — match it to the phenotypeCG97 · TA290

Voiding, mod–severeOffer an alpha-blocker
Prostate >30 g or PSA >1.4and high progression risk (e.g. older) → 5-ARI
Storage / OABOffer an antimuscarinic
Antimuscarinic unsuitableMirabegron (TA290)

Bothersome moderate–severe LUTS with prostate >30 g or PSA >1.4 ng/mL → consider alpha-blocker + 5-ARI. Storage symptoms persisting on an alpha-blocker → consider adding an antimuscarinic.

Usual starts: tamsulosin 400 micrograms OD · finasteride 5 mg OD or dutasteride 500 micrograms OD · solifenacin 5 mg OD or tolterodine 2 mg BD · mirabegron 50 mg OD.

8The review clockCG97 · QS45 statement 6

DrugFirst reviewThen
Alpha-blocker4–6 weeksEvery 6–12 months
5-ARI3–6 monthsEvery 6–12 months
AntimuscarinicEvery 4–6 weeks until stableEvery 6–12 months

Success = less bother and better quality of life on the IPSS, with acceptable side effects. A 5-ARI can take up to 6 months to work (SPC).

9Not improving? Check before you step upCG97

  • Retention: palpable bladder, rising creatinine, recurrent UTI → urology, not an antimuscarinic
  • Right target: voiding treated as voiding, storage as storage?
  • Long enough: weeks for an alpha-blocker, months for a 5-ARI?
  • Taking it, at the right time of day?
  • Nocturia driver: re-read the chart for nocturnal polyuria
  • Red flags since last time: haematuria, abnormal DRE, PSA

10The drug ladderCG97 · TA290 · SPCs

Step 1 · conservativeMild or non-bothersome: lifestyle and watchful waiting, with review.
Step 2 · alpha-blockerModerate–severe LUTS: alfuzosin, doxazosin, tamsulosin or terazosin. Works within days to weeks.
Step 3 · add by phenotypeEnlarged prostate → + 5-ARI. Persistent storage symptoms → + antimuscarinic, or mirabegron if unsuitable.
Step 4 · referBothersome despite treatment, or complications → urology for a surgical or procedural opinion.

11Side effects and MHRA warningsMHRA DSU · SPC

Mirabegron (Oct 2015)Severe hypertension, stroke and TIA reported: contraindicated in severe uncontrolled hypertension (systolic ≥180 and/or diastolic ≥110); check BP before and during
Finasteride, dutasteride (Apr 2024, May 2026)Finasteride: depression, suicidal ideation, sexual dysfunction that may persist after stopping. Dutasteride: precautionary mood warning. Seek advice promptly if any occur
Alpha-blockersFirst-dose hypotension (doxazosin, terazosin); tell the cataract surgeon
AntimuscarinicsAvoid in retention; dry mouth, constipation, confusion; count the total anticholinergic burden

12Annual review checklistevery man on treatment

  • IPSS and bother score against baseline
  • Adherence; still needed? Step down if mild for a long time
  • Postural symptoms, falls (alpha-blocker); BP on mirabegron
  • Mood and sexual function on a 5-ARI (MHRA)
  • Anticholinergic burden, cognition, constipation
  • PSA if on a 5-ARI or per plan: interpret as about half
  • New haematuria, UTIs, retention, neurological symptoms
  • Planned cataract surgery? Tell the eye team about alpha-blockers

13Safety-netsay it, write it

  • Can't pass urine, painful swollen lower tummy → same day for a catheter; A&E if severe pain or unwell.
  • Any visible blood in the urine, even once → contact us.
  • Fever, shivers, pain between the legs or in the back → same day.
  • Dizziness or falls on the outlet tablet; low mood on the shrinking tablet → tell us.

14Refer and never-eventsCG97 · NG12

  • Refer to urology: recurrent or persistent UTI · retention · renal impairment from lower urinary tract dysfunction · suspected cancer · stress incontinence. Consider referral for bothersome LUTS despite conservative and drug treatment.
  • Keep in primary care: uncomplicated LUTS responding to conservative measures and drugs, reviewed on the CG97 clock.
  • Before a procedure: the urology team chooses (TURP, laser, others); raise his sexual priorities, including ejaculation.
  • Never: PSA during a UTI · an antimuscarinic in retention · a PDE5 inhibitor solely to treat LUTS · explain away visible haematuria.

15Special situationsCG97 · NG12 · SPC

SituationWhat to do
Acute retentionCatheterise the same day; offer an alpha-blocker before the trial without catheter. Recurrence is common: make sure he knows it is an emergency.
Nocturnal polyuriaTreat the cause (heart failure, oedema, diabetes, sleep apnoea, evening fluids, diuretic timing); consider a late-afternoon loop diuretic. Desmopressin only after other causes are excluded: check sodium 3 days after the first dose and stop if below normal.
PSA on a 5-ARIExpect about half the pre-treatment value after 6 months; record the on-treatment level as the new baseline and assess any confirmed rise.
Mixed symptomsAlpha-blocker first; add an antimuscarinic for persisting storage symptoms (check emptying first). Review both at each visit.
Older or frailFalls and postural hypotension with alpha-blockers; cognition and anticholinergic burden with antimuscarinics.
