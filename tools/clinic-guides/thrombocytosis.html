Thrombocytosis: the high platelet count CLINIC GUIDE

Platelets above the laboratory range (usually >450 × 10⁹/L) in adults 18+. Not for children, pregnancy, or known ET, PV or myelofibrosis already under haematology (follow the specialist plan).
NICE NG12 (suspected cancer, updated 2026)

A count of 1000 or more without symptoms and with a clear reactive cause needs prompt review and specialist advice, not automatic admission. The count correlates poorly with thrombotic risk (BSH).

1Confirm it is real and persistentBSH 2025

  • Look back at previous FBCs: a single high count does not establish persistence.
  • Spurious high count: red-cell fragments or very small red cells can be counted as platelets by the analyser. Ask for a blood film if the count does not fit.
  • Transient causes: recent surgery, trauma, bleeding or infection. Treat the cause, then repeat the FBC.
  • Repeat at a clinically judged interval — sooner with symptoms, a rising count, thrombosis, bleeding or an abnormal film.
  • Persistent = raised for at least 3 months (BSH 2025); longer observation may suit mild or variable counts.

No count separates reactive from clonal: reactive counts can exceed 1000, and ET can sit just above 450. Do not wait for 600 or 700 before testing.

  • Blood film: persistent or odd
  • Ferritin ± transferrin saturation

  • U&E, LFTs: baseline
  • CXR if aged 40+ (NG12)
  • Urine dip; culture if UTI
  • JAK2 V617F if persistent and unexplained
  • FIT or OGD if GI symptoms or IDA

Reactive thrombocytosis: no aspirin for the count itself, no cytoreduction and no NSAIDs because the platelets are raised.

7ET or PV — cut the clot riskBSH · NICE CVD pathways

Stop smokingSmoking adds to arterial clot risk. Offer behavioural support with drug treatment or a nicotine vape, and the stop-smoking service (NG209).
BP, lipids, diabetesManage through the usual NICE pathways (NG136, NG238, NG28). Thrombocytosis does not change the usual targets.
Fluids and movingAvoid dehydration, especially in heat, illness or flying. Keep mobile on long journeys; travel prophylaxis is decided on VTE risk, not the count.
Alcohol, surgeryNo more than 14 units a week, spread out. Tell surgeons and anaesthetists: the peri-operative plan is agreed with haematology.

8When and where to referNG12 · BSH 2025 · NG128 · NG158

999Stroke, ACS, suspected PE, splanchnic or cerebral vein thrombosis, digital ischaemia, major bleeding, collapse.
Same-day haematologySymptomatic count ≥1000, bleeding with extreme count, new or enlarging spleen, film suggesting leukaemia, CML or myelofibrosis, recent thrombosis.
Urgent haematologyPersistent unexplained count at any age (even JAK2-negative or below 700) · positive JAK2, CALR, MPL or BCR-ABL1 · spleen, B symptoms, high WCC or Hb · unusual-site clot.
Primary careClear reactive cause settling with treatment. Refer if it becomes unexplained, disproportionate, progressive or other counts change.

Other specialties for the reactive cause: respiratory (mass on chest X-ray), gastroenterology (IBD, upper GI symptoms), rheumatology (RA, vasculitis). Use the local haematology referral pathway and include previous counts, film, ferritin, CRP and any JAK2 result.

9Follow-up and monitoringBSH · shared care

  • Repeat FBC once the presumed cause is treated: target back within the laboratory range
  • Iron deficiency: FBC and iron studies after replacement
  • Still raised and unexplained at 3 months → JAK2 per local pathway or urgent haematology
  • NG12 test results (CXR, ultrasound, OGD) reviewed and actioned
  • On cytoreduction: bloods at the intervals in the shared-care protocol; never stop or change the dose without specialist advice
  • ET or PV: annual review of clots, bleeding, symptoms and cardiovascular risk
  • Very high or rapidly rising count on monitoring → same-day haematology discussion
  • Re-refer: count rising or a clot on treatment, intolerance of treatment, pregnancy planned
  • Discharge from monitoring once the count is normal and the cause resolved
  • Diagnosis and plan recorded in the problem list

10Pitfalls and never-eventsBSH · NG12

  • Never start aspirin or cytoreduction for suspected ET in primary care, or advise self-starting aspirin.
  • Never wait for a count of 600 or 700 before testing or referring persistent unexplained thrombocytosis.
  • Never forget the NG12 routes: a raised count at 40+ is a reason to consider a chest X-ray.
  • Never take normal CRP or ESR as proof of ET, or a negative JAK2 as proof it is reactive.
  • Thrombosis at an unusual site (portal, splenic, hepatic or cerebral vein) may be the first sign of an MPN.
  • Known ET with a falling count and a growing spleen may be progression to myelofibrosis: tell haematology.
  • Ferritin can be normal or high in inflammation: check transferrin saturation before ruling out iron deficiency.
  • Pregnancy with ET or PV → early haematology and obstetric referral; do not stop cytoreduction or start aspirin or LMWH yourself.

11Safety-nettell the patient

999: face, arm or speech symptoms (even if they settle), chest pain, sudden breathlessness, sudden vision loss, a cold or blue finger or toe, heavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain with swelling, confusion or a fit. Same day: one-sided leg swelling, burning red hands or feet, unusual bleeding or bruising, severe headache.

12Say it like thisplain English

"Your platelets, the cells that help blood clot, are a bit high. Most often that is the body reacting to an infection, inflammation or low iron, and it settles once we treat the cause. I'll recheck the blood test, and if it stays high without a reason we'll ask a blood specialist to look at it."
