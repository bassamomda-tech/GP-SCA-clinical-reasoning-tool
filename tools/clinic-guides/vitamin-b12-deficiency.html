1Is the level real?NICE NG239

Normal B12, strong suspicionAnti-intrinsic factor antibodies can interfere with the assay and give a falsely normal B12: treat on the clinical picture and discuss with the laboratory
PregnancyTotal B12 falls normally: use active B12 as the first test
Combined pillCan lower total B12 without true deficiency: consider active B12
Nitrous oxideB12 may look normal: test homocysteine or MMA first
Injection or supplementLevels are high afterwards and mean nothing: test before the first dose
Normal Hb and MCVDo not rule out deficiency: neurology can come first

Indeterminate result with symptoms: send MMA, and consider starting B12 while waiting. No need to repeat a clearly low level before treating.

A negative anti-IF antibody does not exclude autoimmune gastritis: if suspicion remains, consider gastric parietal cell antibody, gastrin or gastroscopy with gastric-body biopsy (NG239).

4Work-up checklist — send and whyNICE NG239

  • FBC + film: macrocytosis, hypersegmented neutrophils, other cytopenias
  • Folate: often low too; treat B12 first or together
  • Anti-IF antibody if autoimmune gastritis suspected
  • MMA if indeterminate with symptoms
  • Homocysteine or MMA if nitrous oxide use
  • Ferritin: mixed deficiency can mask MCV
  • tTG IgA + total IgA if GI or malabsorptive features
  • LFTs, U&E; TFTs if clinically indicated

Ask every patient about diet, metformin, PPIs, gastric or bowel surgery, autoimmune disease and nitrous oxide. Examine vibration sense, joint position, gait and Romberg.

5Reading the result in four stepsNICE NG239

1 · Neurology?Check vibration, joint position, gait, Romberg, mood and memory. Any neurological feature → IM B12 today and specialist advice, whatever the level.
2 · Which band?<180 ng/L treat · 180–350 with symptoms → MMA and consider treating · >350 look elsewhere unless the picture fits.
3 · Which cause?Diet, medicines, surgery or bowel disease, nitrous oxide; otherwise test anti-IF antibody for autoimmune gastritis.
4 · Anything else?Read the whole FBC and film: other cytopenias, an abnormal film or a macrocytosis that does not fit → haematology.

6Replacement by causeNICE NG239 · SPC

CauseReplacement regimenDuration
Neurological features (any cause)Urgent specialist advice. If not immediately available: hydroxocobalamin 1 mg IM on alternate days until no further improvement, then 1 mg every 2 monthsLifelong if the cause persists
Autoimmune gastritis, total gastrectomy, complete terminal ileal resectionHydroxocobalamin 1 mg IM three times a week for 2 weeks, then 1 mg every 2–3 monthsLifelong IM — never stop
Other malabsorption (e.g. coeliac, partial surgery)Consider IM as above; if oral, cyanocobalamin at least 1 mg dailyWhile the cause persists
Medicine-induced (metformin, PPI)IM, or oral cyanocobalamin 1 mg daily, by judgement and preference, while the medicine continues; review whether the medicine is still neededWhile on the medicine
Indeterminate + symptomsConsider starting replacement for the likely cause while the MMA result is awaitedReview when the MMA result is back
Diet-related (vegan)Oral cyanocobalamin 50–150 micrograms daily; no response → increase the dose or switch to IM. Advise fortified foods or a supplementStop once improved if diet corrected; else continue

Low folate too: start B12 first or at the same time — never folic acid alone. Pregnancy: same doses, review symptoms at 1 month.

7Follow-up checklistNICE NG239 · MHRA

  • Review at about 3 months (1 month in pregnancy; earlier if severe): symptoms, FBC response, adherence
  • Target: symptoms resolved or plateaued, Hb and MCV back to normal
  • Not improving → recheck cause and adherence, increase oral dose or switch to IM; consider other diagnoses
  • Do not routinely use B12 levels to monitor people on regular IM treatment
  • Oral, cause fixed and symptoms resolved → stop; cause unknown or ongoing → continue and follow up
  • Lifelong IM: recall system for injections every 2–3 months (every 2 with neurology)
  • Symptoms return before the next injection → reassess dose and frequency
  • Metformin: check B12 if symptoms; consider periodic checks if risk factors (MHRA)

8When and where to referNICE NG239 · NG12

Same daySuspected SACD, progressive weakness or ataxia, nitrous oxide with neurology, severe anaemia or pancytopenia, acute confusion
Suspected cancer pathwayUpper GI criteria met (NG12), e.g. dysphagia, or 55+ with weight loss and upper GI symptoms
Urgent haematologyMacrocytosis or cytopenias not explained by B12, abnormal film
GastroenterologyAutoimmune gastritis with new or worsening dyspepsia, nausea or vomiting; uncertain diagnosis needing biopsy
NeurologyNeuropathy not improving after about 3 months of replacement
GPMost cases: replace, review, keep the schedule going

Routine surveillance gastroscopy is not recommended in autoimmune gastritis (NG239): refer on symptoms.

9Who to test, and when to recheck the diagnosisNICE NG239 · MHRA

SymptomsTiredness, sore red tongue, mouth ulcers, pins and needles, numbness, unsteadiness, memory or mood change — with or without anaemia.
Risk groupsAutoimmune disease or a first-degree relative with pernicious anaemia · gastric or bariatric surgery · ileal Crohn's · vegan diet.
Medicines and drugsMetformin (MHRA: test if symptoms) · long-term PPI · recreational nitrous oxide.
Poor responseNo improvement at review → adherence, wrong route, a second cause (iron, folate, thyroid, marrow) or another neurological diagnosis.

10Special groupsNICE NG239 · MHRA

Pregnancy, breastfeedingActive B12 as the first test. Same doses; review at 1 month. A breastfed baby of a deficient mother who is not feeding or developing well → same-day paediatric assessment.
Nitrous oxideAsk directly and without judgement. Homocysteine or MMA first. Neurological symptoms → same day, IM B12 now. Stopping the gas is essential.
VegansEveryone needs a reliable B12 source: fortified foods or a supplement. Confirmed deficiency needs a treatment dose, then prevention.
Autoimmune gastritisAsk about thyroid and other autoimmune disease; TFTs if indicated. Plan injections around long trips abroad. New dyspepsia → refer.

11Pitfalls and never-eventsNICE NG239 · MHRA

  • Never give folic acid alone before B12 is known to be normal or is being replaced: it can let nerve damage progress.
  • Never stop injections in autoimmune gastritis because the Hb or B12 is now normal.
  • Never delay IM B12 for MRI or MMA when there are neurological features.
  • A normal B12 does not exclude deficiency after nitrous oxide or with assay interference.
  • Do not attribute confusion, macrocytosis or pancytopenia to B12 without excluding other causes.
  • Metformin is usually continued while B12 is replaced.

12Self-care and the conversationNICE NG239

Vegans: fortified plant milks, cereals or yeast, or a daily supplement · keep every injection appointment, and plan ahead before long trips · come back the same day with new tingling, leg weakness, poor balance or falls · come back sooner if symptoms return before the next injection is due · the Pernicious Anaemia Society has patient information.

"Your B12 is low. Your body needs it for healthy blood and nerves. We'll top it up and look for the reason. If the problem is that your stomach can't absorb it, the injections are for life, like insulin in type 1 diabetes — stopping them lets it run down again and can damage the nerves."
