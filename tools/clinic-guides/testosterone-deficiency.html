1Is it real? Two good samples, and symptomsBSSM 2023

TimingTwo samples on separate days, 7–11 am, ideally fasting. Afternoon results run lower and cannot diagnose; private one-off results need repeating.
Acute illnessIllness, surgery, crash dieting and heavy alcohol suppress testosterone. Repeat once recovered.
SHBGObesity, diabetes and hypothyroidism lower SHBG (total looks low); ageing, liver disease, hyperthyroidism raise it. Borderline → SHBG + calculated free T.
DrugsOpioids, glucocorticoids, anabolic steroids (ask directly), and prolactin-raising antipsychotics. Symptoms alone never justify treatment.

PhysicalLess muscle and body hair, central fat, hot flushes, fragility fracture, anaemia
Mood (least specific)Low mood, poor concentration, low energy: think depression first
TestED or low desire; type 2 diabetes; obesity; long-term opioids, antipsychotics or anticonvulsants

5Work-up checklistBSSM 2023

  • Testosterone ×2, 7–11 am
  • LH, FSH if low
  • SHBG (+ albumin) → free T
  • Prolactin if LH/FSH not raised
  • FBC, haematocrit
  • PSA after counselling; DRE if indicated
  • HbA1c, lipids, BP · TSH, LFTs, ferritin
  • Sleep apnoea screen
  • Fertility plans — ask now

7Before any testosterone — contraindicationsBSSM 2023 · SPC

  • Do not start: locally advanced or metastatic prostate cancer · male breast cancer · wish to preserve or achieve fertility · haematocrit >0.54 · severe heart failure (NYHA IV).
  • Specialist caution: untreated severe sleep apnoea · recent major cardiovascular event · thrombophilia or recurrent thrombosis · unexplained prostate nodule or raised PSA · significant LUTS · treated localised prostate cancer.

8Baseline record before startingBSSM 2023

  • Two results with dates
  • LH, FSH ± prolactin
  • FBC, haematocrit
  • PSA; DRE if indicated
  • CV risk, sleep apnoea
  • Fertility discussion
  • Gel-transfer advice
  • Who prescribes and monitors

10Follow-up on testosteroneBSSM 2023 · NG12

  • 3, 6 and 12 months, then yearly: testosterone (timing per product), FBC, PSA, symptoms, BP, weight
  • Haematocrit ≥0.54 → withhold or reduce; specialist advice
  • PSA rise >1.4 ng/mL in any 12 months, or >0.4 ng/mL a year over more than 2 years → urology
  • Prostate feels malignant, or PSA above age threshold → NG12
  • New wish for children → stop and refer
  • No benefit despite good levels → reassess, consider stopping
  • Functional cause fixed → consider a trial off, under specialist guidance
  • Annual review: still indicated? benefit? new contraindications?

12Pitfalls and never-eventsBSSM 2023

  • Never diagnose on one result, an afternoon sample or symptoms alone.
  • Never start testosterone with low LH/FSH before endocrine assessment.
  • Never prescribe before the fertility conversation is had and documented.
  • Never use testosterone to treat infertility or ED without confirmed deficiency.
  • Never leave testosterone on repeat without an annual review.
  • Do not miss sleep apnoea, depression, opioids or anabolic steroids.

13Functional causes; say itBSSM · plain English

Weight loss, treating sleep apnoea, cutting alcohol and reviewing opioids can lift testosterone without lifelong treatment; supplements (zinc, vitamin D) are not a substitute.

"Testosterone is highest first thing, so one afternoon test can't tell us much. I'd like two morning samples, plus a few other hormones that show where any problem starts. If we do treat, it switches off sperm production, so tell me now if you might want children. Weight, sleep and some medicines can lower it too, and fixing those can help."
