Insomnia in adults CLINIC GUIDE

Short-term and chronic insomnia, and long-term hypnotic users. Adults 18+. Not for children (see the children's sleep pathway), or for treating sleep apnoea, narcolepsy or restless legs, which are routed here only.

!Not routine insomnia: act firstNG225 · NG202 · NG217 · DVLA

  • Suicidal thoughts with severe or persistent insomnia, depression or substance use → ask directly about intent, plan and means; active intent or cannot keep safe → same-day crisis assessment; immediate danger → 999. Do not use risk tools or scales to predict suicide (NG225).
  • Reduced need for sleep with elevated or irritable mood, grandiosity or psychosis → possible mania: same-day mental-health assessment.
  • Sleepy at the wheel or near misses → must not drive until controlled (DVLA); suspected OSA in vocational drivers or safety-critical workers → rapid sleep-service assessment.
  • Nocturnal seizures (tongue biting, injury, post-ictal confusion) → urgent first-seizure referral (NG217). Nocturnal chest pain or breathlessness → its own pathway. Alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal → manage the withdrawal, don't sedate over it.

1Characterise itTA922 · CKS

  • Type: getting to sleep (anxiety, delayed phase), staying asleep (pain, OSA, nocturia, alcohol, depression), early waking (mood, alcohol, circadian timing).
  • Short-term: under 3 months, usually triggered. Chronic: 3 or more nights a week for 3 months or more, with daytime impairment.
  • 2-week sleep diary: bedtime, time to fall asleep, time awake, rise time, naps, caffeine, alcohol.
  • Partner's account: snoring, apnoeas, leg movements, dream enactment.
  • Expectations: sleep need falls with age; brief night waking is normal.

2Work-up checklisttargeted, no "insomnia panel"

  • Sleep diary, 2 weeks
  • ISI (severity, monitoring)
  • Epworth score
  • STOP-BANG if OSA possible
  • BMI, neck, upper airway
  • PHQ-9 and GAD-7
  • Caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, cannabis
  • Medicines: SSRIs, steroids, beta-blockers, diuretics, decongestants
  • Ferritin if restless legs
  • TFTs only if features
  • Driving and safety-critical work
  • Pregnancy, bipolar disorder, epilepsy

No routine polysomnography for insomnia alone. Insomnia itself has no NG12 criterion; drenching night sweats with lymphadenopathy or other features → assess on merit.

5CBT-I: the first-line treatmentTA922 · HTG624

Sleep restrictionWindow set from the diary; fixed wake time 7 days a week; widen as sleep consolidates. Compression if frail.
Stimulus controlBed for sleep and sex only; awake and frustrated → get up, dim light, return when sleepy.
Cognitive workTackle catastrophic beliefs about sleep; teach one relaxation method.
DeliveryFace-to-face where available. Sleepio (HTG624) instead of hygiene advice or pills; assess first if OSA possible.

7What success looks likeagree it at the start

  • Better daytime function and less distress, not a set number of hours.
  • Sleep diary and ISI against the baseline.
  • Every hypnotic has a stop date agreed when it is started.
  • Long-term users: lower dose or stopped, with fewer falls and less next-day impairment.

8Before any hypnoticMHRA · SPC

  • Distress or impairment is severe; non-drug measures considered
  • OSA, COPD or respiratory disease assessed
  • No opioids, gabapentinoids or alcohol misuse (respiratory depression)
  • Older or frail: falls risk; lower dose
  • Driving and safety-critical work discussed and recorded
  • Pregnancy, substance misuse history
  • Defined quantity, no automatic repeat

9Drug options: short, gated, with stop datesTA77 · TA922 · SPC

Short-term, severeZ-drug, lowest dose, a few days, rarely beyond 2 weeks. Licence: 4 weeks maximum, including any taper.
Aged 55+Melatonin MR 2 mg (licensed product): short-term primary insomnia, up to 13 weeks; check local formulary status.
Chronic (TA922)Daridorexant if ≥3 nights a week for ≥3 months, considerable daytime impact, and CBT-I tried and failed, unavailable or unsuitable.
Not routineSedating antihistamines, amitriptyline, quetiapine. Mirtazapine only for depression. Benzodiazepines: no advantage over Z-drugs.

10Side effects and fixesSPC · MHRA

Next-day drowsinessNo driving if not fully alert; lower dose or stop
Falls, confusion (older)Stop or taper; compression rather than restriction
Tolerance, wearing offExpected with nightly use: back to CBT-I, not a higher dose
Rebound on reducingSlow the pace; rebound is not relapse
Sleepier in CBT-I week 1–2Expected; extra care with driving

11Deprescribing long-term usersMHRA · SPC

  • An invitation, not an ambush: frame it as protecting memory, falls and driving.
  • Taper the current drug in small, patient-paced steps; slower after years of use. Diazepam switch only in selected patients.
  • CBT-I alongside; agree a plan for out-of-hours requests.

12Review checklistafter CBT-I or any drug, and yearly for long-term users

  • Diary and ISI vs baseline; daytime function
  • Real CBT-I, or only a leaflet? Engagement
  • Causes re-checked: OSA, RLS and iron, mood, nocturia, pain, menopause, thyroid
  • Substances honestly counted; medicine timing
  • Mood and suicidal thoughts asked about directly
  • Next-day impairment, falls, driving
  • Z-drug: stopped at the agreed date, not repeated
  • Melatonin MR: licensed for up to 13 weeks; stop if no benefit
  • Daridorexant: assessed within 3 months; stop if inadequate
  • Long-term hypnotic: taper offered; co-prescribed opioid reviewed

14Never-events and special groupsMHRA DSU Mar 2020 · DVLA

  • Never start a hypnotic with an opioid (or alcohol, gabapentinoids): potentially fatal respiratory depression · repeat hypnotics automatically · stop a long-term benzodiazepine abruptly (seizures, delirium) · let CBT-I delay urgent care for suicidality, mania or withdrawal.
  • Supervise or modify sleep restriction in bipolar disorder, epilepsy, untreated OSA, pregnancy, frailty and safety-critical jobs.
  • Older adults: lower doses, gentler taper; often the biggest gains from stopping.
  • Drivers: next-day impairment and DVLA duties discussed and documented.
