Chronic heart failure CLINIC GUIDE

For suspected new heart failure and the regular review. Adults 18+. Not for acute decompensated heart failure, heart failure in pregnancy, or children.

Page 1 · Diagnose and start suspected HF → NT-proBNP → echo → phenotype → four pillars

!Act todayNICE NG106 · local acute-HF pathway

  • Breathless at rest, pulmonary oedema, hypoxia, hypotension or poor perfusion, new confusion → same-day emergency assessment — not an NT-proBNP and a 6-week echo.
  • Chest pain, syncope, or a fast or very slow irregular pulse with symptoms → 999.
  • Known HF getting worse: severe AKI, K⁺ ≥6.0, or no response to the agreed diuretic plan → same-day assessment; routine titration stops.
  • Aged 40+ with 2 or more unexplained symptoms from cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, weight loss, appetite loss (1 or more if ever smoked) → urgent chest X-ray (NG12) — even when HF seems the obvious explanation.

1Suspect, then measure NT-proBNPNG106

NT-proBNPAction
>2,000 ng/LSpecialist assessment and echo within 2 weeks
400–2,000 ng/LSpecialist assessment and echo within 6 weeks
<400 ng/LHF less likely — look for another cause

Falsely low: obesity, African or African–Caribbean family background, diuretics, ACEi, ARB, ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA. A "normal" result in a breathless obese patient does not exclude HF.

Falsely high: older age, AF, CKD, LVH, pulmonary hypertension, PE, COPD, sepsis. It is a diagnostic test, not a routine serial monitor.

3Work-up and causeNG106

  • 12-lead ECG (AF, old MI, LBBB)
  • Chest X-ray
  • U&E, eGFR, LFTs, TFTs
  • FBC, ferritin + TSAT
  • HbA1c, lipids
  • Urine dip; ACR if CKD likely

Find the driver: ischaemic heart disease, hypertension, valve disease, AF, alcohol, chemotherapy, family history of cardiomyopathy, amyloid (older, LVH, carpal tunnel). Grade NYHA I–IV at every contact.

4HFrEF — all four pillarsNG106 (Sept 2025)

ACE inhibitorramipril 1.25–2.5 mg → 10 mg OD
Beta-blockerHF-licensed: bisoprolol 1.25 → 10 mg OD
MRAspironolactone 25 → 50 mg OD
SGLT2idapagliflozin or empagliflozin 10 mg OD

Offer all four as first-line — the MRA is no longer an add-on kept for EF ≤35%. Start at low doses, then titrate each to the maximum tolerated dose, one change at a time. GPs can start all four.

ACEi not tolerated (not angioedema) → ARNI (sacubitril/valsartan; TA388: EF ≤35%) with the other three; seek HF-specialist advice before starting it; stop the ACEi 36 hours before. ARNI unsuitable → consider an ARB.

ECG before starting a beta-blocker. Start it only when stable and not congested.

5Diuretic — for congestion, not prognosisNG106

Loop diuretic (e.g. furosemide 20–40 mg) relieves breathlessness and oedema. Titrate to the lowest dose that keeps the person dry; recheck U&E after each increase. No blanket dose-doubling rule.

Capable patients: a written self-adjustment plan — the trigger, the dose range, how long, when bloods are due, who to call.

6HFpEF — treat the driversNG106 (Sept 2025)

Consider an MRA and an SGLT2i; diuretic for congestion. The comorbidity list is the treatment list: BP to target, AF rate and anticoagulation, weight loss, diabetes, sleep apnoea, CKD. ACEi, ARB and beta-blocker only for another indication.

7Check before starting each pillarNG106 · SPC · MHRA

ACE inhibitorNa⁺, K⁺, eGFR and BP before; recheck 1–2 weeks after. Pregnancy plans. Angioedema history → no ACEi or ARNI.
Beta-blockerECG and heart rate first; stable and not congested. Warn: more tired for the first week or two — it passes.
MRAK⁺ and eGFR before and after starting. No K⁺ supplements or salt substitutes; warn about breast tenderness.
SGLT2ieGFR against the licence; volume status (review the diuretic); type 1 or previous DKA → specialist. Sick-day rules in writing.

10Before every step-upNG106 · SPC

  • Stable? — not congested, no recent decompensation
  • Bloods — K⁺, sodium, eGFR since the last change
  • BP — symptomatic hypotension? (asymptomatic low BP needs no action)
  • Pulse — slow or irregular → ECG before a beta-blocker step
  • Adherence and side effects; one change at a time
  • Saboteurs — NSAIDs, K⁺ supplements or salt substitutes, trimethoprim

11Titrate to targetintroduce all four early at low dose · titrate one at a time · U&E 1–2 weeks after RAAS changes

Step 1 · start all fourACEi, beta-blocker, MRA and SGLT2i at low doses over a few weeks. Diuretic alongside if congested.
Step 2 · titrateIncrease one drug at a time; beta-blocker in slow steps per the SPC schedule. Review within 2 weeks of each change.
Step 3 · still symptomaticOn maximum tolerated doses of all four → consider switching the ACEi to an ARNI (HF-specialist advice; 36 h washout).
Step 4 · specialistIvabradine (sinus rhythm, HR ≥75, EF ≤35%), CRT/ICD, IV iron, digoxin, advanced HF and palliative care.

13Review checklistat least 6-monthly · NG106 · QS9

  • Functional capacity: NYHA class, symptoms, exercise tolerance
  • Fluid status: weight trend, oedema, JVP, chest
  • Rhythm: pulse; ECG if irregular (AF → stroke risk)
  • U&E, K⁺, eGFR; FBC; ferritin + TSAT
  • Medicines: all four on? at target? adherence, side effects
  • Cognition, nutrition, mood; smoking and alcohol
  • Cardiac rehab done? Vaccines up to date?
  • Written plan, weight trigger, sick-day rules
  • Future care: devices discussed? advance care planning started?

15Red flags and never-eventsNG106 · MHRA · SPC

  • Decompensating but well (weight up, ankles, orthopnoea; SpO₂ fine): increase the loop diuretic per the agreed plan, recheck U&E, review within days; keep the beta-blocker going.
  • AF found: rate control with the beta-blocker; stroke and bleeding risk on the AF pathway; rhythm strategy is a specialist decision.
  • Sick days: pause the SGLT2i; review ACEi, MRA and diuretic by BP, fluid and bloods.
  • Never: ACEi + ARNI together · ACEi + ARB + MRA together · verapamil or diltiazem in HFrEF · NSAIDs · stop the pillars because the EF has improved.
