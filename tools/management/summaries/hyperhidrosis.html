Hyperhidrosis

For: adults with excessive sweating, primary focal or secondary. Not for: sweating with menopausal hot flushes (use the HRT protocol); the suspected cancer routes below are the adult NG12 criteria.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
Int. Hyperhidrosis SocietyNICE TA1175NICE IPG487NICE NG12BNF · GM formulary
Core rule: ask the sleep question — young-onset, bilateral, focal sweating that stops in sleep is primary and needs no tests; generalised, unilateral, nocturnal, new after 25 or changed sweating needs a cause found, and an old “primary” label never covers new night sweats.

0Act now — secondary and serious causes first

  • Serotonergic medicine + sweating with clonus, hyperreflexia, rigidity, agitation, altered mental state or hyperthermia (serotonin syndrome) → 999
  • Severe hypertension with headache, palpitations, sweating, chest pain, neurological symptoms or collapse (phaeochromocytoma crisis) → 999 do not wait for metanephrines.
  • Thyrotoxicosis (grossly raised T3/T4) with cardiac instability → 999
  • Unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly, particularly with fever, drenching night sweats, pruritus, breathlessness or weight loss → consider Suspected cancer pathway (lymphoma). Tests must not delay it; night sweats alone are not a criterion.
  • Pallor, persistent fatigue, unexplained fever, recurrent infection, generalised nodes, unexplained bruising, bleeding or petechiae → very urgent FBC Within 48 h (leukaemia).
  • Aged 40+ with 2 or more unexplained cough, weight loss, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain or appetite loss (1 or more if ever smoked) → Urgent chest X-ray; haemoptysis at 40+ → Suspected cancer pathway. Suspected TB → discuss with the local TB/respiratory service.

1Confirm — the history makes the diagnosis

Primary focal: visible excessive focal sweating for ≥6 months, no secondary cause, plus ≥2 of: bilateral and symmetrical · impairs daily activities · at least weekly · onset <25 · family history · stops during sleep.

2Check — classic primary disease needs NO tests

☐ Drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, opioids, tamoxifen, bupropion, cholinesterase inhibitors; changes or withdrawal), alcohol  ☐ Temperature, pulse, BP, weight  ☐ Thyroid, nodes, spleen  ☐ Skin (erythrasma, intertrigo, fungal)  ☐ Neuro screen if unilateral

Tests only on suspicion (TSH, FBC, HbA1c, LFTs, CXR). Stereotyped episodes of headache, palpitations, sweating and hypertension → metanephrines per local endocrine guidance.

3Decide

  • Secondary → treat the cause, not the sweat. Drug-induced → reduce or switch. Hot flushes → menopause pathway.
  • Primary → a recognised, treatable condition, not hygiene; treat by site, severity and local pathway.
  • Self-care: antiperspirant not deodorant, natural fibres or dark clothes, cut personal triggers; NHS Talking Therapies self-referral; work adjustments.

5Treat — least invasive first; none curative

1 · All sitesAluminium chloride 20%: bone-dry skin at night, wash off in the morning; start 2 nights a week. GM: self-care.
2 · Palms / solesRefer for iontophoresis. Not in pregnancy or with a pacemaker.
3 · Axillae, severeBotulinum toxin (specialist), oral antimuscarinic, or glycopyrronium cream when both fail or are unsuitable (adults; TA1175).
Last resortSympathectomy: irreversible; refractory palmar or craniofacial only.
  • Dermatitis: pause, consider a short mild topical steroid, restart less often.
  • Oral antimuscarinics: start low, experienced clinician or local protocol; not in angle-closure glaucoma, retention, GI obstruction, myasthenia. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Re-score the HDSS at every step; partial response → fix technique first. Antimuscarinic: tolerability, anticholinergic burden.
  • Annually: still needed? Re-screen the drug list. Any new nocturnal or constitutional symptom → re-run the secondary work-up.

7Refer

  • Dermatology: severe axillary disease despite topical treatment (record HDSS and impact); palmoplantar or refractory disease.
  • Endocrine Urgent: positive phaeo tests, suspected carcinoid, uncontrolled thyrotoxicosis.
  • Neurology: unilateral sweating, compensatory sweating, autonomic neuropathy.

8Never

  • Let an old “primary” label explain new night sweats or a changed pattern.
  • Treat the sweat in secondary disease without looking for the cause.
  • Delay a suspected cancer referral for FBC or other tests.
  • An oral antimuscarinic without heat-illness counselling.
  • Sympathectomy before non-surgical options have failed.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 On a serotonergic medicine: “Sweating with twitching, stiffness, agitation, confusion or a high temperature — call 999.”   Within a week “Drenching night sweats with weight loss, fevers or lumps — contact us to be seen within a week, and we'll investigate.”   Same-day Tablets: “Take care in hot weather and heatwaves; stop them and contact us the same day if you have trouble passing urine, blurred vision, confusion or severe constipation.”   Antiperspirant: “If it stings, use it less often with a moisturiser — don't just stop.”
