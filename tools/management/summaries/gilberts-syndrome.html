Gilbert's syndrome (adults)

For: adults aged 18 and over with an isolated raised bilirubin. Not for: neonates, infants, children or young people (different differential — discuss or refer). Abnormal liver enzymes or conjugated jaundice → abnormal LFTs or jaundice pathway.

Core rule: Gilbert's is a pattern, not one result — persistent isolated unconjugated bilirubin, normal liver enzymes, no haemolysis, otherwise well; never use it to explain new jaundice, abnormal LFTs or symptoms.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Unsafe presentation: sepsis or cholangitis, confusion or encephalopathy, bleeding or markedly prolonged INR, severe abdominal pain, rapidly progressive jaundice, significant vomiting or dehydration → Same-day assessment — do not wait to confirm a Gilbert's pattern.
  • Clinical jaundice at 40 and over → Suspected cancer pathway (pancreatic, NG12), even if the biochemistry looks benign.
  • Prolonged INR → Urgent discussion. Check INR urgently if unwell, significant jaundice or concern about liver function.

1Confirm — the typical pattern, against previous results

FeatureExpected in Gilbert's
BilirubinPersistent, isolated, predominantly unconjugated; usually mild
ALT/AST, ALP, GGTNormal
HaemolysisNone (FBC normal)
PatientOtherwise well; stable or non-progressive on previous results

A single result is not enough. No bilirubin cut-off confirms or excludes it; unconjugated >40 µmol/L → consider rarer causes (e.g. Crigler–Najjar). Genetic testing and imaging rarely needed.

2Check — no fasting provocation test

☐ Repeat LFTs with split bilirubin + albumin  ☐ FBC  ☐ Previous results  ☐ Medicines, OTC, herbal, recreational, alcohol

Reticulocytes, film, LDH, haptoglobin (± DAT) only if anaemia, rising bilirubin, suspected haemolysis or uncertainty. INR and ultrasound not routine in a classic, incidental case. Use usual lab sampling conditions.

3Decide — not Gilbert's if…

  • Conjugated or mixed bilirubin, or bilirubinuria → hepatobiliary work-up; image by the pattern.
  • Abnormal ALT/AST, ALP or GGT, low albumin → investigate as abnormal LFTs.
  • Anaemia, high reticulocytes, raised LDH, low haptoglobin → investigate haemolysis; DAT if autoimmune suspected.
  • Substantially elevated, rising or atypical bilirubin → reassess; discuss with clinical biochemistry or hepatology.
  • Began after a new medicine → assess for drug-induced liver injury.

5Treat — no treatment: reassure, advise, record

  • Reassure: benign and lifelong; no chronic liver disease, normal life expectancy. No treatment or special diet.
  • Triggers (fasting, illness, dehydration, stress, poor sleep, strenuous exercise) make it more noticeable → normal meals and hydration; avoid prolonged fasting.
  • Record as a problem-list diagnosis, not an allergy; add an oncology prescribing alert where the record supports it.
  • Irinotecan: document it and tell oncology; the oncologist decides on UGT1A1 testing and dose. Not a GP reason to prescribe, withhold or dose-adjust.
  • Atazanavir raises unconjugated bilirubin → read with ALT, ALP, GGT; don't stop antiretrovirals without the HIV team.
  • Fatigue is not a typical consequence → investigate separately. Pregnancy: don't assume jaundice is Gilbert's.

6Monitor

  • Once a typical diagnosis is established: no routine bilirubin monitoring or follow-up.
  • Repeat bilirubin only if uncertain, rising or atypical.
  • Future isolated unconjugated rise with normal LFTs is expected; new abnormalities, rising bilirubin or new symptoms → reassess.

7Refer — route by the abnormality

  • Conjugated or mixed; abnormal enzymes; low albumin; haemolysis; substantially elevated or progressive bilirubin; hepatosplenomegaly; weight loss, pain, pruritus or systemic illness; diagnostic uncertainty; under 18.
  • Possible other inherited disorder, or genetic testing → clinical biochemistry or hepatology. Follow local pathways.

8Never

  • Diagnose Gilbert's from a single isolated bilirubin.
  • Attribute conjugated or mixed bilirubin, bilirubinuria, abnormal liver enzymes or haemolysis to Gilbert's.
  • Use presumed Gilbert's to defer assessment of clinical jaundice at 40 and over.
  • A deliberate fasting or starvation-provocation test.
  • Record it as an allergy, or prescribe, withhold or dose-adjust irinotecan because of it.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “Contact us the same day (or NHS 111 if we're closed) if your skin or eyes become more yellow, your urine turns dark, your stools go pale, you start itching, or you have tummy pain, weight loss or feel unwell.”   Future tests: “The same result on its own is expected — new symptoms or other abnormal results still need checking.”   Chemotherapy: “If you're ever offered chemotherapy, make sure the team knows you have Gilbert's.”
