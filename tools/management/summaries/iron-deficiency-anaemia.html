Iron deficiency anaemia in adults

For: adults in primary care with iron deficiency, with or without anaemia. Not for: pregnancy (follow local maternity guidance), children (paediatric guidance), CKD/dialysis (NG203), known IBD or bariatric surgery (specialist/local pathways), haematological disease (haematology advice).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BSG 2021ACPGBI/BSG FIT in IDA 2025NICE NG12 (updated Apr 2026)NICE HTG690BSH 2022
Core rule: iron deficiency is a finding, never a diagnosis — replace iron and find the source together; IDA in any man or post-menopausal woman is GI loss until proven otherwise.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Haemodynamic compromise, suspected active GI bleed, severe symptomatic anaemia (chest pain, syncope, severe breathlessness) or very rapid Hb fall → Same-day hospital assessment; judgement, not an Hb cut-off.
  • IDA at any age → FIT. FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g or rectal mass (don't wait for FIT) → Suspected cancer pathway (colorectal). FIT <10 does not exclude cancer — persisting concern or no sample → still refer.
  • Unexplained IDA, any man or post-menopausal woman → secondary care even if FIT negative: bidirectional endoscopy or Urgent non-cancer pathway (local IDA pathway).
  • Dysphagia, or 55+ with weight loss and upper abdominal pain, reflux or dyspepsia → Suspected cancer pathway (upper GI).
  • Visible haematuria 45+ (unexplained), or non-visible with dysuria or raised white cells 60+ → Suspected cancer pathway (urology); investigate GI too.

1Confirm — ferritin in context

Anaemia: Hb <130 g/L men, <120 g/L women; usually MCV <80 fL, but a normal MCV does not exclude IDA.

FerritinMeaning → action
<15 µg/LHighly likely → treat and investigate
15–30 µg/LLikely → treat and investigate
30–100 µg/LRead with CRP; if inflamed consider TSAT or reticulocyte Hb
>100 / >150Functional deficiency possible / absolute less likely

Normal ferritin does not exclude deficiency in inflammation, infection, malignancy, liver disease, CKD or obesity.

2Check — first-line work-up

☐ FBC, ferritin ± CRP  ☐ Anti-tTG IgA + total IgA (on gluten)  ☐ Urinalysis  ☐ FIT  ☐ Periods, GI symptoms, NSAIDs/anticoagulants/PPI, diet, blood donation, family history of GI or endometrial cancer

Don't miss: thalassaemia trait — microcytosis with normal ferritin.

3Decide — who the patient is sets the hunt

  • Man or post-menopausal woman → GI loss until proven otherwise.
  • Pre-menopausal woman → usually menstrual or dietary; still screen GI symptoms.
  • Malabsorption → coeliac, PPI, H. pylori, gastric or bariatric surgery.
  • Low ferritin, normal Hb → find the cause; treat if symptomatic.
  • Menorrhagia → treat the bleeding (tranexamic acid, LNG-IUS). Diet: no tea or coffee with meals.

4Target

2–4 weeksHb rising 10–20 g/L per month

5Treat — replace and investigate together

Step 1 · oralOne ferrous salt tablet once daily (e.g. ferrous sulfate 200 mg); not TDS or modified-release.
Step 2 · intolerantAlternate days, lower dose, other preparation or with food.
Step 3 · 2–4 weeksPoor rise → reassess, never escalate: adherence, loss, absorption, wrong diagnosis.
Step 4 · IV ironConsider if oral fails, not tolerated or absorbed, or rapid correction needed.
  • Don't delay iron for tests, unless it may interfere with imminent colonoscopy prep.
  • IV iron: dose per SPC/local protocol; resuscitation facilities; monitor ≥30 min. Ferric carboxymaltose → phosphate if repeated or at risk.
  • Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • FBC 2–4 weeks; poor response → anti-tTG if not done.
  • Hb at 3 months → confirm cause found, every investigation actioned.
  • Ferritin selectively, never more than monthly. Periodic FBC if cause ongoing.
  • Second episode, no clear benign cause → GI tests, even if previously negative.

7Refer — routine; keep iron going

  • Gastro: positive anti-tTG; negative bidirectional endoscopy; suspected IBD; refractory or recurrent.
  • Haematology: thalassaemia; refractory despite adherence; suspected haematological malignancy.
  • Gynaecology: menorrhagia not controlled (LNG-IUS first-line); fibroids or polyps.

8Never

  • Replace iron without finding the source, or stop when the Hb normalises — stores are still empty.
  • Defer endoscopy because the patient is on iron or anticoagulated.
  • Let a FIT <10 override clinical concern, or end investigation in a man or post-menopausal woman.
  • Give iron for thalassaemia trait (microcytosis, normal ferritin) unless there is concurrent deficiency.
  • Re-prescribe iron without investigating, or blame rectal bleeding on haemorrhoids untested.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “Black or bloody stools, vomiting blood, difficulty swallowing, worsening abdominal pain, significant weight loss, or worsening breathlessness or chest pain — contact us the same day.”   Iron tablets: “They turn your stools black — that is the iron. Tarry, sticky, strong-smelling black stools are different: same-day contact.”   Duration: “Carry on about three more months after your blood count is normal.”   Tests: “The tablets fix the level; the tests tell us why. Please go ahead with the camera test even if you feel better.”
