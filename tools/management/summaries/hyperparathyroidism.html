Primary hyperparathyroidism

For: adults with raised albumin-adjusted calcium and suspected or confirmed PHPT in primary care. Not for: children (paediatric or endocrine advice), pregnancy (urgent specialist advice), suppressed PTH (hypercalcaemia pathway), tertiary disease in CKD (renal team).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG132 (2019)Society for Endocrinology 2016NICE NG12SPCs
Core rule: the calcium and the patient decide how fast; the PTH decides where — and a suppressed PTH is not PHPT.

0Act now

  • Calcium above 3.5 mmol/L, or confusion, drowsiness or arrhythmia at any level → 999 / emergency
  • Calcium 3.0–3.5 mmol/L, vomiting, AKI, polyuria with poor intake → Same-day assessment or specialist discussion.
  • Suppressed PTH → not PHPT: think malignancy or myeloma; suspected cancer pathway if NG12 criteria are met.
  • Very high calcium and PTH, neck lump or hoarseness (carcinoma), or pregnancy → Urgent endocrine

1Confirm

Albumin-adjusted calcium; no fasting; no ionised calcium. Measure PTH with a same-time calcium when:

Calcium (twice)Measure PTH
2.6 mmol/L or aboveYes
2.5 mmol/L or aboveIf PHPT suspected

Raised or inappropriately normal PTH = PTH-mediated. Specialist advice if PTH is above the midpoint with suspected PHPT.

2Check

☐ Urine calcium (exclude FHH; clearance ratio below 0.01 = clue only) ☐ 25-hydroxyvitamin D ☐ U&E, eGFR ☐ thiazides, lithium, supplements ☐ family history, age under 40

Then: DXA (spine, hip, distal radius) + renal tract ultrasound.

3Decide — refer for surgery

  • Refer if symptoms, renal stones, fragility fracture or osteoporosis, or calcium 2.85 mmol/L or above.
  • Consider referral for everyone else with confirmed PHPT.
  • Planning pregnancy: offer surgery.

5Treat

EveryoneHydration; normal dietary calcium; correct vitamin D deficiency
MedicinesReview thiazides; never stop lithium without the prescriber
Fracture riskConsider a bisphosphonate
SpecialistCinacalcet: 2.85 with symptoms, or 3.0 mmol/L or above, if no surgery
  • Doses per BNF and local formulary. Cinacalcet does not improve bone density.

6Monitor — not operated

  • Calcium and eGFR: at least yearly.
  • DXA: every 2–3 years. Renal ultrasound: if a stone is suspected.
  • New feature → revisit surgical referral.

7Refer

  • Endocrinology: FHH uncertainty, genetic features, discordant results.
  • Lithium-associated: prescriber + endocrine.

8Never

  • Leave a calcium above 3.5 mmol/L at home because the patient looks well.
  • Call a suppressed PTH primary hyperparathyroidism.
  • Decide on surgery before excluding FHH and correcting vitamin D.
  • Advise a low-calcium diet.
  • Stop lithium without the prescriber.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you become very confused, very drowsy or your heart races irregularly.”   Same-day “Contact us the same day if you can't keep fluids down, are very thirsty and passing lots of urine, or get severe pain in your side.”   Everyday: “Drink plenty, especially in hot weather or with a stomach bug.”
