Whooping cough (pertussis)

For: suspected or confirmed pertussis at any age in primary care, and their household. Not for: contact chemoprophylaxis decisions (the health protection team, HPT, leads) or infants with red flags (paediatrics). Aged 40+ with an unexplained cough → NG12 (block 2).

Core rule: notify on suspicion, ask “who lives with you?” (a baby under 1 or a pregnant woman makes it urgent), and send any infant with apnoea or cyanosis by 999.

0Act now — young babies die of this; the whoop may never appear

  • Infant with apnoea, cyanosis, bradycardia, choking spasms, severe breathing difficulty or seizures → 999
  • Infant with poor feeding, weight loss or exhaustion → Same-day paediatrics  Any suspected pertussis under 3 months, even if well between bouts → Urgent paediatric discussion; low threshold under 6 months.
  • Seizures or encephalopathy at any age → 999  Hypoxia, dehydration from vomiting, suspected pneumothorax → Same-day admission
  • Suspected pneumonia (fever returning, tachypnoea, focal signs) → Same-day assessment; admit if hypoxic.  Priority-group contact (block 4) or a cluster → Phone the HPT now
  • Newborn exposed to maternal pertussis around delivery → Urgent HPT / paediatric advice on chemoprophylaxis, whatever the maternal vaccination.

1Confirm — suspect before day 14

UKHSA case definition: cough ≥14 days, no other cause, + paroxysms, post-tussive vomiting, whoop or infant apnoea; or contact with a case in the last 21 days. Vaccinated people: often just a paroxysmal cough. Typical features → act before day 14.

2Check — every case

☐ Household: baby under 1, pregnancy, works with infants  ☐ Pregnant?  ☐ Vaccines  ☐ Rib fracture, syncope, hernia, pneumothorax

Don't miss: 40+, unexplained cough + 1 more unexplained symptom, or ever smoked → offer chest X-ray ≤2 weeks (NG12). Unexplained haemoptysis → suspected cancer pathway.

3Decide — notify, then treat inside the window

  • Notify on suspicion (statutory), via the local UKHSA HPT: in writing within 3 days; phone immediately if urgent.
  • Inside the current UKHSA window → consider a macrolide; pregnant or priority group 1 contact, onset ≤21 days → treat.
  • Beyond 21 days → benefit usually limited; follow HPT. Antibiotics mainly stop spread.
  • Contacts: HPT decides chemoprophylaxis; not routinely for classes or workplaces.

4Priority groups — check current HPT definitions

GroupWho
1Under 2 months: born <32 weeks, or mother not vaccinated in pregnancy (after 16 weeks, ≥2 weeks before delivery). 2 months to under 1 year with <3 doses
2Pregnant from 32 weeks; works with young infants or pregnant women; lives with an infant not fully vaccinated

5Treat — drug by age and pregnancy (UKHSA)

Under 1 monthClarithromycin
1 month+ (not pregnant)Azithromycin or clarithromycin
PregnancyErythromycin, ideally starting ≥3 days before delivery
Macrolide unsuitableCo-trimoxazole — not in pregnancy or under 6 weeks
  • Check first: QT and interacting drugs (e.g. statins), renal and hepatic function. Infants: warn about projectile vomiting.
  • Dose and duration per UKHSA table — doses per BNF/BNFC and local formulary.
  • Everyone: fluids, small frequent feeds, rest, paracetamol or ibuprofen. Cough medicines do not help.

6Exclude and follow up

  • Nursery, school, work: until 48 hours after starting an antibiotic, or 21 days from cough onset if untreated (UKHSA exclusion table).
  • Healthcare and childcare staff → HPT and occupational-health advice. Avoid newborns and late pregnancy for the same period.
  • Cough lasts up to 2–3 months. After 21 days, reassess if worse, pneumonia or a vulnerable contact.

7Prevent — vaccinate as well as treat

Every pregnancy: offer at 16–32 weeks, ideally ~20; not before 16; after 32 still offer up to labour.  Infants: 6-in-1 at 8, 12, 16 weeks, then per UKHSA schedule; catch up.  Health workers with pregnant women or young infants, none in 5 years → offer.

8Never

  • Wait for a swab or antibody result before notifying or treating.
  • Send home an infant under 3 months with suspected pertussis without paediatric discussion, even if well between bouts.
  • Let a “100-day cough” label skip NG12 in someone 40+ with an unexplained cough.
  • Co-trimoxazole in pregnancy or under 6 weeks.
  • Over-the-counter cough and cold medicines for children under 6.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if a baby has pauses in breathing, turns blue or grey, or is struggling to breathe, or if anyone has a fit or is hard to wake.”   Same-day “Contact us the same day if a baby under 6 months has coughing bouts, a fever comes back, breathing becomes fast or painful, or you can’t keep fluids down.”   Expectations: “The cough can last two or three months; the antibiotic stops you passing it on. Stay off until 48 hours after starting it, and keep away from newborns and anyone heavily pregnant.”
