Urticaria

For: adults and children with acute, chronic spontaneous or inducible urticaria in primary care. Not for: full anaphylaxis treatment and observation (use the anaphylaxis protocol) or angioedema without wheals as the main problem (use the angioedema protocol); omalizumab and immunosuppression are specialist-only.

Core rule: airway, breathing or circulation compromise means IM adrenaline and 999, never an antihistamine first; angioedema without wheals means think ACE inhibitor or HAE; and a single wheal lasting over 24 hours means think urticarial vasculitis.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected anaphylaxis (acute airway, breathing or circulation compromise; urticaria alone is not anaphylaxis) → IM adrenaline 500 micrograms (adults, children over 12), thigh; lie flat, legs raised; repeat after 5 minutes if ABC problems persist → 999
  • Angioedema of tongue, lip or larynx without wheals → ACE inhibitor or HAE: antihistamines and steroids generally ineffective; treat as anaphylaxis if not excluded; airway involved → 999. ACE inhibitor (any duration) → stop permanently.
  • Sudden or rapidly progressive swelling of the lips, mouth or tongue → 999
  • Wheal over 24 hours in one place, painful, purpuric or bruising, or joint pain, fever, abdominal pain → possible urticarial vasculitis: urinalysis, BP, FBC, renal function, LFTs, ESR/CRP → Same-day GP assessment, then dermatology.
  • Urticaria with unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly (fever, weight loss, night sweats) → consider lymphoma referral; FBC, LDH → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Child: urticaria + joint swelling, haematuria, abdominal pain (HSP) → Same-day paediatric assessment

2Check — history does the work; investigate minimally

☐ Duration per wheal  ☐ Drugs incl. OTC ibuprofen, ACE inhibitor  ☐ Angioedema, HAE family history  ☐ Systemic symptoms, nodes, joints  ☐ Chronic: FBC, ESR/CRP, TFT + anti-TPO only

Don't miss: angioedema without wheals → C4 (normal C4 doesn't exclude HAE). IgE testing only for a convincing, reproducible history.

3Decide — by type

  • Acute: regular antihistamine; new drug in last 6 weeks → stop it. Chronic: regular, not as needed; UAS7.
  • Inducible: avoidance + antihistamine; cold → never swim alone in cold water. NSAID urticaria: avoid non-selective NSAIDs.
  • Pregnancy: loratadine or cetirizine; breastfeeding → loratadine.

5Treat — one agent, taken regularly

Step 1Non-sedating H1, licensed dose: cetirizine 10 mg, loratadine 10 mg or fexofenadine 180 mg OD
Step 2 · after 2–4 weeksSame agent up to 4× (off-label; document, explain), e.g. cetirizine 10 mg QDS
Step 3Consider adding montelukast (off-label; MHRA neuropsychiatric warning) and refer
SpecialistOmalizumab (TA339: 12+, UAS7 ≥28, failed H1 + LTRA); then ciclosporin
  • Don't combine H1 antihistamines; no regular chlorphenamine. Severe flare: a short oral steroid course may be considered. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Acute: review at 6 weeks if not resolved (then it is chronic).
  • Chronic: every 2–4 weeks while titrating (UAS7), then 3-monthly; annual review incl. DLQI, TFT.
  • Urticarial vasculitis: urinalysis, BP, renal function, CRP/ESR each review.

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent ones in block 0)

  • After suspected anaphylaxis or throat swelling → allergy service (urgency by severity and local pathway); two auto-injectors, training, written plan.
  • Not controlled on 4× antihistamine → dermatology Routine
  • Suspected HAE → immunology. Urticarial vasculitis → dermatology; systemic → rheumatology.

8Never

  • An antihistamine as the first treatment for anaphylaxis, or a delay to adrenaline.
  • Rechallenge or switch to another ACE inhibitor after angioedema — record “avoid all ACE inhibitors permanently”.
  • Repeated or maintenance oral steroids for chronic urticaria.
  • Broad allergy or food panels in chronic urticaria without clinical pointers.
  • Call a wheal lasting over 24 hours, painful or bruising, simple urticaria.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden swelling of the lips, mouth, tongue or throat, difficulty breathing or swallowing, or feeling faint — call 999 and use your adrenaline pen if you have one.”   Pen: “Call 999 whenever you use it, even if you feel better; no better after 5 minutes — use the second pen.”   ‘-pril’ tablet: “Face or lips swell — stop it and contact us the same day; tongue, mouth or throat swells — call 999.”   Same-day “Spots that last more than a day, hurt or bruise — contact us the same day.”   Chronic: “Take the antihistamine every day, not just when it flares.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
