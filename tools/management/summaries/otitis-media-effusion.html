Otitis media with effusion (glue ear)

For: children under 12 with suspected or confirmed glue ear in primary care. Not for: acute otitis media or ear pain (→ ear pain and otitis protocol, NICE NG91), or adults — a persistent one-sided effusion in an adult → adult ENT pathway.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG233 (2023)NG233 decision tableNICE NG91 (2018)BNFC
Core rule: fluid without infection — no antibiotics, no steroids, nothing from the pharmacy shelf; exclude the red flags and special groups, confirm with formal audiology, observe actively for 3 months, then decide with the family.

0Act now — check before any observation

  • Acutely unwell, ear pain and fever with post-auricular swelling, tenderness or a pushed-forward pinna (?mastoiditis) → Same-day paediatric or ENT assessment.
  • Foul or persistent discharge, attic crust or retraction pocket (?cholesteatoma, chronic suppurative otitis media) → Urgent ENT — do not observe.
  • Down's syndrome, cleft palate or other craniofacial anomaly → formal audiology + individualised specialist plan (cleft, ENT or paediatric service) — not routine observation alone. Learning disability → individualised plan, not automatically surgery.
  • Loss that does not fit an effusion (sensorineural, asymmetric, progressive, non-organic, or persists after the fluid clears) → audiology and ENT.

1Confirm — the child may never mention their ears

Common featuresAssociated features
Hearing difficulty, ear discomfort, tinnitus, delayed speech and languageBehaviour, poor attention, clumsiness, balance problems, poor educational progress

Concern from a parent, teacher or health visitor is a valid trigger. Often bilateral and fluctuating. A dull or retracted drum alone is not definitive → refer to the local paediatric audiology pathway (age-appropriate hearing test + tympanometry).

2Check — every child

☐ Otoscopy where feasible, documented  ☐ Development and upper respiratory health  ☐ Laterality, hearing level, impact on speech, behaviour, school  ☐ Special groups  ☐ Passive smoke exposure

Don't miss: acute otitis media (pain/fever + bulging drum) — a red or dull drum alone is not; wax; otitis externa; co-existing sensorineural or permanent loss.

3Decide — by hearing status (NICE NG233)

  • No hearing loss → no treatment; explain why, and safety-net.
  • Bilateral + hearing loss → active observation, reassess hearing after 3 months.
  • Unilateral + hearing loss → consider reassessing at 3 months; persistent loss affecting daily life → same options discussion.
  • Significant impact on communication, speech, behaviour, education or daily life → consider intervening before 3 months.
  • Support throughout: face the child, cut background noise, visual aids, tell school (seat near the front); consider auto-inflation if the child can cooperate.

5Treat — persistent loss: the NG233 decision table, a shared decision

MonitorMonitoring and support, if the family prefers to avoid devices or surgery
Auto-inflationConsider if the child can cooperate
DevicesConsider air-conduction hearing aids (best if hearing not fluctuating) or bone-conduction devices (fluctuating hearing, or aids unsuitable)
GrommetsConsider for OME-related hearing loss (not fluid alone) ± adjuvant adenoidectomy unless a palate abnormality — ENT
  • Discuss options relevant to hearing level, laterality, developmental needs, family preference and local availability.
  • Post-grommet otorrhoea: consider non-ototoxic topical drops (e.g. ciprofloxacin) for 5–7 days; avoid oral antibiotics unless systemic illness or another indication. Doses per BNFC and local formulary.

6Monitor — review points

  • 3 months: re-test hearing; review speech, behaviour, education and daily life.
  • After grommets: keep the ear dry for 2 weeks; hearing test at 6 weeks — resolved → discharge, persisting → investigate.
  • Recurrent otorrhoea → advise ear plugs or headbands in water. Not recovering → reconsider sensorineural or permanent loss.
  • Later: consider a 1-year hearing test if recurrence could be missed; higher-risk groups → individualised plan.

7Refer — urgent ones are in block 0

  • Suspected OME → local paediatric audiology pathway (Bolton routes where local).
  • ENT / audiology: persistent loss after 3 months, significant developmental or school impact, or diagnostic uncertainty.

8Never

  • Antibiotics, oral or nasal steroids, decongestants, antihistamines, leukotriene antagonists, mucolytics or PPIs to treat OME.
  • Antibiotics for a red or dull drum in a well child with an OME picture.
  • Routine observation alone for Down's syndrome, cleft palate or craniofacial anomalies.
  • Observe a foul discharge, attic crust or retraction pocket.
  • Present grommets as automatic, or dismiss one-sided loss because the other ear hears.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “If she gets ear pain with a fever, swelling or redness behind the ear, or a smelly or ongoing discharge from the ear, get her seen the same day.”   Plan: “It's fluid, not infection — antibiotics and decongestants don't help. Most clears by itself; we'll re-test hearing in three months, and grommets are only one option.”   Recurrence: “Glue ear can come back — book a review if her hearing, speech, behaviour or school progress gets worse, or sooner if it's really holding her back.”
