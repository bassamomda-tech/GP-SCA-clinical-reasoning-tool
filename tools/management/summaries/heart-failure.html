Chronic heart failure in adults

For: adults with suspected or confirmed chronic heart failure. Not for: decompensation (block 0 → local acute-HF pathway) or children. AF with HF → also the AF protocol; end of life → palliative protocols.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG106 (Sept 2025 update)QS9TA267NG12ESC 2023
Core rule: in HFrEF start all four pillars low and titrate to target; fix each obstacle (often by cutting the diuretic) rather than stopping a pillar.

0Act now — check first, every time; NYHA-grade at every contact

  • Chest pain or syncope → 999. Severe breathlessness, pulmonary oedema, hypoxia below baseline, hypotension or hypoperfusion, new confusion, significant AKI, severe hyperkalaemia, severe arrhythmia, or failed diuretic plan → Same-day emergency assessment (local acute-HF pathway) — not a 6-week echo; titration stops.
  • K⁺ ≥6.0 → Urgent assessment: repeat, ECG, local hyperkalaemia protocol; hold ACEi/ARB/ARNI/MRA on acute advice.
  • Decompensating but walking, talking, SpO₂ OK (weight up, orthopnoea, oedema) → raise loop diuretic per the individualised plan or HF-nurse advice, U&E in 3–5 days, keep the beta-blocker → Review at 48 h; worse → same day.

1Confirm — NT-proBNP sets the echo clock; EF sets the treatment

NT-proBNPMeaningAction
>2000 ng/LHigh riskSpecialist + echo ≤2 weeks
400–2000 ng/LPossible HFSpecialist + echo within 6 weeks
<400 ng/LHF unlikelyLook elsewhere — beware false-lows

False-low (“normal” does not exclude HF): obesity, African or African-Caribbean background, diuretics, ACEi, ARB, ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA. False-high: age, AF, CKD, LVH, pulmonary hypertension, sepsis, PE, COPD.

2Check — baseline at diagnosis

☐ ECG, CXR  ☐ U&E, FBC, LFT, TFT, HbA1c, lipids  ☐ Ferritin + TSAT  ☐ Urinalysis (ACR if CKD possible)  ☐ Cause: IHD, BP, valve, AF, alcohol, chemotherapy, family history, amyloid

Don't miss (NG12): aged 40+, ≥2 unexplained of cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, weight or appetite loss (≥1 if ever smoked) → offer CXR ≤2 weeks, even if “HF”.

3Decide — split the phenotype

  • HFrEF (LVEF ≤40%) → offer all four pillars first-line.
  • HFmrEF (41–49%) → consider the same four (ACEi or ARB); ask the HF team if uncertain.
  • HFimpEF (was ≤40%, now higher) → continue all prognostic treatment.
  • HFpEF (≥50% + objective abnormality) → consider an MRA and an SGLT2i + diuretic; treat BP, AF, obesity, diabetes, OSA, CKD.
  • Everyone: daily weights, cardiac rehab if stable, no routine fluid restriction, minimal alcohol, vaccines, screen mood, written plan.

4Target

WhatGoal
Each pillarTarget or maximally tolerated dose — not just started
Loop diureticLowest dose that keeps them decongested
Creatinine, ACEi/ARB/ARNIRise ≤30% (eGFR fall ≤25%) OK if stable, other causes excluded

5Treat HFrEF — all four low within weeks; titrate 2–4-weekly

Pillar 1 · ACEiRamipril 1.25–2.5 → 10 mg (cough → ARB: candesartan 4–8 → 32 mg)
Pillar 2 · Beta-blockerBisoprolol 1.25 → 10 mg; only when stable, not congested; double no sooner than 2-weekly
Pillar 3 · MRASpironolactone 25 → 50 mg (eplerenone if gynaecomastia or post-MI)
Pillar 4 · SGLT2iDapagliflozin or empagliflozin 10 mg OD; check eGFR vs licence; avoid in type 1 diabetes or prior DKA unless specialist-directed
  • Symptomatic hypotension: reduce diuretic first if euvolaemic, stagger doses, drop nitrates/alpha-blockers/CCBs. Asymptomatic → no action.
  • HR <50: ECG, reduce beta-blocker last. K⁺ 5.5–5.9: stop K⁺-raisers, recheck; MRA per SPC. CKD → slower, not none.
  • Diuretic is not a pillar: furosemide 20–40 mg; no blanket dose-doubling. Still symptomatic on all four → consider ACEi → ARNI (36 h washout; never after ACEi angioedema). Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • U&E + K⁺: before and 1–2 weeks after every RAAS or MRA start or rise; then 3–6-monthly.
  • Review ≤2 weeks after any change, then ≥6-monthly: doses, adherence, rehab, devices, care plan.
  • Iron: symptomatic HFrEF, ferritin <100 µg/L or TSAT <20% and Hb <150 g/L → consider IV iron (not oral); find the cause.

7Refer — HF team / cardiology

  • Every new diagnosis: specialist confirmation; HF nurse from day one.
  • Escalation: symptomatic on four pillars; EF ≤35% after ~3 months optimal therapy → ICD/CRT assessment; kidneys or K⁺ blocking pillars.
  • Cause: ischaemia, valve disease, amyloid (elderly + LVH + carpal tunnel), familial cardiomyopathy.
  • ≥2 admissions in 6 months → advanced HF assessment + parallel palliative planning.

8Never

  • Stop a pillar for asymptomatic low BP, a ≤30% creatinine rise or early beta-blocker tiredness.
  • ACEi + ARB + MRA together, or ACEi + ARNI together.
  • Accept a “normal” BNP in an obese or treated breathless patient.
  • Stop prognostic therapy because EF improved (specialist only).
  • NSAIDs (incl. OTC), pioglitazone, verapamil, diltiazem; K⁺ salt substitutes with MRA.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Chest pain or fainting — call 999. Breathless at rest or lying flat, waking gasping, new confusion, passing much less urine or swelling fast — same-day call.”   Weight: “Weight up by the amount on your plan (often 2 kg in 3 days) — ring us or the HF nurse; don't wait for breathlessness.”   Sick days: “Vomiting, diarrhoea or can't eat or drink — contact us. The ‘flozin’ is usually paused; we'll advise on the others. Never stop insulin without diabetes advice.”   Beta-blocker / painkillers: “You may feel more tired for the first fortnight — it passes. No ibuprofen-type drugs; paracetamol is fine.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
