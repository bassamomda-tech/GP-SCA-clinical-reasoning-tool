UTI in women

For: women and people with a female urinary system aged 16 and over, without a catheter: lower UTI, recurrent UTI, first assessment of pyelonephritis. Not for: men, children or catheter-associated UTI (use those pages), or hospital sepsis care. More on recurrence: Recurrent UTI page.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG109NG111NG112 (Dec 2024)UKHSA tools (2025)NG12
Core rule: screen for sepsis, pyelonephritis and pregnancy first, diagnose on symptoms (no dipstick at 65 and over), and never let a dipstick overrule the history.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected sepsis with a urinary source (fever or low temperature, rigors, fast breathing, tachycardia, low BP, mottled skin, new confusion, low urine output) → NG253 risk (NG255 if pregnant or ≤6 weeks after); high risk → Ambulance
  • Pyelonephritis (loin pain, fever, rigors, vomiting) with signs of more serious illness, vomiting or unable to take oral treatment → Same-day hospital. Stable, taking oral medicines → same-day assessment, MSU, oral treatment.
  • Suspected pyelonephritis in pregnancy → Same-day obstetric / specialist advice; systemically unwell, vomiting, dehydrated, unable to take oral treatment or septic → hospital the same day.
  • Worse at any time → Reassess same day. No better at 24–48 h → review culture, second-choice drug, rethink the diagnosis.
  • Unexplained visible haematuria at 45+ without UTI, or persisting or recurring after successful UTI treatment; lab-confirmed non-visible haematuria at 60+ with dysuria or raised white cells → Suspected cancer pathway (NG12).

1Confirm — on symptoms, split by age (UKHSA)

GroupWhat decides itAction
Under 65, ≥2 key signsDysuria, new nocturia, cloudy urineUTI likely; no dipstick needed
Under 65, 1 key sign or other new symptoms (none → look for another cause)Dipstick: nitrite +, or leukocyte and blood + → likely; leukocyte + nitrite − → culture; all − → less likelySupports, never replaces, assessment
65 and overNew dysuria alone, or ≥2 new: fever, frequency or urgency, incontinence, delirium or functional decline, suprapubic pain, visible haematuriaNo dipstick. Culture before antibiotics

Confusion or a positive urine alone is not UTI → look for other sources.

2Check — before prescribing

☐ Pregnancy possible?  ☐ eGFR before nitrofurantoin  ☐ Trimethoprim in past 3 months?  ☐ Previous cultures

Culture: pregnant, 65+, pyelonephritis, recurrent or relapsing, treatment failure, resistance likely, atypical or equivocal.

Mimics: thrush, BV, trichomonas, STI urethritis, genitourinary syndrome of menopause → offer STI testing where at risk.

3Decide — who gets an antibiotic

  • Non-pregnant → consider a back-up (use if no better in 48 h or worse) or an immediate prescription: severity, complication risk, past cultures, her preference.
  • No back-up in pregnancy, systemic illness, pyelonephritis or high complication risk.
  • Pregnant → MSU first, then an immediate antibiotic.
  • Asymptomatic bacteriuria → treat only in pregnancy, by culture. Self-care: paracetamol or ibuprofen, fluids.

5Treat — NICE NG109 / NG111

First · not pregnantNitrofurantoin 100 mg MR BD 3 days (eGFR ≥45), or trimethoprim 200 mg BD 3 days if resistance risk low
Second · 48 h failure or unsuitableNitrofurantoin (if not first), pivmecillinam 400 mg then 200 mg TDS (3 days) or fosfomycin 3 g once — as the local lab tests
Pregnant · 7 daysNitrofurantoin (not at term; GM: from 34 weeks use another) → cefalexin 500 mg BD → amoxicillin 500 mg TDS if sensitive
PyelonephritisCefalexin 500 mg BD–TDS 7–10 days (incl. pregnancy). Co-amoxiclav or trimethoprim only if sensitive; ciprofloxacin only if others unsuitable (MHRA)
  • Trimethoprim: hyperkalaemia with ACEi, ARB, spironolactone or renal impairment; methotrexate. Doses per BNF and GM pathway.

6Monitor

  • Culture: change only if resistant and not improving. Proteus or same-organism relapse → stones?
  • Visible haematuria: confirm it has gone. Pregnancy: repeat culture per local maternity protocol.

7Recurrent and refer — ≥2 in 6 months or ≥3 in 12

  • Culture to confirm; fix spermicide, constipation, poor emptying, low fluids, atrophy, diabetes.
  • Ladder: self-care, then consider → vaginal oestrogen, peri/postmenopausal (review ≤12 months) → single dose after a trigger → methenamine or daily antibiotic (review ≤6 months).
  • Specialist advice: recurrent upper UTI, unexplained recurrent lower UTI, pregnancy, same-organism relapse, Proteus, stone, obstruction, retention.
  • Aged 60+, recurrent or persistent unexplained UTI → consider a Non-urgent bladder cancer referral.

8Never

  • A dipstick at 65+, or an antibiotic for a positive urine or confusion alone.
  • Antibiotics for asymptomatic bacteriuria outside pregnancy.
  • Nitrofurantoin, fosfomycin or pivmecillinam for pyelonephritis.
  • Trimethoprim in pregnancy (specialist only); nitrofurantoin at term.
  • Repeat empirical antibiotics for visible haematuria without proven UTI.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Collapse, difficulty breathing, or blue, grey or blotchy skin or lips — call 999.”   Same day “No better in 48 hours, worse, fever or shivers, back or side pain, vomiting or can't keep tablets down, confusion, faintness, much less urine, or very unwell — contact us that day.”   Pregnant: “Fever, side pain, vomiting or worse urinary symptoms — contact maternity the same day.”   Nitrofurantoin: “New breathlessness, cough, chest pain, yellow skin, dark urine or pale stools — seek advice.”   Routine: “Blood in the urine after treatment, or repeated infections — book in.”
