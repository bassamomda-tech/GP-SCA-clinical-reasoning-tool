Animal and human bites

For: human, cat, dog and other pet bites in primary care, adults and children. Not for: insect bites (NICE NG182), wild or exotic animal bites (microbiology advice), infants under 1 month (specialist advice).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG184 (2020)NICE CKSGreen Book ch.30, ch.18UKHSA rabiesBHIVA/BASHH 2021
Core rule: a small wound over a knuckle or joint is a surgical emergency until proved otherwise — clean every bite, then decide antibiotics, tetanus, rabies and viruses.

0Act now

  • Sepsis, rapidly spreading infection, systemically unwell → 999 or Same-day admission
  • Hand, joint or tendon, including a fight bite; facial wound; deep, crushing or contaminated wound; fracture or tooth → Same-day surgical assessment
  • Any bat contact in the UK, or mammal bite abroad → Same-day rabies risk assessment (health protection team or UKHSA RIgS).
  • Human bite with blood, source high-risk → Same-day PEP discussion (within 72 hours). Safeguarding concern → same day.

1Assess and clean

Record animal or human, site, depth, blood drawn?, function and infection. Irrigate thoroughly; leave open (face: specialist may close); elevate. Swab only infected wounds.

High-risk areas: hands, feet, face, genitals, over cartilage, poor circulation. At-risk people: diabetes, immunosuppression, asplenia, decompensated liver disease.

2Check

☐ Tetanus status and wound risk ☐ rabies exposure (bat, abroad) ☐ hepatitis B immunity after human bite ☐ penicillin allergy, pregnancy ☐ safeguarding

  • 3 days prophylaxis; 5 days treatment — doses per BNF/BNFC and local formulary.

6Tetanus, rabies, viruses

  • Tetanus: booster ± immunoglobulin by status and wound risk (Green Book ch.30).
  • Human bite with blood: accelerated hepatitis B vaccine ± immunoglobulin; baseline hepatitis C and HIV tests. HIV PEP rarely indicated.

7Review

  • Worsening rapidly, or no improvement within 24–48 hours → reassess; consider IV antibiotics and referral.

8Never

  • Treat a fight bite with oral antibiotics alone.
  • Close a bite wound routinely.
  • Dismiss bat contact because no bite mark is visible.
  • Give doxycycline in pregnancy.
  • Prescribe antibiotics when the skin is not broken.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you feel very unwell, shivery, confused or faint.”   Same-day “Contact us the same day if the redness spreads, it gets more painful or swollen, you get a fever, or you can't move a finger or joint properly.”   Review: “If you're not improving within 2 days of the antibiotics, come back.”
