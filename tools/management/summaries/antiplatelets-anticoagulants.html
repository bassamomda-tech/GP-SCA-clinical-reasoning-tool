DOACs, antiplatelets and anticoagulation

For: adults starting, reviewing, switching or stopping an antiplatelet or anticoagulant in primary care. Not for: the AF anticoagulation decision (atrial fibrillation), diagnosing DVT or PE, warfarin management (anticoagulation service), pregnancy (specialist LMWH) or cancer-associated VTE (with the cancer/VTE team).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG196 (AF)NG158 (VTE)NG128 (stroke/TIA)UKCPA/BSH peri-operativeNG12 (updated April 2026)
Core rule: dose every DOAC by Cockcroft–Gault creatinine clearance (not eGFR) against its indication-specific SPC criteria in both directions, give every DAPT and VTE course a visible stop or review date, and never stop specialist-mandated DAPT unilaterally.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Major bleed (haemodynamic compromise, critical site, Hb fall needing transfusion) → 999 / ED now; local reversal protocol.
  • Any head injury on an anticoagulant, or an antiplatelet other than aspirin alone → Same-day ED, even if well; 999 if neurological symptoms, loss of consciousness, significant mechanism or deterioration.
  • Visible haematuria aged ≥45 → Urgent suspected cancer referral — never blame the drug.
  • Iron-deficiency anaemia, rectal bleeding aged ≥50, or unexplained weight loss with abdominal pain aged ≥40 → FIT; ≥10 µg Hb/g → Urgent colorectal pathway (NG12).

Don't miss: anaemia means occult bleeding until explained. High ORBIT → reduce risk, not withhold.

5Treat — doses per SPC, BNF and local formulary

  • VTE: apixaban 10 mg BD × 7 days → 5 mg BD; rivaroxaban 15 mg BD × 21 days → 20 mg OD; edoxaban or dabigatran after ≥5 days LMWH.
  • Stroke/TIA, PAD → clopidogrel 75 mg OD. DAPT per the discharge plan.
  • PPI where indicated (DAPT, prior ulcer); with clopidogrel lansoprazole or pantoprazole, not omeprazole/esomeprazole.
  • Inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin, St John's Wort) → avoid or seek advice.
  • Switch per the SPC. Missed dose: apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban → take now, then as usual (rivaroxaban VTE loading differs); dabigatran → only if ≥6 h before the next. Check the PIL.

6Monitor

At least annually: recalculate CrCl, re-apply SPC criteria; FBC, LFTs; indication, stop date, adherence. More often if CrCl <60, frail or >75, illness, fluctuating renal function or new interacting drugs.

7Refer — specialist input

  • Cardiology: any change to mandated DAPT · triple therapy · recent stent needing surgery.
  • Haematology: APS · recurrent VTE on treatment · extremes of weight · suspected DOAC failure. Anticoagulation service: warfarin, bridging, complex switches.
  • Procedures: local/BSH protocol; DOAC bridging usually unnecessary.

8Never

  • Dose a DOAC on the lab eGFR, or reduce it without meeting the criteria.
  • Transfer the AF apixaban rule to VTE (VTE 2.5 mg BD is extended prevention only).
  • Stop, shorten or extend specialist-mandated DAPT unilaterally (recent stent needing surgery → cardiology first) — or leave it with no stop date.
  • Blame the antithrombotic for unexplained anaemia, haematuria or rectal bleeding.
  • Prescribe aspirin for AF stroke prevention or primary prevention.
  • Stop the antithrombotic for nuisance bruising or brief nose or gum bleeds — fix the modifiables.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Black or bloody stools, vomiting or coughing up blood, a bleed that will not stop, or a sudden severe headache, confusion, weakness or slurred speech — call 999 or go to the emergency department straight away and say you are on a blood thinner.”   Head injury: “Go to the emergency department straight away, even if you feel well — bleeding inside the head can be delayed.”   Same day: “Blood in your urine, heavy periods, frequent nosebleeds or large unexplained bruises — call 111 or contact us the same day.”   Medicines: “Don't stop the tablet without advice, avoid ibuprofen-type painkillers, and carry your alert card.”
