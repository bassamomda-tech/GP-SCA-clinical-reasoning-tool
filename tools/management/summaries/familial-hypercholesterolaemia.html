Familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH)

For: adults with suspected or confirmed FH, and their families. Not for: ordinary risk-scored lipid management (use the lipid management protocol). Homozygous FH, children and pregnancy are specialist-led (blocks 0 and 7).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG71 (updated 2019)QS41NG238TA393 · TA394 · TA733TA1002
Core rule: never risk-score FH with QRISK — treat on the diagnosis to a ≥50% LDL-C fall from the untreated baseline, and make sure the family is actually tested: diagnosing one person is diagnosing a family.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected homozygous FH: adult LDL-C >13 mmol/L, child/young person >11 mmol/L, or a child with two affected parents, tendon or cutaneous xanthomata or childhood coronary disease → Urgent specialist FH centre — do not apply Simon Broome/DLCN first.
  • Pregnant while on a statin → stop it immediately; Urgent obstetric referral for fetal assessment. Early exposure does not automatically indicate termination.
  • Severe muscle pain with dark urine or weakness → stop the statin; Same-day CK and renal function (possible rhabdomyolysis).
  • Chest pain that is severe, spreads, or lasts >15 minutes → 999. New exertional chest pain or breathlessness → Same-day

1Confirm — thresholds prompt assessment, not diagnosis

Suspect: adult TC >7.5 mmol/L, especially with CHD before 60 in the patient or a first-degree relative, or tendon xanthomata (child: TC >6.7 or LDL-C >4.0).

Record search (TC)Under 30: >7.5 mmol/L30 and over: >9.0 mmol/L

Two LDL-C measurements where feasible — but very high LDL-C, childhood xanthomata or premature CHD → Urgent referral without waiting. Simon Broome or DLCN (if competent): possible/definite or DLCN >5 → refer for assessment and DNA testing. Negative or no genetic test → clinical diagnosis can stand.

2Check — exclude secondary causes before labelling or referring

☐ Hypothyroidism  ☐ Nephrotic syndrome, CKD  ☐ Cholestasis, liver disease  ☐ Uncontrolled diabetes  ☐ Alcohol, obesity, diet  ☐ Pregnancy  ☐ Drugs: steroids, ciclosporin, retinoids, some antipsychotics, antiretrovirals  ☐ Baseline transaminases  ☐ Untreated LDL-C recorded

3Decide — treated on the diagnosis

  • No QRISK (it underestimates). Treatment can start before specialist review when indicated.
  • Cascade (specialist-led): 1st- and 2nd-degree relatives, 3rd where feasible, incl. children; DNA test if mutation known, else LDL-C.
  • Women: pregnancy is not contraindicated — plan contraception and pre-conception care.
  • Children at risk → test by age 10 (earlier if homozygous suspected).
  • Lifestyle (adjunct only): diet, stop smoking (critical), activity, weight, alcohol.

4Target

LDL-C ≥50% fall from the untreated baseline (none → highest reliable historical level, documented) — not a primary-prevention target. Also record achieved LDL-C and CVD status.

5Treat — lifelong

Step 1High-intensity statin: atorvastatin or rosuvastatin at the maximum licensed or tolerated dose
Step 2<50% fall despite adherence → add ezetimibe 10 mg once daily
Step 3Still <50% → specialist lipid service (PCSK9 inhibitors, inclisiran, apheresis only if NICE, NHSE and GM criteria met)
Homozygous FHSpecialist FH centre only (e.g. evinacumab per TA1002, apheresis)
  • Before every step: adherence (statin hesitancy), dose titrated, thyroid and alcohol rechecked.
  • Muscle aches ≠ automatic intolerance: assess; lower dose, another statin or intermittent dosing. None tolerated → ezetimibe and refer.
  • Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Full lipids 2–3 months after any start or change → <50% fall: escalate.
  • Transaminases before treatment and after starting or a dose change (NG238). CK only if muscle symptoms.
  • Annually, lifelong: lipids, adherence, CV risk factors, pregnancy planning — and check relatives are actually being tested.

7Refer — specialist lipid service

  • Criteria met (block 1) → assessment and DNA testing.
  • Refractory: <50% fall on maximum statin plus ezetimibe, or no statin tolerated.
  • Affected children → paediatric lipid specialist; pregnancy or pre-conception → specialist.
  • Cascade: confirm the specialist service started it; never contact relatives without consent.

8Never

  • QRISK or any population risk calculator in FH — it gives false reassurance.
  • Label FH before excluding secondary causes, or delay a homozygous referral to score criteria.
  • Rely on "tell your relatives" — the cascade has to be organised and checked.
  • A statin in pregnancy: stop lipid-lowering medicines 3 months before trying to conceive; restart only after breastfeeding ends.
  • Start or titrate a statin in a child without an agreed specialist pathway.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Chest pain that is severe, spreads to your arm, neck, jaw or back, or lasts more than 15 minutes — call 999. New chest pain or breathlessness on exertion — contact us the same day.”   Muscles: “Severe muscle pain with dark urine — stop the statin and get seen the same day (111 if we are closed). Lesser aches — contact us rather than just stopping.”   Pregnancy: “Planning a pregnancy — tell us first. Pregnant on a statin — stop it and contact us the same day.”   Family: “Your close relatives should be tested — early treatment can prevent heart attacks in them too.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Block 0 excluded (homozygous FH, pregnancy on a statin) Secondary causes excluded; criteria applied and referred Untreated LDL-C and transaminases recorded; pregnancy plans discussed Statin started; 2–3-month lipids and annual review booked; cascade started Safety-net given and documented
