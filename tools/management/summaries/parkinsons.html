Parkinson's disease

For: adults with diagnosed Parkinson's disease under specialist shared care. Not for: unclassified tremor (start with the tremors pathway), essential tremor (use the essential tremor protocol) or dementia work-up (use the dementia protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG71MHRASPSDVLAParkinson's UK
Core rule: give every Parkinson's medicine at the individually prescribed time, never stop it abruptly, never prescribe a dopamine blocker, and exclude reversible causes before calling deterioration progression.

0Act now — missed doses and dopamine blockers cause most harm

  • Acute severe rigidity or akinesia, hyperthermia, confusion, autonomic instability or markedly raised CK after missed doses (NMS-like state) → 999 emergency admission; restore dopaminergic therapy under specialist or acute medical guidance.
  • Cannot swallow or absorb Parkinson's medicines (illness, vomiting, surgery) → Parkinson's team or acute medical service Same-day. Do not substitute a dopamine agonist patch for levodopa yourself.
  • New swallow change → medication review Same-day; do not crush modified-release tablets unless the product information permits.
  • Recurrent aspiration or pneumonia → Urgent review; aspiration precautions, oral care, SALT.

1Confirm — the specialist makes the diagnosis

Bradykinesia plus rigidity or tremor. Diagnosis and initial treatment are specialist-led; primary care continues and monitors under the shared-care plan.

2Check — every review

☐ Doses taken at the prescribed times  ☐ Wearing-off, dyskinesia, freezing, falls  ☐ Lying and standing BP  ☐ Mood, cognition, hallucinations  ☐ Swallow, weight, drooling, bowels, bladder  ☐ Impulse control, asked privately (and family or carers, with consent)  ☐ Daytime sleepiness and driving  ☐ Carer strain

Don't miss: a new dopamine antagonist (often an out-of-hours anti-emetic) or anticholinergic.

3Decide — worse is not automatically progression

  • Check first: timing, offending drugs, infection, constipation or retention, dehydration, pain, sleep loss, hypoxia, metabolic upset, delirium.
  • New hallucinations or cognitive fluctuation → general medical assessment before specialist advice.
  • Impulse-control disorder (gambling, spending, binge eating, hypersexuality) → assess financial, safeguarding and relationship harm; do not stop the agonist abruptly; specialist for gradual reduction.
  • Everyone: exercise; physiotherapy, SALT, dietitian, OT, PD nurse, carer support.

5Treat — non-motor symptoms, within the shared-care plan

ConstipationExclude obstruction or impaction; review fluids and drugs; laxative
Orthostatic hypotensionReview antihypertensives, diuretics, dopaminergics; midodrine or fludrocortisone specialist-led
DroolingNot anticholinergics alone; SALT, glycopyrronium; botulinum toxin via specialist
REM-sleep behaviour disorderInjury risk to patient and partner; specialist advice for melatonin or clonazepam
  • Nausea: domperidone, lowest dose (generally up to 10 mg three times daily), usually ≤1 week; not with QT prolongation, cardiac or severe hepatic disease, electrolyte disturbance, QT-prolonging drugs or potent CYP3A4 inhibitors. Doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • Psychosis: reversible causes first; tolerated hallucinations may need no drug; specialist advice (quetiapine may be considered; clozapine specialist-led).

6Monitor

  • Regular shared-care review: block 2; treat depression; on change → specialist or PD nurse (first contact). Advancing disease: palliative and anticipatory planning, place of care.
  • Coughing or choking with food, wet voice, chest infections, weight loss → prompt SALT.
  • DVLA: everyone with Parkinson's must tell the DVLA. Sudden sleep onset or severe sleepiness → no driving or hazardous work.

7Refer — urgent routes are in block 0

  • Wearing-off, dyskinesia, freezing, psychosis, dementia, impulse control, severe autonomic or refractory non-motor symptoms, any dopaminergic dose change → Parkinson's specialist or PD service.
  • Advanced therapies: apomorphine, intestinal gel or deep brain stimulation assessment.

8Never

  • Stop Parkinson's medicines abruptly, or delay doses for drug rounds or meals.
  • Prescribe metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, haloperidol or typical antipsychotics; olanzapine or risperidone only if specialist-directed.
  • Crush modified-release tablets unless the product information permits.
  • Stop a dopamine agonist abruptly for an impulse-control disorder.
  • Accept decline as progression before checking timing, drugs, illness and delirium.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “High fever, severe stiffness, suddenly being unable to move, confusion or heavy sweating — especially if medication has been stopped — call 999.”   Same-day “If you cannot swallow or keep down your Parkinson's tablets, contact us or your Parkinson's team urgently the same day. Doses must not be missed.”   Driving: “If you feel very sleepy in the day or fall asleep suddenly, do not drive or do hazardous work, and tell us. Everyone with Parkinson's must tell the DVLA.”   Medicines: “Urges to gamble, spend, shop or eat — tell us, or ask your family to; do not stop your tablets suddenly. Check with us before any new medicine.”
