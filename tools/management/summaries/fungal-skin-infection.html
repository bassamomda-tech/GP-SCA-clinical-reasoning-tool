Fungal skin infection (tinea)

For: dermatophyte infection of skin, scalp and nails in adults and children in primary care. Not for: cellulitis from the toe webs (treat on its own merits — cellulitis protocol), or a pigmented nail band (suspected melanoma — block 0). Candida and erythrasma are mimics, not tinea.

BAD tinea capitis 2014BAD onychomycosis 2014PCDS tineaUKHSA T. indotineae 2025GMMMG formulary
Core rule: cream for the full course on skin, tablets for every scalp, confirm a nail before months of tablets, never a steroid on its own — and when terbinafine fails, re-sample rather than repeat it.

0Act now — when this is not routine ringworm

  • Cellulitis from toe-web tinea with systemic upset (especially diabetes) → Same-day; then treat the toe webs.
  • On an oral antifungal: widespread rash, blistering, jaundice, or on terbinafine dark urine, pale stools, severe pruritus, persistent nausea, abdominal pain, marked fatigue → stop it, Same-day
  • New, changing, widening or irregular longitudinal pigmented nail band, especially pigment on the nail fold → Urgent suspected cancer (skin) pathway for nail-unit melanoma.
  • Kerion (boggy, tender, pustular scalp swelling) → sample, start oral antifungal promptly, Urgent dermatology advice; never incise.
  • Extensive or recurrent groin or body tinea after terbinafine (T. indotineae?), or significant immunosuppression → sample, Urgent dermatology; don't wait for culture.

2Check — sample if tablets are likely or the picture is odd

☐ Microscopy and culture (edge scrapings, clipped nail, plucked hairs)  ☐ Feet, nails  ☐ Household, pets  ☐ Steroid? Stop, then scrape

Nails: confirm before tablets. Scalp: sample, then start if typical. Skin: sample if extensive, refractory or failed; clinical diagnosis fine if localised. Negative but convincing → repeat.

3Decide — treat by site

  • Localised skin → topical, all sites incl. feet. Scalp → always oral, plus shampoo.
  • Nails → optional if asymptomatic; justified for pain, function, distress, diabetes or cellulitis.
  • Extensive or refractory skin → dermatology/microbiology advice before tablets.
  • Pregnancy → avoid tablets for uncomplicated disease: topical imidazole; defer nails.

5Treat — doses per BNF/BNFC and local formulary

SkinTerbinafine 1% cream, or clotrimazole 1% / miconazole 2% (flexures, candida possible)
Inflamed flexuralDiagnosis secure: hydrocortisone 1% only with an imidazole, a few days
ScalpT. tonsurans → terbinafine (<20 kg 62.5 mg · 20–40 kg 125 mg · >40 kg 250 mg daily). Cat/dog Microsporum → griseofulvin 15–20 mg/kg/day
Nails (confirmed)Terbinafine 250 mg daily. Or itraconazole pulses 200 mg BD 7 days a month: 2 finger, 3 toe
  • Scalp: ketoconazole or selenium shampoo for child and household. Terbinafine off-label in children — document consent.
  • Before tablets: terbinafine — liver, renal, CYP2D6; itraconazole — heart failure, interactions; griseofulvin — pregnancy.
  • GM: amorolfine not routinely prescribed; topical antifungals may be self-care.

6Monitor

  • Terbinafine: LFTs at baseline and after 4–6 weeks (SPC); LFTs rise → stop.
  • Itraconazole: stop if heart failure or hepatitis symptoms. Miconazole + warfarin → avoid or discuss with anticoagulation; INR.
  • Not clearing: full course, all sites? → re-scrape → species ID, susceptibility.

7Refer — routine dermatology

  • Diagnostic doubt despite sampling; extensive, recurrent, atypical or resistant disease.
  • Scalp still active with persistent fungus after an adequate course.
  • Nail failed oral treatment or oral contraindicated; non-dermatophyte mould.

8Never

  • A steroid alone, or any potent steroid, on a possible fungal rash — tinea incognito.
  • Months of nail tablets before mycology confirms infection.
  • Cream alone for scalp ringworm, or incision and drainage of a kerion.
  • Repeat terbinafine for groin or body tinea it has not cleared — sample and involve dermatology/microbiology.
  • Itraconazole with simvastatin or in heart failure; griseofulvin in pregnancy (contraception; men not to father a child within 6 months).

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “Spreading redness and swelling from the feet with feeling unwell — be seen today.”   Tablets: “Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, pale stools, severe itching, persistent sickness, tummy pain, marked tiredness or a widespread or blistering rash — stop them and be seen today.”   Cream: “Whole course, not just until the itch stops; treat your feet; no steroid cream alone.”   Scalp: “School is fine once treated; don't share combs, hats or towels; pets may need a vet.”
