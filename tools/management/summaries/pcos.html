PCOS, now PMOS

For: women and adolescents with suspected or confirmed PCOS (polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome). Not for: postmenopausal bleeding (NG12) or androgen excess after the menopause (endocrinology); ovulation induction is for fertility services.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
International PCOS guideline 2023RCOG GTG 33 (archived Dec 2024)NICE NG257 · PH38 · NG246UKMEC 2025SfE 2025
Core rule: ask how fast it came on and exclude the mimics before labelling PCOS, screen glucose, BP and lipids at every weight, and give endometrial protection when bleeds are 3–4 months apart or more.

0Act now — speed, severity and age at onset are the clues

  • Rapid virilisation (deep voice, clitoromegaly, temporal balding, hirsutism over months) or testosterone above about 5 nmol/L (reliable assay) → Urgent endocrinology (androgen-secreting tumour). No combined pill first — it masks it.
  • New hirsutism or scalp hair loss after the menopause → check testosterone → Urgent endocrinology (hyperthecosis, tumour).
  • Postmenopausal bleeding aged 55+, not attributable to HRT → Suspected cancer pathway (endometrial); under 55 → consider one. Pelvic or abdominal mass or ascites → ovarian suspected cancer pathway.
  • Low mood with suicidal thoughts or self-harm → Same-day crisis assessment; 999 if in immediate danger.

1Confirm — two of three, after mimics are excluded

FeatureWhat counts
Cycles≥3 years post-menarche: <21 or >35 days or <8 a year (1–3 years: >45). Any >90 days
Androgen excessFerriman–Gallwey ≥4–6 (by ethnicity), acne, female-pattern hair loss; or raised testosterone/FAI
Ovaries≥20 follicles or ≥10 mL on transvaginal scan, or raised AMH (adults)

Within 8 years of menarche: needs cycles and androgen excess, no scan or AMH; not met → "increased risk", treat and reassess.

2Check — exclude mimics, then map the risks

☐ Pregnancy test ☐ Testosterone + FAI ☐ TSH ☐ Prolactin, 17-OHP, FSH/oestradiol if indicated ☐ 75 g OGTT (any BMI) ☐ Lipids ☐ BP, weight ☐ Mood, eating, sleep apnoea ☐ Bleeds in last year

Don't miss Cushing's (striae, bruising, proximal weakness).

3Decide — treat the goal she names

  • Lifestyle for all: modest weight loss may help if excess; preventing gain is valid. Screen for disordered eating first.
  • Cycles, hirsutism, acne → combined pill first line if suitable and acceptable.
  • Adults, metabolic features (esp. BMI ≥25) → consider metformin.
  • Weight medicines → NICE TA875/TA1026 criteria and GMMMG position, not PCOS alone.
  • Wanting a baby → folic acid, OGTT, stop the pill, refer early.

4Target — goals and thresholds

GoalThreshold or action
EndometriumNo CHC/LNG-IUS → bleed at least every 3–4 months
HbA1c 42–47Intensive lifestyle programme; yearly test
HbA1c ≥48Confirm, then diabetes pathway

5Treat — cycles, endometrium, hair

First choiceCombined pill (20–30 µg). UKMEC: BMI 30–34.9 = 2; ≥35 = 3
AlternativeLNG-IUS, or cyclical progestogen (not contraceptive)
Facial hairCosmetic ± eflornithine twice daily; stop if no benefit at 4 months
Hair at ~6 monthsConsider an anti-androgen or specialist advice, with effective contraception
  • Before the pill: BP, smoking, migraine with aura, VTE history. Metformin: renal function, start low.
  • Tirzepatide: non-oral or added barrier method 4 weeks after start and each dose rise; stop ≥1 month before pregnancy (semaglutide 2 months).
  • Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Yearly: weight, BP, bleeds and endometrial plan, mood, eating, sleep, contraception, pregnancy plans.
  • Glucose every 1–3 years (OGTT preferred). Lipids by result and CV risk.
  • Pregnancy: OGTT before 20 weeks if not done before, and at 24–28 weeks.
  • Hair: baseline score or photos; compare at 6 months.

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent in block 0)

  • Trying to conceive, anovulatory, aged 36+ or other factors → earlier fertility referral, not waiting 12 months.
  • Suspected mimic or unclear adolescent → endocrinology (paediatric if under 16).
  • Persistent abnormal bleeding or failed endometrial protection → gynaecology.
  • Hirsutism uncontrolled at 6 months → endocrinology or dermatology advice.

8Never

  • A combined pill before testing when onset is rapid or virilising.
  • Scan or AMH within 8 years of menarche; routine endometrial scans.
  • Skip glucose screening because she is slim; leave long gaps between bleeds unprotected.
  • Anti-androgens or GLP-1/tirzepatide without effective contraception; letrozole in routine primary care.
  • Co-cyprindiol first line, for contraception alone or with another hormonal contraceptive.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Urgent “Voice deepening, hair growth speeding up over weeks to months, or body shape changing — contact us within a few days; don't wait for your review.”   Within a week “Any bleeding a year or more after your periods have stopped — contact us within a week.”   Combined pill: “Painful, swollen leg — contact us the same day; chest pain or sudden breathlessness — call 999.”   Mood: “Thoughts of harming yourself — contact us or NHS 111 the same day, or 999 if you are in immediate danger.”   Routine “No period for over 3 months without a pill or coil protecting the womb lining, or bleeding between periods or after sex — book an appointment.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
