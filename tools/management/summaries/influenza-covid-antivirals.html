Influenza and COVID-19 antivirals

For: adults with suspected influenza or confirmed COVID-19 in primary care, including pregnancy and kidney disease. Not for: children's doses (use the BNFC), patients needing oxygen or hospital care (block 0), or COVID-19 treatment for children (specialist-led).

UKHSA influenza antivirals (2025)NICE TA878NICE TA1056Green Book ch.19 · ch.14aSPCs
Core rule: assess severity first; an antiviral never replaces admission. Then match the drug to the virus, the risk group and the clock: 48 hours for influenza, 5 days for COVID-19.

0Act now

  • Severe breathlessness, cyanosis, can't complete sentences, reduced consciousness, shock or seizure → 999
  • Saturation <94% on air (or below own target), respiratory rate ≥25, heart rate >130, systolic ≤90, new confusion, sepsis or pneumonia with systemic upset → Same-day hospital
  • Severely immunosuppressed with complicated influenza, severe dehydration, or rapid deterioration on day 5 to 10 of COVID-19 → Same-day hospital
  • Suspected outbreak in a care home (two or more linked cases within 48 hours) → Same-day health protection team
  • Febrile child → NICE NG143 traffic-light assessment; red features → Same-day paediatric assessment.

1Confirm — which virus, and is it circulating?

Influenza: treat on clinical grounds in at-risk patients when UKHSA surveillance shows flu circulating or there is a local outbreak; no test needed. COVID-19: treatment needs a positive test and symptom onset within 5 days.

2Check — every patient

☐ Saturation, respiratory rate, pulse, BP, mental state  ☐ Day of symptom onset  ☐ Risk group  ☐ Creatinine clearance or eGFR  ☐ Pregnancy  ☐ Full medicines list, including herbal (Paxlovid)  ☐ Vaccination status.

3Decide — who gets an antiviral

  • Influenza, at-risk group (65 or over, pregnant, under 6 months, chronic disease, diabetes, immunosuppression, BMI 40 or more) → offer oseltamivir, ideally within 48 hours.
  • Influenza, previously healthy → usually no antiviral, unless at serious risk of complications.
  • COVID-19 → only NICE TA878 highest-risk groups (for example transplant, immunosuppression, cancer on treatment, CKD stage 4 or 5). Age 70 or over, BMI 35 or more, diabetes or heart failure alone no longer qualify.

4Prophylaxis and vaccines

  • Flu contact in an at-risk group, unvaccinated or unlikely to be protected → offer oseltamivir 75 mg once daily for 10 days (within 48 hours) or zanamivir (within 36 hours).
  • Check flu and COVID-19 vaccine eligibility for the current season (Green Book chapters 19 and 14a).

5Treat — adults; doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

Influenza first lineOseltamivir 75 mg twice daily for 5 days. CrCl 30–60: 30 mg twice daily; 10–30: 30 mg once daily
Influenza resistanceZanamivir 10 mg inhaled twice daily for 5 days; caution in asthma and COPD (bronchospasm)
COVID-19 first lineNirmatrelvir 300 mg + ritonavir 100 mg twice daily for 5 days; eGFR 30–60: 150 mg + 100 mg; not if eGFR <30
COVID-19 if Paxlovid unsuitableMolnupiravir 800 mg twice daily for 5 days; not in pregnancy; contraception advice
  • COVID-19: refer the same day to the local COVID-19 treatment service, following the local route. Sotrovimab was discontinued in the UK in February 2026.
  • Paxlovid interactions: amiodarone, simvastatin, some anti-epileptics, rifampicin, St John's wort are contraindicated; plan for DOACs, tacrolimus, ciclosporin, statins (Liverpool checker).

6Monitor

  • Paused drugs (for example atorvastatin) → restart usually 3 days after the last Paxlovid dose.
  • Warfarin → check INR after the course. Combined hormonal contraception → extra precautions.
  • Worse after initial improvement → think secondary bacterial pneumonia (NICE NG138).

7Refer — non-emergency

  • Severely immunosuppressed, or suspected oseltamivir resistance → virology or specialist advice on zanamivir.
  • Pregnant with COVID-19 considered for treatment → obstetric and COVID-19 treatment service advice.
  • Transplant drugs interacting with Paxlovid → transplant team.

8Never

  • Send home a hypoxic or septic patient because an antiviral has been prescribed.
  • Prescribe Paxlovid without checking every medicine for interactions.
  • Give molnupiravir in pregnancy.
  • Withhold oseltamivir from an at-risk patient while waiting for a flu test.
  • Stop an anticoagulant for Paxlovid without an individual plan.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Struggling to breathe, lips going blue, or very drowsy or confused — call 999.”   Same day “More breathless, chest pain, can't keep fluids down, or worse after getting better — contact us or NHS 111 the same day.”   Medicines: “No new medicines or herbal remedies while on Paxlovid without checking with a pharmacist.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Severity assessed; red flags excluded Virus and timing established (circulating flu, positive COVID-19 test) Risk group, renal function, pregnancy and interactions checked Antiviral or referral to the COVID-19 service arranged today Safety-net, prophylaxis for contacts and vaccines covered
