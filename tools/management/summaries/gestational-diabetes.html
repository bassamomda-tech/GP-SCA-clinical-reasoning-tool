Gestational diabetes

For: screening, diagnosis, shared care and postnatal follow-up of gestational diabetes (GDM). Not for: confirmed pre-existing type 1 or 2 diabetes in pregnancy (diabetes-in-pregnancy team), DKA (DKA protocol) or hypertension in pregnancy (that protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG3NICE NG133NICE QS109SPC (emc)
Core rule: diagnose on the 75 g OGTT (never on HbA1c), look for pre-existing diabetes behind it, refer to the joint clinic within 1 week — and book the 6–13 week postnatal fasting glucose.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected DKA (vomiting, abdominal pain, thirst, breathlessness, ketones — even with normal-looking glucose) → 999 obstetric and medical admission. Check blood ketones if unwell.
  • Weight loss, osmotic symptoms, ketonuria or very high glucose (possible new type 1) → Same-day specialist discussion; no routine OGTT.
  • Raised BP, proteinuria, headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain, brisk reflexes → Same-day maternity assessment; fitting or reduced consciousness → 999
  • Reduced fetal movements → contact the maternity unit immediately → Same-day assessment; do not wait until the next day.
  • HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol or diagnostic-range glucose → Urgent diabetes-in-pregnancy team discussion; do not label on HbA1c alone.
  • Symptomatic hyper- or hypoglycaemia, targets persistently unmet, accelerating growth or polyhydramnios → Same-day antenatal diabetes team discussion.

1Confirm — screen by risk factor, diagnose on OGTT

Any ONE risk factor → 75 g OGTT at 24–28 weeks: BMI >30 kg/m² · previous baby ≥4.5 kg · previous GDM · first-degree relative with diabetes · high-prevalence ethnic family origin. Previous GDM → self-monitoring or OGTT soon after booking; repeat at 24–28 weeks if normal.

Glycosuria 2+ once or 1+ twice → consider testing. Thirst, polyuria, recurrent thrush → test at any gestation.

2Check — at diagnosis

☐ HbA1c  ☐ BP, urine  ☐ Fetal movements  ☐ Steroids, antipsychotics  ☐ Record: previous GDM, birthweights

Don't miss: new type 1; monogenic diabetes (parent with lifelong “borderline” glucose) → specialist.

3Decide — fasting glucose picks the rung

  • All GDM → joint diabetes-antenatal clinic within 1 week; send both OGTT values, HbA1c, BMI, medicines.
  • Fasting <7, no complications → 1–2 week diet and exercise trial.
  • Fasting ≥7 → offer insulin now. 6.0–6.9 with macrosomia or hydramnios → consider insulin.
  • Lifestyle: low-GI carbs, post-meal walks, dietitian; no weight loss; stop-smoking referral. Not her fault.

4Target — unless problematic hypos

Fasting capillary glucose<5.3 mmol/L
1-hour (or 2-hour) post-meal<7.8 (or <6.4) mmol/L
Growth and liquor scansAbout 4-weekly, 28–36 weeks
Birth, uncomplicated GDMBy 40+6; earlier if complications

5Treat — by the antenatal diabetes team

Step 1Diet and exercise, 1–2 weeks (fasting <7)
Step 2Targets unmet → metformin; declined, contraindicated or ineffective → insulin
Step 3Insulin ± metformin — straight here if fasting ≥7
AlternativeConsider glibenclamide only if metformin fails or unsuitable AND insulin declined or inappropriate
  • Insulin or glibenclamide: teach hypo treatment; DVLA rules where relevant.
  • Seen by GP: diary, BP, urine, movements; concerns to the team the same day — never change treatment alone. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — after the birth

  • At birth: stop GDM drugs (unless the team advises otherwise); check maternal glucose before transfer; neonatal glucose.
  • 6–13 weeks: fasting plasma glucose (no OGTT); after 13 weeks → fasting glucose, or HbA1c if fasting not possible.
  • Fasting <6.0 → low probability · fasting 6.0–6.9 or HbA1c 39–47 → high risk, prevention · HbA1c ≥48 or diabetes range → confirm, treat as type 2.
  • Annual HbA1c; code GDM, set recall, Diabetes Prevention Programme where eligible.

7Refer — and plan ahead

  • Pre-existing diabetes confirmed → aspirin 75–150 mg daily from 12 weeks to birth unless contraindicated, retinal and renal checks, fetal-heart scan.
  • Macrosomia → consultant-led birth discussion. Next pregnancy → early testing.

8Never

  • Diagnose GDM — or pre-existing diabetes — on HbA1c alone.
  • Book a routine OGTT for weight loss, ketones or osmotic symptoms.
  • Give aspirin for GDM alone (other pre-eclampsia risk factors may qualify).
  • Stop insulin at birth in pre-existing diabetes without a plan.
  • Let the postnatal test and annual HbA1c go uncoded.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Being sick, tummy pain, very thirsty or breathless — even if your sugars look fine — or a fit: call 999.”   Same-day “Baby moving less — ring the maternity unit straight away, don't wait until tomorrow. Bad headache, vision problems, pain under the ribs or sudden swelling — maternity unit today.”   After birth: “Fasting blood test 6–13 weeks after the birth, then HbA1c yearly; thirst, passing lots of urine or weight loss — get tested that day.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
