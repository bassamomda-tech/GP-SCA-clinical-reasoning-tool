Anxiety disorders in adults

For: adults with GAD, panic, agoraphobia, social or health anxiety, OCD or PTSD. Not for: under 18s. Severe or complex anxiety in pregnancy or the year after birth → perinatal mental health team (CG192).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG113CG159CG31NG116NG23 (updated Apr 2026)
Core rule: consider physical and substance causes, ask about suicide every time, name the actual disorder because it picks the therapy, put psychology first, and if an SSRI is started, start low, warn about early jitteriness and review early.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Imminent risk, intent, a plan, inability to stay safe, severe self-neglect or psychosis → Same-day mental-health assessment (local crisis pathway); ideation with a plan → crisis team or 999
  • Chest pain that could be acute coronary syndrome — current pain, or pain in the last 12 hours with an abnormal or unavailable ECG → 999; do not wait for an ECG. New, atypical or changing chest symptoms in known panic → Same-day
  • Thyroid storm (hyperpyrexia, tachycardia, altered consciousness) or phaeochromocytoma crisis (severe hypertension, headache, sweating) → 999
  • Alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal (severe anxiety, tremor, sweating, confusion) → seizure risk → Same-day medical assessment.
  • New SSRI with suicidal ideation (especially under 30, first 2 weeks) → Same-day review or crisis team.

1Confirm — the disorder picks the therapy

GAD: excessive, hard-to-control worry in several domains ≥6 months, with ≥3 physical or cognitive symptoms and impairment; exclude the specific disorders first.

DisorderTherapy (± SSRI)
GADCBT or applied relaxation
Panic ± agoraphobiaPanic-focused CBT; graded exposure. No benzodiazepines
Social anxietyIndividual CBT, not group; SSRI if CBT declined
Health anxietyIts own CBT; test only if indicated
OCDCBT with exposure and response prevention (ERP)
PTSD (>1 month)Trauma-focused CBT or EMDR

GAD-7 5–9 mild · 10–14 moderate · 15–21 severe measures, not diagnoses. Score PHQ-9 too.

2Check — targeted by history and examination

☐ Medicines, AUDIT-C, caffeine, cannabis, stimulants  ☐ TSH if thyroid features  ☐ ECG if palpitations, syncope, chest symptoms or QT risk  ☐ Pregnancy, menopause  ☐ Suicide risk, past mania, domestic abuse, safeguarding

Don't miss: weight loss, a lump, rectal bleeding, haemoptysis → NG12 on its own merits.

3Decide — stepped care, psychology first

  • Mild → education, monitoring, then guided self-help or digital CBT; give the NHS Talking Therapies link.
  • Marked symptoms or impairment (clinical, not score) → individual CBT or applied relaxation, or an SSRI, by preference.
  • Menopausal symptoms → NG23: consider HRT for low mood, CBT for low mood or anxiety.
  • Lifestyle: cut caffeine gradually, alcohol, exercise, breathing; debt or isolation → social prescriber.

4Target — success is restored function, not zero worry

FunctionWork, relationships, activities; not GAD-7 alone
Adequate trialTherapeutic dose, disorder-appropriate time (12 weeks in OCD, social anxiety); continue per disorder

5Treat — drugs at Step 3, ideally alongside therapy

1st · SSRISertraline (off-label for GAD: document consent); consider a low start (e.g. 25–50 mg).
2ndAnother SSRI (escitalopram), then an SNRI: venlafaxine (monitor BP) or duloxetine.
3rdOnly if SSRIs and SNRIs cannot be tolerated → consider pregabalin (controlled drug; misuse risk). Not for panic or PTSD.
BenzodiazepinesNot routine. Crisis only: fixed quantity and end date, usually ≤2–4 weeks.
  • Before a switch: adherence, dose, duration, diagnosis, bipolarity, substances, therapy accessed. Warn of jitteriness in weeks 1–2. OCD: higher doses (sertraline up to 200 mg). Check doses in the SPC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Review within 1 week if under 30 or at risk (then weekly for a month), otherwise 1–2 weeks; then 2–4-weekly for 3 months.
  • Hyponatraemia (older, diuretics): confusion, headache, nausea → Same-day sodium; seizures → 999.
  • Stopping: taper slowly; pregabalin over at least 1 week, never abruptly.

7Refer — name the disorder on the referral

  • Talking Therapies: PTSD → trauma track; OCD → OCD track; health anxiety → its own CBT track.
  • CMHT: not responding to therapy plus SSRI, psychosis or mania, substance misuse, complex PTSD, severe OCD, high suicide risk.

8Never

  • A benzodiazepine for routine GAD, panic or PTSD.
  • Label everything “anxiety” — PTSD, OCD, social and health anxiety need their own therapy.
  • An antidepressant before screening for suicide risk, past mania/hypomania and safeguarding.
  • Repeat tests or reassurance in health anxiety without a clinical indication.
  • Switch before an adequate trial, or stop pregabalin abruptly.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you ever feel unsafe or have thoughts of harming yourself — 999 or A&E, NHS 111 option 2, Samaritans 116 123.”   First fortnight: “If you feel more anxious or agitated, or get new dark thoughts, in the first couple of weeks — contact us the same day.”   Don't stop dead: “Stopping suddenly can make you feel dizzy and unwell — we reduce them slowly together.”   Same week increased anxiety on a new SSRI not settling after 2 weeks, or new panic attacks when previously stable.

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
