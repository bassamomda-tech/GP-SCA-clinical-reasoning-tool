Essential tremor

For: adults with suspected or confirmed essential tremor. Not for: tremor not yet classified (start with the tremors pathway), suspected Parkinson's (use the Parkinson's protocol) or thyrotoxic tremor (use the hyperthyroidism protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG127NG71HTG474MHRADVLA
Core rule: sort rest from action tremor first, refer parkinsonian or atypical tremor untreated, exclude thyroid and drug causes, and treat essential tremor by its impact on function, not by its appearance.

0Act now — tremor with any of these is not essential tremor

  • Confusion, seizures or autonomic instability after stopping or cutting alcohol (severe withdrawal) → Urgent admission
  • Agitation, hyperreflexia and clonus after a serotonergic drug change (serotonin syndrome) → stop the drug → Same-day hospital; 999 if hyperthermia, rigidity or reduced consciousness.
  • Lead-pipe rigidity, hyperthermia and autonomic instability on an antipsychotic or dopamine blocker (NMS) → stop the drug → 999
  • Sudden focal symptoms, sudden severe headache or altered consciousness → 999 stroke/haemorrhage pathway.
  • Tachycardia, heat intolerance, weight loss → TSH and free T4 Urgently; suspected thyroid storm (fever, confusion, arrhythmia) → 999 emergency hospital care.
  • Young-onset or atypical tremor with hepatic, psychiatric or other neurological features (possible Wilson's disease) → Urgent neurology/hepatology; a normal caeruloplasmin does not exclude it.

1Confirm — at the bedside, not by scan

Suspected essential tremor: bilateral postural and kinetic tremor, absent at rest, with no other neurological signs; hands, head or voice; family history common. Rest tremor suggests, but does not establish, parkinsonism. Atypical → refer: marked asymmetry, rest tremor, dystonia, gait disorder, ataxia or rapid progression.

2Check — every new tremor

☐ Rest · arms outstretched · finger-nose · distribution; bradykinesia with decrement, rigidity, gait, cerebellar and dystonic signs  ☐ TSH (free T4 if abnormal)  ☐ Drug and substance review: β₂-agonists, lithium, valproate, SSRIs, amiodarone, theophylline, thyroid excess, caffeine  ☐ Alcohol use, AUDIT-C  ☐ Glucose, FBC, U&E, LFTs if indicated; lithium level if on lithium

Not routine: MRI in typical essential tremor; DaTscan only via neurology.

3Decide — treat impact, not amplitude

  • Everyone: explain (usually not dangerous, may progress) — often enough. Reduce caffeine; address anxiety and sleep.
  • Writing, eating, drinking, work or social life affected → drug treatment (block 5); OT for adaptive equipment.
  • Drug-induced or physiological → remove the cause. Valproate: do not stop abruptly — review with the specialist.
  • Suspected Parkinson's → refer quickly, untreated (NG71).
  • Alcohol self-medication: ask without judgement; offer treatment instead.

5Treat — when function is impaired

First linePropranolol if no contraindication: initially 40 mg 2–3 times daily; usually 80–160 mg daily
Second linePrimidone if propranolol unsuitable or fails: 50 mg in the evening, increased over 2–3 weeks
Specialist advice onlyCombinations; atenolol, topiramate (PPP), gabapentin
Refractory · specialistBotulinum toxin, DBS, focused ultrasound
  • Before propranolol: asthma, pulse, BP, heart block, heart failure, PVD/Raynaud's, diabetes, verapamil/diltiazem.
  • Primidone: warn about sedation, dizziness, ataxia, falls; specialist advice in pregnancy. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • After each dose change: response, pulse, BP, bronchospasm, sedation → lowest effective dose. Annually: still affecting life?
  • New rest tremor or slowness → reconsider Parkinson's and refer.
  • On lithium, any worse tremor → level Same day with renal function; >1.5 mmol/L is toxicity.
  • DVLA: essential tremor if it affects safe driving; Parkinson's always. Document advice.

7Refer — urgent routes are in block 0

  • Prompt neurology: parkinsonian or asymmetric tremor; slowness, stiffness, balance or gait problems; cerebellar signs; rapid progression; dystonic or functional tremor if uncertain; troublesome head or voice tremor.
  • Routine: disabling despite, or not tolerating, first-line treatment; diagnosis uncertain.
  • Progressive sub-acute loss of central neurological function → consider direct-access MRI ≤2 weeks (NG12).

8Never

  • Start levodopa or other dopaminergic treatment before specialist assessment of suspected Parkinson's.
  • Recommend alcohol as a treatment or a diagnostic test.
  • Stop valproate abruptly; use topiramate outside specialist advice and its Pregnancy Prevention Programme.
  • Propranolol in asthma, bradyarrhythmia or decompensated heart failure.
  • Image typical essential tremor routinely, or call it “benign”.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Shaking with confusion, fever, stiffness, heavy sweating, jerking or a fit — or a sudden severe headache or weakness on one side — call 999.”   New medicine: “If the tremor gets much worse after starting a new medicine, contact us within a week for a drug review.”   Lithium: “Any worse shaking — contact us the same day for a blood level.”   Changing pattern: “If the shaking starts when your hands are resting, or you are slowing down or stiffening up, come back — that changes the diagnosis.”
