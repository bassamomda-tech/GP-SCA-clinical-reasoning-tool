Frailty and medication review

For: adults aged 65 and over with frailty, multimorbidity or polypharmacy in primary care. Not for: acute delirium management (same-day assessment), people under 65 (describe function; NG56 principles), or the last days of life (care of the dying).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG56 (2016)NICE NG249 (2025)NICE NG5 (2015)STOPP/START v3 (2023)CFS v2 · eFI
Core rule: a sudden decline is acute illness until proved otherwise; frailty is the background, not the diagnosis — then review every medicine against what matters to the person, one change at a time.

0Act now — acute illness hiding behind frailty

  • NEWS2 5 or more, suspected sepsis, hypotension, hypoxia or reduced consciousness → 999 unless an agreed treatment-escalation plan says otherwise.
  • New confusion, drowsiness, falls or “off legs” over hours to days → 4AT and look for the cause (infection, AKI, retention, constipation, pain, medicines) Same-day
  • Fall with possible fracture, head injury on anticoagulant, long lie or syncope → Same-day or emergency assessment.
  • Medication harm — bleeding, hypoglycaemia, bradycardia, postural hypotension with falls, hyponatraemia, lithium or digoxin toxicity → hold the culprit, review Same-day

2Check — primary-care CGA

☐ What matters most ☐ Falls in 12 months, gait, postural BP ☐ Cognition (6CIT/Mini-Cog), mood ☐ Full medicines list incl. OTC ☐ Weight, MUST ☐ Continence ☐ Vision, hearing, feet ☐ Function, carer ☐ FBC, U&E/eGFR, LFT, TFT, bone profile. Don't miss: delirium, cancer, depression.

3Decide — NG56 approach to care

  • For frailty, falls, many services or many medicines: establish goals and treatment burden, then one individualised plan with a named coordinator.
  • Consider a medicines review for 15 or more regular medicines, or 10–14 with additional risk.
  • Weigh time to benefit of preventive drugs against prognosis and wishes. Strength and balance exercise for falls.

5Treat — the structured medication review

1 ReconcileWhat is actually taken; side effects, adherence, beliefs.
2 IndicationStill needed? Working? Time to benefit? Renal dose?
3 ScreenSTOPP/START v3, anticholinergic burden, falls-risk drugs.
4 Change oneAgreed with the person; taper drugs with withdrawal effects.
  • START: anticoagulation in AF (falls alone are not a contraindication), bone protection, laxative with opioid, vaccines.
  • Never stop for frailty alone: anticoagulants, heart-failure drugs, antiepileptics, Parkinson's drugs, steroid replacement.
  • Creatinine clearance for narrow-margin drugs. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • After each change: review at 2–6 weeks (sooner for antihypertensives, diuretics, heart-failure drugs); restart if symptoms recur.
  • Severe frailty: medication review at least annually, after discharge and after any fall.
  • Re-grade frailty annually and after admission; review ReSPECT after any change.

7Refer — involve

  • Falls service, community therapy, frailty MDT or community geriatrician, clinical pharmacist Routine
  • Memory service for suspected dementia; social care and carer's assessment; palliative care when needs are complex.

8Never

  • Call a sudden decline “just frailty” without looking for delirium, sepsis or a drug.
  • Code frailty from the eFI score alone.
  • Stop several medicines at once, or stop without the person's agreement and a restart plan.
  • Withhold anticoagulation in AF because of frailty or falls alone.
  • Leave advance care planning until the crisis.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Very unwell, struggling to breathe or can't be woken — call 999.”   Same-day “Suddenly more confused, drowsy or unable to stand over a day or two — contact us today, or 111 out of hours.”   After a change: “If symptoms come back after we stop a medicine, don't restart it yourself — tell us within a few days.”   Falls: “Tell us about any fall, even without injury.”
