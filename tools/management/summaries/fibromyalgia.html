Fibromyalgia (chronic primary pain)

For: adults with fibromyalgia or chronic widespread primary pain in primary care. Not for: under-16s (paediatric rheumatology or pain services); 16 and 17-year-olds need specialist advice before an antidepressant.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG193 (2021)EULAR 2016ACR 2016 criteriaNICE NG215
Core rule: make a positive diagnosis after excluding red flags with limited tests, then build the plan on exercise and psychological support; do not start opioids, gabapentinoids, NSAIDs, paracetamol or benzodiazepines.

0Act now — it may be something else

  • Over 50 with new headache, jaw claudication or visual disturbance → Same-day GCA pathway.
  • Suspected cauda equina or cord compression → 999 or emergency department. Suicidal thoughts with plan or intent → Same-day crisis assessment
  • Joint swelling, morning stiffness over an hour, rash, raised ESR or CRP, proximal weakness → consider inflammatory disease; rheumatology.
  • Weight loss, fever, night sweats, bone pain → investigate; suspected cancer pathway referral if NG12 criteria met.

1Confirm — a positive diagnosis

Widespread pain for 3 months or more with fatigue, unrefreshing sleep and cognitive symptoms. ACR 2016 criteria as context: WPI 7 or more with SSS 5 or more, or WPI 4 to 6 with SSS 9 or more; pain in at least 4 of 5 regions. Can coexist with other conditions.

2Check

☐ FBC, ESR or CRP  ☐ U&E, LFTs, bone profile  ☐ TSH, HbA1c  ☐ Selective: vitamin D, CK, coeliac serology  ☐ PHQ-9, GAD-7  ☐ Statins and alcohol. Not routine: ANA, rheumatoid factor, imaging.

3Decide

  • Offer a supervised group exercise programme; encourage long-term activity.
  • Consider CBT or ACT for pain; a single course of acupuncture.
  • Consider an antidepressant for adults 18 and over, after a full discussion.
  • Do not offer TENS, ultrasound, interferential or biofeedback.

4Treat — off-label; doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

Amitriptyline10 mg at night, increase gradually, usually no more than 50 mg; toxic in overdose
Duloxetine30 mg once daily, up to 60 mg; watch blood pressure
SSRIsCitalopram, fluoxetine, paroxetine or sertraline (for example sertraline 50 mg)
Do not startOpioids, gabapentinoids, NSAIDs, paracetamol, benzodiazepines, antipsychotics, trigger-point injections
  • Already on them: review together; reduce gradually; never stop abruptly.

5Monitor

  • 2 to 4 weeks after starting: side effects, adherence.
  • 8 to 12 weeks: meaningful benefit? If not, stop and try another option.
  • Every review: activity, sleep, mood, work, new symptoms.

6Refer — non-emergency

  • Rheumatology: inflammatory features or abnormal tests.
  • Pain management service: severe impact, complex medicines to reduce.
  • Mental health: moderate or severe depression or anxiety, trauma.

7Never

  • Start opioids or gabapentinoids for fibromyalgia.
  • Order ANA or rheumatoid factor without clinical features.
  • Put new symptoms down to fibromyalgia without assessing them.
  • Stop opioids, gabapentinoids or benzodiazepines abruptly.
  • Give large quantities of amitriptyline when there is suicide risk.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you might harm yourself, call 999 or go to the emergency department.”   Same day “New headache with jaw pain or sight problems — contact us the same day.”   “Weight loss, fevers, night sweats, swollen joints, new weakness or numbness — come back so we can check.”
