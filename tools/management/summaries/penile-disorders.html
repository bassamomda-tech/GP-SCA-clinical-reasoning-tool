Penile disorders

For: men presenting with a penile lesion, foreskin problem, curvature or a penile emergency in primary care. Not for: detailed balanitis management (use the balanitis protocol), or a boy's non-retractile foreskin, which is usually physiological (never force it back).

Core rule: examine every lesion properly and refer the persistent or suspicious one rather than re-treating it as infection — and recognise the four emergencies: Fournier's, ischaemic priapism, penile fracture and paraphimosis.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Genital/perineal pain out of proportion to the skin, spreading redness, fever, systemic illness (Fournier's) → Immediate emergency transfer; stop an SGLT2 inhibitor; never wait for crepitus or imaging.
  • Painful erection ≥4 hours not from sexual stimulation = ischaemic priapism → Immediate emergency/urology; record onset time; no Doppler first. Sickle cell: painful erection >1 hour is an emergency.
  • Crack during sex, sudden loss of erection, rapid haematoma (penile fracture) → 999 or immediate urology; repair within 24 h.
  • Retracted foreskin that cannot be reduced (paraphimosis) → Same-day urology; emergency transfer if unwell, glans discoloured or ischaemic, or severe pain. Reduction only by a trained clinician. Sepsis or urinary obstruction with any penile condition → Immediate emergency transfer.
  • Penile mass or ulcerated lesion (STI excluded), lesion persisting after STI treatment, unexplained persistent foreskin/glans symptoms, or erythroplasia of Queyrat → consider a Suspected cancer pathway referral (NG12). Clinically suspicious → refer without waiting; palpate and document both groins.

1Confirm — classify by site

SiteKey clues
ForeskinPathological phimosis (scarring, often lichen sclerosus/BXO); paraphimosis
Glans, skinBalanitis; lichen sclerosus: white, atrophic, shiny plaques, white preputial ring; erythroplasia of Queyrat (PeIN)
StructuralPeyronie's: plaque, curvature, active-phase pain
InfectiveHSV painful ulcers; syphilis painless indurated ulcer, non-tender nodes; discharge; warts

Lichen sclerosus is often misdiagnosed as recurrent thrush; never assume Candida from erythema alone.

2Check — consent, chaperone where appropriate

☐ Retract only if safe — never forcibly; record it  ☐ Glans, sulcus, frenulum, meatus, shaft, scrotum, both groins  ☐ Sexual history, SGLT2i, priapism drugs, diabetes, sickle cell  ☐ STI screen/NAAT, syphilis serology (lesion PCR), HSV NAAT if indicated  ☐ Subpreputial swab if recurrent or not resolving  ☐ HbA1c if recurrent candidal  ☐ HIV test if STI or risk factors

Painless ulcer → urgent syphilis testing, full STI screen. Uncertain → biopsy or specialist.

3Decide

  • Persistent or suspicious lesion → refer, not another cream; if likely infective, review after treatment.
  • Peyronie's → refer by bother, function, pain and preference, not angle.
  • Circumcision: conservative first; NHS for pathological phimosis or BXO.
  • Everyone: water or emollient washing, no soap; offer stop smoking; HPV vaccine for eligible GBMSM up to 45.

5Treat — by diagnosis

Candidal balanitisConsider clotrimazole 1% BD until resolved, usually 7–14 days. Advice before systemic antifungals
Lichen sclerosus (BXO)Consider clobetasol 0.05% ointment per BASHH/local regimen; review. Phimosis not improving → urology
First-episode herpesAciclovir 400 mg TDS or valaciclovir 500 mg BD for 5 days; adjust in renal impairment; GUM referral
Peyronie'sUsually stabilises; NSAIDs for active-phase pain; explore distress; surgery once stable
  • Bacterial balanitis: hygiene; no routine topical antibiotics; culture-directed per GM guideline.
  • Syphilis → GUM (benzathine penicillin first line, BASHH 2024). Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Lichen sclerosus: individualised long-term review; new ulcer, nodule or bleeding → Suspected cancer pathway without delay.
  • STI: HIV test with every new STI; partner notification via GUM.
  • Sickle cell: written priapism emergency plan; haematology input.

7Refer — non-emergency

  • Same-day urology recurrent or stuttering priapism; phimosis or meatal stenosis with obstructive symptoms or difficulty passing urine.
  • Routine urology: Peyronie's; phimosis needing circumcision; recurrent candidal balanitis (exclude BXO); poor stream without difficulty passing urine.
  • GUM: syphilis, herpes, discharge, warts, contact tracing.
  • Suspected cancer: LS not responding to clobetasol; possible PeIN.

8Never

  • Forcibly retract a non-retractile foreskin.
  • Re-treat a persistent lesion as infection, or treat lichen sclerosus with antifungals for months.
  • Delay transfer for imaging in Fournier's or priapism, or wait for crepitus.
  • Prescribe by phone for "recurrent thrush" without examining.
  • Promise collagenase or offer vitamin E or colchicine to reduce Peyronie's curvature.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 / A&E now “A foreskin stuck behind the head of the penis, an erection lasting more than 3 hours (a painful one over 1 hour with sickle cell disease), a sudden crack with swelling and bruising during sex, or rapidly spreading painful redness or swelling with fever — call 999 or go to A&E straight away.”   ≤7 days “A new sore, lump, hardening, bleeding, colour change, discharge or difficulty pulling the foreskin back — book an appointment within a week.”   Painless ulcer: “This needs urgent syphilis testing.”
