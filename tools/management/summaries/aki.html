Acute kidney injury (AKI) in adults

For: adults with a creatinine rise, an AKI alert or suspected AKI in primary care. Not for: children, or stable CKD with no acute change (use the CKD protocol, which also holds long-term CKD care).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG148KDIGO 2012NICE QS76UKKA hyperkalaemia 2023NG12 (myeloma)
Core rule: stage against the patient's own baseline creatinine (not eGFR), screen potassium and fluid overload first, stop NSAIDs and review every other medicine individually, and never skip the 3-month review.

0Act now — screen the killers before anything else; do not wait for repeat bloods

  • K⁺ ≥6.5 mmol/L, or hyperkalaemic ECG changes at any K⁺ → 999  K⁺ 6.0–6.4 with AKI → Same-day hospital; ECG at K⁺ ≥6.0.
  • Pulmonary oedema (breathless at rest, crackles, falling saturations); confusion, asterixis or pericardial rub; shock (systolic <90 mmHg, reduced GCS) → 999
  • Anuria with deterioration, suspected pyonephrosis with systemic illness, or sepsis with hypotension, hypoxia, altered mental state or rising lactate → 999
  • Stage 2 or stable stage 3; oliguria; creatinine doubling in 24–48 h; unable to drink; cause unclear; GN, vasculitis, AIN or myeloma suspected; HF, cirrhosis, frailty, transplant → Same-day hospital
  • Rhabdomyolysis (dark urine, severe myalgia, high CK) → Same-day hospital, 999 if unstable. Obstruction (retention, bilateral flank pain) → catheterise retention → Same-day urology
  • Aged 60+, AKI with raised calcium or leukopenia → Very urgent tests for myeloma within 48 hours; persistent bone pain or unexplained fracture → the same tests (see full protocol); suggestive → suspected cancer pathway.

1Confirm — any one criterion (NICE)

Creatinine ≥26 µmol/L rise in 48 h, or ≥50% in 7 days, or urine <0.5 mL/kg/h for >6 h.

Baseline = stable pre-illness value (7–365 days). Stage on creatinine, not eGFR; uncertain → manage to the highest plausible stage.

2Check — every suspected AKI

☐ U&E + K⁺  ☐ ECG if K⁺ ≥6.0  ☐ Volume: pulse, BP, JVP, crackles, urine output  ☐ Dipstick  ☐ FBC  ☐ Bladder  ☐ Drug chart

Don't miss: blood + protein on dipstick → possible GN → urgent nephrology. USS ≤24 h if cause unclear or obstruction possible; ≤6 h if pyonephrosis.

3Decide — very few stay at home

Home only if all apply (usually stage 1): well; clear reversible cause; able to drink; no significant oliguria; K⁺ not significantly raised; no major comorbidity or intrinsic features; reliable follow-up. Otherwise → same-day hospital.

4Target — find and fix the cause (often multifactorial)

TypeCluesAction
Pre-renalD&V, sepsis, bleeding, HF, drugsRestore perfusion

5Treat — stop, support, treat the cause

StopNSAIDs and avoidable nephrotoxins → paracetamol
HoldMetformin, SGLT2 inhibitor; lithium + urgent level and advice
Review eachACEi/ARB, diuretics — withhold if hypovolaemic, hypotensive or high K⁺
Fluid · causeDry and able to drink → oral fluids. Overloaded → none. Sepsis → 999
  • Each decision: indication, perfusion, K⁺, fluids — stopping can harm (e.g. heart failure). Document the restart plan.
  • Never stop insulin in type 1. SGLT2 inhibitor + vomiting with normal glucose → urgent ketones. Digoxin, DOACs, opioids, gabapentinoids, methotrexate → adjust or withhold. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • U&E often 24–48 hours, until recovered. Rising creatinine or K⁺, or falling urine output → Same-day hospital
  • 1 week: U&E. Restart held drugs once stable, euvolaemic, K⁺ acceptable → recheck 1–2 weeks later.
  • 3 months (sooner if higher risk): U&E, ACR, BP, medicines. CKD only if >3 months: eGFR <60 twice ≥90 days apart, or confirmed ACR ≥3 mg/mmol. Monitor ≥3 years.
  • Code the AKI even if recovered.

7Refer — beyond block 0

  • Stage 3 → nephrology discussion within 24 hours.
  • Urgent nephrology haematuria + proteinuria, vasculitis or AIN, AKI on CKD 4–5, nephrotic syndrome, not recovering.
  • After recovery, discuss with nephrology: eGFR <30, significant albuminuria, recurrent or unexplained AKI, progressive decline.

8Never

  • Extra fluid when overloaded (raised JVP, crackles) — causes pulmonary oedema.
  • Staging, or a baseline, from eGFR.
  • An NSAID left running — “triple whammy” with ACEi/ARB + diuretic.
  • Watchful waiting once a same-day threshold is crossed.
  • A skipped 3-month review, or CKD diagnosed before 3 months.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “No urine at all and feeling unwell, new breathlessness at rest, new confusion or drowsiness, severe weakness, palpitations or chest pain — call 999.”   Same-day “Not improving despite pausing the agreed tablets, passing much less urine, or you keep vomiting — contact us the same day.”   While unwell: “Drink to thirst unless you have a fluid limit, pause only the tablets we agreed, restart once eating and drinking normally — contact us if unsure or it lasts more than 24–48 hours. Avoid ibuprofen, even over the counter.”
