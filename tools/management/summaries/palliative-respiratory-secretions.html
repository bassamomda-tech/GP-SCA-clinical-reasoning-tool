Noisy respiratory secretions in the last days of life

For: adults thought to be in the last days of life with noisy upper-airway secretions, at home or in a care setting. Not for: sudden distress, stridor, choking or major bleeding (block 0), or a person who may not be dying (use the care of the dying adult protocol).

NICE NG31QS144GMMMG palliative 2025Cochrane CD005177BNF
Core rule: assess the cause and explain to the family first; an antimuscarinic is a reviewed trial for the dying person's distress — judge it by their comfort, not by whether the sound stops.

0Act now — when it is not "just secretions"; act within the person's wishes and care plan

  • Sudden acute breathlessness, stridor, choking or massive haemoptysis → a different emergency: crisis medication from the anticipatory plan and Now ring the 24-hour palliative or out-of-hours number; 999 only if it matches the person's wishes and escalation plan.
  • Not certain the person is dying (stabilising, recovering, possibly reversible) → reassess; do not default to end-of-life care.
  • New agitation or restlessness after an antimuscarinic → check for urinary retention and the drug itself (atropine, hyoscine hydrobromide); Same-day specialist palliative advice.
  • Breathless, crackles or oedema on SC/IV fluids → pulmonary oedema: consider reducing hydration ± diuretic per goals and specialist advice.
  • Tumour-related secretions, bronchorrhoea or haemoptysis → specialist palliative advice; plan for a catastrophic bleed if relevant.

1Assess the cause — NG31 asks this first

Pooled throat secretions in someone too weak to cough or swallow; usually distresses family more than the person — assess the person directly.

CauseCluesResponse
Pooled secretionsGurgling, varies with positionExplain, reposition; trial if distressed

2Check

☐ Conscious? Distressed? ☐ Throat or chest? ☐ Fever, sputum ☐ Recent choking, vomiting, feeds ☐ Fluids running, how much ☐ Antimuscarinic in the home with indication, dose, route and maximum PRN frequency

Don't miss: fluid overload from hydration — same noise, no antimuscarinic will treat it.

3Explain, then non-drug measures — everyone

  • Explain: the cause, unlikely to cause discomfort, the plan; say "noisy breathing", not "death rattle".
  • Comfortable, but family distressed → support first; a trial only after shared discussion.
  • Reposition side or semi-prone (no forced head-down); mouth care; sips if safe.
  • No routine deep suction; gentle oral suction for visible secretions only if tolerated.
  • Review hydration daily; consider reducing or stopping for overload, oedema, secretions from fluids or if unwanted.

5Antimuscarinic trial — if the dying person appears distressed

1 · DecideConsider a trial of one: atropine, glycopyrronium, hyoscine butylbromide or hyoscine hydrobromide (NG31 does not rank them).
2 · ChooseGlycopyrronium or butylbromide may be preferred if delirium or sedation is a concern; hydrobromide and atropine cross the blood–brain barrier.
3 · Give early, PRN SCFrom the anticipatory supply, e.g. glycopyrronium 200 micrograms SC PRN (GMMMG). Dries new, not pooled, secretions.
4 · Recurring and helpingSame drug by syringe pump (CSCI), PRN still available; check compatibility. Switch, never add a second.
  • Write the hyoscine salt in full. Side effects: dry mouth (more mouth care), urinary retention, constipation, tachycardia. Doses per BNF and local formulary (GMMMG).

6Review — a trial must be reviewed

  • Preferably 4-hourly, at least 12-hourly: comfort, agitation, sedation, dry mouth, retention, PRN use.
  • Still distressing after 12 h → consider changing or stopping (may take up to 12 h to work).
  • Unconscious and comfortable → do not escalate just to stop the sound.
  • Daily: hydration; still dying, or stabilising?

7Specialist palliative care advice

  • Not responding to repositioning plus a trial; diagnostic uncertainty (secretions, oedema, infection, aspiration).
  • Family very distressed or in conflict → hospice family support; bereavement support afterwards.

8Never

  • Start a drug without assessing the cause — oedema from fluids will not respond.
  • Leave a trial unreviewed, or escalate to silence a comfortable, unconscious person.
  • Routine deep suction; two antimuscarinics at once.
  • Miss central effects of atropine or hydrobromide as "terminal agitation" and sedate more.
  • Promise silence, or treat the rattle as proof that death is imminent.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

“The noisy breathing may come and go; turning her onto her side often helps. The nurses will review the medicine within the next few hours and at least twice a day.”   Straight away, day or night “If she seems to struggle to breathe, looks distressed or in pain, is restless or agitated, or you're worried, ring the district nurse, hospice or out-of-hours number on the care plan — don't wait. Severe distress, choking or major bleeding → follow the agreed escalation plan; 999 only if consistent with her wishes and goals of care.” Care plan shared with district nurses and out-of-hours.
