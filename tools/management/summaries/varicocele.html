Varicocele

For: adolescents and men with a scrotal swelling that may be a varicocele. Not for: acute scrotal pain (suspected torsion: immediate surgical/urology assessment), a lump in the testis itself (NG12 pathway), or a prepubertal boy (paediatric urology).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG257 (Mar 2026)NICE NG12 (updated Apr 2026)ESUR-SPIWG 2020EAU 2025EAU/ESPU 2024
Core rule: a typical varicocele is left-sided and empties lying flat; image one that is isolated right-sided, sudden in onset or non-reducing, and always feel each testis separately.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Sudden severe testicular pain (nausea/vomiting, high-riding or horizontal testis, onset in sleep) → immediate surgical/urology assessment Same-day, now — do not delay for ultrasound, urine tests or a sexual-health appointment. A varicocele does not cause acute severe pain.
  • Isolated right-sided, sudden-onset or non-reducing varicocele, or any in a prepubertal boy (plus paediatric urology) → scrotal and renal/retroperitoneal ultrasound via the local pathway, stating the reason; urgency set by onset, pain, failure to reduce and abdominal findings. Abdominal mass, systemic illness or suspected malignancy → Urgent escalation
  • Non-painful enlargement or change in shape or texture of the testis → consider a suspected cancer pathway referral Urgent · NG12. Unexplained or persistent testicular symptoms → consider a direct access ultrasound.
  • Aged 45 and over with unexplained visible haematuria without UTI, or persisting/recurring after a treated UTI → suspected cancer pathway (bladder or renal) Urgent · NG12

1Confirm — examine standing, Valsalva, then lying flat

Typical: left-sided, soft ‘bag of worms’ above the testis, fills standing, empties lying flat. Not clearly separable from the testis → investigate.

GradeFinding
1 · 2 · 3Palpable only on Valsalva · palpable standing at rest · visible
SubclinicalSeen only on ultrasound → not treated

2Check — every new varicocele

☐ Side ☐ Empties lying flat ☐ Each testis: size, consistency, lump ☐ Abdomen/loin mass ☐ Volumes recorded (adolescents)

Urine only if urinary symptoms, suspected renal disease or reported haematuria. Doppler ultrasound if uncertain, atypical, volume concern, unexplained pain or lesion to exclude.

3Decide — ask what bothers him

  • Typical, asymptomatic → explanation only; no routine imaging or follow-up. Ache → block 5.
  • Couple trying to conceive → semen analysis; repeat an abnormal result at about 3 months (sooner if azoospermia or severe oligozoospermia).
  • Clinical varicocele + reduced semen parameters → refer the couple to consider radiological or surgical treatment, taking female factors into account.
  • Subclinical, or normal semen → no repair for fertility.

4Target — adolescents: watch the testis

Varicocele-side testisAction
Smaller by >2 mL or 20% on two assessmentsPaediatric urology
One marginal orchidometer readingNo surgery; re-measure

5Treat — no medicine shrinks a varicocele

Step 1Explanation: common, benign, does not turn into cancer; most men have normal fertility
Step 2Snug briefs or athletic support; rest lying down; less prolonged standing
Step 3Paracetamol or an NSAID as needed for a flare of aching
Step 4 · persistent painRoutine urology; ligation or embolisation is urology-led
  • NSAIDs: avoid or use cautiously in renal disease, peptic ulcer, anticoagulation, significant cardiovascular disease or NSAID-sensitive asthma.
  • Doses per BNF and Greater Manchester formulary.

6Monitor

  • Typical adult varicocele: no routine follow-up.
  • Adolescents: record both testicular volumes at intervals through puberty.
  • Trying to conceive: semen analysis at the NG257 time point; refer the couple with both partners' results.
  • Change in character (right-sided, stops emptying, testicular lump, visible haematuria) → re-examine and act on block 0.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Urology: persistent troublesome pain despite support and analgesia. First exclude other scrotal, groin, hip or neuropathic causes; pain may persist after repair.
  • Fertility/urology: clinical varicocele with reduced semen parameters on repeated analysis, alongside assessment of the female partner.
  • Paediatric urology: persistent volume difference >2 mL or 20%, bilateral palpable varicoceles, symptoms or significant distress.

8Never

  • Let a coexisting varicocele reassure you about a lump in the testis.
  • Delay suspected torsion for ultrasound, urine testing or a sexual-health appointment.
  • Call an isolated right-sided, sudden or non-emptying varicocele typical without imaging.
  • Treat, or scan to hunt for, a subclinical varicocele.
  • Refer straight for surgery for fertility without semen analysis and assessment of both partners.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden, severe pain in the testicle — go to A&E or call 999 straight away.”   Within a week “A swelling on the right side, one that stops going down when you lie flat, a lump in the testicle itself, or blood in your urine — book to see us within a week.”   Ache: “Supportive underwear and painkillers help; come back if it keeps bothering you.”
