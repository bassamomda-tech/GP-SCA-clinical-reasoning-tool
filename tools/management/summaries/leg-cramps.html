Leg cramps

For: adults with cramp-like leg pain in primary care, including pregnancy. Not for: restless legs (restless legs protocol), claudication (local PAD pathway) or suspected DVT (Wells pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
MHRA quinine (2010)Cochrane 2015NICE CG147NICE NG12SPC (emc)
Core rule: cramp is a diagnosis of exclusion — rule out limb ischaemia, DVT, claudication and MND first; stretching is the treatment, and quinine is a last resort with a 4-week trial and 3-monthly review.

0Act now — the mimics are the whole risk

  • Sudden painful, pale or mottled, cold leg with absent or reduced pulses, sensory loss or weakness (acute limb ischaemia) → 999 — do not wait for ABPI or imaging.
  • Unilateral calf swelling, pain, warmth or tenderness → Same-day 2-level Wells DVT pathway (interim anticoagulation if ultrasound delayed). Pregnancy → pregnancy-specific pathway.
  • Severe or symptomatic hypokalaemia (arrhythmia, ECG changes, weakness, paralysis) → 999 or Same-day correction
  • Rest pain, tissue loss, gangrene or non-healing ulcer (chronic limb-threatening ischaemia) → Urgent vascular
  • Progressive objective weakness, wasting, bulbar symptoms, widespread fasciculations or mixed upper/lower motor neuron signs → Urgent neurology
  • Statin + muscle pain or weakness → CK, renal function. CK markedly raised (especially >10× ULN) or dark urine (rhabdomyolysis) → stop statin, Urgent assessment

1Confirm — a true cramp, not a mimic

True cramp: sudden painful involuntary contraction, palpable tightening, calf or foot, <10 min, relieved by stretching.

2Check — targeted, not routine

☐ Drugs: diuretics, statins, beta-agonists, nifedipine, clonazepam withdrawal, alcohol, dialysis  ☐ Pulses, neurology, calf, muscle bulk, thyroid, fluids  ☐ Frequent, severe, new or progressive cramps, or risk factors → U&E, calcium, magnesium if indicated (request separately), HbA1c, TFTs

Typical occasional cramps, normal examination → may need no tests. Unexplained lump increasing in size → consider urgent direct-access ultrasound (NG12).

3Decide — idiopathic or secondary

  • Idiopathic (most nocturnal cramps over 50) → conservative, in primary care.
  • Secondary → treat the cause (step 1). Do not stop a statin for isolated uncomplicated cramps without assessing the whole picture.
  • Pregnancy → stretching, reassurance, hydration and medicine review; avoid quinine; no magnesium unless deficient or specialist-advised.
  • Self-care: hydration (keep any fluid plan), footwear, untucked sheets, warm-down, less alcohol and caffeine.

5Treat — cause first, then the ladder

Step 1 · alwaysCorrect documented electrolytes; review culprit drug; treat thyroid, diabetes, CKD
Step 2 · first-lineCalf and hamstring stretches nightly before sleep: wall, knee straight, heel down, 30 s each leg
Step 3 · not routineMagnesium only for shown or strongly suspected deficiency; elemental dose, renal function
Step 4 · last resortQuinine sulfate 200–300 mg at night: very painful or frequent cramps disturbing sleep, steps 1–3 failed
  • Before quinine: QT risk, interactions (warfarin, digoxin), renal/hepatic disease. Contraindicated: haemoglobinuria, myasthenia gravis, optic neuritis, tinnitus.
  • No routine vitamin E or B. Refractory → reassess, specialist advice. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Quinine at ~4 weeks: no clear benefit → stop.
  • Quinine about every 3 months: reassess need, lowest dose, ask about bleeding, tinnitus, vision; trial a stop.
  • Annually: medication review; electrolytes on diuretics or in CKD.

7Refer — routine

  • Confirmed PAD → vascular assessment, supervised exercise, CV risk reduction.
  • Refractory cramps, diagnostic doubt, or suspected neurological, vascular or metabolic disease.

8Never

  • Call a swollen, warm calf "cramp", or exclude DVT in pregnancy on D-dimer alone.
  • Treat reproducible walking pain as cramp — it delays revascularisation.
  • Quinine routinely, before conservative measures, in pregnancy (unless specialist-advised), or without 3-monthly review.
  • Fixed over-the-counter magnesium for everyone, or magnesium assumed to be in a U&E.
  • Homan's sign to rule DVT in or out.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden cold, pale, painful leg, or palpitations with muscle weakness — call 999.”   Same-day “New swelling, pain or warmth in one calf, or new or worsening numbness or weakness in your legs — contact us the same day.”   Walking pain: “Calf pain that comes on when you walk and eases with rest — book a GP appointment.”   Quinine: “Stop it and contact us the same day for ringing ears, hearing or vision change, confusion, bruising or bleeding. Keep it away from children.”
