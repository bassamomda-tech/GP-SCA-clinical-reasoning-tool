Suspected pulmonary embolism

For: adults with suspected or confirmed PE in primary care, including follow-up. Not for: pregnancy or postpartum (urgent obstetric medicine via the local RCOG-based pathway — NG158 does not cover pregnancy); suspected DVT alone (use the DVT protocol); AF anticoagulation (use the AF protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG158 (updated 2023)BTS 2018RCOG Green-topNG12 (April 2026)ESC 2019
Core rule: assess stability before any score; D-dimer belongs only to the Wells-unlikely group (≤4) — Wells-likely goes straight to same-day imaging; and PE DOAC dosing has a loading phase that AF dosing does not.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Hypotension, shock, peri-arrest physiology or severe deterioration → 999 — do not score, D-dimer or wait for CTPA.
  • Syncope or pre-syncope with risk factors (can be the only sign of a large PE) → Same-day assessment; 999 if hypotension, shock or severe compromise.
  • Stable suspected PE: Wells >4, or Wells ≤4 with a positive D-dimer → Same-day acute medical / ambulatory care for CTPA and treatment.
  • Suspected PE in pregnancy or postpartum → Same-day obstetric medicine (RCOG pathway); therapeutic LMWH while investigating if suspicion is genuine, unless contraindicated.
  • Major bleeding, or head injury, while anticoagulated → A&E same day, however well they feel.
  • On treatment with deteriorating observations or hypotension (possible recurrent or high-risk PE) → 999

1Confirm — two-level Wells, then the fork

Think PE in any breathless or collapsed patient with risk factors — isolated breathlessness or tachycardia, syncope, a “chest infection” not settling, new AF, hypoxia. A normal chest, temperature or X-ray does not exclude it.

Wells: DVT signs 3 · PE most likely diagnosis 3 · HR >100 1.5 · immobilisation or surgery in previous 4 weeks 1.5 · previous DVT/PE 1.5 · haemoptysis 1 · active cancer 1.

WellsNext step
Likely >4Same-day CTPA (V/Q if unsuitable). No D-dimer. Imaging delayed → offer interim anticoagulation via the local pathway.
Unlikely ≤4D-dimer, result ≤4 hours (else offer interim anticoagulation). Negative → stop interim, reassess. Positive → same-day imaging.

2Check — record it all, with your reasoning

☐ BP, HR, RR, SpO₂, mental state  ☐ Risk factors (surgery, immobility, cancer, VTE, pregnancy, oestrogen)  ☐ Chest X-ray where appropriate  ☐ Bleeding risk  ☐ FBC, renal, hepatic, PT, APTT — don't delay treatment; review ≤24 h

Don't miss: “chest infection” not responding → reconsider the diagnosis, not the antibiotic.

3Decide — whose decision

  • Diagnosis and outpatient care → local acute / ambulatory pathway; no home management from general practice without one.
  • Active cancer → consider a DOAC or LMWH by tumour site, bleeding risk, renal function and interactions.
  • Unprovoked → history, examination, baseline bloods; new symptoms → NG12 route.
  • Severe renal impairment → specialist advice.

4Duration — formal review at 3 months

SituationDecision
Provoked, factor resolvedConsider stopping at 3 months (not automatic)
Unprovoked or ongoing riskConsider continuing; weigh bleeding risk
Active cancerAt least 3–6 months; review at 3–6 months
Long termReview annually

5Treat — VTE doses, not AF doses

Apixaban10 mg BD for 7 days, then 5 mg BD
Rivaroxaban15 mg BD for 21 days, then 20 mg OD with food
Edoxaban or dabigatranOnly after at least 5 days of LMWH
DOAC unsuitableLMWH ± VKA, per local protocol
  • Before any DOAC: creatinine clearance (Cockcroft–Gault), weight, liver, interactions, antiplatelets/NSAIDs. Pregnancy → therapeutic LMWH.
  • Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • First contact: agent, loading dose and step-down, duration, adherence. Every review: bleeding; falling Hb → find the source.
  • DOAC bloods: FBC, renal, liver at baseline; renal ≥ annually (more often in CKD, frailty, illness).
  • 3–6 months: still breathless → recurrent VTE, other causes (anaemia, cardiac, COPD, deconditioning); echo where appropriate.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Haematology: unprovoked PE duration, cancer-associated, suspected antiphospholipid syndrome, recurrent VTE.
  • CTEPH / pulmonary hypertension: unexplained breathlessness after PE.
  • Trigger: stop combined pill or HRT if causal; safe contraception.

8Never

  • A D-dimer to “screen” a Wells-likely patient — it delays imaging and a negative result does not rule out PE there.
  • A Wells score or D-dimer that delays 999 transfer for an unstable patient.
  • AF DOAC doses for PE — the loading phase is missed.
  • The non-pregnant Wells/D-dimer pathway in pregnancy, or a DOAC in pregnancy or breastfeeding.
  • A blanket cancer screen for unprovoked PE, or routine thrombophilia testing during the acute event or while continuing anticoagulation.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you suddenly become more breathless, have chest pain or collapse, call 999 — don't wait.”   Blood thinner: “Tell us the same day about any unusual bleeding; heavy bleeding, or any knock to the head, means A&E straight away.”   Duration: “Usually at least three months; with no obvious trigger we often continue for longer.”   Follow-up: “Still breathless in a few months — come back; there is a specific thing we check for.”
