Palliative anxiety and depression

For: adults with advanced illness, including the last days of life, with low mood or anxiety. Not for: delirium as the main problem (use the palliative delirium protocol); pregnancy or breastfeeding (separate perinatal pathway and specialist discussion).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG222 (updated Dec 2025)CG113NG225NG31Local palliative 2025
Core rule: ask plainly about suicide, consider delirium and other mimics before labelling depression, offer support to everyone, and use an antidepressant only when prognosis and route allow weeks for benefit.

0Act now — each needs a same-day decision

  • Suicidal plan, intent or means → Same-day crisis or liaison mental health; shared plan for stored medicines (who holds them, limit quantities).
  • Psychotic depression or refusing food and drink from depressive beliefs → Same-day psychiatric assessment
  • Sudden change in behaviour, alertness or attention → delirium until shown otherwise; find the cause → Same-day
  • Agitation, tremor, sweating, fever on serotonergic drugs → stop them → Same-day; high fever, rigidity, seizures or reduced consciousness → 999
  • Mood change after a steroid, or akathisia after an antiemetic or antipsychotic → review, reduce or stop it → Same-day review
  • Low mood with thirst, confusion, constipation → hypercalcaemia? Calcium if it fits goals of care → Same-day decision

1Confirm — distress or disorder?

Understandable distressLikely depressive disorder
MoodSad about losses; still some pleasurePervasive, out of proportion
Self, futureGrief; can still planGuilt, worthless, hopeless
DeathWishes it would come soonPersistent wish to die

Lean on psychological, not somatic, symptoms. Tools (PHQ-9, Brief Edinburgh) only if they add value. Anxiety: ask what is feared; don't force a GAD label.

2Check — drivers and mimics

☐ Suicide, stored medicines ☐ Delirium ☐ Pain, breathlessness, insomnia ☐ Steroids, antiemetics, withdrawal, alcohol ☐ Calcium, sodium only if it changes management ☐ What worked before

Don't miss: hypoactive delirium looks like depression.

3Decide — support first, drug only if time allows

  • Everyone → time, honest information, family, spiritual and practical help, psychology.
  • Less severe depression → psychological help first; no routine antidepressant unless preferred.
  • Disorder persists, patient wants a drug, prognosis and route allow time → consider an SSRI or mirtazapine.
  • Prognosis of weeks or days → support, symptom control, specialist palliative input.
  • Already on one → continue if helping; withdraw gradually where possible.

5Treat — no antidepressant clearly better; previous responder first

Sertraline25–50 mg daily (25 mg if frail). Fewer interactions.
CitalopramMax 40 mg; 20 mg over 65 or hepatic impairment. QT risk.
Mirtazapine15 mg at night if frail; insomnia, anorexia. Sedation, falls.
BenzodiazepineLorazepam SL; midazolam SC. Doses per local guidance.
  • Check: tramadol (serotonin), QT drugs (methadone, haloperidol, ondansetron), NSAID or anticoagulant (gastroprotect).
  • Continuous anxiety → specialist palliative advice; diazepam is not routine.
  • With an opioid: benzodiazepine only if justified, lowest dose, monitored. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Antidepressant: 1 week if 18–25 or suicide risk, else 1–2 weeks; response 2–4 weeks; judge at 4–8 weeks.
  • New confusion or falls on an SSRI or mirtazapine → think hyponatraemia.
  • Benzodiazepine: every visit — sedation, delirium, slow breathing.
  • Re-look early with deterioration, new delirium or a new steroid, antiemetic or opioid.

7Refer

  • Severe depression without psychosis, ideation without plan, or unclear vs delirium → Urgent psychiatry
  • Last-days distress not settling → Same-day specialist palliative
  • Palliative team: complex distress, request to hasten death, prognosis too short for a drug.
  • Local: community palliative team, local Hospice, district nurses; out of hours GP and hospice advice line.

8Never

  • A suicide risk score or low/medium/high label to decide care.
  • A diagnosis of primary depression before delirium has been considered.
  • A benzodiazepine for akathisia — reduce or stop the causative drug.
  • Long-term benzodiazepine for GAD, or escalating doses for delirium.
  • Citalopram or escitalopram with QT prolongation or a QT-prolonging drug.

9Safety-net — say it to patient and carers, then document it

999 “Very drowsy with slow breathing after a medicine dose, or in immediate danger of harming themselves — call 999.”   Same-day “Talk of ending life or a plan, sudden confusion or agitation, seeing or hearing things — ring us, out of hours or the palliative team today.”   Within a day: “Worse despite the plan, new side effects (restlessness, sickness, diarrhoea, falls) or sleep broken down.”   Antidepressant: “It takes a few weeks. If you ever feel you can't go on, tell us straight away.”
