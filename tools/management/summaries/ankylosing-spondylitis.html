Axial spondyloarthritis (ankylosing spondylitis)

For: over-16s with suspected or established axial SpA. Not for: under-16s (outside NG65); red-eye or cauda equina management (use those protocols).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG65QS170NICE TA383 and later TAsBSR 2025 axSpAMHRA 2023 (JAK)
Core rule: back pain that started before 45 and has lasted over 3 months → count the NG65 criteria; normal CRP/ESR, negative HLA-B27 or a normal X-ray never rule it out — and new pain after minor trauma in a rigid spine is a fracture until proven otherwise.

0Act now — screen for these first

  • New or changed neck or back pain after even minor trauma (fall from standing) in established axSpA → unstable spinal injury: avoid moving or realigning → Same-day ED / spinal team for CT ± MRI (plain X-ray misses it).
  • Bilateral sciatica, saddle or perineal numbness, new bladder or bowel disturbance, sexual dysfunction → Same-day emergency MRI via the cauda equina pathway.
  • Painful red eye, photophobia or blurred vision → Same-day ophthalmology. No steroid drops without ophthalmological examination.
  • Fever or significant infection on a TNF, IL-17 or JAK inhibitor → Same-day assessment; CRP may be blunted; consider TB. Serious → ask rheumatology about withholding.
  • New pain over 50, weight loss, past cancer, fever, immunosuppression, progressive neurology → think cancer or infection first (NG59).

1Confirm — the NG65 referral rule; rheumatology diagnoses

Low back pain that started before 45 and has lasted longer than 3 months → count 9 criteria: onset <35 · waking in second half of night · buttock pain · better with movement · better within 48 h of NSAIDs · first-degree relative with SpA · current/past arthritis, enthesitis or psoriasis (not uveitis).

2Check — tests support the referral; none decides it

☐ Eyes, skin, bowel, heels, family history  ☐ CRP and ESR  ☐ FBC, renal function and LFTs before regular NSAIDs  ☐ HLA-B27 only for exactly 3 criteria  ☐ Imaging per local arrangements: SI-joint X-ray, then inflammatory-protocol MRI if nondiagnostic

Don't “exclude” it with lumbar X-ray or standard lumbar MRI, or screen nonspecific back pain with HLA-B27.

3Decide — start while the referral is pending

  • Everyone → specialist physio, structured exercise, lifelong (modify, don't stop, in flares); stop smoking; NASS; hydrotherapy may help as an adjunct.
  • Symptomatic → offer an NSAID at the lowest effective dose, with gastroprotection; assess GI, renal and CV risk.
  • Persistently active despite NSAIDs and exercise → rheumatology (advice line or expedited).

5Treat — exercise → NSAID → rheumatology

Step 1 · everyoneSpecialist physiotherapy and structured exercise
Step 2 · first-line drugRegular NSAID + gastroprotection; poor relief after 2–4 weeks at max tolerated dose → consider switching
AdjunctParacetamol may be used. No routine opioids
Step 3 · rheumatologyTNF, IL-17 or JAK inhibitor, chosen and prescribed by rheumatology
  • Sulfasalazine / methotrexate treat peripheral joints only, not axial disease.
  • JAK inhibitor (MHRA): 65+, long-term smoker, or CV or cancer risk → only if no alternative.
  • IL-17 inhibitor + new diarrhoea or rectal bleeding → consider IBD. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — shared care

  • NSAID: BP, renal function, GI symptoms at start, after dose rises, then at least yearly → stop or switch if eGFR falls, BP rises or GI bleed.
  • Biologic or DMARD: per shared-care protocol. Flu yearly; pneumococcal, COVID-19, Shingrix as eligible.
  • CV and fracture risk periodically; spinal DXA can read falsely high — hip is more reliable.
  • Annual review: function, work, mood, eyes, skin, bowel, pregnancy plans; DVLA if AS affects driving.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Suspected axSpA → rheumatology (routine or early arthritis pathway): criteria, HLA-B27, CRP/ESR, extra-articular features, NSAID response.
  • Persistent diarrhoea, blood or weight loss → calprotectin, gastroenterology. New psoriasis → treat, tell rheumatology.
  • Pregnancy planning → rheumatology advice; never stop a biologic abruptly. No NSAIDs from 20 weeks unless essential.

8Never

  • Ruling out axSpA on normal CRP/ESR, negative HLA-B27 or X-ray — or a fracture in a fused spine on a normal plain film.
  • Steroid eye drops (new or reused) without ophthalmological examination.
  • Routine opioids, long-term oral steroids, methotrexate or sulfasalazine for spinal symptoms.
  • Stopping or restarting a biologic without a specialist plan, or a live vaccine without specialist approval.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day, now “Numbness between your legs or around your bottom, bladder or bowel trouble, or weakness in both legs — go to A&E straight away.”   Same-day After a fall: “New or different neck or back pain after any fall or accident — go to A&E the same day; don't wait to see if it settles.”   Same-day Eye: “A painful red eye, light hurting or blurred vision — get it seen by an eye service the same day.”   Same-day On a biologic: “Fever or feeling unwell with an infection — contact us or your rheumatology team the same day. Don't stop or restart your medicine yourself; tell anyone offering a vaccine.”
