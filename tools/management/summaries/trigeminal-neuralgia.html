Trigeminal neuralgia

For: adults with suspected or diagnosed trigeminal neuralgia in primary care. Not for: other neuropathic pain (use the neuropathic pain protocol), post-herpetic neuralgia or giant cell arteritis (use those protocols).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG173 (updated 2020)NG127BNF / MHRAFSRHEAN 2019
Core rule: carbamazepine first-line (not the usual neuropathic-pain four), MRI in the early work-up for everyone, and any rash, sore throat, fever or bruising on carbamazepine means stop it and be seen the same day.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Rash, mucosal lesion, blistering, facial oedema or fever on carbamazepine → stop it, Same-day assessment; suspected SJS/TEN → Emergency assessment. Never restart.
  • Sore throat, mouth ulcers, fever, unusual bruising or bleeding → withhold, Same-day FBC; suspected neutropenia, thrombocytopenia or pancytopenia → urgent hospital assessment.
  • Persistent facial numbness, facial weakness or abnormal neurological signs → Urgent neuroimaging (NG127). Under 40, bilateral, hearing or ear symptoms, previous optic neuritis → expedite MRI and neurology.
  • Active suicidal intent → Same-day mental-health assessment; do not leave the patient unsupported.
  • Cannot maintain oral intake (weight loss, dehydration) → Same-day assessment.
  • Pregnant on carbamazepine → do not stop abruptly; Urgent specialist review.

1Confirm — clinical diagnosis, then screen for a secondary cause

Brief, paroxysmal, electric-shock or stabbing pain in a trigeminal division (usually V2/V3), unilateral, triggered by innocuous stimuli (touch, chewing, talking, cold wind, brushing teeth), pain-free between attacks.

Think again: continuous pain, burning or allodynia → another facial-pain syndrome. Sensory loss, facial weakness, cranial-nerve or hearing signs, bilateral, onset under 40, optic neuritis, malignancy or poor response → secondary (MS, tumour): expedite.

Differentials: dental, TMD, post-herpetic neuralgia, cluster headache, GCA, sinus.

2Check — before starting carbamazepine

☐ Cranial nerves  ☐ MRI for everyone  ☐ FBC, U&E with sodium, LFTs  ☐ Pregnancy intention, contraception, co-prescriptions  ☐ HLA-B*15:02 if Han Chinese or Thai  ☐ Eating, weight, mood. Don't miss: a normal examination does not exclude secondary TN.

3Decide

  • Offer carbamazepine as initial treatment while arranging work-up — not amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin or pregabalin.
  • Not tolerated, ineffective or contraindicated → early specialist referral. Severe pain → consider referral at any stage.

4Support — alongside the drug

  • Triggers: avoid cold wind (scarf), chewing, shaving, light touch.
  • Flares: soft or liquid, lukewarm food; check weight and hydration. Soft brush, lukewarm water; exclude dental causes.
  • Mood: ask about self-harm when pain is severe — a reason to expedite.

5Treat — carbamazepine

Start100 mg once or twice daily
TitrateGradually, with early review; many respond ~200 mg 3–4 times daily
MaintainLowest effective dose; reduce during remissions
SpecialistNo escalation without advice (max per SPC; GM 1,600 mg/day); oxcarbazepine, lamotrigine, gabapentin, baclofen
  • Enzyme inducer: pill, implant, some emergency contraception unreliable → copper IUD, LNG-IUD or DMPA. DOACs generally avoided; warfarin closer INR.
  • Fetal risk: specialist advice before conception; MHRA advice incl. folic acid. Counsel on dizziness, diplopia and driving. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • FBC, U&E with sodium, LFTs: baseline, after titration, then per SPC/local policy; any time if symptomatic. Sodium especially if elderly, on diuretics, drowsy or confused.
  • Co-prescriptions at every dose change. Plasma levels not routine (toxicity, adherence, pregnancy, interactions).

7Refer — neurology or cranial-facial pain service

  • Early: refractory, intolerant or contraindicated; severe pain, functional impairment or nutritional compromise. No MRI possible → consider specialist review.
  • Surgery (microvascular decompression, ablation) is a specialist decision — refer via neurology.

8Never

  • The usual neuropathic-pain ladder first-line for trigeminal neuralgia.
  • Skip MRI because the examination is normal.
  • Assume a combined pill or implant is reliable on carbamazepine.
  • Restart carbamazepine after a suspected serious skin reaction, or stop it abruptly in pregnancy.
  • Keep escalating the dose, or trial drug after drug, without specialist advice.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “Any rash, blistering, mouth sores or facial swelling, or a sore throat, mouth ulcers, fever, unusual bruising or bleeding — stop the carbamazepine and contact us the same day for a blood count. Severe dizziness, double vision, confusion or marked drowsiness — contact us urgently; don't drive if dizzy or seeing double.”   Mood: “If you feel you cannot carry on, tell us the same day. If you might act on these thoughts or cannot keep yourself safe — call 999 or go to A&E now.”   Eating: “If the pain stops you eating or drinking, contact us the same day.”   Contraception: “This tablet can stop the pill and implant working. If you become pregnant, don't stop it suddenly — contact us urgently.”
