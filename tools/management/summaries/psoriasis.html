Psoriasis

For: adults with psoriasis managed in primary care. Not for: children and young people (specialist at presentation), erythroderma or generalised pustular psoriasis (same-day specialist), or starting systemic treatment (specialist, shared care).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG153 (updated 2017)QS40NG65NG12 (April 2026)MHRA
Core rule: exclude erythroderma and generalised pustular psoriasis first, treat by body site in timed courses with 4-week steroid breaks and a booked review, and check joints, heart and mood every year.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Erythroderma (inflammation over most of the skin, scaling, shivering, unwell) or generalised pustular psoriasis (sheets of sterile pustules on red skin, fever, malaise) → Same-day specialist assessment and treatment.
  • Acute, unstable or rapidly worsening widespread psoriasis without those features → Urgent specialist advice via the local dermatology pathway, not another topical course.
  • Suspected psoriatic arthritis → rheumatology as soon as suspected; new-onset inflammatory arthritis → Urgent rheumatology
  • Solitary fixed, scaly, crusted, bleeding, ulcerated or indurated lesion that does not behave like psoriasis → reassess, biopsy or refer; suspicion of SCC → Consider suspected cancer pathway (NG12).

1Confirm — type, severity and impact at diagnosis and every review

RecordHow
TypePlaque, guttate (look for streptococcal infection), scalp, flexural, nail, palmoplantar pustular

MoodAssess for depression in everyone, and when escalating treatment

2Check — beyond the skin

☐ PEST (adults): ≥3 → rheumatology referral or advice  ☐ Ask about inflammatory back pain, dactylitis, enthesitis, morning stiffness, swollen joints  ☐ All adults: discuss smoking, alcohol, weight, BP, diabetes, lipids  ☐ Aggravating drugs: lithium, beta-blockers, antimalarials, steroid withdrawal

Don't miss: a low PEST score does not exclude psoriatic arthritis, and PEST misses axial disease.

3Decide — for everyone

  • Everyone: emollients, enough active treatment, how to apply it, a plan with dates; help with smoking, alcohol and weight.
  • Severe psoriasis (needing phototherapy, systemic treatment or admission) → offer a formal QRISK-based CV risk assessment.

5Treat — trunk and limbs (CG153)

Step 1 · up to 4 weeksPotent steroid OD + vitamin D OD (one am, one pm). Improving at 4 weeks → max 8 weeks.
Step 2 · by 8 weeksNot controlled → vitamin D alone twice daily
Step 3 · after 8–12 weeksPotent steroid BD ≤4 weeks, or coal tar OD–BD
Step 4 · not enoughRefer: phototherapy or systemic (block 7)
  • Combined calcipotriol + betamethasone OD ≤4 weeks if BD steroid or coal tar can't be used, or for adherence.
  • Scalp: potent OD ≤4 weeks → new formulation / descale → combined product ≤4 weeks → very potent BD 2 weeks (adults), coal tar or refer.
  • Face, flexures, genitals: mild/moderate steroid inadequate → offer a calcineurin inhibitor BD ≤4 weeks (adults, off-label, expert-started).
  • Limits: calcipotriol ≤100 g/week; Dovobet ≤15 g/day, 100 g/week, ≤30% of body (SPC). Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • New topical: review at 4 weeks (2 in children; ~2 for very potent scalp or face/flexure/genital steroid).
  • Annually: PsA, CV risk factors, mood, smoking, alcohol, weight, steroid quantities. Severe: formal CV risk at least 5-yearly.
  • Shared care (methotrexate, ciclosporin): FBC, U&E, LFTs; BP on ciclosporin. Biologics: no live vaccines unless specialist advises; low threshold to assess infection.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Phototherapy or systemic: not controlled topically and significant impact and >10% BSA or PASI >10, disabling local disease, or phototherapy relapse.
  • Dermatology: uncertain diagnosis; guttate needing phototherapy; nail disease with major impact; any child at presentation.
  • Rheumatology: dactylitis; persistent or multisite non-mechanical enthesitis.

8Never

  • Potent or very potent steroids on the face, flexures or genitals.
  • Repeat potent steroids with no 4-week break and no planned review.
  • Start or abruptly stop systemic steroids without specialist input — rebound pustular or erythrodermic flare.
  • Methotrexate daily — once weekly, day in full, shared care only.
  • Exclude psoriatic arthritis on a low PEST score.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “If redness spreads over most of your skin, or you get widespread pus spots, shivers, a fever or feel unwell, contact us, or NHS 111 if we are closed, the same day.”   Methotrexate: “Once a week only. If you take too much, get medical help straight away, the same day.”   Steroid creams: “Redness, burning or stinging after stopping — see us before restarting.”   Emollients: “No smoking or naked flames — residue on fabric catches fire fast.”
