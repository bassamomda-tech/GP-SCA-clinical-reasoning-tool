Tension-type headache

For: people with recurrent headache that fits tension-type headache, including medication-overuse headache (MOH). Not for: new or undifferentiated headache (use the headache pathway); migraine (use the migraine protocol); suspected giant cell arteritis (use the GCA protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG150 (updated June 2025)ICHD-3BASH (2019)NICE NG12 (April 2026)
Core rule: screen red flags and examine before the label, diagnose positively and actively reconsider migraine, count headache days and medication days (including bought codeine), and cap acute medicines by days a month — never opioids.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Thunderclap headache (maximal within 5 minutes) → 999
  • Fever, neck stiffness, non-blanching rash or altered consciousness → 999; benzylpenicillin if meningococcal, only if no delay.
  • New or atypical headache in pregnancy or postpartum → Same-day obstetric or emergency
  • New or changed headache over 50 → assess for GCA, inflammatory markers if possible; visual symptoms or ischaemic features → Same-day GCA pathway
  • Focal neurological sign, personality change or new seizure → Urgent imaging and referral, same-day if acute. Papilloedema → Same-day. New headache with immunosuppression, HIV or cancer → Urgent imaging
  • Progressive, sub-acute loss of central neurological function → consider NG12 direct-access MRI ≤2 weeks; children and young people with new abnormal cerebellar or central function → consider very urgent referral ≤48 h

1Confirm — positively, not by exclusion

Bilateral · pressing or tightening · mild to moderate · not worse with routine activity; no nausea or vomiting; ≤1 of photophobia or phonophobia; 30 min–7 days; normal neurological exam.

Headache daysClass
<1 / 1–14 a monthInfrequent / frequent episodic
≥15 a month for >3 monthsChronic → assess every patient for MOH

Reconsider migraine: nausea, photo- and phonophobia, worse on stairs, dark room.

2Check — before any primary-headache label

☐ Onset, pattern change, pregnancy  ☐ BP  ☐ Neurological exam; fundoscopy if competent  ☐ Headache and medication days, incl. bought  ☐ 4–8 week diary  ☐ Sleep, mood

Don't miss: carbon monoxide (worse at home, others affected).

3Decide — who gets what

  • Chronic → consider acupuncture, up to 10 sessions over 5–8 weeks.
  • Amitriptyline (BASH) → consider after discussing uncertainty and adverse effects; esp. poor sleep, low mood, widespread pain.
  • MOH present → withdrawal comes first; consider starting prophylaxis at the same time.
  • Everyone: sleep, meals, hydration, caffeine, stress, posture — alongside, not instead.

4Target — acute-medicine days

MedicineLimit (say it, write it)
Any acute headache medicineAim ≤2 days a week where possible
Paracetamol, aspirin, NSAIDNever ≥15 days a month
Triptan, opioid, combination analgesicNever ≥10 days a month

5Treat

AcuteAspirin (not under 16), ibuprofen 400 mg up to TDS with food, or paracetamol 1 g up to QDS (lower if low weight, frail, liver disease)
MOH withdrawalStop overused simple analgesics, triptans, combinations abruptly ≥1 month; taper opioids, benzodiazepines
PreventAcupuncture or amitriptyline 10 mg at night, titrated gradually to usual 25–75 mg
No benefitAt 75 mg after 8–12 weeks → stop; revisit the diagnosis, especially migraine
  • Before every step: recount medication days; exclude ongoing overuse; reconsider migraine. Amitriptyline: falls in the elderly, QT, suicidal ideation in the young; not with MAOI within 14 days, urinary retention, angle-closure glaucoma.
  • Avoid codeine combinations; no triptans. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • 4–8 weeks after withdrawal or start: diary, titration; reassess the diagnosis once overuse is gone.
  • 3 months: response at adequate dose. 6 months: continue? Taper, never stop abruptly; reinforce the limit.
  • Any time: changed pattern = new presentation → re-screen block 0.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Neurology: diagnostic doubt; failed adequate amitriptyline trial; daily headache after withdrawal; suspected cluster; uneasy despite normal exam.
  • Headache service: opioid or barbiturate overuse, repeated failed withdrawal, major psychiatric comorbidity.
  • Physio or psychology: neck and posture, anxiety, low mood, chronic pain.

8Never

  • Opioids for tension-type headache, at any stage.
  • "Take paracetamol as needed" without a stated days-a-month limit.
  • The label without a red-flag screen, BP and neurological examination.
  • "Something stronger" for daily headache before asking medication days, including bought co-codamol.
  • Amitriptyline judged at 10 mg or 3–4 weeks, or left on repeat indefinitely.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A headache like a thunderclap — worst ever, full intensity within seconds or minutes — or fever with a stiff neck or rash, weakness or numbness, confusion or a first fit: call 999.”   Same-day “A headache that changes or keeps getting worse, a new headache over 50, worse lying flat, coughing or straining, visual disturbance, jaw pain on chewing or scalp tenderness, or headache in pregnancy or after birth — contact us the same day.”   Withdrawal: “The first days to weeks may be worse — that is expected; we will review you in 4 to 8 weeks.”
