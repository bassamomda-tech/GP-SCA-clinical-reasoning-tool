Corticosteroids in palliative care

For: adults with advanced illness started on, or taking, dexamethasone. Not for: adrenal insufficiency replacement (Addison's disease) or children. Emergencies: MSCC, SVCO.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG234NICE NG243NatPSA 2020 Steroid CardJBDS 08 (2023)Scottish Palliative Care Guidelines 2025
Core rule: name the target, the dose source and a review date; give it by 2pm, check glucose later in the day, stop a trial that isn't helping, and never stop a prolonged course suddenly.

0Act now — steroids as part of an emergency, or the cause of one

  • Cancer + back or neck pain with neurological symptoms or signs (MSCC) → dexamethasone 16 mg orally now; MSCC coordinator Immediately; whole-spine MRI within 24 hours; immobilise if unstable. Possible lymphoma, no known cancer → acute oncology/haematology immediately.
  • Raised ICP with reduced consciousness, new focal neurology, seizures, papilloedema, persistent vomiting or rapidly worsening headache → oncology/neurosurgery Same-day
  • SVCO with stridor, airway compromise, severe breathlessness, neurological deterioration, syncope or haemodynamic instability → 999. Other suspected SVCO or raised ICP → acute oncology Same-day, if it fits the goals of care. The steroid must not delay definitive treatment.
  • Adrenal crisis (vomiting, collapse, hypotension, confusion on long-term steroids) → hydrocortisone 100 mg IM or IV now, do not wait for tests → 999; 0.9% sodium chloride if suitable.
  • Suicidal thoughts or psychosis → Same-day assessment. Very high glucose, ketones or unwell → Same-day advice. Unexpectedly unwell (steroids mask sepsis and perforation) → Same-day assessment.

1Confirm — name the target before you start

Record indication, target symptom, dose and route, review date, duration, who reviews, taper/stop plan on the care plan for out-of-hours teams. Appetite or nausea: is there a better option?

2Check — the start bundle

☐ Baseline glucose where appropriate  ☐ Oral candida  ☐ GI risk (aspirin, NSAID, anticoagulant, ulcer or bleed) → consider a PPI  ☐ Warn about insomnia, mood change, psychosis  ☐ Card eligibility  ☐ Live vaccines, zoster, checkpoint inhibitor

Every review: proximal myopathy, thrush, sleep, mood, glucose. PJP prophylaxis → ask the specialist team.

3Decide — Steroid Emergency Card and sick days

  • Offer a card if oral dexamethasone about 500 micrograms daily or more for 4 weeks or longer; teach sick-day rules.
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea stops absorption → parenteral hydrocortisone urgently: emergency injection if they have one, and 999

4Target — glucose (JBDS 08)

WhenAction
Late morning–early eveningCheck then, not fasting
Repeatedly >about 12 mmol/L, symptoms, ketonesTreatment review (local guidance); reduce the steroid if possible

Steroid fallingReduce glucose-lowering too. Type 1: never omit basal insulin

5Treat — dexamethasone dose by indication

IndicationDose
MSCC, neuro signs (NICE)16 mg now, then daily until surgery/radiotherapy, then taper; ruled out → stop
No neuro signs (NICE)Not routine; consider for severe pain or haematological malignancy
Raised ICP*8–16 mg now, then 4–8 mg twice daily
SVCO*16 mg now, then 8 mg twice daily
Bowel obstruction*6–16 mg parenterally, trial often 4–7 days
Anorexia*2–4 mg each morning; review after 1 week
Nausea, nerve or liver painLocal guidance or specialist advice
  • *Local palliative guidance, not NICE. Morning, or second dose by 2pm. SC: check the concentration (GM: 4 mg oral ≈ 3.3 mg SC). Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — review, reduce, stop

  • Symptom trials: review in 3–7 days; no benefit → stop. Emergency regimen → ask the specialist first.
  • <3 weeks, ≤6 mg, no repeat courses or risk factors → may stop without taper (local rule).
  • Benefit → lowest effective dose, e.g. halve every 3–5 days to 2 mg, then 500 micrograms every 5–7 days. Over 4 weeks: NG243 taper.

7Refer — and the last days

  • Palliative care: uncertain indication or dose, difficult weaning, complications. Endocrine: adrenal insufficiency. Diabetes team: uncontrolled glucose.
  • Last days: stop if not helping; continue SC if it controls symptoms. Stopping in brain tumour → anticipatory seizure medicine.

8Never

  • Stop a prolonged, repeated or high-dose course abruptly — plan the taper.
  • Let the steroid delay MRI, oncology discussion or definitive treatment.
  • Give steroids routinely without neurological signs, or prophylactically for asymptomatic brain metastases.
  • Rely on a fasting glucose, or give a dose after 2pm.
  • Use a generic dose range, or the 750 microgram equivalence for card eligibility.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “New weakness or numbness in your legs, trouble passing urine or controlling your bowels, a severe headache with vomiting or drowsiness, a fit, face swelling with breathlessness, or vomiting and can't keep the steroid down — call 999 straight away, if hospital care fits your wishes; otherwise the 24-hour number on your care plan.”   Same-day “Very high sugar readings, feeling unexpectedly unwell, low mood, thoughts of harming yourself or strange thoughts — contact us the same day.”   Steroid card: “Carry it, and don't stop the steroid suddenly without talking to us.”
