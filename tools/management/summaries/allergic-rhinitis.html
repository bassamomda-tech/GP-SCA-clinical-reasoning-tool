Allergic rhinitis

For: adults and young people with bilateral nasal symptoms tied to pollen, dust mite, animals or work (children: doses and licensed ages per BNFC). Not for: unilateral or destructive nasal disease (ENT pathway), chronic rhinosinusitis with polyps, non-allergic rhinitis, or asthma treatment (use the asthma protocol, NG245).

Core rule: allergic rhinitis is bilateral and painless; a steroid spray fails from poor technique or irregular use before it fails as a drug — check both before stepping up, and never give a depot steroid injection.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Unilateral blockage or discharge, blood-stained discharge or epistaxis, facial pain or swelling, a visible mass, proptosis, cranial nerve signs, a neck lump, severe crusting or septal destruction → Urgent ENT via the local head-and-neck suspected-cancer pathway. A normal anterior examination does not exclude it; crusting → think vasculitis, cocaine.
  • Oral itch progressing to throat tightness, breathing difficulty, vomiting or collapse → treat as anaphylaxis: 999 + adrenaline, then refer to allergy.
  • Severe asthma attack → 999. Orbital or intracranial complications, severe epistaxis, rapidly progressive destructive nasal disease → Same-day emergency hospital

1Confirm — the history carries the diagnosis

Two or more of blockage, rhinorrhoea (front or back), sneezing or itch, for ≥1 hour on most days, on both sides, with a trigger (pollen, dust mite, animals, work).

Intermittent: <4 days a week or <4 consecutive weeksPersistent: ≥4 days a week and ≥4 consecutive weeks
Mild: sleep, daily activities, school/work, leisure unaffectedModerate–severe: any one impaired

Skin prick or specific IgE if the trigger is unclear or immunotherapy is considered; a positive test without symptoms is not allergy.

2Check — every new patient

☐ Both sides: polyps, perforation, mass  ☐ Chest: wheeze, cough, night waking → NG245 objective testing  ☐ Eyes; oral itch with raw fruit  ☐ OTC decongestant, cocaine, work exposures  ☐ Pregnancy, breastfeeding, driver or student

Better on holidays → occupational rhinitis, often before occupational asthma: refer early.

3Decide — start at the step that matches severity

  • Everyone: allergen reduction, saline douching (before the spray), technique shown.
  • Mild or intermittent → non-sedating oral antihistamine or an intranasal antihistamine.
  • Moderate–severe, persistent or blocked → regular intranasal corticosteroid every day; pollen → start about 2 weeks before the season.
  • Mild-to-moderate seasonal → local OTC policy.

4Target — review plan

2–4 weeksControlled → continue through the season, then stop. Not → checks (block 5), step up
AnnuallyPollen: agree a pre-season start date. Perennial: lowest effective dose

5Treat — adult doses; children per BNFC

MildCetirizine or loratadine 10 mg once daily; fexofenadine 120 mg once daily (seasonal, 12+); or intranasal azelastine
Moderate–severeMometasone or fluticasone propionate 50 micrograms/spray, 2 sprays each nostril once daily (mometasone → 1 spray once controlled)
Step upAzelastine + fluticasone propionate, 1 spray each nostril twice daily (12+), replacing the single spray
Targeted add-onsWatery rhinorrhoea → ipratropium 0.03%, 2 sprays each nostril 2–3 times daily. Eyes → drops. Blocked → decongestant ≤5 consecutive days
  • Before every step: watch the technique (aim outwards, away from the septum; gentle sniff), daily use, exposure, diagnosis.
  • Oral prednisolone only as exceptional rescue for severe, time-critical symptoms, alongside the spray. Doses: SPC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Every review: OTC decongestant use (stop it), eyes, chest.
  • Nosebleeds → re-check the angle or reduce; persistent → check for septal perforation.
  • Children, prolonged steroid → monitor height; betamethasone drops short-term only. High dose or several routes → care in glaucoma, cataract.

7Refer — routine

  • Allergy, immunotherapy: clear IgE disease, matching sensitisation to a dominant allergen, moderate–severe despite optimal treatment and adherence. HDM SLIT tablet: NICE TA1045 (ages 12–65, persistent moderate–severe HDM rhinitis).
  • Allergy, other: diagnostic doubt, occupational, pollen-food syndrome with systemic reactions, difficult asthma.
  • ENT: polyps or structural obstruction not responding. Check the local allergy referral pathway first.

8Never

  • A depot intramuscular steroid (e.g. triamcinolone) — harm is prolonged and cannot be withdrawn.
  • Sedating antihistamines (chlorphenamine, promethazine) in students, drivers, safety-critical work or older adults.
  • Unilateral, bloody or painful nasal symptoms treated as hay fever.
  • Montelukast started solely for allergic rhinitis.
  • A decongestant beyond 5 consecutive days, or oral or nasal decongestants routinely in pregnancy.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Throat tightness, difficulty breathing, vomiting or collapse — call 999.”   Urgent “Blockage or bleeding on one side, or face pain or swelling — tell us; it needs an urgent ENT check.”   Spray: “Every day, aimed outwards; it builds over days. Decongestant spray — never more than 5 days in a row.”   Montelukast: “Sleep problems, nightmares, mood change or suicidal thoughts — stop it and contact us immediately or get urgent help.”
