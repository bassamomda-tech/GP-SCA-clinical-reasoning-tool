Scabies

For: adults and children with classical scabies, and their close contacts, in primary care. Not for: crusted scabies or infants under 2 months (specialist-led, block 0); care-home outbreaks are co-ordinated with the infection-prevention lead and Health Protection Team. Impetiginised patches → impetigo protocol alongside the scabicide.

Core rule: treat the index case and every close contact from the last 8 weeks on the same day, twice, 7–14 days apart — and treat thick, crumbly scale in a frail or immunosuppressed person as crusted scabies until proven otherwise.

0Act now — more than ordinary scabies

  • Crusted scabies: thick crumbly scale, often little itch, in someone frail, immunosuppressed, neurologically impaired or on potent topical steroids → Same-day dermatology or ID input Alert the setting’s infection-control lead; do not rely on topical treatment or a standard two-dose course.
  • Secondary infection with systemic features: spreading cellulitis, fever, or an unwell child with widespread infected excoriations → Same-day acute assessment
  • Severely immunosuppressed or frail patient unable to apply treatment → Same-day specialist
  • Infant under 2 months with suspected scabies → Same-day specialist advice No routine primary-care alternative.
  • Care-home outbreak with ongoing transmission despite co-ordinated treatment → Same-day specialist plus HPT advice.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis in general practice

Night-time itch + someone else itching. Burrows: finger webs, wrists, axillae, waist, buttocks, genitals; infants also palms, soles, scalp, face.

Frail elderly: subtle signs, back may be affected — look for crusting. A negative scraping, or no itch in a recent contact, does not exclude it.

2Check — before you prescribe

☐ Crusting, infection  ☐ Contacts (8 weeks)  ☐ Pregnancy, breastfeeding, age, weight  ☐ Care home?  ☐ Sexually acquired?

Don’t miss: eczema, bites, bullous pemphigoid in the elderly; lice can coexist.

3Decide — who to treat

  • Everyone with skin contact in the 8 weeks before diagnosis (household, partners, hands-on carers), itchy or not → start the same day, repeat together. Brief social contact carries little risk.
  • Offer STI testing if sexually acquired, genital lesions, new partner or STI symptoms — not routinely.

4Home measures and exclusion — UKHSA

  • Day 1 laundry at ≥50°C, or sealed in a bag ≥72 hours. No fumigation.
  • No routine exclusion: avoid close skin contact until 24 hours after the first treatment; children who cannot (e.g. under 5) stay off until then. Carers: gloves and aprons until 24 hours after treatment.

5Treat — two doses, 7–14 days apart

1st · GM primary carePermethrin 5%, cool dry skin, neck down (head too if affected, elderly, immunosuppressed, infants); off after 8–12 h. From 2 months; safe in pregnancy.
1st · BASHH alternativeIvermectin 200 micrograms/kg orally. Not routinely commissioned in GM — specialist or exceptional use.
Permethrin unsuitableMalathion 0.5% aqueous, whole body, off after 24 h; repeat at 7 days. Check stock, age (BNF).
Crusted · specialistRepeated topical scabicide + multiple oral ivermectin doses + keratolytics, with infection control.
  • Adult: one to two 30 g tubes per application. Ivermectin: not in pregnancy; generally not when breastfeeding; <15 kg specialist only.
  • Itch: emollient, crotamiton, night sedating antihistamine (caution in older adults), short topical steroid. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Review — itch outlasts the mite

  • Itch may take at least a month to settle (BAD); itch alone is not failure.
  • New burrows or lesions, or itch not easing 4 weeks after dose 2 → check application and contacts; re-treat everyone together, consider switching agent.
  • Sore eczematous skin → emollient, topical steroid, no scabicide. Nodules → not failure.

7Refer — non-urgent routes (urgent ones in block 0)

  • Dermatology (routine or A&G): diagnostic doubt; persists after two correct courses with contacts treated; troublesome nodules.
  • Sexual health: sexually acquired — STI screen, partner notification.
  • Care home ≥2 linked cases in 8 weeks → infection-prevention lead + HPT advice; mass treatment, all starting within 24 hours. Not statutorily notifiable.

8Never

  • Treat the index case alone, or contacts one by one as they start itching.
  • Give a single application — the second dose catches mites hatched from surviving eggs.
  • Start a standard two-dose course and discharge in crusted scabies.
  • Repeat scabicide for itch alone — irritant dermatitis drives more itch.
  • Give oral ivermectin in pregnancy, or prolonged potent topical steroids without review.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “If the skin becomes hot, red and spreading, or you feel feverish or unwell, contact us the same day.”   Everyone: “Everyone you live with uses it the same night, even if not itching — then again in a week.”   Application: “Bedtime, cool skin, neck to soles; leave on all night; re-apply to hands after washing; hot wash tomorrow.”   Itch: “It can carry on for a month. New tracks or spots, or not easing a month after the second treatment — come back.”
