Atrial fibrillation in adults

For: adults with AF in primary care — stroke prevention (Stream A) and rate/rhythm (Stream B). Not for: pregnancy (no DOAC — specialist, LMWH), mechanical valve or moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis (warfarin, specialist input), AF with heart failure (use the heart-failure protocol), antiplatelet, VTE and peri-procedural detail (use the antithrombotics protocol).

Core rule: run two streams for life — anticoagulate by CHA₂DS₂-VASc whatever the symptoms or AF pattern, dose the DOAC by creatinine clearance (not eGFR), and never stop it because the rate is controlled and the patient feels well.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Fast AF with chest pain, syncope, hypotension or severe breathlessness → 999 Never delay emergency cardioversion for anticoagulation.
  • Any new focal neurology, even if resolved in minutes → stroke → 999  Major bleed, head injury or neurological change on an anticoagulant → 999 / admit
  • Suspected pre-excitation (young, very fast rate, delta wave — may be invisible in AF) → NO beta-blocker, verapamil, diltiazem or digoxin. Very fast rate → 999; otherwise Urgent cardiology
  • Reliably new onset <48 h in a well patient, poorly tolerated but stable fast AF, or decompensating heart failure → Same-day cardiology / ambulatory care. Uncertain onset = longer-duration AF.
  • Severe thyrotoxicosis, sepsis, significant anaemia or electrolyte disturbance as AF → treat the driver urgently; still answer the stroke question.

1Confirm — the diagnosis needs a trace

12-lead ECG (irregularly irregular, no P waves), or a clinician-read multi-lead recording or single-lead strip ≥30 s. Pulse or smartwatch alone never diagnoses AF; a symptomatic alert earns an ECG.

Paroxysmal self-terminating, <7 days · persistent >7 days or needs cardioversion · permanent accepted. Pattern and symptoms decide rhythm control only — never anticoagulation.

2Check — hunt the driver; baseline sets the dose

☐ Driver: TFTs, alcohol, BP, sleep apnoea, infection, anaemia, electrolytes, murmur, HF, ischaemia  ☐ FBC, U&E, weight + CrCl (Cockcroft-Gault), LFTs, HbA1c  ☐ Echo if it would change treatment

Don't miss: iron-deficiency anaemia, rectal bleeding or visible haematuria on a DOAC → investigate per NG12, never "the DOAC".

3Decide — Stream A: score on the components

CHA₂DS₂-VASc: heart failure/LV dysfunction 1 · hypertension 1 · age ≥75 2 · diabetes 1 · stroke/TIA/thromboembolism 2 · vascular disease 1 · age 65–74 1 · female 1

  • Score ≥2 (same in women as men) → offer a DOAC.
  • Man scoring 1 → consider anticoagulation.
  • 0 in a man, or 1 from female sex alone → none; re-score at least annually. Aspirin is not an alternative.
  • ORBIT → fix BP, NSAIDs, alcohol, antiplatelets, anaemia, falls; never a veto. Anticoagulation impossible → specialist (LAA occlusion).

4Target

Resting heart rate<110 bpm if asymptomatic; towards <80 only if still symptomatic
Warfarin INRAF 2–3; mechanical valve by valve (commonly 2.5–3.5)

5Treat — DOAC dose by CrCl, not lab eGFR

Standard doseReduce when
Apixaban 5 mg BD (usual first)2.5 mg BD if ≥2 of: age ≥80, weight ≤60 kg, creatinine ≥133 µmol/L
Rivaroxaban 20 mg OD, with food15 mg OD if CrCl 15–49
Edoxaban 60 mg OD30 mg OD if any of: CrCl 15–50, weight ≤60 kg, ciclosporin, dronedarone, erythromycin, ketoconazole
Dabigatran 150 mg BD110 mg BD if age ≥80 or on verapamil. Contraindicated if CrCl <30

No DOAC if CrCl <15. Inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin, St John's wort…) → avoid DOACs or seek advice.

Rate · 1stBeta-blocker (bisoprolol; not sotalol); or diltiazem MR / verapamil — never in HFrEF or with a beta-blocker (specialist only)
ThenAdd digoxin (alone only if sedentary). Uncontrolled on 2 → rhythm referral; no third rate drug
  • Before every step: adherence, maximum tolerated dose, drivers rechecked. Doses per BNF, SPC and local formulary.

6Monitor — annual review, both streams

  • At least annually: re-score; FBC, LFTs (falling Hb → find the bleed); weight, alcohol, BP, OSA, ORBIT fixables, antiplatelets.
  • CrCl annually; 6-monthly if CrCl 30–60 or frail; after AKI → re-dose. Every review: adherence, rate, symptoms.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Early rhythm referral — consider in new-onset AF: symptomatic, young/active, AF-related LV dysfunction, recurrent, reversible trigger, preference, failed rate control.
  • Triple-positive antiphospholipid syndrome or CrCl <15 → haematology. Significant murmur not yet echoed.

8Never

  • Stop the anticoagulant for controlled rate, "only paroxysmal" AF, or after ablation.
  • Dose a DOAC off lab eGFR, or under-dose without meeting the criteria.
  • Withhold anticoagulation for a high ORBIT score or "they fall".
  • An AV-nodal blocker in pre-excitation; verapamil/diltiazem in HFrEF.
  • Blame bleeding or anaemia on the DOAC without investigating; let triple therapy run on via repeats.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Face, arm, speech — 999, even if it passes off. Racing heart with chest pain, fainting or severe breathlessness — 999.”   Bleeding: “Black stools, vomiting blood, blood in urine, a bleed that won't stop, or any head injury — A&E; say you're on a blood thinner.”   Golden rule: “Never stop the blood thinner because you feel well — that is what it is for. Ask us before ibuprofen or herbal remedies.” Alert card.
