Urethritis in men

For: sexually active men with urethral discharge or dysuria in primary care. Not for: acute scrotal pain until torsion is excluded (block 0); full gonorrhoea or chlamydia management (use the gonorrhoea and chlamydia protocols). Sexual health leads microscopy, gonorrhoea, M. genitalium and partner notification.

BASHH NGU 2026BASHH gonorrhoea 2025BASHH chlamydia 2026 · M. genitalium 2025BASHH epididymo-orchitis 2020NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)
Core rule: discharge or dysuria in a sexually active man needs STI assessment unless another cause is clearly more likely — exclude torsion, send a first-void NAAT before antibiotics where safe, route to sexual health, and make sure partners are reached.

0Act now — these do not wait for a sexual health appointment

  • Sudden severe scrotal pain (high-riding testis, vomiting, young man) = torsion, even with urethral symptoms → Immediate urology / A&E or 999; don't wait for tests or ultrasound.
  • Fever, rigors or very unwell (pyelonephritis, acute bacterial prostatitis) → Same-day hospital. Fever, pustular rash, migrating joint pain or tenosynovitis (disseminated gonococcal infection) → Same-day admission
  • Red painful eye with photophobia (anterior uveitis) → Same-day ophthalmology. Genital ulcers or rash → HSV PCR + syphilis serology → Sexual health same week
  • Penile mass or ulcer with STI excluded, or a lesion persisting after STI treatment; non-painful testis enlargement or change → consider Suspected cancer pathway (NG12).

1Confirm — symptoms suggest it; microscopy confirms it

Confirmed NGU needs objective urethral inflammation: generally ≥5 PMNL/HPF on a smear (≥10 with some methods; 5–9 grey zone).

Gonorrhoea: days' incubation, purulent discharge, positive partner. Chlamydia (commonest) and M. genitalium (persistent NGU) cause most NGU; trichomonas if a female partner has it.

Mimics: UTI (older man, no sexual risk, nitrites → culture), prostatitis, trauma or instrumentation, balanitis, stones, malignancy, HSV, adenovirus.

2Check — before antibiotics where safe

☐ Sexual history: partners, sites, condoms, abroad, chemsex, PrEP/HIV  ☐ Examine scrotum, meatus, lesions  ☐ First-void urine NAAT (first 15–30 mL)  ☐ Throat/rectal swabs if exposed  ☐ HIV + syphilis; hepatitis B/C by risk  ☐ Urine culture if UTI or prostatitis possible

Don't miss: a NAAT taken after antibiotics may be falsely negative.

3Decide — sexual health is the best route

  • Gonorrhoea likely (purulent discharge, positive partner or NAAT) → same-day or next-day sexual health, before treating. No access and high risk → culture where feasible, local ceftriaxone pathway.
  • Gonorrhoea unlikely, significant symptoms, access delayed → starting doxycycline after the NAAT is reasonable.
  • Don't delay treatment if systemically unwell, severe epididymo-orchitis (torsion excluded) or follow-up uncertain.

5Treat — BASHH; test first where safe

NGU / chlamydiaDoxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days
Doxycycline unsuitableAzithromycin 1 g day 1, then 500 mg days 2–3 — specialist or local pathway only
GonorrhoeaCeftriaxone 1 g IM once, after culture, via sexual health or local pathway; add doxycycline if chlamydia or NGU
Epididymo-orchitis (STI)Ceftriaxone 1 g IM + doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 10–14 days (BASHH); review if not better in 72 h
  • Doxycycline: full glass of water, stay upright, not just before bed; separate from iron, calcium, magnesium, antacids; photosensitivity.
  • Persistent or recurrent: check adherence and re-exposure → sexual health for M. genitalium (macrolide-resistance testing) and trichomonas.
  • Doses: NICE, SPC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Chase the NAAT: chlamydia or gonorrhoea positive → sexual health for partner notification (and ceftriaxone for gonorrhoea).
  • Reactive arthritis → treat infection and partners; consider rheumatology if persistent or severe.

7Refer — non-urgent routes

  • Sexual health, same week (sooner if gonorrhoea likely): all men with urethral symptoms, ideally before treatment; positive NAATs; persistent NGU; PrEP, doxyPEP, 4CMenB.
  • Urology: epididymo-orchitis with a persistent mass, recurrence, urinary pathogen, structural abnormality or failure to improve.

8Never

  • Treat as a "water infection" with trimethoprim or nitrofurantoin — misses STIs and delays partner notification.
  • Give single-dose azithromycin 1 g, or blind repeat azithromycin for persistent symptoms.
  • Substitute an oral regimen empirically for suspected gonorrhoea.
  • Delay a torsion referral for tests or ultrasound — Doppler can be falsely negative.
  • Repeat empirical antibiotics for persistent symptoms without objective urethritis.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

A&E / 999 “If your testicle suddenly becomes very painful, go to A&E or call 999 straight away.”   Same-day “If it becomes swollen, or you get a fever, contact us or NHS 111 to be seen the same day.”   Sex: “No sex, including oral and with condoms, until the 7-day course is finished, symptoms have gone and partners are treated.”   Partners: “Anyone from the last month or so needs testing; the clinic can tell them without your name.”
