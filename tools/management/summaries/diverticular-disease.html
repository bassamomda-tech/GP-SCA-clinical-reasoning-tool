Diverticular disease and acute diverticulitis

For: adults with diverticulosis, diverticular disease or suspected acute diverticulitis. Not for: complicated disease or pregnancy (hospital — block 0); suspected cancer runs in parallel on the NG12 route.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG147NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)NICE HTG690 (FIT)ESCP 2020GM antimicrobial guidelines
Core rule: exclude complicated disease first; if systemically well with uncomplicated disease, consider no antibiotic; safety-net everyone; and never let the diverticular label hide a colorectal cancer.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Generalised guarding, rigidity or peritonism, haemodynamic instability or collapse → 999 (possible perforation; do not stage it clinically).
  • Suspected sepsis (NEWS2 ≥5), massive fresh rectal bleeding, or obstruction (distension, vomiting, absolute constipation) → 999
  • Suspected abscess (swinging fever, tender mass, not resolving by 72 hours), uncontrolled pain, unable to take oral medication, persisting or worsening at home, pregnancy, or immunocompromise with frailty or comorbidity → Same-day surgical assessment
  • Pneumaturia, faecaluria, recurrent mixed-organism UTIs (fistula), or recurrent diverticular bleeding → Urgent colorectal surgery

1Confirm — which state, then grade the severity

StateFeaturesPlan
DiverticulosisNo symptomsLifestyle only
Diverticular diseaseIntermittent pain, altered bowel habit; no inflammationNo antibiotic
Acute diverticulitisConstant left iliac fossa pain, fever, raised markersGrade severity

Uncomplicated = localised tenderness, systemically well, tolerating oral intake, social support. Block 0 = complicated. No CRP or WBC cut-off replaces clinical judgement.

2Check — every suspected episode

☐ NEWS2  ☐ Tenderness pattern, mass, PR  ☐ FBC, CRP, U&E  ☐ Urine dip + MSU  ☐ Pregnancy test (reproductive age)  ☐ NSAIDs, immunosuppression

Contrast CT if diagnosis uncertain, markers raised, severe or complications suspected — not routine if well. Pyuria is common without UTI.

3Decide — who gets antibiotics

  • Well, uncomplicated, not immunocompromised → consider no antibiotic; re-present if symptoms persist or worsen.
  • Unwell (not needing hospital), immunosuppressed or significant comorbidity → offer antibiotics.
  • Chronic pain, no fever or raised CRP → fibre, fluid, antispasmodic (mebeverine); no antibiotic.
  • Lifestyle: after recovery fibre gradually to 25–30 g a day with fluid, 150 min a week of activity, weight, stop smoking. Nuts and seeds are safe.

4Target — expected recovery

WhenGoalIf not
First few daysFever settling, pain easing, taking fluidsAny “no” → lower threshold for hospital

5Treat — by severity

Step 1 · wellConsider no antibiotic: paracetamol 1 g QDS, fluids, light diet
Step 2 · antibioticCo-amoxiclav 500/125 mg TDS, 5 days. Penicillin allergy → cefalexin (caution) or trimethoprim + metronidazole 400 mg TDS, 5 days
Step 3 · not improvingReassess urgently; markers or CT if indicated. do not extend antibiotics by phone
Step 4 · complicatedHospital: IV antibiotics, drainage or surgery
  • Before prescribing: renal function, allergy type (cefalexin may be unsuitable after immediate or severe beta-lactam allergy), pregnancy, interactions. Ciprofloxacin only on specialist advice.
  • Pain: avoid NSAIDs where possible; opioids only if unavoidable, briefly, with a laxative. Do not routinely stop low-dose aspirin. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • 48–72 h review when indicated: frail, uncertain diagnosis, borderline features, comorbidity, immunosuppression, no imaging, on antibiotics.
  • After recovery, colonoscopy or CT colonography if complicated, persistent symptoms, bleeding, anaemia, bowel change, equivocal CT or no recent colorectal check; not always after one CT-confirmed uncomplicated episode. Code and recall.

7Refer — cancer routes run in parallel

  • NG12: FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g → Suspected cancer pathway; rectal mass → consider referral without FIT. Mass, bowel change, iron deficiency, rectal bleeding, or 40+ with weight loss and pain → FIT first; low FIT must not delay referral if concern persists.
  • Elective colorectal discussion: complicated disease, stricture, persistent symptoms or recurrences affecting quality of life — no fixed episode count.

8Never

  • Manage generalised peritonism at home.
  • Colonoscopy during the acute episode.
  • A reflex antibiotic when well and uncomplicated, or for chronic pain.
  • Extend antibiotics by phone when not improving — reassess.
  • Let the label absorb new bowel change, bleeding or anaemia — cancer mimics it.

9Safety-net — say it, give written information, then document it

999 “Severe tummy pain that spreads across the whole abdomen, a rigid tummy, high fever, collapse, or a large amount of fresh blood from the back passage — call 999. Don't wait for your appointment.”   Same-day “If the pain worsens, the fever returns or rises, you start vomiting or cannot keep fluids down — contact us the same day.”   Fistula: “Air or stool in your urine, or repeated urine infections — contact us the same day.”   Follow-up: “If we arrange a camera test or scan after you recover, please don't skip it.”
