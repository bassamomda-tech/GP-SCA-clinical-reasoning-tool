Burns and scalds

For: minor thermal burns and scalds in adults and children in primary care, and deciding who goes to the burns service. Not for: major burns, inhalation, high-voltage or significant chemical injuries (999), suspected non-accidental injury (same-day safeguarding and burns service).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BBA referral 2012BBA first aid 2018NICE CKSNICE CG89Green Book ch.30
Core rule: cool for 20 minutes, measure the burn with the palm or a chart (not by eye), apply the referral thresholds as written, and ask whether the story fits.

0Act now

  • Inhalation features (enclosed space, soot, hoarseness, stridor), large burn, shock, high-voltage electrical, significant chemical, major trauma → 999; keep cooling, keep warm.
  • Children 2% TBSA or more, adults 3% or more; full thickness; circumferential; chemical, electrical or friction → Same-day burns service
  • Face, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum, major joints → Same-day discussion with the burns service.
  • Child unwell after a burn (fever, rash, diarrhoea, lethargy) → Same-day for toxic shock syndrome; 999 if shocked.
  • Story doesn't fit, delay, immersion pattern → Same-day safeguarding + burns service.

1First aid and assess

Cool running water 20 minutes (helps up to 3 hours after); no ice or creams; cling film in strips. Palm and fingers ≈ 1% TBSA; Lund and Browder for children. Don't count erythema.

DepthClue
Superficial epidermalRed, no blisters
Superficial partialBlisters, brisk refill, painful
Deep partial / fullBlotchy or white, poor refill, less pain → refer

Re-assess depth at 48–72 hours — burns often look deeper.

5Treat — below the thresholds

CleanLeave small blisters intact
DressNon-adherent + absorbent; erythema: emollient only
AnalgesiaParacetamol ± ibuprofen
TetanusPer Green Book ch.30
  • No prophylactic antibiotics. Cling film is first aid only. Doses per BNF/BNFC and local formulary.

6Review

  • 48 hours, then every 3–5 days: depth, infection, healing.
  • Infection signs → swab, treat, discuss with burns service.
  • Healed: moisturise; sun protection for at least a year.

7Refer

  • Not healed by 2 weeks, hypertrophic scarring or contracture → burns service.

8Never

  • Count simple redness in the %TBSA.
  • Use the rule of nines in a child.
  • Leave cling film on as a long-term dressing.
  • Accept a scald story without checking it fits.
  • Dismiss a feverish child with a small burn.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for trouble breathing, a hoarse voice after a fire, or feeling faint and clammy.”   Same-day “Contact us the same day if your child gets a temperature, rash, sickness or diarrhoea or is unusually sleepy, or the burn gets more painful, red or smelly.”   Healing: “Come back if it hasn't healed in two weeks.”
