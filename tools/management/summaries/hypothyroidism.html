Hypothyroidism, including subclinical

For: adults with primary or subclinical hypothyroidism. Not for: pregnancy or planning it (specialist/local pathway), under 16s (paediatric or endocrine), central hypothyroidism (endocrinology).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG145BTANICE NG12MHRALocal formulary
Core rule: read the pattern, not just the TSH; never diagnose subclinical disease on one result; cortisol first if adrenal insufficiency is possible; dose by age and heart.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Doses missed for weeks, then drowsiness, confusion, hypothermia (myxoedema coma) → 999 emergency admission.
  • Suspected adrenal crisis (hypotension, vomiting, severe hyponatraemia, hypoglycaemia, hyperkalaemia, altered consciousness) → emergency hydrocortisone, 999 — don't wait for cortisol.
  • Suspected adrenal insufficiency (pigmentation, postural dizziness, hyponatraemia) → Same-day assessment; if stable, 9am cortisol before any levothyroxine and discuss with endocrinology.
  • New pericardial effusion, heart failure, bradyarrhythmia or long QTc → Same-day cardiology; compromised → 999
  • Unexplained thyroid lump → consider Suspected cancer pathway (NG12), whatever the TSH. Aged 45+ with persistent hoarseness or neck lump → NG12 head-and-neck. Acute compressive neck symptoms → Same-day; stridor → 999
  • On levothyroxine, positive pregnancy test → Same-day contact: dose up about 25–30% now, urgent TFTs, specialist pathway.

2Check — baseline

☐ TSH + FT4  ☐ Anti-TPO once (subclinical)  ☐ Pregnancy status or plans  ☐ Medicines and supplements  ☐ Neck, pulse, reflexes

ECG if bradycardic or cardiac disease. Not: routine FT3, repeat antibodies, ultrasound of a normal neck.

3Decide — treat, trial or monitor

  • Overt → treat, regardless of symptoms.
  • Subclinical, TSH ≥10 on 2 tests 3 months apart → consider levothyroxine.
  • TSH above range but <10, under 65, symptomatic → consider a 6-month trial; consider stopping if no better once TSH is in range.
  • TSH <10 and asymptomatic, or 65+ → usually monitor: yearly if anti-TPO positive, prior RAI or surgery, or goitre; else 2–3-yearly.

4Target

TSH within the reference range — no lower-half target, no suppression. After thyroid cancer it may be deliberately suppressed (<0.1): check the oncology letter.

5Treat — levothyroxine

Start · under 65, no CVDConsider 1.6 micrograms/kg/day, nearest 25 (60 kg → 100; 80 kg → 125)
Start · 65+, CVD, severeOr possible adrenal insufficiency: consider 25–50 micrograms/day
Titrate25 microgram steps on TSH; most settle on 75–150
Over-replacedSuppressed TSH → reduce by 25; recheck 6–8 weeks
  • Before every rise: adherence, timing, iron, calcium or antacids (4 h apart), PPI, oestrogen, weight. >2 micrograms/kg/day → coeliac serology.
  • Empty stomach, 30–60 min before breakfast or at bedtime 3 h after eating. Angina → drop back, then 12.5–25 microgram steps.
  • No T3 or thyroid extract started in primary care. Doses: SPC, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • TSH from about 6 weeks after any start or change → about 3-monthly until stable → annually on the register (check for over-replacement).
  • Starting HRT or COCP → TSH at 8 weeks. Amiodarone, lithium → TFTs 6-monthly.
  • Untreated subclinical: TSH ≥10 twice 3 months apart, or symptoms with a rising TSH → revisit the decision.

7Refer — endocrinology

  • Pituitary picture → endocrinology; with headache, visual field defect or galactorrhoea → Urgent with MRI pituitary.
  • TSH high on >200 micrograms a day after adherence, absorption and coeliac checked; angina blocking titration.
  • Unwell despite optimal TSH and mimics excluded (T3 is their call); amiodarone or lithium that must continue.

8Never

  • Levothyroxine before cortisol is checked when adrenal insufficiency is suspected — it can precipitate adrenal crisis.
  • Titrate to a “normal TSH” in central hypothyroidism.
  • Treat subclinical disease on a single result.
  • Suppress the TSH or keep escalating for non-thyroidal symptoms — AF and fracture risk.
  • Let a normal TFT talk you out of examining, or referring, a thyroid lump.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If doses have been missed for weeks and you become very drowsy, confused and cold — call 999.”   Chest pain: “New chest tightness after a dose increase — call us the same day. Severe, at rest or over 15 minutes — call 999.”   Too much: “Palpitations, shakiness, sweating or weight loss — contact us within a few days, the same day if your heart is racing or irregular.”   Pregnancy: “Planning — tell us beforehand. Positive test — contact us the same day.”
