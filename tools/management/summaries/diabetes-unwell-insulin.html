Feeling unwell on insulin — sick-day insulin

For: adults with type 1 or insulin-treated type 2 diabetes who are unwell but clinically stable and able to self-manage. Not for: suspected DKA or HHS (local JBDS hospital pathway); pregnancy, children and young people, or pumps without specialist advice (block 3). Which other drugs to pause → sick day rules protocol.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG17NICE NG28JBDS-IP DKA (2023)MHRA SGLT2TREND-UK (consensus)
Core rule: never stop basal insulin, even when not eating — test glucose AND ketones; blood ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L is suspected DKA and goes to hospital, not to a home dose.

0Act now — screen every time, before any dose advice

  • Blood ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L (or urine 2+ with deterioration) = suspected DKA → 999 / urgent hospital Don't delay transfer for subcutaneous insulin; continue basal if safe.
  • Any ketones with vomiting, or unable to keep fluids down, drowsy or confused, Kussmaul breathing, severe dehydration, suspected sepsis, MI or stroke, or unable to self-manage → 999 / urgent hospital whatever the glucose.
  • Suspected HHS (marked hyperglycaemia, severe dehydration, absent or mild ketones) → Urgent hospital
  • On an SGLT2 inhibitor with nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, unusual fatigue or fast breathing → suspect DKA even if glucose is normal: stop it, urgent blood ketones and venous acid–base Same-day
  • Blood ketones >1.5 to <3.0, or starting to vomit → Same-day specialist / urgent advice (out of hours 111); hospital if ketones rise or do not fall, or unwell.

1Confirm — the ketone level, not the glucose, defines the risk

Blood ketones (mmol/L)Action
<0.6Normal: usual insulin, test more often
0.6–1.5Repeat within 2 h; same-day diabetes-team advice
>1.5 to <3.0High DKA risk: same-day specialist advice; home dosing only if well
≥3.0Suspected DKA → hospital (block 0)

Prefer blood ketones (urine lags). Glucose <4 → fast-acting carbohydrate ± glucagon; do not stop basal.

2Check — triage, then test more often

☐ Conscious level, breathing ☐ Fluids kept down ☐ Pulse, postural BP, NEWS2 ☐ Pregnancy? ☐ SGLT2 inhibitor, pump, missed doses ☐ Precipitant (infection, sepsis, MI)

Type 1: glucose and ketones 4–6-hourly, including overnight. Type 2 on insulin: glucose ≥4 times a day; ketones if glucose >11 or unwell. Glucose >11 and/or ketones ≥0.6 → both 2-hourly, including overnight.

3Decide — is home management safe?

  • Home only if clinically stable, able to drink and able to self-manage, with an individualised diabetes-team sick-day plan.
  • Specialist advice first: pregnancy, under 18, recent DKA, frailty or cognitive impairment, CKD 4–5 or dialysis, heart failure with fluid restriction, severe or unaware hypos, pumps, concentrated, basal-only or twice-daily mixed insulin.

4Target — carbohydrate and fluids alongside the insulin

  • Glucose >11: sugar-free fluids. Not eating or glucose low: sugary fluids, soup, milk, yoghurt — never omit basal.
  • Small frequent sips if safe; individualise in heart failure, CKD, dialysis, frailty. Oral fluids do not treat DKA.

5Treat — extra insulin ON TOP of usual basal and bolus

Method A · ketones <0.6Glucose 11–17 → +2 units to each dose; 17–22 → +4; >22 → +6. Total daily dose >50 units → double it.
Method B · ketones >1.5 to <3.0Only if well, drinking, self-managing, with same-day specialist advice: rapid-acting insulin 10% of total daily dose every 2 h.
Method B dosesTDD ≤14 → 1 unit · 15–24 → 2 · 25–34 → 3 · 35–44 → 4 · 45–54 → 5
Stop · askTDD >54 units, or any doubt → specialist team or GP. Ketones ≥3.0 → hospital, not Method B.
  • Only within the individualised or locally adopted plan; never extrapolate the tables.
  • Count recent correction doses — no stacking. Pump: check the set; correct by pen per the pump plan. Doses per the plan, BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — recheck, then taper

  • Recheck both 2-hourly while glucose >11 or ketones raised. Stop repeat doses, get advice if ketones are not falling, glucose is falling fast, intake is poor or a hypo occurs.
  • Recovery: taper extra doses gradually; keep fast-acting carbohydrate to hand; test until back to baseline. Restock in-date ketone strips.

7Refer — diabetes team (urgent routes in block 0)

  • Glucose or ketones not controlled despite extra insulin → Same-day specialist (out of hours 111).
  • Big dose changes or near-admission → review the sick-day plan with GP or DSN. Use the Bolton / Greater Manchester specialist contact pathway.

8Never

  • Stop or halve basal insulin because the patient is not eating.
  • Give home doses for blood ketones ≥3.0, or delay transfer for subcutaneous insulin.
  • Be reassured by a normal glucose on an SGLT2 inhibitor.
  • Rely on oral fluids when the patient is vomiting or cannot keep fluids down.
  • Keep sick-day doses going into recovery — taper to avoid rebound hypos.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Vomiting with any ketones, can't keep fluids down, drowsy or breathless, or ketones 3 or more — that is a hospital job; call 999.”   Same-day “Ketones up, or you start to vomit — recheck within 2 hours, keep sipping, and ring us or the diabetes team today; out of hours, 111.”   Insulin: “Never stop your insulin; follow your plan for extra doses, then step back down slowly.”   Kit: “Keep an in-date ketone meter and strips at home.”
