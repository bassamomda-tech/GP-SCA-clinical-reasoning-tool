Lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) in men

For: men with LUTS in primary care. Not for: prostatitis (use the prostatitis protocol); prostate detail is on the benign prostatic enlargement page.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG97NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)NICE TA290MHRA Drug Safety UpdatesGMMMG formulary
Core rule: exclude retention, renal harm and cancer first; split voiding from storage; conservative care before a matched drug.

0Act now — screen before treating symptoms

  • Cauda equina (new saddle anaesthesia, bilateral leg weakness, retention or incontinence, absent anal tone) → 999 / ED for urgent MRI.
  • Acute retention → Same-day catheterisation (local pathway), record residual; severe pain, sepsis, instability or no safe route → A&E / 999. Offer an alpha-blocker before TWOC.
  • Chronic retention, may be painless: residual >1 litre, palpable/percussible bladder, renal impairment or hydronephrosis → Urgent urology, renal function, upper-tract imaging; high-pressure with severe systemic disturbance → Same-day
  • Rising creatinine with LUTS = obstructive uropathy until proven otherwise → Same-day urology if significantly elevated or rising.
  • Urosepsis / pyelonephritis (fever, rigors, loin pain) → Same-day; sepsis criteria → 999
  • Malignant-feeling prostate (whatever the PSA) or PSA above age range · ≥45 unexplained visible haematuria, or persisting/recurring after treated UTI · ≥60 non-visible haematuria + dysuria or raised WCC · testicular lump → Suspected cancer pathway

1Confirm — voiding or storage? not a synonym for BPH

PatternSymptomsPoints to
VoidingHesitancy, weak stream, straining, incomplete emptyingObstruction, often BPE

IPSS mild 0–7, moderate 8–19, severe 20–35; bother (QoL ≥3) drives treatment. 3-day chart for storage or nocturia.

2Check — initial assessment

☐ Dipstick, all  ☐ Abdomen, genitals, DRE  ☐ PSA after discussion  ☐ Medicines (anticholinergics, diuretics, decongestants)  ☐ eGFR / PVR if renal harm or retention suspected

NG12 PSA: >2.5 ng/mL at 40–49, >3.5 at 50–59, >4.5 at 60–69, >6.5 at 70–79. Not within 6 weeks of UTI.

3Decide — conservative first

  • All: less evening fluid, caffeine, alcohol; bladder training, double voiding, pelvic floor; review medicines.
  • Mild → watchful waiting; review 6–12 months, sooner if IPSS worsens ≥3.
  • Offer drugs if bothersome and conservative care fails or is unsuitable.

5Treat — by phenotype; mixed needs both

VoidingAlpha-blocker: tamsulosin MR 400 micrograms OD (or alfuzosin, doxazosin)
+ Large prostate>30 g or PSA >1.4 ng/mL → add 5-ARI: finasteride 5 mg or dutasteride
StorageRetention excluded → antimuscarinic (not oxybutynin if frail); unsuitable → mirabegron (check BP)
Nocturnal polyuriaConfirmed, drivers treated → consider timed loop diuretic or desmopressin (normal sodium)
  • Also ED → tadalafil 5 mg OD instead of, not with, an alpha-blocker.
  • Counsel: alpha-blocker — postural drop, falls, tell cataract surgeon. 5-ARI — sexual effects, depression (ask history), breast changes; women of childbearing potential must not handle broken tablets.
  • Doses per BNF, SPC and local (GM) formulary.

6Monitor

  • Antimuscarinic: stream, PVR if indicated, cognition; annual anticholinergic burden.
  • 5-ARI: mood, sexual function. PSA ~halves by 6 months — allow for it; investigate a confirmed rise from nadir.
  • Desmopressin: sodium before, after starting (≥ day 3), after dose rises; low or symptoms → stop.
  • PSA, DRE, eGFR only when indicated — not automatically annual.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Prompt urology: recurrent UTI, stones, hydronephrosis, stricture, neurogenic bladder, large or rising PVR with complications.
  • Routine: bothersome despite ≥6 months optimised drugs · failed TWOC · uncertain diagnosis.
  • ≥60, recurrent unexplained UTI → consider non-urgent bladder cancer referral.

8Never

  • An antimuscarinic in retention, narrow-angle glaucoma or severe constipation.
  • Reassure on a normal PSA if the prostate feels malignant or DRE was not done.
  • Blame visible haematuria on a UTI without confirming infection and resolution.
  • Desmopressin for BPH nocturia, low sodium, heart failure or moderate/severe CKD.
  • Homeopathy, acupuncture or phytotherapy for LUTS.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Can't pass urine with a painful full bladder — urgent catheter; severe pain or very unwell, heavy bleeding with clots, fever with shaking, saddle numbness or leg weakness — A&E or 999.”   Blood: “Visible blood in urine always needs checking, even if it settles.”   Alpha-blocker: “Dizzy, falls or faints — come back.”   Bladder tablet: “Trouble emptying — see us promptly.”   5-ARI: “Takes six months; tell us about low mood, suicidal thoughts or sexual problems.”
