Recurrent UTI

For: adults with recurrent UTI in primary care. Not for: a single acute episode (UTI in women or men protocols), children (UTI in children protocol) or catheter bacteriuria. Pregnancy or men → specialist advice.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG112 (updated 2024)NG109NG111NG12 (April 2026)MHRA (April 2023)
Core rule: never let a UTI close the haematuria question — re-check the urine once the infection has cleared; then work the prevention ladder in order, daily antibiotics last, with a review within 6 months.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Fever, rigors, loin pain, tachycardia, hypotension or new confusion → sepsis tool (NG253); high-risk → 999 Consider hospital if dehydrated, unable to take oral treatment, pregnant or high complication risk.
  • Diabetes with pyelonephritis not improving on antibiotics (emphysematous pyelonephritis, abscess) → Same-day hospital and urgent cross-sectional imaging.
  • New back pain, saddle anaesthesia, leg weakness or new incontinence → Same-day emergency assessment and MRI. Painful retention → Same day
  • Unexplained visible haematuria at 45+ without UTI, or persisting or recurring after successful treatment; non-visible haematuria at 60+ with dysuria or raised white cells → Suspected cancer pathway (NG12). Re-test once the infection clears, but never delay a referral already indicated.

1Confirm — recurrence, then the pattern

≥2 UTIs in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months, clinically compatible and culture-supported where possible (NG112).

PatternMeansNext
Relapse: same organism soon after treatmentInadequate treatment or a focus (stone, prostate, stricture, residual urine)Imaging; usually specialist
Reinfection: different organism, or a longer gapRe-ascent; the usual pattern in womenPrevention ladder

Negative cultures → rethink: menopausal atrophy, STI, thrush, bladder pain syndrome, overactive bladder, stones, bladder cancer.

2Check — look for the trigger

☐ Culture at baseline and for breakthrough, atypical or early recurrent episodes  ☐ Intercourse, spermicide, fluids, voiding, constipation  ☐ Menopause and atrophy, cystocoele; men: gentle PR  ☐ Diabetes; SGLT2 inhibitor (review with diabetes team, don't stop reflexively)  ☐ HbA1c, eGFR when indicated

Post-void residual if poor emptying, prolapse, neurological disease, relapse or a man. Ultrasound for men, relapse, upper UTI, stones, obstruction or unknown cause — not routine in women with an apparent cause. Pneumaturia → CT (fistula).

3Decide — treat infection, not bacteriuria

  • Asymptomatic bacteriuria in men or non-pregnant women, older people with confusion or falls, or a catheter → no antibiotics. Exceptions: pregnancy (treat on culture) and before a urological procedure breaching the mucosa.
  • Acute episode: culture first; treat per the acute UTI protocols, guided by previous cultures.

5Treat — prevention ladder, in order (NG112)

Step 1 · everyoneFluids, void regularly and after sex, treat constipation, avoid spermicide
Step 2 · peri/post-menopausalConsider vaginal oestrogen, review ≤12 months. Past breast cancer: non-hormonal first; specialist if on an aromatase inhibitor
Step 3 · considerTrigger → single dose: trimethoprim 200 mg, nitrofurantoin 100 mg, amoxicillin or cefalexin 500 mg. Not enough → methenamine 1 g BD (women, not pregnant)
Step 4 · last resortDaily at night: trimethoprim 100 mg, nitrofurantoin 50–100 mg, amoxicillin 250 mg or cefalexin 125 mg, chosen on cultures
  • Nitrofurantoin only if eGFR ≥45. Methenamine: treat the current UTI first; no citrate sachets or acetazolamide; check SPC contraindications. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Any prophylaxis: review within 6 months — episodes, breakthrough cultures, side effects; step down or stop? Methenamine then 12-monthly.
  • Nitrofurantoin long term: baseline U&E and LFTs; breathlessness, cough or jaundice → stop, investigate (MHRA).
  • Trimethoprim long term: blood counts; K⁺ and U&E if older or on ACEi/ARB.
  • Vaginal oestrogen: post-menopausal bleeding → NG12; never blame the cream.

7Refer

  • Specialist advice: men, recurrent upper UTI, women with no cause found, pregnancy, children; relapse, Proteus, stones, significant residual, failed ladder.
  • 60+, recurrent or persistent unexplained UTI → consider Non-urgent bladder cancer referral.
  • Cause: prostate → male LUTS; cystocoele → urogynaecology; voiding dysfunction → pelvic floor physio.

8Never

  • Let an antibiotic course close the haematuria question.
  • Antibiotics for asymptomatic bacteriuria outside pregnancy.
  • Daily antibiotics before the lower rungs, or without a 6-month review.
  • Nitrofurantoin long term with eGFR below 45, or for pyelonephritis.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 Same day “Fever with shivering, back or side pain, vomiting, confusion or feeling very unwell — get help the same day — you may need hospital or a 999 ambulance. Painful and can't pass urine — same day.”   48 hours: “Not improving 48 hours into treatment — contact us.”   Blood: “If you see blood in your urine, tell me even if you also have an infection; we'll check it has gone.”   Nitrofurantoin: “Breathlessness, cough or yellow skin — tell us.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
