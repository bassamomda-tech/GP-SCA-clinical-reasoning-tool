Mastitis and breast abscess

For: lactational and non-lactational breast inflammation, infection and abscess in primary care. Not for: a breast lump or skin change without inflammation (NICE NG12 breast criteria) or high-risk sepsis (999, NG253/NG255). Antibiotic choice, dose and duration: local Greater Manchester antimicrobial formulary.

ABM Protocol #36 (2022)NICE CKSNICE NG12NG253 · NG255 sepsisNG194
Core rule: physiological feeding, not forced emptying; antibiotics when bacterial infection is likely; and never give a second empirical course without ultrasound — a lump or redness that will not settle may be an abscess or a cancer.

0Act now — sepsis, an undrained abscess, a cancer that looks like infection

  • Possible sepsis (e.g. RR ≥25, systolic ≤90, HR >130, confusion, mottled skin): NG255 if pregnant or recently pregnant, else NG253; high risk → 999
  • Spreading cellulitis, necrotic skin, pain out of proportion, immunosuppression, no oral route, suspected toxic shock → 999 / Same-day hospital
  • Suspected abscess (discrete tender lump, fluctuance) or no response at 48 h of the right antibiotic → breast-service ultrasound Same or next working day (Same day if unwell, rapidly progressive, very painful or skin necrosis)
  • Aged 30+ with an unexplained breast lump (with or without pain), or 50+ with discharge, retraction or other change in one nipple → refer Suspected cancer pathway (NG12).
  • Skin changes that suggest breast cancer (redness, oedema or peau d’orange not responding; inflammatory cancer may have no mass) → consider Suspected cancer pathway

Soft painless milk cyst = galactocele. Not feeding? Think periductal mastitis (smokers).

2Check — every consultation

☐ Obs and mental state (NEWS2)  ☐ Both breasts and axillae: lump, fluctuance, peau d’orange, nipple change  ☐ Feeding, pumping, fissure, baby’s weight  ☐ Prior antibiotics; same-site episodes  ☐ Smoking

Culture if no response at 48 h, recurrent, hospital-acquired, severe or penicillin-allergic.

3Decide — who needs antibiotics

  • Systemically well → block 4 first. Start antibiotics if no clear improvement after about 12–24 h of effective feeding measures, or sooner if worsening, systemically unwell or clinically infected (e.g. nipple fissure). Fever alone is not proof.

4Feeding measures — first-line; continue with any antibiotic

  • Feed to the baby’s needs, affected side usually included; no extra pumping or “emptying”; too painful → express for comfort.
  • Cold packs, ibuprofen and/or paracetamol, supportive bra; no deep massage, oils or soaks.
  • Infant-feeding support (health visitor or team) for every lactational case (NG194).

5Treat — local formulary choice, dose and duration

Lactational · 1st lineFlucloxacillin (ABM: 500 mg four times daily; 10–14 days commonly used).
Penicillin allergyErythromycin or clarithromycin per local guidance; check interactions.
Non-lactationalLocal pathway: consider co-amoxiclav (anaerobes).
Not better at 48 hCulture, ultrasound, reconsider cancer. No second empirical course.
  • Breastfeeding: penicillins and macrolides are compatible; watch the baby for diarrhoea, rash or thrush (SPS).
  • Abscess: ultrasound-guided aspiration, often repeated, pus for culture; surgery if very large, skin necrosis or failed aspiration. Keep feeding.
  • Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — short intervals, a definite end point

  • 48 h on antibiotics: improving → complete the course; not clearly better → block 0 (ultrasound), culture.
  • End of course: redness, lump and pain gone. Lump, skin change or unilateral nipple change persisting after inflammation settles → Urgent breast assessment; NG12 if unexplained.

7Refer — urgent ones are in block 0

  • Recurrence at the same site → Urgent breast service imaging and culture, even if it improves.
  • Recurrent periductal mastitis or mammary duct fistula → routine breast surgical. Offer stop-smoking support.
  • Under 30, unexplained lump → NG12: consider non-urgent referral — but a lump persisting after mastitis still needs imaging.

8Never

  • Repeat antibiotics for “mastitis that won’t shift” without ultrasound.
  • Assume a residual lump is an abscess, or that breastfeeding explains a lump at 30+.
  • Advise pumping after feeds to “empty” the breast, or deep massage.
  • Advise stopping breastfeeding because of mastitis or its antibiotics.
  • Exclude sepsis on a single normal vital sign.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you are breathing fast, confused or very drowsy, or your skin is cold, blotchy or mottled, call 999.”   Same day “If it is getting worse, you feel shivery and unwell, you can’t feed or drink, or the pain becomes much worse, call us the same day.”   12–24 h: “Keep feeding as your baby needs, no extra pumping, cold packs and ibuprofen. Not clearly better within 12 to 24 hours — call us.”   Lump: “If a lump appears, or is left once the redness has gone, ask for an urgent appointment — we’ll want it scanned.”
