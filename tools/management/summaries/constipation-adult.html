Constipation in adults

For: adults with new or chronic constipation, including opioid-induced constipation and faecal impaction. Not for: children, or palliative care (use the palliative constipation page); IBS with constipation → IBS page; opioid review → opioids in non-cancer pain page.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BDA 2025NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)NICE HTG690 (FIT)NICE TA345 · TA651 · TA211GMMMG formulary
Core rule: before any laxative, exclude a constipating drug and screen the red flags — and never start a bulk-forming laxative, or give oral laxatives, when obstruction is suspected.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected obstruction or acute abdomen — severe or escalating colicky pain, progressive distension, persistent (especially faeculent) vomiting, no flatus or stool, peritonism, unable to tolerate fluids, new constipation after abdominal surgery → do not escalate laxatives; Same-day assessment
  • Rectal or anal mass, or unexplained anal ulceration → consider Suspected cancer pathway; don't wait for FIT.
  • Offer FIT: unexplained change in bowel habit or iron-deficiency anaemia at any age; abdominal mass (refer at the same time if concerning); 40+ weight loss and abdominal pain; under 50 rectal bleeding with abdominal pain or weight loss; 50+ rectal bleeding, abdominal pain or weight loss; 60+ any anaemia. FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g → Suspected cancer pathway
  • FIT <10 µg Hb/g or sample not returned does not exclude colorectal cancer → do not delay referral if symptoms persist or concern stays high.
  • Overflow diarrhoea or a palpable loaded colon → check for obstruction, then treat the impaction first, before any ladder step.

1Confirm — and find the cause

Infrequent, hard or difficult stools, straining or incomplete evacuation; chronic if present for more than about 3 months. Ask: recent change, bleeding or melaena, weight loss, family history, opioid dose, mobility, cognition, fluid restriction.

DrugsOpioids, iron, calcium-channel blockers, antimuscarinics
Other causesHypothyroidism, hypercalcaemia, diabetes, IBS, mechanical

2Check

☐ Drug list  ☐ Thyroid, calcium, glucose where indicated  ☐ Abdomen and hydration  ☐ PR, with consent and chaperone, if impaction, rectal mass, anorectal disease or evacuation disorder suspected  ☐ FIT if block 0 criteria

Don't miss: avoid or seek advice on PR or rectal treatment in severe thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, recent rectal surgery or significant immunosuppression.

3Decide — cause first, then non-drug measures

  • Constipating medicine → switch where possible; on opioids, is the opioid still needed?
  • Uncomplicated: fibre gradually, with fluid unless fluid-restricted (heart failure, advanced CKD, hyponatraemia); activity; unhurried routine after meals; footstool.
  • Impaction or established opioid-induced → treat now; lifestyle must not delay it. Starting regular opioids → usually a proactive bowel regimen.

4Target

GoalSoft, formed, regular stool; combine classes if needed
ThenLowest effective regimen; ongoing maintenance if chronic or while an opioid continues

5Treat — the laxative ladder

Step 1 · bulk-formingIspaghula husk, with fluid, if diet alone fails
Step 2 · osmoticMacrogol (often local first choice; typically 1–3 sachets daily) or lactulose 15 mL BD
Step 3 · add a stimulantStools soft but infrequent: senna 7.5–15 mg or bisacodyl 5–10 mg at night
Opioid-inducedOsmotic + stimulant. Optimised but inadequate → consider naloxegol or naldemedine
  • Before naloxegol/naldemedine: exclude obstruction; check renal function, CYP3A4 inhibitors.
  • Impaction: exclude obstruction → product-specific high-dose macrogol ± stimulant; rectal treatment if oral fails or is unsuitable, unless contraindicated.
  • Macrogol products are not interchangeable; check renal function, hydration and fluid restriction. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • 2–4 weeks: inadequate → step up or add a second class.
  • Not responding? Re-check impaction, drugs, thyroid, calcium, glucose, IBS-C; try osmotic + stimulant at adequate doses.
  • Once controlled: reduce; don't stop just because stools temporarily improved. New alarm feature → investigate.

7Refer — specialist

  • Refractory despite optimised measures → consider specialist referral. Prucalopride may be considered in women meeting NICE TA211; others → specialist advice. Linaclotide only within GM criteria.
  • Impaction failing oral and rectal treatment; suspected metabolic, neurological or structural cause.

8Never

  • Start a bulk-forming laxative in established opioid-induced constipation, faecal impaction or suspected obstruction.
  • Give oral laxatives, or high-dose macrogol, if obstruction, ileus or perforation is suspected.
  • Let laxatives delay investigation or referral of a red flag.
  • Treat leaking loose stool as "diarrhoea" without checking for overflow.
  • Close the case on a FIT <10 µg Hb/g, or because there is no weight loss or bleeding.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 Same-day “Severe or worsening tummy pain, a swelling tummy, repeated vomiting, not keeping fluids down, or passing no wind or stool — stop the laxatives and contact us or NHS 111 the same day; if the pain is severe or you feel very unwell, call 999 or go to A&E.”   Within a week “Bleeding from the bottom, weight loss, a tummy or back-passage lump, or a bowel change that persists — contact us to be seen within a week. A normal stool test does not rule everything out.”
Overflow: “Leaking loose stool while constipated can mean a blockage of hard stool — contact us within a few days, the same day if you also have tummy pain, swelling or vomiting.”   Fibre: “Increase fibre slowly and drink enough — unless you have been told to limit fluids.”
