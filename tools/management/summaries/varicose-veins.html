Varicose veins

For: adults with varicose veins in the legs (pregnancy: block 4). Not for: acute unilateral swelling (use the DVT pathway), arterial disease (peripheral arterial disease), cellulitis; active ulcers go to the local leg-ulcer/wound service.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG168NICE CG147Local leg-ulcer & compression protocolSPC (emc)
Core rule: refer on symptoms and complications, not appearance; confirm adequate arterial supply before any compression; and teach every patient with prominent veins the bleeding first aid.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Bleeding varicosity → lie flat, leg above the heart, firm continuous pressure ≥10 minutes. Continues, heavy, faint, or on an anticoagulant → 999 After any bleed, even if stopped → Immediate vascular referral
  • Acute unilateral swelling (suspected DVT) → two-level Wells and local DVT pathway Same-day; no compression until done. Breathless or chest pain → 999
  • Acute limb ischaemia, or ischaemic symptoms under compression → remove compression; 999 / emergency vascular
  • Superficial vein thrombosis extensive, rapidly extending, near a deep-vein junction, significant risk factors or DVT symptoms → Urgent duplex / vascular
  • Ulcer infected with systemic illness, ischaemic, atypical, rapidly progressive or severely painful → Urgent vascular / secondary care

1Confirm — clinical; the vascular service does the duplex

Ask: aching, heaviness, itching, swelling, cramps. Examine: distribution, skin, chronic venous insufficiency.

Troublesome symptoms affecting quality of life → a referral criterion alone. Complications (skin change, ulcer, bleeding, thrombophlebitis) change the plan. Appearance only → generally not a routine NHS referral (local commissioning).

Swollen leg, also: cellulitis, ruptured Baker's cyst, heart failure, lymphoedema, dependent oedema.

2Check — before any compression

☐ DVT excluded  ☐ Pedal pulses  ☐ ABPI where appropriate  ☐ Diabetes, CKD, older age or calcification → toe pressures or vascular assessment. Don't miss: ABPI may read falsely high; pulses do not exclude PAD.

3Decide — referral rests on step 1

  • Any CG168 criterion met (block 7) → refer, alongside conservative measures. Explain natural history and funding honestly.
  • Everyone: walking, avoid prolonged standing or sitting, elevate legs at rest, emollients, weight support where appropriate — never delay referral for weight loss.
  • No oral drug cures varicose veins; drugs treat complications.

4Compression — always after the arterial check

5Treat — the skin complications

  • Venous eczema: emollient plus a short topical corticosteroid course, potency for severity; no topical antibiotics unless indicated. Warmth, spreading erythema, pain or systemic illness → assess for cellulitis.
  • Superficial thrombophlebitis: assess extent, location, progression and risk factors (previous VTE, cancer, pregnancy, thrombophilia). Analgesia or an NSAID alone only if limited and low-risk; anticoagulation per local VTE pathway.
  • Active venous ulcer: local leg-ulcer/wound service plus vascular assessment; antibiotics only for clinical infection.
  • Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — each review; reassess if worse

  • Symptoms: significant quality-of-life impact → refer. Skin: chronic venous changes → routine vascular referral, treat eczema meanwhile.
  • On compression: tolerance; re-confirm arterial supply; adjust per local protocol. Borderline arterial result → reduced compression or vascular advice.
  • New bleeding, ulcer or thrombophlebitis → blocks 0 and 7.

7Refer — vascular service, NICE CG168

  • Symptomatic primary or recurrent varicose veins affecting quality of life — even without skin change.
  • Skin changes of chronic venous insufficiency: pigmentation, eczema, lipodermatosclerosis.
  • Superficial vein thrombosis with suspected venous incompetence.
  • Venous leg ulcer (broken skin below the knee not healed within 2 weeks) — plus local leg-ulcer service; healed venous ulcer to prevent recurrence. Bleeding → immediately (block 0).

8Never

  • Compression before checking the arterial supply — it can cause ischaemia.
  • A stocking for acute unilateral swelling before the DVT pathway is followed.
  • Compression hosiery instead of referral when intervention is suitable and criteria are met.
  • Delay vascular referral while waiting for weight loss.
  • Skip referral because a bleed has stopped, or let the patient stand or walk to get help.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Bleeding: “Lie down, raise the leg above your heart, press firmly for at least 10 minutes without checking. Keeps bleeding, heavy, you feel faint, or on a blood thinner — 999. Even if it stops, contact us the same day.”   Clot: “Sudden painful swelling of one calf — us or NHS 111 the same day; breathless or chest pain — 999.”   Ulcer: “A break in the skin or a sore around the ankle — contact us within a week; rapidly worse, very painful, hot spreading redness or unwell — the same day.”   Stockings: “Increasing pain, numbness, a pale, blue or cold foot, worse ulceration or marked swelling — take them off and contact us the same day; foot stays pale, cold, numb or very painful — 999.”
