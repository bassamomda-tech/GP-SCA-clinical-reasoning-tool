Acute cough, CAP and COPD exacerbation in adults

For: adults with acute bronchitis, community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) or a COPD exacerbation. Not for: children (separate paediatric pathway). Undifferentiated cough → acute cough pathway. Local pathways supersede this summary.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG250 (2025)NG120NG253NG115 · NG114NG12
Core rule: “chest infection” is not a diagnosis — grade severity with full observations and CRB-65 plus judgement; a septic patient goes by 999 with a pre-alert, never home on oral antibiotics.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Possible sepsis or respiratory failure: new confusion, severe breathlessness, cyanosis, shock, high RR, low BP, tachycardia, marked hypoxia, mottling, temperature extremes, low urine output → 999 + pre-alert Oxygen if indicated; do not delay transfer for tests or treatment.
  • CRB-65 ≥2 and septic, hypoxic or clinically unwell → 999 + pre-alert
  • Drowsiness, headache or rising CO₂ on oxygen when at risk of hypercapnia (type 2 failure) → target 88–92% → 999 for blood gases.
  • Older patient with cough and new confusion or falls → lower the threshold to examine, investigate and admit; no fever is not reassurance.
  • Cannot take oral treatment → Same-day hospital assessment  A low NEWS2 or normal temperature never excludes sepsis.

1Confirm — which of the three?

DiagnosisDiscriminating features
Acute bronchitisCough ± sputum, systemically well; usually viral. Wheeze or scattered crackles do not establish pneumonia.
CAPFocal signs, fever, tachypnoea, hypoxia, systemic upset. A clear chest does not exclude it.
COPD exacerbationWorsening breathlessness, cough or sputum from baseline needing a change in treatment. Focal signs, pleuritic pain, hypoxia or confusion → think pneumonia.

2Check — face to face if severity is uncertain

☐ RR, SpO₂, HR, BP, temperature, consciousness  ☐ Chest examination  ☐ CRB-65  ☐ Baseline, comorbidity, frailty, immunosuppression  ☐ Cough duration, haemoptysis, weight loss, night sweats, pack-years, TB risk

Remote review cannot exclude pneumonia, hypoxia or sepsis. CXR if diagnosis or severity uncertain. Don't miss: PE, heart failure, pneumothorax, lung cancer, TB.

3Decide — place of care (CAP: CRB-65 + judgement)

Confusion · RR ≥30 · BP <90 systolic or ≤60 diastolic · age ≥65

ScoreRiskPlace of care
0LowUsually primary care, with safety-netting
1IntermediateShared decision: primary care, virtual ward, SDEC, hospital at home or hospital
2IntermediateConsider hospital referral; lower threshold with frailty, comorbidity, hypoxia
3–4HighConsider hospital; 999 if unstable

CRB-65 omits hypoxia: SpO₂ 90–92% matters even at score 0.

4Target — oxygen if hypoxic or unstable

Most adults: SpO₂ 94–98%. At risk of hypercapnic failure (previous hypercapnia, severe COPD — not every COPD patient): 88–92%.

5Treat — to the severity

  • Bronchitis: do not routinely offer an antibiotic; self-care. Systemically very unwell → offer immediate. Higher risk of complications → consider immediate or back-up.
  • CRP if pneumonia not diagnosed and need unclear: <20 mg/L no routine antibiotic · 20–100 consider back-up · >100 offer. Never overrides judgement.
  • Low-severity CAP: offer amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily, 5 days. Penicillin allergy → doxycycline 200 mg day 1 then 100 mg daily (5 days total) or clarithromycin. Stop at 5 days if stable; review if worse or not improving.
  • COPD exacerbation: exclude the mimics; more bronchodilator. Breathlessness limiting activity → consider prednisolone 30 mg daily, 5 days. Consider an antibiotic by sputum purulence, severity and risk (amoxicillin, doxycycline or clarithromycin; 5 days).
  • Moderate/high-severity CAP: no home oral antibiotics outside a formal supported-care pathway. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • No routine resolution CXR. Consider one ~6 weeks after discharge if smoker, over 50, persistent or worsening symptoms, or weight loss; record who reviews it. A normal film does not exclude cancer.
  • Recovery: COPD and spirometry review, inhaler technique, self-management plan; flu, pneumococcal, COVID vaccines; stop smoking.

7Refer

  • 40+: unexplained haemoptysis, or CXR suggests cancer → Suspected cancer pathway 2+ unexplained symptoms (cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, weight or appetite loss), or 1 if ever smoked → CXR ≤2 weeks Persistent or recurrent infection → consider urgent CXR.
  • Suspected TB (cough ≥3 weeks, fever, night sweats, weight loss, haemoptysis) → Urgent TB-service advice Mask and room per local policy; a normal CXR does not exclude TB.
  • Respiratory: recurrent or non-resolving infection, complex COPD, bronchiectasis, empyema.

8Never

  • Send a septic patient home on oral antibiotics because the label felt routine.
  • Be reassured by a low CRB-65 without reading oxygen saturation.
  • Be reassured by no fever in the elderly, frail or immunosuppressed.
  • Delay a 999 transfer for investigations or treatment.
  • Withhold an antibiotic without a specific safety-net: what to watch for, when, and who to call.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you become confused or muddled, very short of breath, or your lips turn blue — call 999.”   Same day “Getting more breathless, a fever that won't settle, not eating or drinking, or getting worse rather than slowly better — contact us the same day.”   Back-up script: “Only use it if things change, and contact us the same day.”   Expected course: “The cough commonly takes 3 to 4 weeks; if it isn't improving by then, book a review.”
