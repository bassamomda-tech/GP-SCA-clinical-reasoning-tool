Gender dysphoria and gender incongruence

For: adults and young people with gender incongruence or dysphoria in primary care, including people on clinic, bridged or self-sourced hormones. Not for: starting routine gender-affirming hormones (specialist-led only). Under-18s → local NHS paediatrics or CAMHS, not the gender service directly.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
GMC trans healthcareRCGP position statementNHS England / GNRSSNHS SPS bridgingNHS screening
Core rule: offer the referral today without gatekeeping, ask about suicide and self-harm every time, ask about self-sourced hormones so that honesty is safe, and screen by anatomy, not recorded gender.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Imminent risk, active intent, plan, access to means, can't stay safe, or recent serious self-harm → Same-day urgent mental-health assessment or emergency services.
  • Suicidal thoughts, no immediate intent or plan → safety plan, crisis contacts, ask about means, short prescriptions; prompt review and mental-health referral.
  • Haematocrit rising on testosterone, particularly ≥0.54 → repeat promptly → Urgent specialist advice; no venesection before assessing the cause.
  • VTE symptoms on oestrogen → Same-day assessment as for any patient, plus Urgent specialist advice on stopping oestrogen.
  • Raised K⁺ on spironolactone → repeat with renal function → Same-day if severe.
  • Raised prolactin with headache or visual symptoms, or worsening LFTs with jaundice or unwell (review cyproterone) → Urgent specialist advice

1Confirm — the gender service assesses; no proof needed

A request is enough: no psychiatric diagnosis, mental-health pre-assessment, proof of identity, “living in role” or trial period. Document consent and the request.

2Check — first visit and every review

☐ Name, pronouns, record updated  ☐ Suicide and self-harm asked directly (PHQ-9/GAD-7 don't replace this)  ☐ “Anything you've sourced yourself?”  ☐ Abuse, safeguarding  ☐ Screening by anatomy:

  • Cervix → screen; registered male is not invited, so the practice recalls. Breast: by age, hormones, surgery. AAA: trans women registered female → local programme.
  • Prostate: PSA may be lower on feminising treatment; a low PSA does not exclude disease.
  • Recorded gender changed → review every screening, register and recall.

3Decide — self-sourced hormones and bridging (adults)

  • Always: confirm products, harm reduction, specialist advice, bloods and BP where appropriate.
  • Bridging → consider only exceptionally, all three met: self-prescribing from an unregulated source (or highly likely to); to reduce serious harm, self-harm or suicide risk; specialist advice sought. Within competence; document; NHS SPS resources.
  • Declining to prescribe is not declining to monitor. No routine initiation in primary care.

5Treat — general practice care through the wait

  • Mental health: treat as for anyone (SSRI, Talking Therapies), in parallel with the referral.
  • Shared injecting equipment → hepatitis B vaccine and BBV screen.
  • Fertility: hormones can suppress it; recovery not guaranteed. Discuss preservation before any treatment, incl. bridging.
  • Testosterone is not a contraceptive; stop it in pregnancy. Contraception by anatomy (CoSRH).
  • Cyproterone, finasteride: check MHRA advice and SPC. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — per the specialist or shared-care protocol

  • Testosterone: haematocrit at baseline, then per protocol; check smoking, sleep apnoea.
  • Spironolactone: K⁺, renal function, BP. Hormone levels: read by formulation and timing.
  • BP, lipids, CV risk: baseline, then at least annually; scores not validated, so calculate both.
  • Consider DXA after gonadectomy with time off hormones. Mental health every review.

7Refer — other routes

  • Under 18: see alone for part; safeguarding, Gillick (16+ presume capacity). New puberty blockers only in the PATHWAYS trial; hormones only from NHS CYP Gender Services. Check current national policy.
  • Shared care offered → don't refuse wholesale; confirm the local protocol is current.
  • New breast or prostate symptoms → usual suspected-cancer pathways; urology advice if PSA is hard to interpret.
  • Fertility preservation → fertility service. Cervical screening not tolerable in practice → sexual-health service.

8Never

  • Gatekeep: demand a diagnosis, pre-assessment or proof, or delay the referral because of a mental-health problem.
  • Start puberty blockers or hormones for under-18s in primary care, or prescribe, continue or share care for hormones from unregulated providers.
  • Refuse all involvement with self-sourced hormones, or decline to monitor them.
  • Screen by recorded gender, or blame chest pain, headache, abdominal pain or low mood on gender or hormones.
  • Provide, endorse or refer for conversion practices.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you feel you can't keep yourself safe, call 999. For urgent mental-health help, call NHS 111 and choose option 2, or your local crisis line.”   Hormones: “Never inject into a vein or share needles, don't double a missed dose, and bring the packet or a photo next time.”   Testosterone: “It isn't a contraceptive, even if your periods stop.”   The wait: “The wait is likely to be years. I will keep seeing you throughout.”
