Palliative pain and opioids

For: adults with cancer-related pain or palliative pain in primary care. Not for: children, or chronic non-cancer pain (use the chronic pain and opioid prescribing protocols); neuropathic pain detail is in the neuropathic pain protocol.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
GMEC/GMMMG 2025NICE CG140NG234NG31SPS
Core rule: uncontrolled pain is an emergency, but more opioid is not always the answer — exclude MSCC and opioid toxicity, treat each pain by its mechanism, and give every opioid with a rescue dose, a laxative and a current JIC prescription.

0Act now — oncological and opioid emergencies

  • Known cancer + new limb weakness, numbness, bladder or bowel dysfunction, or radicular/band-like pain (suspected MSCC) → Same-day MSCC coordinator/acute oncology immediately; dexamethasone 16 mg orally as soon as possible if neurological symptoms or signs before imaging; whole-spine MRI within 24 h. 999 if neurology deteriorating, suspected instability, uncontrolled pain or unsafe to transfer.
  • Pain crisis: NRS ≥8 not responding to breakthrough → Same-day phone palliative care; consider admission or urgent SC syringe driver.
  • New drowsiness, delirium, hallucinations, myoclonus or hyperalgesia after escalation (opioid-induced neurotoxicity) → withhold escalation; renal function, reversible causes, CNS depressants → Same-day specialist advice. Naloxone only for respiratory depression: small titrated doses.
  • SVCO with airway compromise, stridor, severe dyspnoea, syncope, cerebral symptoms or haemodynamic compromise → 999 + acute oncology; other suspected SVCO → Same-day acute oncology.
  • Pathological fracture → immobilise, no weight-bearing, analgesia, Same-day emergency/orthopaedic assessment; 999 for severe pain, deformity, neurovascular compromise, unsafe transfer or suspected spinal instability.
  • Brain metastases + new severe headache, photophobia, neck stiffness, or morning headache, vomiting, papilloedema → Same-day oncology/neurology; no lumbar puncture in primary care.

1Assess — each pain separately, and the whole person

NRS 0–10 at rest AND on movement; Abbey scale if unable to self-report. Total pain: psychological, social, spiritual too.

BoneNSAID + opioid; radiotherapy via oncology
Liver capsule / colicDexamethasone / hyoscine butylbromide
NeuropathicOne of amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin, pregabalin; steroid if nerve compression

2Check — before prescribing

☐ Renal, liver function ☐ Opioid doses actually taken ☐ Bowels, obstruction ☐ Sedation, delirium, myoclonus, RR ☐ Benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, alcohol ☐ Goals, place of care ☐ Carer, OOH access

Don't miss: pain worse despite escalation + drowsiness or myoclonus = toxicity, not a need for more opioid.

3WHO ladder

  • Mild (NRS 1–3): paracetamol ± NSAID (with PPI). Moderate (4–6): codeine, dihydrocodeine or tramadol — often skipped for low-dose strong opioid.
  • Severe (7–10): oral morphine first choice. Moderate/severe renal or hepatic impairment → specialist advice before strong opioids.

4Convert — approximate; check SmPC and local table

  • Oral codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol → oral morphine ÷ 10. Oral morphine → oral oxycodone ÷ 1.5–2; SC morphine ÷ 2; SC diamorphine ÷ 3.
  • Switching opioid: reduce 25–50%; new rescue dose.

5Start and titrate morphine

1 · Start (opioid-naive)MR morphine 10–15 mg 12-hourly, or IR 5 mg 4-hourly. Frail/elderly: IR 2.5 mg 4-hourly.
2 · Rescue doseIR about 1/10 to 1/6 of the 24-h dose, 4-hourly PRN, local maximum. Recalculate at every change.
3 · Titrate every 24–48 hAdd IR used to the regular 24-h dose; ÷ 2 for 12-hourly MR. ≥3 rescue doses/24 h → advice.
4 · Day one, alwaysRegular stimulant laxative ± osmotic; PRN antiemetic by likely cause.
  • Incident pain: IR about 30 min before; transmucosal fentanyl only on specialist advice. Can't swallow / last days: SC syringe driver. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Every 24–48 h while titrating (phone if needed), monthly when stable: NRS, PRN use, bowels, sedation, mood, carer.
  • NRS 6–7 despite analgesia → phone review within 24 h. Warn about driving when starting or changing dose.
  • Schedule 2 CDs: monthly supplies of both regular and PRN.

7Refer and plan ahead

  • Same-day specialist palliative care: uncontrolled despite rescue, renal impairment, rotation, SC titration, bowel obstruction.
  • Oncology: single-fraction radiotherapy for bone metastases; coeliac plexus block for pancreatic cancer.
  • Plan: GSF register; ACP, DNACPR/ReSPECT via EPaCCS; JIC box — reissue the PRN at every titration, tell district nurses; SR1; carer.

8Never

  • Wait for MRI before dexamethasone 16 mg in suspected MSCC with neurological signs.
  • Escalate the opioid for drowsiness + myoclonus + pain, or give naloxone for OIN without respiratory depression.
  • Start an opioid without a laxative, or titrate without reissuing the JIC PRN dose.
  • Switch opioids at 100%, or start a patch or methadone for unstable pain.
  • Stop the regular opioid when adding breakthrough doses.

9Safety-net — say it to patient and carer, then document it

999 “New weakness or numbness in the legs, or trouble passing urine or controlling the bowels → ring the 24-hour acute oncology line or 999 now. Very hard to wake, or slow or shallow breathing → ring 999 now.”   Same-day “Pain not settling after the extra dose, or new confusion or twitching → ring the 24-hour palliative care or GP number the same day.”   “The laxative is part of the treatment, because these medicines always slow the bowels. At the doses we're using, the evidence shows this does not shorten life.”
