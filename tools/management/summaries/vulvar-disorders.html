Vulvar disorders

For: women with persistent vulval itch, pain, soreness, a lesion or a swelling. Not for: children (local safeguarding and paediatric protocols; do not apply adult algorithms). Full detail: recurrent thrush, genital herpes and menopause protocols.

Core rule: examine every persistent vulval symptom before the next prescription — repeated blind antifungal prescribing is how lichen sclerosus and vulval cancer get diagnosed late.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Unexplained vulval lump, ulceration or bleeding → consider a suspected cancer pathway referral Urgent · NG12. Irregular pigmented lesion, bleeding or rapid change → suspected melanoma route Urgent · NG12
  • Persistent, indurated, ulcerated or treatment-resistant lesion, or a suspicious new lesion in lichen sclerosus → biopsy by an experienced clinician or Urgent referral
  • Bartholin abscess → drain in the practice if skilled, otherwise gynaecology; always if severe or septic Same-day. Bartholin mass aged 40 or over (especially solid, irregular, fixed or recurrent) → Urgent gynaecology
  • Suspected first-episode genital herpes in late pregnancy → obstetric and sexual-health advice Same-day. Suspected chancroid → GUM same-day
  • Child or vulnerable adult with features of abuse → local safeguarding and SARC pathways Same-day; minimum necessary examination only; police if acute assault.

1Confirm — symptom, skin and pH separate the causes

2Check — consent, chaperone, good light

☐ Inspect systematically, including perianal skin ☐ Swab ± STI tests ☐ pH (adjunct only) ☐ HSV PCR from an ulcer ☐ HbA1c if recurrent candida ☐ Ferritin, U&E, LFT, TFT if generalised itch ☐ Pregnancy status

Biopsy LS only if atypical, eroded, ulcerated, nodular or not responding.

3Decide

  • Thrush: clotrimazole 500 mg pessary or fluconazole 150 mg, single dose; pregnancy → topical imidazole 7 days.
  • Recurrent thrush = ≥4 symptomatic episodes in 12 months, ≥2 lab-confirmed → speciate first; C. albicans → fluconazole 150 mg every 72 h × 3, then weekly for 6 months, after checking pregnancy, interactions, liver.
  • GSM → offer vaginal oestrogen (even on systemic HRT) ± moisturisers or lubricants. Breast cancer history → non-hormonal first; on an aromatase inhibitor, discuss with oncology/menopause teams.
  • Self-care: emollient as soap substitute, no douching; offer smoking cessation.

5Treat — the diagnosis, not the symptom

LS · inductionClobetasol propionate 0.05% ointment nightly 4 weeks → alternate nights 4 weeks → twice weekly 4 weeks
LS · maintenanceIndividualised, often 1–2× weekly, long term. Thin film to all affected skin; 30 g ≈ 3 months
GSMEstradiol 10 microgram vaginal tablet daily 2 weeks, then twice weekly; continue as long as needed
VulvodyniaExplanation, irritant avoidance, pelvic-floor physio, psychosexual support; medicines individualised
  • First-episode herpes: aciclovir 400 mg TDS or valaciclovir 500 mg BD, 5 days. Contact dermatitis (product-linked): remove cause, hydrocortisone 1%.
  • Not settling? Reassess diagnosis, adherence, allergy, infection, VIN. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • LS: review after induction, then at least annually: symptoms, architecture, new lesions.
  • GSM: review at 3 months, then yearly; bleeding on it → assess.
  • Recurrent thrush: review at 6 months; relapse common — swab if symptoms, restart if confirmed.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Vulval clinic / dermatology: suspected erosive LP, refractory LS, uncertain diagnosis, or not responding to appropriate treatment within 3 months.
  • Vulvodynia persistent or severe → specialist vulval or pain service; psychosexual therapy.
  • Non-albicans or azole-resistant candida, or HSV suppression → GUM / specialist.

8Never

  • Repeat antifungal or steroid prescriptions, including by phone, without examining.
  • Assume vulval itch after the menopause is benign, or GSM is just ageing.
  • Start 6 months of fluconazole without microbiological confirmation and speciation.
  • Oral fluconazole in pregnancy.
  • A forensic or repeated genital examination outside your competence.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “A hot, rapidly growing, very painful swelling or a fever — or new genital sores late in pregnancy — contact us the same day.”   Within a week “Any new lump, bleeding, ulcer or quick change in the skin — contact us to be seen within a week.”   Clobetasol: “Cover all the affected skin thinly; it repairs the skin rather than thinning it; keep up the maintenance.”
