Hidradenitis suppurativa

For: adults and adolescents with hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) in primary care. Not for: children before puberty (refer to paediatric dermatology) or pregnancy (no tetracyclines; seek dermatology advice).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BAD HS guideline (2018)NICE TA392NICE TA935NICE NG12
Core rule: recognise HS early, treat it as chronic inflammation (not repeated infection) with a full 12-week course, and refer before tunnels and scarring set in.

0Act now

  • Fever, sepsis or rapidly spreading cellulitis → Same-day assessment (NICE NG51, NG141).
  • Non-healing ulcer, hard or growing mass, or bleeding in long-standing perianal or gluteal HS → Suspected cancer pathway referral (SCC).
  • Suicidal thoughts with plan or intent → Same-day crisis assessment
  • Perianal disease with bowel symptoms → faecal calprotectin; gastroenterology as indicated (Crohn's).

1Confirm — clinical diagnosis

Painful deep nodules, abscesses, tunnels and scarring in the axillae, groin, inframammary folds, perineum or buttocks, recurring (two or more flares in 6 months). Swab only if cellulitis.

HurleyFeatures
IAbscesses, no tunnels or scarring
IIRecurrent abscesses with tunnels and scarring, separated lesions
IIIDiffuse, interconnected tunnels across an area

2Check

☐ Hurley stage per site  ☐ Pain score, DLQI, PHQ-9  ☐ Smoking, BMI  ☐ HbA1c, BP, lipids  ☐ Bowel and joint symptoms  ☐ Pregnancy and contraception before tetracyclines.

3Decide

  • Everyone: smoking cessation, weight loss, loose clothing, antiseptic wash, dressings, regular analgesia; avoid long-term opioids.
  • Mild, localised → topical clindamycin. Widespread or Hurley II → 12-week tetracycline.
  • Hurley II or III, or failure at 12 weeks → dermatology.

4Treat — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

MildClindamycin 1% topical twice daily for 12 weeks
WidespreadLymecycline 408 mg or doxycycline 100 mg once daily for 12 weeks
FlareAnalgesia, warm compresses; incision and drainage relieves pain but recurs
SpecialistClindamycin + rifampicin, adalimumab (TA392), secukinumab (TA935), surgery
  • Tetracyclines: not in pregnancy, breastfeeding or under 12 years.
  • Rifampicin (specialist) reduces hormonal contraception, DOAC and warfarin effect.

5Monitor

  • 12 weeks: pain, lesion count, DLQI, side effects → continue or refer.
  • Each review: smoking, weight, mood, cardiometabolic risk.
  • On biologics: treat infections promptly, tell the specialist, no live vaccines.

6Refer — non-emergency

  • Dermatology: Hurley II or III, not controlled after 12-week tetracycline, major impact, doubt, children.
  • Gastroenterology or rheumatology for Crohn's or inflammatory arthritis.
  • Psychological support: NHS Talking Therapies.

7Never

  • Treat recurrent flexural 'boils' with repeated short antibiotic courses without naming HS.
  • Judge a tetracycline before 12 weeks.
  • Give a tetracycline in pregnancy or under 12 years.
  • Ignore a non-healing ulcer in long-standing perianal HS.
  • Miss low mood: ask about suicidal thoughts.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “High temperature, shivery or unwell, or redness spreading quickly — contact us the same day.”   “A sore that will not heal, keeps bleeding or forms a hard lump, or feeling low or thinking of harming yourself — tell us.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Sepsis, cancer and mental health red flags excluded HS named and Hurley stage recorded Comorbidity, pregnancy and contraception checked Lifestyle, 12-week treatment and review or referral booked Safety-net given and documented
