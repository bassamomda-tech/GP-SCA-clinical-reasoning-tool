UTI in children

For: infants, children and young people under 16 with a suspected UTI, first contact in primary care. Not for: hospital treatment of infants under 3 months or of the septic child (refer). Related pages: Fever in a child, Recurrent UTI, Constipation in children, Nocturnal enuresis.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG224NG143NG254NG109 / NG111NG12 (April 2026)
Core rule: under 3 months is a hospital problem; above that, get a clean sample, apply the NG224 dipstick rule for the age, classify the episode, and request the imaging at the time of diagnosis.

0Act now — assess the child before the urine

  • Under 3 months with a suspected UTI (febrile or not; may be hypothermic) → Immediate paediatric referral, urine for urgent microscopy and culture. Never an oral prescription alone; do not delay for a sample.
  • Any NG254 high-risk sepsis feature (mottled or ashen, non-blanching rash, reduced responsiveness, grunting, tachypnoea, reduced urine output) → 999 transfer, pre-alert. Red NG143 traffic-light feature → Immediate paediatric assessment. Do not delay treatment for urine.
  • Cannot tolerate oral fluids or antibiotics, persistent vomiting or clinical dehydration → Same-day paediatric assessment.
  • Atypical UTI (seriously ill, poor urine flow, abdominal or bladder mass, raised creatinine, septicaemia, no response in 48 h, non-E. coli) or febrile UTI with a known renal tract abnormality → Same-day paediatric discussion; ultrasound during the acute infection.
  • Palpable abdominal mass, unexplained enlarged abdominal organ or unexplained visible haematuria → consider a Very urgent (48 h) NG12 referral for Wilms' tumour or neuroblastoma, alongside the UTI pathway. Re-examine after voiding.

1Confirm — a sample that can be believed, read by age

Test any child with symptoms or signs of UTI, or unexplained fever ≥38 °C. Clean-catch (mid-stream if toilet-trained); pad only if impossible (never cotton wool or gauze) — read a positive cautiously. Not cultured within 4 h → fridge or boric acid.

2Check — examine and send culture

☐ Traffic-light observations  ☐ Hydration  ☐ Loins, abdomen, bladder  ☐ Genitalia  ☐ Spine  ☐ BP and growth (recurrent or atypical)

Culture: positive dipstick under 3 years, single positive at 3+, upper UTI, high or intermediate risk, recurrent, no response in 24–48 h, mismatch.

Don't miss: another source of fever; diabetes (glucose same day); adolescent STI or pregnancy.

3Decide — classify and write it in the notes

  • Upper: bacteriuria + fever ≥38 °C, or below 38 °C with loin pain or tenderness → consider paediatric referral (infant, unwell, urinary abnormality, recurrent, dehydrated, vomiting, uncertain).
  • Lower: bacteriuria without fever or systemic features.
  • Recurrent: ≥2 upper; or 1 upper + ≥1 lower; or ≥3 lower — count only credible, culture-backed episodes.
  • Typical: E. coli, responds within 48 h, no atypical features.

5Treat — NICE NG111 / NG109, 3 months and over

Upper · well enough for oralCulture first. Cefalexin 7–10 days; co-amoxiclav only if culture shows sensitivity. IV via paediatrics.
Lower · 3 daysTrimethoprim or nitrofurantoin if resistance risk low; second: amoxicillin (if sensitive) or cefalexin.
On the cultureSwitch to the narrowest effective agent.
Not better at 48 hAtypical: reassess in person, culture, acute ultrasound, paediatrics.
  • Nitrofurantoin: lower tract only, not under 3 months. Trimethoprim: not in the first trimester — ask about pregnancy.
  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen, fluids (no ibuprofen if dehydrated). Prophylaxis not routine after a first UTI; may be considered in recurrent UTI, specialist-guided. Doses per BNFC and GM guideline.

7Refer

  • Paediatric nephrology: bilateral renal abnormality, impaired renal function, DMSA scarring, faltering growth, raised BP, proteinuria.
  • Bladder and bowel service: dysfunctional voiding, daytime wetting, resistant constipation (CG99).
  • Safeguarding (genital injury, STI, disclosure, recurrent UTI with neglect) → local procedures; UTI alone is not abuse.

8Never

  • Manage an infant under 3 months with an oral prescription alone.
  • Delay antibiotics in a septic child to obtain a urine sample.
  • Diagnose UTI on a contaminated sample.
  • Nitrofurantoin for upper UTI; a second blind course at 48 h.
  • Call a mass a full bladder without re-examining after voiding.
  • Antibiotics for asymptomatic bacteriuria in a well child.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Floppy, unresponsive, very drowsy or hard to wake, mottled or blue, struggling to breathe or grunting, a rash that does not fade, a fit, or getting rapidly worse — call 999.”   Same day “Not improving after 24–48 hours of antibiotics, or worse at any point, vomiting doses, drinking much less, far fewer wet nappies, new loin pain or high fever — contact us the same day.”   Future fevers: “Mention this infection and bring a clean-catch sample.”   First simple UTI: “No routine repeat urine test, daily antibiotic or scan.”
