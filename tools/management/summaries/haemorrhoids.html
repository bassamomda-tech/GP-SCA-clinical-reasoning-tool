Haemorrhoids in adults

For: adults with rectal bleeding, perianal symptoms or suspected haemorrhoids. Not for: children; anal fissure (see anal fissure); undifferentiated rectal bleeding (see the rectal bleeding pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NICE HTG690 (FIT)GM formularySPC (emc)
Core rule: never attribute rectal bleeding to haemorrhoids unexamined — visible piles do not exclude colorectal disease, so examine and apply the NG12 and FIT criteria before you reassure.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Haemodynamic instability, syncope or presyncope with ongoing bleeding, major or rapidly increasing bleeding, severe symptomatic anaemia, severe abdominal pain or peritonism → 999 / ED with the last anticoagulant dose and renal function.
  • Suspected perianal abscess, painful irreducible prolapse, urinary retention, rapidly progressive swelling, severe pain with diagnostic uncertainty, fever or systemic illness → Same-day surgical assessment
  • Thrombosed external haemorrhoid (sudden severe pain, tense tender bluish lump) without the above → Urgent primary-care assessment; same-day if immunosuppressed, significantly frail or pain uncontrolled. Excision may be considered in selected early, severe cases.
  • Rectal mass, unexplained anal mass or unexplained anal ulceration → consider Suspected cancer pathway — do not wait for FIT.
  • NG12 colorectal features → offer quantitative FIT: change in bowel habit, iron-deficiency anaemia, abdominal mass; 40+ unexplained weight loss with abdominal pain; under 50 rectal bleeding with unexplained abdominal pain or weight loss; 50+ unexplained rectal bleeding, abdominal pain or weight loss; 60+ anaemia. FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g → Suspected cancer pathway. Abdominal mass with high concern → do not delay urgent referral for FIT.
  • Iron-deficiency anaemia → GI investigation per local pathway, even if piles are visible; do not attribute it to haemorrhoids.

1Confirm — examine before you label

Typical internal piles: painless bright-red blood on the paper or coating the stool; may prolapse or cause mucus and itch. Pain suggests thrombosis, strangulation, fissure, abscess or another diagnosis.

2Check — minimum, with consent and chaperone

☐ Bleeding: amount, duration, bright, dark or mixed  ☐ Bowel habit, weight loss, family history  ☐ Perianal inspection, DRE if tolerable, abdomen  ☐ Anticoagulants or antiplatelets  ☐ FBC, ferritin

Don't miss: a normal DRE does not exclude proximal disease. Too painful to examine → treat and plan reassessment, not reassurance.

3Decide

  • Uncomplicated → generally self-care (GM OTC policy).
  • Persistent symptoms despite appropriate conservative treatment → may justify referral for outpatient intervention — grade alone does not decide.
  • Anticoagulants or antiplatelets → do not stop without a clear indication and specialist advice; never blame bleeding on them alone.
  • Pregnancy → prevent constipation; short-term local relief; check product information.

4Target — treatment goals

GoalDetail
StoolsSoft, formed; no straining or long toilet sitting — procedures fail if constipation continues
TopicalsShort course; no repeats without reassessing the diagnosis

5Treat — soften first; topicals relieve, they don't treat

SoftenFibre, fluids + ispaghula (GM first choice) or macrogol if stools hard.
TopicalFor the dominant symptom; short course, per product information.
OutpatientBanding: internal grade I–II, selected III.
Colorectal teamOperative or specialist treatment.
  • Laxative cautions: obstruction, impaction, severe pain or vomiting, fluid restriction, frailty, pregnancy.
  • Thrombosed, not excised: analgesia, stool softening, conservative care with safety-netting.
  • Steroid creams: atrophy, sensitisation; not on ulcerated or infected skin. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — each review

  • Persistent bleeding despite treatment → re-examine; reconsider fissure, IBD, proctitis, neoplasia; consider lower-GI assessment.
  • New bowel-habit change, weight loss or anaemia → apply NG12.
  • FIT <10, no sample or negative → safety-net actively; it does not exclude cancer. Ongoing concern → further assessment.

7Refer — routine, via Bolton/GM colorectal pathways

  • Outpatient intervention: persistent symptoms despite appropriate conservative treatment.
  • Colorectal surgery: grade III–IV, substantial external component, circumferential prolapse, recurrence or failed previous procedures.

8Never

  • Attribute rectal bleeding to piles unexamined, or take a previous diagnosis of piles as a current one.
  • Wait for FIT with a rectal mass, unexplained anal mass or ulceration, or let a negative FIT close persistent concern.
  • Explain iron-deficiency anaemia by haemorrhoids, or bleeding by anticoagulation alone.
  • Repeat steroid or anaesthetic creams without reassessing the diagnosis.
  • Miss an abscess or strangulated prolapse behind a "thrombosed pile".

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Heavy or non-stop bleeding, or feeling faint — call 999 or go to A&E.”   Same-day “A painful swelling with fever, a lump that won't go back, or difficulty passing urine — contact us or NHS 111 the same day. Sudden severe pain with a tense lump — contact us the same day.”   Within a week “Blood mixed in or darker, a change in bowel habit, weight loss, tiredness or pallor, or bleeding that keeps going despite treatment — contact us to be seen within a week.”   Stools: “Fibre, fluids and not straining stop them coming back.”
