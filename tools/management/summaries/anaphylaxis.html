Anaphylaxis

For: adults and children with suspected anaphylaxis in the surgery, and their follow-up. Not for: skin-only reactions without A/B/C compromise (block 3); cardiac arrest (start ALS); IV adrenaline (specialist only).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
Resuscitation Council UK 2021NICE NG258 (May 2026)NICE CG183MHRA DSU June 2023BNF/BNFC
Core rule: an airway, breathing or circulation problem after a likely trigger is anaphylaxis — give IM adrenaline 1 mg/mL into the thigh now, repeat at 5 minutes, keep them lying down and send everyone by 999.

0Act now — recognise and treat in the same minute

  • Sudden A, B or C problem after a likely trigger (or diagnosis uncertain but A/B/C compromise) → IM adrenaline now, summon help, remove the trigger → 999
  • A/B/C problems persist 5 minutes after a dose → repeat IM adrenaline every 5 minutes; IV/IO crystalloid if shocked → 999
  • Still compromised after 2 appropriate IM doses = refractory, peri-arrest → highest level of help (critical care / ALS) 999; cardiac arrest → start ALS.
  • Tongue, pharyngeal or laryngeal angioedema → airway emergency; treat as anaphylaxis if an allergic mechanism is possible → 999
  • Own auto-injector used, or improved after one dose → still 999 and hospital; the surgery does not observe and discharge.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis anchored on A, B or C, not the rash

ProblemLook for
AirwaySwelling, hoarseness, stridor, throat closing
Breathing↑ RR, wheeze, fatigue, cyanosis, SpO₂ <94% (normal SpO₂ does not exclude)
CirculationPale, clammy, tachycardia, low BP, faint, confusion, collapse
Skin / mucosaSupportive only; subtle or absent in 10–20% of reactions

Mimics: vasovagal faint (bradycardic, recovers lying flat), panic, asthma, ACE-inhibitor or hereditary angioedema. In doubt with A/B/C compromise → give adrenaline.

2Check — never delay adrenaline or transfer for any of these

☐ A–B–C–D–E  ☐ SpO₂, BP, ECG  ☐ Trigger, onset, co-factors (exercise, alcohol, NSAIDs, infection, menstruation, asthma)  ☐ Dose times  ☐ Beta-blocker / ACE inhibitor

Tryptase supports but never makes or excludes it — check the discharge letter.

3Decide — is this anaphylaxis?

  • Yes — A/B/C compromise → treat now (blocks 0 and 5); everyone goes to hospital by 999.
  • No — urticaria, flushing or angioedema without A/B/C compromise → not anaphylaxis: observe, non-sedating oral antihistamine for skin, safety-net (block 9).

4Target — hospital observation from resolution (NG258)

ObserveWhen
≥2 hAll low-risk: responded within 5–10 min to one IM dose given ≤30 min from onset, fully resolved, has 2 AAIs and trained, supervised
≥6 h2 IM doses needed, or previous biphasic reaction
≥12 h>2 doses, severe asthma or respiratory compromise, continuing absorption, late evening or night, can't respond to deterioration, remote from emergency care

Under 16 and not dischargeable → admit under the paediatric team.

5Treat — IM adrenaline 1 mg/mL (1:1000), anterolateral thigh

Adult & child >12 y500 µg = 0.5 mL
Child 6–12 y300 µg = 0.3 mL
6 months–6 y150 µg = 0.15 mL
Under 6 months100–150 µg = 0.1–0.15 mL
  • No contraindications — age, pregnancy, IHD, beta-blockers mean care, not withholding.
  • Lie flat, legs raised or not; semi-recumbent if breathing is easier; left lateral in pregnancy (usual adult dose).
  • High-concentration oxygen. Shocked or poor response → IV/IO crystalloid: adult 500–1000 mL, child 10 mL/kg — never delaying the next IM dose.
  • Wheeze → add salbutamol. Doses per BNF/BNFC, RCUK/BSACI poster, local formulary.

6Monitor — while waiting, then GP follow-up

  • Waiting for 999: stay, observations every few minutes, next dose drawn up, record dose times; ready for BLS.
  • Discharge: 2 AAIs (unless an easily avoided drug cause), brand-specific training, written plan.
  • GP review: allergy coded, AAIs in date, technique re-checked, referral actioned, asthma optimised.

7Refer — everyone, specialist allergy service (NG258)

  • Include trigger, onset time, symptoms, observations, adrenaline doses and times, tryptase, co-factors, medicines.
  • Drug culprit: record drug, reaction and date in the allergy field; tell the patient its name (CG183). Don't label whole classes — ask the specialist.
  • Strict trigger avoidance and carry both AAIs until seen. Vaccine reaction → MHRA Yellow Card.
  • Beta-blocker or ACE inhibitor with venom allergy or recurrent reactions → ask the allergy service; don't stop alone.

8Never

  • Wait for a rash or for monitoring before giving IM adrenaline.
  • Use an antihistamine or steroid as a resuscitation drug, or let it delay adrenaline.
  • Let the patient stand, walk or sit up suddenly — fatal collapse can follow.
  • Give IV adrenaline in the surgery, or nebulised adrenaline instead of IM.
  • Observe and discharge from the surgery — everyone goes by 999.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Any swelling of the throat, trouble breathing or feeling faint — use your pen straight away into the outer thigh, lie down with your legs up and call 999 and say ‘anaphylaxis’, even if you feel better.”   Second pen: “No better or worse after 5 minutes — use the second pen while you wait.”   Carry: “Two in-date pens on you at all times; don't stand or walk after using one.”   Skin-only reaction: “Any throat, breathing or faint symptoms — call 999.”
