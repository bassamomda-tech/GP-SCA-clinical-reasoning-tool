Insomnia in adults

For: adults with acute or chronic insomnia, and long-term benzodiazepine or Z-drug users. Not for: children. Restless legs → restless legs protocol; menopause-driven insomnia → HRT protocol.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE TA922HTG624TA77NG202DVLAGM formulary
Core rule: look for the dangerous and treatable causes first, offer structured CBT-I for chronic insomnia, and prescribe a hypnotic only short-term, at the lowest dose, with a stop date agreed at the first prescription — never an automatic repeat.

0Act now — do not manage these as routine insomnia

  • Suicidal thoughts with active intent, imminent risk, inability to stay safe or severe self-neglect → Same-day crisis assessment; immediate danger → 999. PHQ-9 item 9 is not a risk assessment.
  • Reduced need for sleep with elevated mood, energy, grandiosity (possible mania) → if acute mania, psychosis, risk-taking, inability to self-care or risk to others → Same-day mental health. Hypnotic overdose, especially with alcohol → 999
  • Nocturnal seizure features (tongue biting, tonic-clonic movements, injury, post-ictal confusion) → Urgent, seen ≤2 weeks first-seizure pathway; DVLA rules may apply.
  • Sleepiness that affects, or is likely to affect, driving → no driving until controlled (DVLA); document. Group 2 or safety-critical worker with suspected OSA → Rapid sleep service
  • Nocturnal chest pain or breathlessness → acute chest pain, breathlessness or heart-failure pathway, at its urgency.

1Confirm — characterise before treating

Onset, maintenance or early waking? 2-week sleep diary; ISI for severity and response.

AcuteUnder 3 months, often triggered
Chronic3+ nights a week for 3+ months, with daytime impairment

2Check — causes change, re-check at review

☐ Mood, suicidal thoughts, mania  ☐ OSA if 2+ features (NG202); Epworth, STOP-BANG are aids  ☐ RLS, pain, nocturia, menopause, shift work  ☐ Caffeine, alcohol, cannabis  ☐ Medicine timing; recently stopped sedatives  ☐ Ferritin if RLS, TFTs if thyroid features — no fixed panel

Don't miss: night sweats — examine; unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly → consider NG12 lymphoma referral.

3Decide — CBT-I first

  • Chronic → offer structured CBT-I, face-to-face where available; Sleepio (HTG624) or commissioned digital CBT-I where appropriate. A hygiene leaflet is not CBT-I.
  • Acute → treat the precipitant; consider a very short hypnotic only if distress is severe, the cause likely to resolve, and OSA, alcohol, opioid and driving risk assessed.
  • Supervise or modify sleep restriction in bipolar, epilepsy, untreated OSA, pregnancy, severe depression, frailty, safety-critical work.

6Monitor

  • 2–4 weeks after CBT-I or a hypnotic starts: diary, ISI, function, driving, mood; earlier in frailty, respiratory disease, opioids or alcohol.
  • CBT-I 6–8 weeks not improving → re-check causes, depression, OSA.
  • Z-drug at stop date · melatonin by 13 weeks · daridorexant within 3 months — stop if not working.
  • Long-term users: patient-paced taper with CBT-I alongside; review every 1–2 steps.

5Treat — drugs: short, gated, with a stop date

Short-term, severeZ-drug, lowest dose: GM zopiclone first, zolpidem or temazepam alternatives. A few days, rarely >2 weeks. Older or frail: zopiclone 3.75 mg or zolpidem 5 mg
Aged 55+Melatonin MR 2 mg, licensed ≤13 weeks — not routinely supported by GM formulary
Chronic; CBT-I failed, unavailable or unsuitableConsiderable daytime impairment → daridorexant 50 mg (TA922); 25 mg with moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors or moderate hepatic impairment
Depression with insomniaMirtazapine only with a clear depressive indication
  • Every prescription: stop date agreed now, no automatic repeat, next-day impairment warning. Switch drug only for drug-specific adverse effects.
  • Daridorexant: not in narcolepsy or with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g. clarithromycin).
  • Not routine: sedating antihistamines, amitriptyline, quetiapine. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent in block 0)

  • Sleep service: suspected OSA (NG202), severe parasomnia, circadian disorder, refractory insomnia after genuine CBT-I.
  • Neurology or sleep: suspected narcolepsy (DVLA issue), probable REM sleep behaviour disorder.
  • Mental health: depression, anxiety, PTSD or bipolar driving the insomnia; NHS Talking Therapies where CBT-I is commissioned.
  • Community drug service or specialist prescribing: high-dose or multiple sedatives, alcohol dependence, withdrawal seizures, pregnancy, failed tapers.

8Never

  • Stop a long-term benzodiazepine or Z-drug abruptly (seizures, delirium).
  • Routine hypnotic repeats as maintenance treatment.
  • Start a hypnotic with alcohol, opioids or other sedatives where avoidable (MHRA).
  • Sedate over alcohol, benzodiazepine or opioid withdrawal.
  • Blame night sweats, chest pain or seizures on poor sleep.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you have thoughts of ending your life or feel you can't keep yourself safe, call NHS 111 (option 2) the same day — or 999 if you are in immediate danger.”   Hypnotics: “Drowsy the morning after — don't drive. No alcohol with them; tell us about strong painkillers or other sedating medicines.”   OSA: “Loud snoring with pauses in breathing and daytime sleepiness needs its own assessment — tell us.”
