Polymyalgia rheumatica

For: adults over 50 with new bilateral shoulder and/or hip girdle aching, morning stiffness and a raised ESR or CRP. Not for: suspected giant cell arteritis (same-day GCA pathway; see the GCA page), or true proximal weakness with a raised CK (myositis work-up).

BSR/BHPR PMR 2009BSR GCA 2020NICE NG12 (Apr 2026)NOGG 2024NICE NG243
Core rule: ask about GCA at every visit, take the bloods before the first tablet, start 15 mg, and treat a poor response at 1–3 weeks as a diagnostic alarm — never as a reason to raise the dose.

0Act now — check first, every time (at diagnosis and at every relapse)

  • Possible GCA: new headache, scalp tenderness, jaw or tongue claudication, temporal artery abnormality, limb claudication, disproportionate constitutional symptoms → high-dose prednisolone immediately per the local GCA pathway (Greater Manchester: 60 mg; BSR: 40–60 mg) → Same-day specialist Never wait for ultrasound, biopsy or results.
  • Any visual symptom (visual loss, amaurosis fugax, diplopia) → prednisolone now (60 mg if sight already lost) → Same-day ophthalmology / emergency specialist The 15 mg PMR dose does not protect sight.
  • Fever with a very high CRP, septic symptoms, suspected endocarditis or an acutely swollen joint → assess for infection before steroid wherever clinically safe → Same-day assessment
  • Aged 60+ with persistent bone pain (particularly back pain) or unexplained fracture → offer FBC, calcium, ESR or plasma viscosity, serum protein electrophoresis and serum free light chains; results suggest myeloma → Suspected cancer pathway referral Severe hypercalcaemia, AKI, cord compression or progressive neurological symptoms → consider urgent assessment.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis; all five core features

FeatureRequiredAtypical → think again
AgeOver 50Onset under 60

Also atypical: weight loss, night pain, neuro signs, synovitis, true weakness, raised CK, rash, fever, focal bone pain.

2Check — before the first tablet

☐ FBC  ☐ ESR and/or CRP  ☐ U&E, LFT, bone profile  ☐ Glucose/HbA1c  ☐ CK, TSH, RF  ☐ Serum protein electrophoresis  ☐ Urine dipstick

By context: anti-CCP, ANA, free light chains, chest X-ray. Don't miss: a dramatic response does not exclude cancer — review results first.

3Decide — who starts in primary care

  • Typical, no atypical features, clear screen → start prednisolone in primary care; document exclusions considered.
  • Atypical features → rheumatology before or soon after starting steroid.
  • IM methylprednisolone in milder disease, usually agreed with rheumatology. NSAIDs are no substitute.

4Target — expected course

1–3 weeksSubstantial improvement (supports, not proof)
About 4 weeksESR or CRP usually back to normal
1–2 yearsMost stop treatment; longer is not uncommon

5Treat — prednisolone once daily, in the morning

Step 1 · 3 weeks15 mg daily; review at 1–3 weeks
Step 2 · 3 weeks12.5 mg daily if controlled
Step 3 · 4–6 weeks10 mg daily
Step 4 · slow phaseReduce by 1 mg every 4–8 weeks; slower below 3–5 mg
  • Poor response → reassess (GCA, infection, cancer, RA, myositis), discuss with rheumatology; never a higher dose.
  • Relapse (no GCA features) → back to the last dose that controlled symptoms, then taper more slowly.
  • Bone from day one, no wait for DXA: fragility fracture, woman ≥70, or postmenopausal woman / man ≥50 on ≥7.5 mg for ≥3 months, or FRAX above threshold.
  • Steroid Emergency Card if ≥5 mg daily for ≥4 weeks, plus sick-day advice. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Weeks 1–3, about 6, months 3, 6, 9, 12 and after relapse: symptoms, GCA, BP, weight, glucose, mood, eyes, infection, falls; ESR/CRP where useful.
  • Rising ESR, no symptoms → look for another cause, review sooner.
  • Stopping: 8–9 am cortisol only if adrenal symptoms; review bone protection. Live vaccine → check Green Book ch 6.

7Refer — rheumatology, non-urgent

  • Atypical features (block 1), before or soon after starting steroid.
  • Incomplete response to 15 mg, stuck above about 10 mg, repeated relapse, toxicity, steroid contraindication, or beyond about 2 years.
  • Proximal weakness with a raised CK → rheumatology or neurology.

8Never

  • Treat possible GCA with the 15 mg PMR dose, or delay high-dose steroid for tests.
  • Raise the dose for a poor response instead of rethinking the diagnosis.
  • Start steroid for PMR before the pre-steroid bloods are taken.
  • Increase prednisolone solely because the ESR has risen without symptoms.
  • Stop prednisolone abruptly, or wait for DXA before indicated bone protection.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “A new headache, a sore scalp, jaw pain when you chew, or any change in your eyesight — contact us straight away, or NHS 111 if we are closed; eyesight changes need a same-day eye specialist.”   Response: “No big difference in a week or two — tell us; we look again at the cause.”   Taper: “Don't change the dose or stop suddenly. If the stiffness comes back, we usually go back one step.”   Steroid card: “Carry your Steroid Emergency Card and follow your written sick-day advice if you are ill or vomiting.”
