Stable angina

For: adults with suspected or confirmed stable angina. Not for: suspected ACS (block 0); new undiagnosed chest pain (local rapid-access chest-pain pathway); heart failure or post-MI (use those pages).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG126CG95NG238MHRADVLA
Core rule: a pattern change is acute assessment, not a dose problem — and run two tracks every review: anti-anginals for symptoms, statin and antiplatelet for risk (check they are still taken).

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Pain 5 min after the second GTN dose; severe pain or pain with collapse, syncope, haemodynamic compromise, marked breathlessness, sweating or dynamic ECG changes → 999 — never wait for repeat GTN or tests.
  • New rest or nocturnal pain, crescendo pattern (more frequent, longer, lower threshold) or rapidly worsening symptoms, now settled → Same-day acute assessment — not titration.
  • Chest pain within 24 h of sildenafil/vardenafil (48 h tadalafil) → no GTN; stop, sit; 999 if pain persists or is concerning; tell the crew the drug and time.
  • Chest pain with a new murmur (possible aortic stenosis) → Same-day assessment and echocardiography.

1Confirm — angina, and stable

(1) Constricting chest, arm, neck, jaw or shoulder discomfort; (2) on exertion; (3) relieved by rest or GTN within about 5 min.

Known CAD (MI, PCI, CABG), recurrent symptoms → cardiology or functional imaging. No “GTN trial” as a test. Grade CCS I–IV.

2Check — baseline

☐ ECG (old MI, AF, LVH)  ☐ FBC  ☐ TFTs  ☐ HbA1c  ☐ Lipids  ☐ U&E  ☐ BP  ☐ Weight  ☐ Echo if murmur, HF signs or previous MI  ☐ PDE5 inhibitors or poppers?

Don't miss the amplifier before assuming progression: anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, new AF, uncontrolled BP, heart failure, infection, hypoxia.

3Decide — two tracks, run in parallel

  • Everyone: GTN (5-5-999) plus a beta-blocker or CCB.
  • Confirmed CAD → consider aspirin 75 mg daily after assessing bleeding risk and anticoagulants; clopidogrel if unsuitable.
  • Atorvastatin 80 mg unless a lower dose is indicated.
  • ACEi/ARB: consider with diabetes; offer for hypertension, albuminuric CKD, LVSD, post-MI — not angina alone.
  • Lifestyle: smoking cessation every contact, activity within safe limits, Mediterranean-style diet, weight, mood, vaccines.

5Treat — BB = beta-blocker · CCB = calcium-channel blocker

Step 1BB (e.g. bisoprolol) or CCB (diltiazem MR by brand, verapamil or amlodipine); review 2–4 weeks
Step 2Intolerant → switch class. Uncontrolled → BB + dihydropyridine (amlodipine, felodipine, MR nifedipine)
Step 3 · BB/CCB unsuitableLong-acting nitrate (nitrate-free interval), ivabradine, nicorandil or ranolazine
Step 4 · limiting on twoThird drug only awaiting or unsuited to revascularisation → refer
  • Before every step: titrate — “failed” at half dose is not failed; check adherence, amplifiers.
  • Verapamil/diltiazem: not in HFrEF, bradycardia, conduction disease. Ivabradine: sinus rhythm only, not with verapamil/diltiazem.
  • GTN: sit first; use before triggers. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Every review: CCS class, GTN sprays/week, activity avoidance. Rising use or lower threshold → reassess; rest symptoms → block 0.
  • HR and BP each titration; avoid symptomatic bradycardia or hypotension.
  • Lipids 2–3 months after a change, then yearly. Above target → check adherence, dose → consider ezetimibe.
  • Annually: lipids, HbA1c, BP, smoking, weight, mood, 5-5-999 recall, DVLA if Group 2; statin and antiplatelet still taken?

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Cardiology (Heart Team): limiting symptoms despite optimal medical therapy; high-risk anatomy or results. Diabetes alone is not enough.
  • Cardiac rehab: after MI or revascularisation, or activity lost through fear.
  • DVLA: Group 1 — no need to notify; stop driving if symptoms at rest, with emotion or at the wheel. Group 2 — must notify; no driving while symptomatic.

8Never

  • Nitrates, GTN or nicorandil with a PDE5 inhibitor or poppers (24 h sildenafil/vardenafil, 48 h tadalafil; avanafil per product information).
  • Routinely combine a beta-blocker with verapamil or diltiazem in primary care (bradycardia, AV block).
  • Stop a beta-blocker abruptly — taper (rebound angina, ischaemia, arrhythmia).
  • Treat a pattern change (rest, crescendo, new) as a titration problem.
  • Ignore new ulcers (mouth, skin, eye, genital, anal, GI) on nicorandil — stop it, seek specialist advice.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Pain: stop and sit down, one or two sprays. Still there after 5 minutes, another. Still there 5 minutes after that — call 999; straight away if severe, faint or breathless.”   Same day “New pain at rest, or coming more often or more easily — contact us the same day.”   PDE5/poppers: “No spray for 24 hours after Viagra (two days for tadalafil); chest pain then — no spray; sit down, 999 if it persists or worries you; say what you took.”   Beta-blocker: “Never stop it suddenly — see me first.”   Statin/aspirin: “Keep taking them even when you feel well.”
