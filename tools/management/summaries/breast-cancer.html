Reasoning GP · Protocol · 1-page summary

Breast cancer

For: adults with a breast or axillary symptom, people with a family history, and people on breast cancer treatment or follow-up in primary care. Not for: benign inflammatory breast disease (use the mastitis and breast abscess protocol), or bilateral milky discharge (galactorrhoea — prolactin and drug review).

Core rule: an unexplained breast lump at 30 or over is a suspected cancer pathway referral however benign it feels — refer the symptom, never primary-care imaging instead, and never let a family history or a normal test delay it.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Unwell, febrile, rigoring or abnormal temperature on anticancer treatment → acute oncology Immediately — possible neutropenic sepsis; not 38°C alone.
  • Bone or back pain with neurological symptoms or signs (cord compression) → MSCC coordinator or acute oncology Immediately.
  • Suspected DVT or PE on tamoxifen → Same-day assessment.
  • Unexpected vaginal bleeding (esp. postmenopausal) or new visual symptoms on tamoxifen → Urgent gynaecology/specialist.
  • Any NG12 criterion (block 1), incl. a new lump after treatment — never assume scar tissue → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Breast erythema, oedema or rapid inflammatory change not settling promptly with treatment, or atypical → Urgent breast assessment

Unexplained = no satisfactory diagnosis after history and examination; a benign feel does not remove the referral.

2Check — for the referral letter

☐ Chaperone offered, documented  ☐ Both breasts, sitting and lying  ☐ Axillary, supraclavicular nodes  ☐ Discharge spontaneous or expressed  ☐ Pregnancy/lactation, menopause, implants, past cancer or radiotherapy, COCP/HRT  ☐ Family history

Don't order a mammogram instead of referring — a normal one does not exclude cancer.

3Decide — familial risk is CG164, not NG12

  • Three-generation family history, both sides → local familial pathway or validated tool, not a “three relatives” rule. CG164 thresholds include BRCA probability ≥10%, risk >8% at 40–50, lifetime ≥30%. No genetic testing without counselling.
  • Chemoprevention (specialist): high risk → offer tamoxifen (premenopausal) or anastrozole (postmenopausal, unless severe osteoporosis) for 5 years; moderate risk (lifetime 17–30%) → consider. Cuts incidence; mortality benefit not shown.

5Treat — oncology leads; GP supports the care plan

ER+ premenopausalTamoxifen 20 mg OD; duration per oncology
ER+ postmenopausalAnastrozole 1 mg, letrozole 2.5 mg or exemestane 25 mg OD; confirm menopause (FSH)
Ovarian suppressionGoserelin or leuprorelin, continued as oncology plans
HER2+Trastuzumab 12 months; heart checks before and 3-monthly
  • Tamoxifen: avoid paroxetine, fluoxetine (CYP2D6) where possible; agree an alternative with oncology.
  • Contraception: no hormonal method in current breast cancer unless the specialist advises — offer non-hormonal.
  • Hot flushes: venlafaxine (HRT usually contraindicated in ER+). Doses per BNF/local formulary.

6Monitor — follow the written oncology care plan

  • Mammography yearly for 5 years (incl. DCIS); no routine bloods, markers or scans if well.
  • Tamoxifen: bleeding, clots, mood, adherence; LFTs only if the plan says; CYP2D6 drugs at each change.
  • Aromatase inhibitor: fracture risk, bone density per local guidance; bone protection not automatic; joint pain → paracetamol/NSAID.
  • Suspected recurrence (bone pain, lump, nodes, chest or neuro symptoms) → FBC, U&E, LFTs, calcium, LDH; consider CXR.

7Refer — routine

  • Breast clinic: unexplained lump <30; large fibroadenoma, recurrent cysts.
  • Genetics per CG164/local criteria — never instead of an NG12 referral.
  • Breast care nurse, Breast Cancer Now, Macmillan; fertility before chemotherapy.
  • Screening 50–71 three-yearly (over 71 on request) — never instead of referral.

8Never

  • “Watch and wait” an unexplained lump at 30 or over because it feels benign.
  • Let a benign or normal test override persistent clinical concern.
  • Paroxetine or fluoxetine with tamoxifen where an alternative exists.
  • An aromatase inhibitor in a premenopausal woman without confirmed ovarian suppression.
  • Stop the COCP or HRT before the diagnosis is known.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Referral: “Most people referred urgently do not have cancer; this is because of the symptom. No breast-clinic appointment within 2 weeks — contact us. Any new or worsening symptom — tell us straight away.”   On chemotherapy: Immediately “Feverish, shivery or unwell — ring the acute oncology line straight away.”   Endocrine therapy: “New bone pain that does not settle, or a new lump — see me within a week. Back pain with weakness, numbness or bladder or bowel problems — contact us or the acute oncology line straight away.”   Breast awareness: “Any new lump, skin or nipple change or armpit swelling — report it straight away.”
