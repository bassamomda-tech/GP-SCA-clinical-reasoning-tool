Type 1 diabetes in adults

For: adults with type 1 diabetes, or suspected type 1, in primary care with shared specialist care. Not for: children; DKA or acute illness on insulin (use the DKA, sick day rules and feeling-unwell-on-insulin pages); type 2 diabetes (type 2 diabetes page).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG17 (updated 2022)NG238TA151 · TA943DVLAJBDS
Core rule: never stop basal insulin and check ketones whenever unwell; never label an adult type 2 on age or BMI alone; read the whole glucose pattern before changing insulin; prescribe by brand, concentration and device.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Blood ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L, or clinically unwell (vomiting, abdominal pain, Kussmaul breathing) → 999 / immediate admission — glucose can be near-normal (euglycaemic DKA). New type 1 presenting in DKA is the same emergency.
  • Persistent vomiting or unable to keep fluids down → Urgent hospital assessment / 999
  • Ketones 1.5–2.9 → Same-day diabetes team / acute assessment. Ketones 0.6–1.5 → recheck within 2 hours; unwell, rising or persistent → Same-day diabetes team (out of hours, NHS 111)
  • Severe hypoglycaemia (needs another person's help) → IM glucagon or IV glucose plus 999
  • Suspected type 1 with insulin deficiency or ketosis → do not delay insulin or emergency assessment for antibody results. Less acute uncertainty → Urgent diabetes-team discussion
  • Pregnancy with vomiting or hyperglycaemia → Same-day specialist. Active foot disease → Same-day MDT foot service.

1Confirm — is it really type 1?

Clinical diagnosis at any age: rapid onset (weeks of thirst, polyuria, weight loss), ketosis, poor response to oral agents, autoimmunity. Never exclude on age, BMI or ethnicity alone.

All adults at initial diagnosis → two autoantibodies (e.g. GAD and IA-2 or ZnT8) (NG17); negative does not exclude type 1. No routine C-peptide at diagnosis; later only if classification is still uncertain.

2Check — baseline at diagnosis

☐ HbA1c, U&E/eGFR, urine ACR, lipids  ☐ TFTs, coeliac serology  ☐ BP, weight  ☐ Retinal and foot screening  ☐ Structured education (DAFNE or equivalent) for everyone

Don't miss: an eating disorder with insulin omission, and lipohypertrophy.

3Decide — regimen and technology

  • Basal–bolus (MDI) is the standard regimen, started per the local diabetes-team protocol. Pre-mixed only where basal–bolus is not possible, with diabetes-team input.
  • Offer CGM to all adults (real-time or intermittently scanned), with capillary testing as backup.
  • Pump (TA151) or hybrid closed loop (TA943): disabling hypoglycaemia, or HbA1c not met on optimised MDI → specialist.

4Target — individualise

MeasureTarget
HbA1c≤48 mmol/mol if achievable without problematic hypoglycaemia; may be higher
GlucoseWaking 5–7, pre-meal 4–7, 2 hours after meals about 5–9 mmol/L
CGM time in range>70% in 3.9–10 mmol/L, <4% below 3.9 (consensus, not NICE)

5Treat — doses per BNF and local formulary

BasalGlargine or degludec OD. Levemir is being discontinued: no new starts; switch only with the diabetes team
BolusRapid analogue (aspart, lispro, glulisine) with each meal
Fasting highBasal only if a basal pattern with no preceding nocturnal hypo; with daytime hypos, do not just increase basal
Meal high · hyposHigh after one meal → that meal's ratio. Recurrent hypos → reduce the responsible insulin
  • Before any change: several days of data, carb counting, injection sites, hypos driving highs.
  • Hypo <4 mmol/L: 15–20 g fast-acting carbohydrate, recheck at 10–15 minutes, then longer-acting carbohydrate; review the cause.

6Monitor — annual care processes

  • Annually: HbA1c, BP, lipids, eGFR, ACR, feet, retina, injection sites, hypo awareness, mood and eating.
  • Statin: offer atorvastatin 20 mg if over 40, diabetes more than 10 years, nephropathy or other CV risk factors; consider at 18–40.
  • ACEi/ARB if hypertension or albuminuria. SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA: specialist only.

7Refer — diabetes team (urgent ones in block 0)

  • Insulin initiation, titration, Levemir transition, unclear type, impaired awareness, recurrent DKA, HbA1c off target on optimised MDI.
  • Planning pregnancy: specialist review; HbA1c below 48; folic acid 5 mg to 12 weeks; stop statins, ACEi, ARBs before conception.
  • DVLA, Group 1: tell the DVLA if insulin over 3 months, impaired awareness, more than one severe hypo awake in 12 months, or one while driving. Group 2: stop driving and tell the DVLA when insulin starts.

8Never

  • Stop basal insulin, even when not eating or vomiting.
  • Assume type 2 in an overweight adult and start only oral agents.
  • Swap insulins unit-for-unit (Toujeo U300, Tresiba U200), or switch detemir solely through repeat prescribing.
  • Change doses without reading the whole pattern, or invent correction doses without diabetes-team guidance.
  • Use QRISK3 for statin decisions in type 1.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Vomiting, tummy pain, deep fast breathing, or blood ketones 3 or above — 999. Ketones 1.5 to 2.9 — contact us or the diabetes team the same day.”   Sick days: “Keep the insulin going, check glucose and ketones every 2 to 4 hours, sip fluids, use your sick-day plan. If you keep vomiting or cannot keep fluids down — 999.”   Severe hypo: “If you cannot treat it yourself, someone gives glucagon and calls 999. Always carry fast sugar.”   Driving: “5 to drive — test before you set off and every 2 hours. After a hypo, wait at least 45 minutes after your glucose is back above 5.”
