Hepatitis A

For: adults and children with suspected or confirmed acute hepatitis A in primary care. Not for: possible acute liver failure (block 0, same-day hospital); jaundice or abnormal LFTs from another cause (use the jaundice and abnormal LFTs algorithms); contact prophylaxis, which the health protection team decides.

UKHSA hepatitis A (2024)Green Book ch.17Notification Regs 2010BSG 2018NICE NG12 (Apr 2026)
Core rule: check the INR and conscious level, notify on suspicion without waiting for the result, let the health protection team protect contacts within 14 days, and never let HAV IgM explain away jaundice aged 40+ that looks obstructive.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • New confusion, drowsiness, altered behaviour, day-night reversal or asterixis in a jaundiced patient → Same-day hospital; with coagulopathy = possible acute liver failure. Avoid sedatives.
  • Raised or rising INR → Same-day discussion with acute medicine or hepatology.
  • Bleeding or spontaneous bruising, persistent vomiting or dehydration, oliguria or AKI, hypoglycaemia, rapidly rising bilirubin, severe abdominal pain or systemic deterioration, or severe illness in an older adult or chronic liver disease → Same-day admission or discussion
  • Jaundice aged 40 and over → assess urgently for pancreatic and biliary disease (NG12). Persistent, painless, obstructive or cholestatic jaundice, a mass, negative serology, no improvement or uncertainty → Urgent imaging and/or suspected cancer pathway
  • Suspected hepatitis A → notify the proper officer on clinical suspicion; Phone the health protection team ≤24 h, written notification ≤3 days. Do not wait for the result.

1Confirm — HAV IgM, read in context

Suspect: prodrome, then dark urine, pale stools, jaundice; travel, contact, MSM, injecting, food. Incubation 15–50 days.

2Check — one set of bloods on the day

☐ HAV IgM, LFTs, INR  ☐ FBC, U&E, glucose  ☐ Occupation, setting, contacts  ☐ Pregnancy test if relevant

Uncertain, severe, immunocompromised, atypical exposure → hepatitis B, C, E and medicines/alcohol/supplement review now (else if IgM negative). Cholestasis → consider ultrasound.

3Decide — where, and what to tell the team

  • Well, drinking, alert, INR normal → primary care. Notify with onset dates, job, setting, travel, foods; flag contacts pregnant, over 60, immunosuppressed or with liver disease.

5Treat — supportive; no antiviral

ComfortRest, small frequent meals, oral fluids; antiemetic if vomiting (mind QT, sedation, encephalopathy)
Pain or feverParacetamol cautiously: weight, nutrition, alcohol, liver disease, total exposure, combination products
Protect the liverNo alcohol until LFTs normal; review all medicines incl. OTC and herbal; stop non-essential hepatotoxic drugs
HygieneSoap and water, not gel; no cooking for others; no shared towels; no sex incl. oral-anal while infectious
  • Avoid sedatives, opioids; NSAIDs if dehydrated, AKI, bleeding risk, significant liver or renal illness. Oestrogen contraception → check UKMEC. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — by severity and trajectory

  • Early review: hydration, conscious level, INR. Older, unwell, jaundiced or liver disease → repeat within a few days.
  • LFTs and INR until well, INR stable or normal, trend clearly improving. Rising INR, worse jaundice or no improvement → seek advice.

7Refer — hepatology soon, and prevention

  • Hepatology: chronic liver disease; prolonged cholestasis or relapse; persistent or worsening results; negative screen with uncertainty.
  • Prevent: offer vaccine to at-risk patients the team has not covered (travellers, liver disease, MSM, people who inject drugs).

8Never

  • Wait for laboratory confirmation before notifying.
  • Decide the contacts' prophylaxis regimen without the health protection team.
  • Let a positive HAV IgM explain jaundice aged 40+ that is painless, persistent, obstructive or cholestatic.
  • Judge severity by the ALT (it does not predict outcome) — use the INR and conscious level.
  • Let a food handler return on a generic 7-day rule alone.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you are very hard to wake, call 999.”   Same-day “If you become confused or very drowsy, start bruising or bleeding, or can't keep fluids down, contact us or NHS 111 straight away — you need to be seen that day.”   Spread: “Stay off work or school until the public health team says it's safe, usually a week after the yellowness started; wash hands with soap and water, and don't cook for anyone else until then.”
