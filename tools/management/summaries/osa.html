Snoring and obstructive sleep apnoea

For: adults (over 16s) with snoring or suspected obstructive sleep apnoea/hypopnoea syndrome (OSAHS). Not for: children who snore (use the paediatric pathway). Diagnosis, CPAP titration and equipment are led by the sleep service.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG202 (2021)NICE TA139DVLA Assessing fitness to driveBNF
Core rule: if sleepiness may affect driving, advise no driving now and document it — before any diagnosis — then refer for a sleep study; scores support the decision but never make it.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected acute ventilatory failure, severe hypercapnia, new confusion or marked somnolence, decompensated cor pulmonale or heart failure, acute desaturation with cardiorespiratory compromise → Same-day hospital
  • Sleepiness that may affect driving, falling asleep at the wheel or a near-miss → Same-day advice not to drive until controlled; document it; expedite referral.
  • Group 2 (HGV/PSV) driver or vigilance-critical worker with sleepiness → no driving or safety-critical work while sleepy; Urgent sleep clinic
  • Suspected obesity hypoventilation (OHS): BMI ≥30 kg/m² with OSAHS symptoms plus morning headache, oedema, polycythaemia, resting hypoxaemia or somnolence → awake arterial or arterialised capillary gas (PaCO₂ >6.0 kPa); Urgent respiratory/sleep referral
  • Stable signs of cor pulmonale (raised JVP, oedema, loud P2, RV heave) → Urgent clinical assessment. Unstable arrhythmia, nocturnal angina, severe resistant hypertension, pregnancy or major pre-operative assessment → Urgent sleep clinic
  • DVLA: confirmed moderate or severe OSAHS with excessive sleepiness → must notify. Suspected or mild OSAHS with sleepiness → notify if not controlled within 3 months.

1Confirm — the sleep study diagnoses and grades

Assess if two or more of: snoring, witnessed apnoeas, choking in sleep, unrefreshing sleep, waking headaches, sleepiness or fatigue, nocturia, sleep fragmentation or insomnia, cognitive impairment. Take a partner history.

2Check — before referral

☐ BMI, waist-to-height ratio, neck (≥43 cm M / ≥41 cm F)  ☐ BP both arms  ☐ Mallampati, tonsils, retrognathia, nasal airway  ☐ Cor pulmonale signs, AF  ☐ Driving and occupation  ☐ Alcohol, sedatives, opioids  ☐ Bloods only by context (FBC, HbA1c, lipids, thyroid) — they do not diagnose OSAHS

Don't miss: unilateral nasal obstruction with bleeding, unexplained neck lump or persistent hoarseness → ENT/NG12 pathway.

3Decide — who goes where

  • Simple snoring (no apnoeas, sleepiness or cardiovascular impact) → no CPAP or referral; lifestyle. Refer if features develop.
  • Suspected OSAHS, no urgent features → Routine sleep clinic for home respiratory polygraphy.
  • Lifestyle for everyone: weight loss, side-sleeping, less evening alcohol, stop smoking, 150 min a week of activity, sedative review.

5Treat — sleep service provides; primary care sustains

1st · CPAPFixed CPAP: moderate–severe, or mild with symptoms affecting daily life
Alternative · MADCPAP declined or not tolerated → consider a (semi-)customised splint if teeth and gums healthy
ContributorsNasal obstruction → mometasone 100 micrograms each nostril daily; treat hypothyroidism
CPAP intoleranceMask type, humidifier, ramp, auto-CPAP; re-refer, don't let it lapse
  • No first-line drug for OSAHS. Weight management alongside, not instead of, CPAP; bariatric per NICE criteria and local pathways.
  • Opioids, benzodiazepines, Z-drugs: avoid or minimise; never stop essential opioids abruptly. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Sleep service: first follow-up within 1 month, then until symptoms and AHI/ODI optimised; annually once stable.
  • Each review: CPAP use, mask, weight, driving. Annually: BP, HbA1c, lipids, AF, mood.
  • Still sleepy? Adherence, residual AHI/ODI, alcohol, sedatives, short sleep, depression, thyroid, anaemia, narcolepsy → sleep clinic.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • ENT: significant nasal obstruction, tonsils grade 3–4, retrognathia. Tier 3/bariatric: per NICE criteria and local pathways.
  • Respiratory/cardiology: CPAP failure, diagnostic uncertainty, COPD–OSA overlap.
  • Driving resumes once the sleep service confirms control and adherence; Group 2 needs medical confirmation, then at least annual review.

8Never

  • Let a sleepy patient keep driving "until the study", or leave the driving advice undocumented.
  • Diagnose, grade or exclude OSAHS on Epworth, STOP-BANG or a single oximetry ODI.
  • Treat the "insomnia" or sleepiness with sedatives or hypnotics.
  • Diagnose OHS on oximetry alone.
  • Stop CPAP after weight loss, or let it lapse, without sleep-service advice.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Acute breathlessness with blue lips, very low oxygen readings at home, or new confusion or severe drowsiness — call 999.”   Same-day “New or worsening morning headaches, or memory and thinking getting worse — contact us the same day.”   Driving: “Don't drive while you are sleepy — not until it is controlled. We will tell you if you must tell the DVLA.”   CPAP: “If the mask is uncomfortable or too dry, tell us rather than stopping. Avoid evening alcohol and sleeping tablets.”
