Actinic keratosis

For: adults with actinic (solar) keratoses on sun-damaged skin. Not for: pigmented or changing lesions (use the pigmented skin lesions pathway), suspected SCC or BCC beyond referral, or actinic cheilitis (specialist assessment).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
PCDSNICE NG12MHRA SPCsMHRA DSU (Picato)Local formulary
Core rule: the question is not “is this an AK?” but “is any of this an SCC?” — palpate every lesion, examine the whole skin, and never delay a suspected cancer referral for a cream.

0Act now — inspect and palpate every lesion

  • Suspected SCC: tender, rapid growth, marked hyperkeratosis or induration, nodule, ulceration, spontaneous bleeding, cutaneous horn, or no response to an adequate course → consider a Suspected cancer pathway (NG12) referral; check nodes; no cream first.
  • Ear, lip, temple, eyelid or periorbital site → lower referral threshold. Actinic cheilitis or a persistent keratotic lip lesion → low threshold for specialist assessment.
  • Transplant or immunosuppressed: any new, changing, growing, painful, thickened, ulcerated or non-healing lesion → consider a Suspected cancer pathway referral if SCC is suspected.
  • Pigmented, irregular or changing lesion → weighted 7-point checklist; score 3 or more, or any suspicion → Suspected melanoma pathway (NG12)
  • Pearly, rolled-edged or telangiectatic papule (BCC) → Routine dermatology; suspected cancer pathway only if delay could have a significant impact (site, size).
  • Severe treatment reaction: severe pain, extensive weeping, spreading erythema, systemic illness or possible infection → Same-day review

1Confirm — the diagnosis is clinical

Rough, scaly, red macules or papules on sun-exposed skin — often felt better than seen. Dermoscopy or biopsy not routine.

2Check — assess and record

☐ Count, site, thin or hypertrophic  ☐ Whole skin (BCC, SCC, melanoma)  ☐ Immunosuppression; past skin cancer  ☐ Pregnancy, breastfeeding  ☐ NSAIDs, brivudine  ☐ Can they apply it?

3Decide — whether to treat at all

  • Observation (active surveillance) is reasonable for mild, flat, asymptomatic AK, the frail, or by preference — with emollient, sun protection, safety-net.
  • Treat for symptoms, hyperkeratosis, field change, concern or high cumulative risk.
  • Everyone, for life: no sunburn or sunbeds; hat, clothing, shade, sunscreen. Prevents; does not treat.

4Target — review the response at

5-FU: after healing (1–2 months after stopping)Imiquimod: after the 4-week treatment-free gap
Diclofenac: about 30 days after completionTirbanibulin: about 8 weeks after starting

5Treat — by lesion count, thickness and site

Few discrete lesionsCryotherapy (liquid nitrogen). Not thick lesions or horns; lower-leg ulceration risk
Thin field · most effective5-FU 5% cream (Efudix) once or twice daily, usually 3–4 weeks; max 500 cm²
Thin field · face or scalpImiquimod 5% (immunocompetent) 3 nights a week for 4 weeks, or tirbanibulin 1% daily for 5 days, ≤25 cm²
Lower-intensity reactionDiclofenac 3% gel twice daily for 60–90 days; max 8 g daily. Better tolerated, less effective
  • First: warn redness and crusting are expected; emollient and simple analgesia. Thick or resistant → dermatology.
  • 5-FU: not in pregnancy, breastfeeding or within 4 weeks of brivudine. Diclofenac: not with NSAID allergy or third trimester. Imiquimod: caution in transplant. Doses per SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • At review (block 4): assess clearance; if inadequate, reassess diagnosis, adherence and lesion type — never re-treat automatically.
  • Persisting after an adequate course → Bowen disease or SCC? Refer if suspicious. Re-examine the skin.

7Refer — SCC features → block 0

  • Dermatology, not routine: uncertainty; extensive, recurrent or hyperkeratotic AK; persisting AK; actinic cheilitis; immunosuppressed with extensive AK.
  • Routine: photodynamic therapy, curettage, 5-FU with calcipotriol (local protocol only); suspected BCC.

8Never

  • Ingenol mebutate (Picato) — licence suspended and withdrawn.
  • Delay a suspected cancer referral for a cream or a primary-care biopsy.
  • Repeatedly freeze a horn or thick lesion, or repeat a cream without reassessment.
  • Field creams on the vermilion or mucosal lip.
  • Overtreat asymptomatic field change in the frail.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

≤7 days “A patch that becomes lumpy, more tender, ulcerated, grows quickly or keeps bleeding — contact us within a week, whether or not you are using the cream.”   Same day “Very painful, weeping a lot, redness spreading well beyond the patch, feeling unwell or it looks infected — stop and contact us the same day.”   Expected: “Worst mid-course — don't stop just because it is red and sore.”   For life: “Sunscreen, hat, sleeves; tell us about anything new or not healing.” 5-FU: “No smoking or naked flames — treated clothing and bedding can catch fire.”
