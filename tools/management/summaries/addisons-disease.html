Addison's disease / adrenal insufficiency

For: adults with suspected or known primary or secondary adrenal insufficiency, and adults at risk from long-term steroids. Not for: children and young people (use BSPED guidance and involve paediatric endocrinology).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG243Society for EndocrinologyNHS Steroid Emergency CardNG12 (Apr 2026)
Core rule: suspected adrenal crisis → hydrocortisone 100 mg IM/IV before any test, then 999; never stop glucocorticoid abruptly; rehearse the sick-day rules at every contact.

0Act now — treat first, confirm later

  • Hypotension or collapse, vomiting, abdominal pain, fever or confusion in known or possible adrenal insufficiency → hydrocortisone 100 mg IM/IV now (anyone can give IM) → 999; random cortisol first only if it does not delay treatment.
  • Persistent vomiting or diarrhoea, cannot absorb oral steroids (known disease or at risk from steroids) → IM hydrocortisone, follow the steroid emergency plan → 999
  • Low Na⁺ + high K⁺ ± hypoglycaemia: unwell → treat as crisis 999; not acutely unwell → Same-day endocrinology discussion, treat empirically while confirming.
  • Hypoglycaemia (especially secondary disease) → glucose IV + hydrocortisone → 999   Purpura + shock (adrenal haemorrhage in meningococcal or severe sepsis) → 999
  • Acute illness and cannot apply the sick-day rules, steroid stopped, or kit missing or expired while unwell → Same-day

1Confirm — think of it; specialist confirms

Fatigue, weight loss, anorexia, nausea, postural dizziness, often mislabelled as depression. Primary disease: salt craving, pigmentation (palmar creases, buccal mucosa, scars) — absence does not exclude.

2Check

☐ U&E, glucose, cortisol, ACTH  ☐ Lying/standing BP  ☐ Steroid use (oral, inhaled, topical, withdrawal)  ☐ Pituitary history  ☐ Autoimmune history

Don't miss: unexplained weight and appetite loss is an NG12 trigger — a normal Synacthen does not close it.

3Decide — who needs what

  • All confirmed or suspected disease → endocrinology, who initiate replacement, card and IM kit.
  • Primary: glucocorticoid + fludrocortisone. Secondary: glucocorticoid only.
  • Steroid risk (e.g. prednisolone ≥5 mg/day for ≥4 weeks, high-dose inhaled or topical) → card per NHS guidance.

4Target — physiological replacement, judged clinically

Under-replacedOver-replaced
Fatigue, postural dizziness, salt cravingWeight gain, hypertension, dysglycaemia, osteoporosis; fludrocortisone: oedema, low K⁺

5Treat — specialist-initiated; GP shared care

GlucocorticoidHydrocortisone 15–25 mg/day in 2–3 doses, largest on waking (e.g. 10/5/5 mg). Prednisolone once daily is an alternative.
Primary onlyFludrocortisone 50 µg daily, titrate to postural symptoms, BP, U&E; up to 300 µg, higher only on specialist advice.
Sick-day rule 1Significant illness, fever needing bed rest, infection needing antibiotics → hydrocortisone ≥40 mg/day in 2–4 doses (or prednisolone ≥10 mg/day) until recovered.
Sick-day rule 2Vomits within 30 min → repeat at double dose once settled. Vomits again, or persistent vomiting/diarrhoea → IM hydrocortisone, then 999.
  • Surgery, major dental work, colonoscopy prep → peri-procedure parenteral cover.
  • Equip: Steroid Emergency Card + MedicAlert; in-date IM kits, patient and ≥1 carer trained; flag the record. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Each review: symptoms, weight, lying and standing BP, pulse; U&E in primary disease; renin only if mineralocorticoid adequacy is uncertain.
  • Every contact: sick-day rules; kit number, location and expiry; card carried.
  • More often after diagnosis, dose change, pregnancy or illness; annually once stable. Autoimmune screen when clinically indicated.

7Refer

  • Low or indeterminate cortisol, abnormal Synacthen → Urgent endocrinology (suggestive electrolytes: same-day discussion).
  • Pituitary lesion on MRI → joint endocrine and neurosurgical care.
  • Bilateral adrenal metastases → suspected cancer pathway for the primary (often lung).

8Never

  • Delay hydrocortisone for tests, referral or the ambulance.
  • Stop glucocorticoid abruptly — it precipitates crisis.
  • Diagnose with a 9am cortisol on oral glucocorticoids without specialist advice.
  • Titrate to random cortisol or cortisol day curves.
  • Withhold steroid before testing in someone unwell or at risk of crisis.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you're vomiting and can't keep tablets down, collapse or feel dramatically worse — inject the hydrocortisone (anyone can), then call 999 and say you have an adrenal problem.”   Sick days: “Fever or an infection needing antibiotics — take the extra dose on your sick-day plan until you're better.”   Every day: “Carry your steroid card, keep your kits in date, never run out, and never stop the tablets suddenly.”
