Migraine in adults

For: adults with migraine, with or without aura, including menstrual migraine and medication-overuse headache (MOH). Not for: tension-type headache (use the tension-type headache protocol); undifferentiated headache (use the headache pathway); suspected cluster headache or complex migraine in pregnancy (specialist-led).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG150 (updated June 2025)BASHNICE TA919 · TA906 · TA973UKMECMHRA
Core rule: count the two numbers — headache days and acute-medication days — at every review, unmask medication overuse before calling anything refractory, and never label new, atypical or focal neurological symptoms as aura.

0Act now — out of the migraine pathway

  • Thunderclap headache (peak within 5 minutes), even if resolved → 999
  • Fever with neck stiffness; persistent focal deficit, reduced consciousness or seizure → 999
  • First or atypical aura (sudden, motor, persistent, monocular, brainstem, first over 50) → Urgent assessment for TIA, retinal or neurological disease; sudden focal = stroke/TIA pathway.
  • New headache over 50 → assess for GCA (ESR, temporal tenderness); GCA with visual symptoms → Same-day emergency
  • Papilloedema → Urgent neurology or ophthalmology (postural or waking headache: raised-pressure warning).
  • New or changing headache in pregnancy or post-partum → Same-day assessment (pre-eclampsia, cerebral venous thrombosis).

1Confirm — formal criteria, not POUND alone

4–72 h; ≥2 of unilateral, pulsating, moderate–severe, worse on activity; plus nausea/vomiting or photo- with phonophobia. Normal examination → no imaging. Typical aura: builds over ≥5 min, lasts 5–60 min, fully reversible.

2Check

☐ 4–8 week diary (attack days, medication days, menstrual link)  ☐ BP and neurology  ☐ All acute medicines, including bought  ☐ Aura and contraception  ☐ Mood, sleep, driving

Don't miss: new headache with cancer, HIV or immunosuppression; daily crescendo.

3Decide — MOH first, then prophylaxis

  • MOH → stop overused medicines ≥1 month (supervised reduction if opioid, benzodiazepine or barbiturate dependence); warn of worsening; reassess at 4–8 weeks.
  • Prophylaxis → consider if frequent, disabling, prolonged, overuse-linked or acute treatment fails (≥4 migraine days a month: BASH/local prompt).
  • All: regular sleep and meals, steady caffeine, hydration.

4Target

Acute daysAgreed maximum, ideally ≤2 a week
PreventiveJudge at 6–8 weeks on target dose (BASH; 3 months GM), not 2 weeks; success = fewer attack days
Ongoing needReview at 3–6 months; 6–12 months controlled → taper over 2–3 months

5Treat — acute ladder, then prevention

Step 1Oral triptan (e.g. sumatriptan) + NSAID or paracetamol, early in the headache, not during aura alone
Step 2Consider metoclopramide or prochlorperazine; non-oral routes if vomiting
Step 3Check dose, timing, absorption → another triptan before abandoning the class
Step 4 · TA919 onlyRimegepant 75 mg ODT: ≥2 triptans fail or unsuitable and NSAID and paracetamol fail
  • Prevention — discuss and consider by comorbidity, pregnancy potential and overdose risk: propranolol MR 80 → 160 mg/day (not in asthma); amitriptyline 10 → 50–75 mg nocte; topiramate; candesartan off-label. No routine combinations.
  • Pregnancy: paracetamol first; sumatriptan after risk–benefit; no NSAIDs in the third trimester. Menstrual: long half-life triptan.
  • Over 65: vascular review before a triptan. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Every review: the diary's two numbers → escalate acute care, adjust prophylaxis or unmask MOH.
  • Candesartan: BP, eGFR and K⁺ at baseline and follow-up.
  • Could conceive: aura versus CHC, topiramate PPP (it can reduce hormonal contraception), pregnancy plans — every review.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Neurology after acute assessment of first or atypical aura.
  • Headache clinic per each NICE appraisal: atogepant, CGRP monoclonals, preventive rimegepant, botulinum toxin (chronic only).
  • Diagnostic doubt, changed pattern, complicated MOH, complex migraine in pregnancy, suspected cluster headache.

8Never

  • Opioids (including co-codamol) or ergots for routine migraine.
  • CHC in migraine with aura (UKMEC 4); aura on CHC → prompt review, never unprotected.
  • Topiramate or candesartan in pregnancy; topiramate only within the PPP.
  • A triptan in coronary disease, stroke or TIA, PVD or uncontrolled hypertension.
  • Escalate prophylaxis or refer before medication overuse is withdrawn.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Thunderclap headache, maximal within minutes, even if it settles; fever with a stiff neck; new weakness, confusion or a first fit — call 999.”   Urgent “First aura, aura over an hour, a changed headache, or a new headache in pregnancy or after birth — contact us.”   Limits: “Usually no more than 2 days a week — more can cause the headaches.”   Topiramate: “Eye pain or blurred vision — stop it, seek urgent review.”
