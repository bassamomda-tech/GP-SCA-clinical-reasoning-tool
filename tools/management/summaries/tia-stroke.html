Stroke and TIA — after the event

For: adults after stroke or TIA — prevention and life after stroke. Not for: first assessment of suspected TIA (full protocol, NG128) or blackouts without focal signs (TLoC pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
Stroke guideline 2023NICE NG128NG236NG196NG238
Core rule: a new deficit is a new stroke — 999, never "the old stroke"; then clopidogrel (a DOAC with no antiplatelet if AF), a high-intensity statin and systolic <130, and confirm the 21-day DAPT course actually stopped.

0Act now — against the recorded baseline, not "normal"

  • Any new focal deficit (face, arm, speech, vision) or sudden worsening beyond baseline → 999 Never a routine appointment.
  • Sudden severe headache, vomiting, reducing consciousness, or a deficit on an anticoagulant → 999 No aspirin; send the anticoagulant name, dose and last dose time.
  • Resolved suspected TIA (incl. amaurosis fugax) → aspirin 300 mg unless contraindicated (anticoagulated or bleeding disorder → specialist advice); no driving → Specialist ≤24 h no ABCD2.
  • Crescendo TIAs (2 or more in 7 days) → Same-day stroke specialist; admit if ongoing or high-risk.
  • Fever, new murmur, splinters, young or IV drug user → Same-day admission (endocarditis).
  • Bleeding, IDA or weight loss on an antithrombotic → NG12 pathway on its own merits.

1Confirm — from the discharge letter, the day it arrives

Record type, cause (cardioembolic, large-, small-vessel, cryptogenic), carotid and rhythm results, the DAPT stop date, and the functional baseline (mRS or plain words) — the yardstick for "is this new?". Cryptogenic → consider prolonged rhythm monitoring.

Not a stroke: progressive over days–weeks or non-vascular territory → re-image (subdural, tumour).

2Check — at each structured review

☐ BP  ☐ Pulse for AF  ☐ Lipids, LFTs, HbA1c, U&E  ☐ DOAC: weight, CrCl  ☐ Mood (adapted for aphasia)  ☐ Fatigue, swallow, continence, cognition  ☐ Falls, FRAX  ☐ Carer

Don't miss: new confusion = UTI, subdural, low sodium or delirium first.

3Decide — the antithrombotic

  • Ischaemic stroke or TIA, no AF → clopidogrel 75 mg OD long term. Unsuitable → aspirin 75 mg + MR dipyridamole 200 mg BD (aspirin alone if dipyridamole unsuitable).
  • AF, any pattern → DOAC, antiplatelet stopped unless a separate documented indication. Mechanical valve or moderate–severe rheumatic MS → warfarin; APS → specialist.
  • After intracerebral haemorrhage → nothing started or restarted automatically (specialist decision); BP is the main lever.
  • Lifestyle: stop smoking (varenicline or NRT + support), alcohol within limits, activity.

6Monitor — and life after stroke

  • 6 weeks, 6 months, then yearly: adherence, DAPT ended, DOAC dose, BP, lipids, HbA1c, function. Lipids, LFTs 3 months after a statin start.
  • Depression (about a third): screen each review → offer psychological and social support; consider an antidepressant. Emotionalism: explain; consider SSRI if persistent or severe.
  • Fatigue: check mood, sleep apnoea, anaemia, thyroid, drugs → pacing.
  • Spasticity: physio, positioning; consider baclofen or tizanidine. Wet voice or cough with food → SLT swallow assessment.

7Refer — and driving

  • Symptomatic carotid stenosis 50–99% → Urgent vascular (surgery ideally ≤7 days).
  • Under 55, recurrent despite treatment, cryptogenic, or suspected dissection, PFO or APS → young-stroke service.
  • Rehab is needs-led, not time-limited → re-refer for any new goal, even years on.
  • DVLA: car — no driving 1 month; notify if residual deficit (field, neglect, cognition, limb); multiple TIAs → 3 months attack-free, notify. Bus/lorry → stop, notify, revoked 1 year.

8Never

  • Attribute a new deficit to the old stroke.
  • Add an antiplatelet to an anticoagulant solely for AF-related stroke prevention.
  • Let a 21-day DAPT course run on repeat.
  • Aspirin when haemorrhage is possible.
  • Blame bleeding on the antithrombotic and skip the NG12 cancer pathway.

9Safety-net — repeat FAST at every review, then document it

999 “Face droops, an arm or leg goes weak, or speech or sight goes — even if it settles in minutes — or sudden severe headache, vomiting or drowsiness: call 999. Don't ring us first.”   Head injury on clopidogrel, a DOAC or warfarin: “Go to A&E straight away.”   Same day “Symptoms that came and went earlier, new headache on a blood thinner, bleeding that won't stop, new confusion.”   Medicines: “Don't stop them yourself — tell us.” Recovery: “A new goal, even years on — come back.”
