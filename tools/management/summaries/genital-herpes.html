Genital herpes

For: adults and young people with a suspected first episode or recurrence of anogenital herpes, including in pregnancy. Not for: a prepubertal child or a neonate (block 0), or extensive or atypical ulcers in immunosuppression (sexual health/infectious diseases).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BASHH 2024 anogenital herpesBASHH/RCOG 2024 HSV in pregnancyNICE NG12 (Apr 2026)BHIVA/BASHH/BIA 2020BASHH STI testing 2023
Core rule: swab every ulcer and test for syphilis and HIV, start aciclovir without waiting for the result, and always ask two questions — can you pass urine, and are you pregnant (how many weeks)?

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Cannot pass urine (bladder palpable, or still unable after trying in a warm bath with topical anaesthetic) → Same-day hospital assessment and bladder decompression; no repeated urethral attempts.
  • Headache, photophobia, neck stiffness, or saddle numbness, constipation or retention → Same-day hospital assessment. Confusion, drowsiness, a seizure or reduced consciousness (encephalitis) → 999.
  • Unable to take fluids or tablets, severe systemic illness, disseminated lesions → Same-day admission, IV antiviral.
  • First episode at 28 weeks or later → start aciclovir, continue to delivery; Same-day obstetric contact. Lesions or prodrome in labour → immediate obstetric assessment. Neonate with possible herpes features → Same-day paediatrics.
  • Genital HSV in a prepubertal child → urgent paediatric assessment and safeguarding review, discussed Same-day with paediatrics and the safeguarding lead.
  • Unexplained vulval lump, ulceration or bleeding; penile mass or ulcer with STI excluded or persisting after STI treatment; unexplained anal mass or ulceration → consider a Urgent suspected cancer pathway referral. A negative HSV PCR or pending syphilis test never delays it.

1Confirm — clinical picture, then the swab

First episodeRecurrence
LesionsMultiple painful blisters → ulcers, often both sidesFewer, often one side, same site
SystemicFever, malaise, tender nodes, dysuriaUsually none; tingling prodrome

HSV PCR with typing from a fresh or de-roofed base. Negative PCR from an old lesion does not exclude herpes.

2Check — every anogenital ulcer

☐ Syphilis serology  ☐ HIV test  ☐ Chlamydia/gonorrhoea NAAT by history and sites  ☐ Voiding  ☐ Pregnancy and gestation  ☐ Immunosuppression  ☐ eGFR before suppressive, prolonged or high-dose use, or if older or frail

Don’t miss: single painless indurated or PCR-negative ulcer → Urgent sexual health.

3Decide — treat on clinical grounds

  • First episode within 5 days, new lesions forming or systemic symptoms → antiviral today.
  • Every first episode → offer sexual-health review (local Bolton route).
  • Recurrences by burden and preference: supportive, episodic, or consider suppression at about ≥6 a year (or fewer if painful, prolonged, distressing, or to cut transmission).

4Target — review points

PlanReview
First episodeDay 5 → 10 days if new lesions, systemic symptoms or not healed
SuppressionAt least annually; pause if agreed, judge on ≥2 recurrences

5Treat — oral antivirals, BASHH 2024

First episodeAciclovir 400 mg three times daily, 5 days (5–10). Or valaciclovir 500 mg twice daily
Symptom reliefSalt-water bathing, analgesia, petroleum jelly, 5% lidocaine ointment; pass urine in a warm bath
RecurrencesEpisodic, started at the first tingle: aciclovir 800 mg three times daily for 2 days. Suppression: aciclovir 400 mg twice daily (three times if breakthrough), or valaciclovir 500 mg once daily
PregnancyRecurrent herpes: offer/discuss aciclovir 400 mg three times daily from 32 weeks to delivery. First episode <28 weeks: treat, inform maternity, then the same. Birth mode is an obstetric decision
  • Every step: renal dosing for aciclovir and valaciclovir; warn of neurotoxicity; safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • New lesions beyond 10 days → re-swab, specialist advice. Pregnancy: no automatic stop at day 5.
  • Breakthrough on suppression → check adherence, renal dosing, diagnosis; repeat PCR, HIV. Immunocompetent: aciclovir three times daily or change drug.
  • Immunosuppressed, progressing despite treatment → swab for resistance; Urgent sexual-health/infectious-diseases referral.

7Refer — non-urgent routes

  • Sexual health, soon: every first episode; doubt, persistent or atypical lesion, uncontrolled recurrences, HIV.
  • Maternity booking: known herpes, or a partner with herpes → flag so 32-week suppression and the birth plan are in place.

8Never

  • Wait for the swab result before starting aciclovir.
  • Diagnose herpes in an ulcer without syphilis serology and an HIV test.
  • Start pregnancy suppression at 36 weeks — it is now 32 weeks.
  • Let a negative PCR reassure you about a persistent, indurated or atypical ulcer.
  • Tell a patient a first episode proves recent infection or infidelity.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you become confused or very drowsy, or have a fit, call 999.”   Same-day “If you can’t pass urine, get a bad headache or stiff neck, can’t drink or take the tablets, or feel very unwell, you need to be seen the same day.”   Transmission: “Avoid sex, including oral sex, until the sores have fully healed; condoms reduce but don’t remove the risk. Tell your midwife in any future pregnancy.”   New baby: “Any concern about the baby — get help the same day; call 999 if the baby has a fit or is hard to wake.”
