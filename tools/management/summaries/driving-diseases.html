Driving and diseases — DVLA fitness to drive

For: adults with a condition or medicine that may affect driving — Group 1 (car/motorcycle), Group 2 (bus/lorry) and taxi/private-hire drivers. Not for: pilots (CAA), seafarers (MCA ENG1) or emergency-service work vehicles (occupational health). Condition look-up: the DVLA fitness-to-drive guide.

DVLA Assessing fitness to driveGMC Confidentiality (2017)NICE CG109NG202MHRA Drugs and driving
Core rule: tell the patient when to stop driving and that they must notify DVLA, document it — and inform DVLA yourself only if they cannot or will not and the risk to others is serious. DVLA, not the GP, decides the licence.

0Act now — stop driving now: advise, notify where required, document

  • Any seizure → Stop driving now, notify DVLA. Group 1: first unprovoked commonly ≥6 months; established epilepsy usually 12 months.
  • TLoC without a firm diagnosis of uncomplicated faint or situational syncope → Stop driving now while awaiting assessment. Exertional syncope, abnormal ECG, heart failure, murmur, unexplained breathlessness or family history of sudden cardiac death → Cardiology ≤24 hours
  • Insulin: ≥2 severe hypos awake in 12 months, impaired awareness, or a severe hypo while driving → Stop driving now, notify DVLA (Group 2: report every severe hypo).
  • Binocular acuity worse than 6/12 or a new field defect → Stop driving now, notify DVLA. TIA/stroke → Stop driving now, usually ≥1 month; notify any persisting deficit. ACS → per DVLA chapter.
  • OSA with excessive sleepiness likely to impair driving → Stop driving now until symptoms are controlled; refer to sleep service (Urgent if HGV/bus). Acute psychosis → no driving until stable, insight restored and specialist confirms fit.

1Confirm — the condition and the licence type

Check the current DVLA chapter for the condition; record which chapter and the date checked.

  • Group 1 (car, motorcycle): most patients.
  • Group 2 (bus, lorry): stricter — seizures and antiseizure medicine over 10 years; ICD generally disqualifying.
  • Taxi / private hire: also local licensing authority standards.

2Check — and document

3Decide — who notifies

  • The patient notifies DVLA (V1 or online); the GP advises and documents, and does not report routinely.
  • Cannot or will not notify (incl. no capacity or insight) and drives with serious risk to others → persuade; tell them first where practicable and safe; inform DVLA's medical adviser, relevant information only (GMC); document.

5Advise — counsel, give written information, document

  • Say it plainly: stop driving? notify DVLA? for how long? Give written information.
  • Medicines (benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, opioids, gabapentinoids, sedating antihistamines/antipsychotics): no driving if impaired, especially on starting, dose rise or with alcohol. Dopamine agonist sleep attacks → do not drive.
  • Hypo while driving: stop safely, switch off the engine, move from the driver's seat, treat, and do not resume until recovered and the required interval has passed. Carry fast-acting glucose.
  • Insurance: not notifying a notifiable condition is an offence (fine up to £1,000) and may leave them uninsured — advise them to check with their insurer.

6Review

  • End of any time-limited ban → confirm the standard is met before resuming; re-counsel if anything changes.
  • Annual review (epilepsy, diabetes, dementia, AF, cardiac, OSA) → record driving status, DVLA notification and whether criteria are met.

7Refer — routine unless stated in block 0

  • Diabetes team: hypo unawareness or recurrent severe hypos. Neurology: epilepsy, new cognitive impairment affecting driving.
  • Ophthalmology: field defect or acuity <6/12 — DVLA still decides eligibility.
  • Driving assessment centre: wants to drive after stroke, with dementia or disability.
  • D4 report: factual history and findings only; agree any fee first.

8Never

  • Advise stopping or changing antiseizure medicine just to meet a driving standard — the specialist manages any change.
  • Use the “3 Ps” as a stand-alone clearance test after TLoC.
  • Let an Epworth or cognitive screening score alone decide driving.
  • Write “fit to drive” on a DVLA form — describe the clinical picture and let DVLA decide.
  • Give DVLA rules alone to a pilot — they must also contact the CAA.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Every case: “The DVLA, rather than your GP, decides whether you can hold a licence. You are legally responsible for telling them; I will note our conversation. If you keep driving when I believe there is a serious risk, I may need to tell DVLA.”   Insulin: “Below 4.0 or feeling hypo — don't drive. If a hypo starts while driving, pull over straight away — don't finish the journey.”   Sedating medicines: “If you feel drowsy or slowed, or fall asleep suddenly, do not drive.”
