Smoking cessation

For: people who smoke, including pregnancy and severe mental illness. Not for: drug treatment under 18 (NRT only, from 12 years); cancer symptoms in a smoker — assess against NICE NG12 on their own merits.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG209 (updated Feb 2025)QS207MHRA DSU Aug 2020SPS interactionsNCSCT
Core rule: Ask – Advise – Act at every contact, pair behavioural support with an effective treatment, and plan clozapine, theophylline and warfarin monitoring with the prescriber before the quit date — the smoke, not the nicotine, drives the interaction.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • On clozapine, stopping or switching to NRT/vape → mental health team Before the quit date; plasma levels when smoking stops or changes (MHRA).
  • Clozapine toxicity: seizure, collapse or new severe confusion → 999; sedation, hypersalivation, tachycardia, constipation → Same-day GP or mental health team.
  • Theophylline → levels, adjust with the respiratory prescriber. Vomiting, tremor, palpitations → Same-day; seizure → 999
  • Varenicline + serious neuropsychiatric symptoms (agitation, depressed mood, suicidal thoughts or behaviour) → stop at once, contact a clinician Immediately
  • Seizure on bupropion → 999; stop bupropion and never restart it.

1Ask and advise — very brief advice, under a minute

AskAsk about smoking and record it at key contacts.
AdviseBest way to stop: specialist support plus medication or a nicotine vape.
ActRefer to the stop smoking service and/or start treatment now.

Not ready to stop? → harm reduction: cut down with licensed nicotine products, or switch fully to a vape.

2Check — before choosing a drug

☐ Clozapine, olanzapine, theophylline, warfarin, insulin, erlotinib, methadone  ☐ Pregnant/breastfeeding  ☐ Renal function (varenicline)  ☐ Seizures, eating disorder, MAOI, alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal (bupropion)  ☐ Age ≥66, recent MI/stroke, unstable angina, arrhythmia (cytisinicline)  ☐ Mental state

Don't miss: haemoptysis, cough, weight loss → NG12 on their merits.

3Decide — shared decision, NG209

  • Support plus varenicline, cytisinicline, combination NRT or a nicotine vape — most likely to work; bupropion a further option. Choose together.
  • Pregnancy: CO test at booking and 36 weeks. Opt-out referral to maternity stop smoking support if smoking, stopped within 2 weeks, CO ≥4 ppm or not engaged. Consider NRT; incentives for validated abstinence.

5Treat — always with behavioural support

Varenicline · 18+0.5 mg daily d1–3, 0.5 mg BD d4–7, then 1 mg BD. Renal function first; reduce in severe impairment.
Cytisinicline · 18–65Fixed 25-day tapering course of 1.5 mg tablets. Not with unstable angina, recent MI/stroke, significant arrhythmia.
Combination NRT · 12+Patch plus short-acting form, dosed by dependence, not count alone; titrate to cravings. Pregnancy: a 16-hour patch can come off overnight.
Nicotine vape · adultsNot licensed; much less harmful than smoking; long-term harms unknown. Switch completely, no dual use.
  • Bupropion: 150 mg daily for 6 days, then 150 mg BD.
  • Don't routinely combine drugs other than combination NRT without specialist advice. Doses per BNF, SPC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Review within 1–2 weeks: cravings, adherence, side effects, mental state, NRT dose, drug levels.
  • Warfarin: extra INR checks; adjust only by INR. Insulin: closer glucose monitoring. Olanzapine: tell the mental health team.
  • Relapse reverses the interaction — ask smoking status at each review.
  • End of varenicline course: consider a further 12 weeks if high relapse risk. Lapse → reassess; switch product rather than give up.

7Refer — routine

  • Stop smoking service (highest yield) or community pharmacy.
  • Before quit date: mental health team, respiratory prescriber, anticoagulation service as relevant.

8Never

  • Assume NRT or a vape prevents the interaction — the smoke induces CYP1A2, not the nicotine.
  • Change clozapine, olanzapine or warfarin doses automatically — coordinate with the prescriber.
  • Varenicline, cytisinicline or bupropion under 18, in pregnancy or when breastfeeding; cytisinicline at 66+.
  • Bupropion with any seizure history or current or past bulimia or anorexia nervosa.
  • Smoking or vaping near home oxygen equipment (fire risk).

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Clozapine: “A fit, collapse or sudden severe confusion — call 999. Very sleepy, drooling, dizzy, racing heart or bad constipation — contact us or your mental health team today.”   Varenicline: “Agitation, low mood or thoughts of harming yourself — stop the tablets and contact us straight away.”   Bupropion: “A fit — call 999; don't take the tablets again.”   Lapse: “If you slip, that isn't failure — it tells us what to change.”
