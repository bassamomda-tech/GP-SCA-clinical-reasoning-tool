Hypertension in pregnancy and postpartum

For: pre-conception, pregnancy, postnatal and lifelong follow-up. Not for: hypertension outside pregnancy and breastfeeding (use the hypertension protocol, NG136).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG133 (updated 2023)NG201NG194HTG630MBRRACE-UK 2025
Core rule: from 20 weeks and after the birth, pre-eclampsia symptoms or BP ≥160/110 mean same-day maternity assessment — never send a symptomatic woman home with a repeat BP.

0Act now — from 20 weeks and throughout the postnatal period

  • Seizure, reduced consciousness, stroke signs, severe breathlessness, collapse, suspected abruption (pain with bleeding, hard tender uterus) → 999 Postnatal too.
  • BP ≥160/110 on repeat, pregnant or postnatal → Same-day, immediate transfer — not a letter.
  • Pre-eclampsia symptoms (severe headache, visual disturbance, epigastric/RUQ pain, vomiting, sudden swelling, breathlessness, suspected HELLP) → Same-day maternity assessment even if BP is normal.
  • New BP ≥140/90 after 20 weeks → Urgent: refer the same day by phone ; same-day assessment if symptoms, significant proteinuria, abnormal bloods or concern.
  • Persistent or severe postnatal headache → BP and assess; Same-day if pre-eclampsia, CVST or another serious cause is possible.
  • Reduced or changed fetal movements → Immediate contact with the maternity unit

2Check — every contact

☐ BP  ☐ Urine dip  ☐ Symptoms  ☐ Gestation / postnatal day  ☐ ACEi/ARB/thiazide  ☐ Aspirin. Don't miss: epigastric pain may be HELLP.

3Decide — pre-conception, early pregnancy, aspirin

  • Could conceive, ACEi/ARB → agree a switch plan. Thiazide → review; don't stop without a replacement plan.
  • Pregnant on ACEi/ARB → stop within 2 working days of notification; substitute; remove from repeats.
  • Aspirin 75–150 mg daily from 12 weeks until the birth if one high-risk factor (previous hypertensive pregnancy, CKD, SLE/APS, diabetes, chronic hypertension) or >1 moderate (first pregnancy, ≥40, interval >10 years, BMI ≥35, family history, multi-fetal). Unlicensed — explain.

5Treat — with obstetrics; doses per BNF and local maternity formulary

Pregnancy 1stLabetalol: offer in gestational hypertension or pre-eclampsia; consider in chronic
Pregnancy nextUnsuitable (e.g. asthma) → consider nifedipine MR; both unsuitable → methyldopa
After birth 1stEnalapril (renal function, K⁺); Black African or Caribbean origin → consider nifedipine (amlodipine if used before)
After birth nextConsider nifedipine/amlodipine + enalapril → add or swap atenolol or labetalol
  • Methyldopa: stop within 2 days of birth; assess mood.
  • Breastfeeding: avoid diuretics and ARBs where possible; check baby's feeding daily for 2 days.
  • Issue obstetric prescriptions promptly; don't change without discussing.

6Monitor

  • Postnatal BP: treated pre-eclampsia every 1–2 days up to 2 weeks; ask about headache and epigastric pain.
  • Home on drugs → review at ~2 weeks; taper per block 4.
  • 6–8 weeks: BP, urine protein, drugs, mood, contraception; code it. Proteinuria 1+ after pre-eclampsia → kidney review at 3 months.
  • For life: raised hypertension and CVD risk — keep checking BP; aspirin next pregnancy.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Chronic hypertension, newly pregnant → Urgent booking (days), obstetric-led clinic.
  • Still on drugs at 6–8 weeks after pre-eclampsia or gestational hypertension → specialist assessment.
  • Chronic hypertension, CKD or diabetes planning pregnancy → pre-conception review.

8Never

  • Reassure a symptomatic woman with one normal surgery BP or a PlGF result.
  • Treat new hypertension after 20 weeks in the surgery and wait.
  • Leave an ACE inhibitor or ARB on repeat in pregnancy, or stop it without a replacement.
  • Dismiss a postnatal headache as migraine or tiredness without measuring BP.
  • Continue methyldopa more than 2 days after the birth.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A fit, collapse, severe breathlessness, or tummy pain with bleeding — call 999.”   Pregnancy: “A bad headache (especially one that doesn't go with paracetamol), blurred vision or flashing lights, pain under your ribs, vomiting, breathlessness, sudden swelling of your face, hands or feet, feeling very unwell, or the baby moving less — call the maternity unit straight away, day or night.”   After the birth: “The same signs can happen for several weeks — get checked the same day: call your maternity unit, GP surgery or NHS 111 straight away.”
