Precocious puberty

For: children with secondary sexual characteristics before 8 (girls) or 9 (boys), first contact in primary care. Not for: acute painful testicular swelling (acute scrotal pathway) or a safeguarding emergency (local child-protection procedures). Diagnosis, imaging and treatment are specialist-led.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
Bradley et al., BMJ 2020NICE NG12 (April 2026)MHRA DSU Jan 2023RCPCH UK-WHO chartsWorking Together 2023
Core rule: never dismiss or "keep an eye on it" — refer every suspected case (every boy promptly, even with a normal examination), after a neurological and visual check and an accurate plotted growth chart.

0Act now — it can be the first sign of a brain lesion

  • Raised-ICP features (reduced consciousness, persistent vomiting with concerning headache, papilloedema, rapidly progressive visual symptoms), new seizure or acute visual loss → Same-day emergency assessment; no endocrine tests first.
  • Newly abnormal central neurological function (visual-field loss, abnormal gait or coordination, cranial-nerve signs such as new squint, focal motor/sensory signs) → consider Very urgent (48 h) NG12 referral; state the abnormality. Early puberty or headache alone does not qualify.
  • Acute painful testicular swelling or suspected torsion → Same-day acute scrotal pathway, not the endocrine route.
  • Testicular mass, persistent asymmetry or firm unilateral enlargement → Urgent paediatric/urology, usually with ultrasound.
  • Any boy with suspected precocious puberty → Prompt, local urgent pathway — pathology, including intracranial, is far more likely than in girls.
  • Unexplained genital injury, a disclosure or concerning circumstances → local child-protection procedures Same-day alongside the medical referral. Precocious puberty alone is not evidence of abuse.

1Confirm — does it meet the definition?

SexCounts as precocious
Girls <8Progressive breast enlargement (firm subareolar disc, Tanner B2 — palpate to exclude lipomastia), clitoromegaly, or menarche
Boys <9Testicular enlargement (≥4 mL, or length >2.5 cm) or penile growth. Penile growth without testicular enlargement may suggest peripheral androgens

Pubic hair and body odour are adrenal and do not by themselves mean central puberty. Vaginal bleeding without breast development is not menarche → urgent paediatric assessment.

2Check — the examination and growth chart are yours

☐ Serial height, weight, BMI on RCPCH UK-WHO charts, plus earlier red book / school nurse measurements  ☐ Tanner stage in words and numbers  ☐ Neuro and visual: acuity, eye movements, pupils, gait, fundoscopy where feasible  ☐ Testes size, asymmetry, mass; abdominal mass; café-au-lait; BP; parental heights  ☐ Sequence, rate of progression, CNS history, a parent's testosterone or oestrogen gel

Don't miss: pubertal signs with slowing growth → hypothyroidism: TFTs and refer. Tests never delay referral.

3Decide — which pattern (for the letter, not a diagnosis)

  • Central: normal sequence with a growth spurt; boys — both testes enlarge. Girls usually idiopathic; boys more often a cause.
  • Peripheral (CAH, adrenal/gonadal tumour, McCune-Albright, exogenous steroids): out of sequence or virilising; boys — prepubertal testes or one enlarged.
  • Benign variants: premature thelarche or adrenarche — non-progressive, normal growth; still assessed to confirm.
  • Lipomastia (no firm disc): reassure if clearly fat and growth normal; refer if in doubt.

5Treat — specialist-led; the GP supports and shares care

  • Central: GnRH analogue depot (e.g. leuprorelin, triptorelin) started and monitored by paediatric endocrinology. Peripheral: treat the source.
  • Shared care: give the depot on time only under a current local shared-care agreement and specialist plan; never start, stop or change dose or interval. Doses per BNFC and the shared-care protocol.
  • Gel exposure: adult washes hands, lets it dry, covers the site, avoids skin contact with the child; do not abruptly stop the adult's essential hormones.
  • Family: explanation, school liaison; weight support without blame.

6Monitor

  • Confirm the referral is made and attended; chase non-attendance.
  • Keep plotting growth; escalate if red flags develop. Observation alone only once a specialist confirms a benign variant.
  • Depot delayed or missed: no automatic catch-up dose → check the plan and contact the paediatric endocrine team.

7Refer — urgency by presentation; red flags in the letter

  • Urgent paediatric all boys; rapid progression in either sex; virilisation or clitoromegaly; pubic hair in infancy; sustained growth acceleration; CNS history or cranial irradiation; polyuria and polydipsia; new headache or visual disturbance; exogenous exposure with progressive changes.
  • Girls urgent if menarche <8, or progressive breast development <8 with growth acceleration.
  • Routine or local discussion isolated, non-progressive thelarche or adrenarche; girls 7–8 with isolated breast development, normal growth, no red flags.
  • Letter: sequence, plotted chart, Tanner stage in words, parental heights, neuro and testicular findings, gel exposure, NG12 met or not.

8Never

  • Dismiss or indefinitely observe suspected precocious puberty without specialist assessment.
  • Reassure a boy because the examination is normal — a normal neuro exam does not exclude an intracranial lesion.
  • Use obesity or parental height to dismiss early puberty.
  • Order random LH or oestradiol, or a brain MRI direct from primary care (unless the local pathway supports it); never let tests delay referral.
  • Photograph a child's genital or breast development for the referral.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If your child has a fit, or is very drowsy or hard to wake, call 999.”   Same day “Headaches — especially in the morning or with being sick — or any change in vision: contact us the same day (111 if we are closed).”   Before the appointment: “Unusual thirst, passing lots of urine, or the changes speeding up — contact us straight away.”   Depot: “If an injection is missed or late, tell us and the specialist team.”
