Impetigo

For: adults and children with suspected impetigo in primary care. Not for: neonates (under 1 month → specialist advice), infected eczema flares (NICE NG190) or cellulitis — use the cellulitis protocol. Children: NG153 age bands or BNFC, never adult doses.

NICE NG153 (2020)UKHSA exclusion guidanceUKHSA scarlet fever (2023)NICE NG190
Core rule: ask how ill the child is — impetigo itches and the child plays; a febrile, miserable child or painful crusts is scalded skin, eczema herpeticum or cellulitis until proven otherwise. One antibiotic route at a time.

0Act now — looks like impetigo but is not, or cannot be managed at home

  • Scalded skin: febrile, irritable child, diffuse tender erythema, superficial blistering, positive Nikolsky sign → Same-day paediatric admission
  • Eczema herpeticum: painful punched-out erosions or vesicles on eczema, often fever → Same-day assessment Start systemic aciclovir promptly (BNF/BNFC); do not wait for swabs.
  • Eyelid swelling with fever, pain on eye movement, restricted movements, proptosis, reduced vision → Same-day assessment Suspected orbital cellulitis is an emergency.
  • Systemically unwell, spreading cellulitis; baby with widespread blistering, poor feeding, fever, lethargy or diagnostic doubt; any neonate → Same-day paediatrics or acute medicine
  • Immunocompromised with widespread impetigo → Refer to hospital the same day
  • Chickenpox with returning fever, looks unwell, or lesions hot, painful, spreading → possible invasive group A strep → Same-day assessment

1Confirm — clinical; no swab for a first typical episode

Non-bullous (most): honey-coloured crusts on a red base, itchy, well. Bullous: flaccid blisters leaving a collar of scale, mainly infants.

MimicClue → action
Herpes simplexGrouped painful vesicles, tingling, same site → antiviral (BNF/BNFC)
Eczema flareTreat the eczema; well → antibiotics not routine (NG190)
Tinea · scabies · adult blistersAntifungal · treat infestation · tense, no crust → dermatology

2Check — every case

☐ Temperature, feeding, how ill  ☐ Extent, blisters  ☐ Near the eyes?  ☐ Eczema, scabies, lice  ☐ Siblings  ☐ True penicillin allergy, pregnancy  ☐ Recent fusidic acid

Swab if treatment fails, frequent recurrence, MRSA suspected, atypical, or the result would alter treatment.

3Decide — bullous? widespread? well?

  • Localised non-bullous, well, not high risk → consider hydrogen peroxide 1% cream.
  • Widespread non-bullous, well → topical or oral antibiotic by shared decision (no NICE lesion count).
  • Bullous, unwell or high risk (immunosuppressed, young infant, cannot apply cream) → oral.

4Exclusion and hygiene — UKHSA

  • Off until crusted and healed, or 48 hours after starting an antibiotic (peroxide alone: crusted and healed); same for food, health and childcare workers. Own towel, wash hands.
  • Scarlet fever too: 24 hours after antibiotics; notify the UKHSA health protection team.

5Treat — 5 days; oral 7 days by judgement

1 · LocalisedHydrogen peroxide 1% cream 2–3 times a day, 5 days. Not near the eyes.
2 · Antiseptic unsuitableFusidic acid 2% TDS, 5 days. Mupirocin 2% TDS only if fusidic resistance or on microbiology advice (MRSA).
3 · OralFlucloxacillin 500 mg QDS. Children QDS: 1 month–1 year 62.5–125 mg · 2–9 years 125–250 mg · 10–17 years 250–500 mg.
True penicillin allergyClarithromycin 250 mg BD (500 mg BD if severe); children weight-banded per BNFC. Pregnant: erythromycin 250–500 mg QDS.
  • Tablets → stop the antibiotic cream. Clarithromycin: check interactions, QT, liver. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Review

  • Worse quickly or significantly → Same-day reassess: cellulitis, scalded skin, herpes, systemic illness.
  • Not improved at course end: after peroxide → topical antibiotic; after topical → offer oral, consider a swab; after oral → swab, narrow-spectrum by result.
  • Frequent recurrence: skin and nasal swabs, consider decolonisation per microbiology; treat eczema, scabies, lice.

7Refer — non-urgent routes (urgent ones in block 0)

  • Consider referral or specialist advice: bullous (especially ≤1 year), systemically unwell, high risk, frequent recurrence.
  • Microbiology: MRSA, fusidic acid resistance, suspected PVL (recurrent boils, household clusters).
  • UKHSA health protection team: nursery or school cluster, scarlet fever outbreak, iGAS or iGAS contact.
  • Adult crusted lesion not healing, sun-exposed, suspicious of SCC → Suspected cancer ≤2 weeks consider referral (NG12).

8Never

  • Combine a topical and an oral antibiotic.
  • Repeat fusidic acid back to back, or use mupirocin routinely.
  • Use hydrogen peroxide near the eyes, or let eye advice delay assessment.
  • Anchor on impetigo in a febrile, miserable child or painful crusts on eczema.
  • Wait for swab results before aciclovir in eczema herpeticum.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “If it spreads quickly, blisters, or she gets a temperature, stops feeding, the eyelid swells, or the skin starts to peel or look burnt — bring her back the same day.”   Not better: “If it hasn’t improved when the 5 days end, come back.”   Exclusion: “Off nursery until the patches have crusted and healed, or 48 hours after starting antibiotics.”   Hygiene: “Wash hands before and after the cream, and keep your own towel.”
