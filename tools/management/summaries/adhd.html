ADHD — children, young people and adults

For: suspected or diagnosed ADHD at any age: recognition, referral, support now, and shared-care prescribing. Not for: diagnosis or starting medication (specialist only); setting-specific, sudden or regressive behaviour change (follow block 0).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG87DSM-5GMMMG shared careSchedule 2 CD rulesEquality Act 2010
Core rule: confirm symptoms and impairment in two or more settings, refer without waiting for a school questionnaire, start non-drug support now, and prescribe only under a current shared-care agreement with BP, pulse and growth checks.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Brief staring spells, unresponsive, abrupt return (absence seizures?) → Urgent paediatric epilepsy specialist, seen ≤2 weeks
  • Self-harm or suicidal thoughts (including on atomoxetine) → Same-day risk assessment and urgent CAMHS or crisis referral; immediate risk or serious injury → 999
  • On ADHD medication: syncope, exertional chest pain, palpitations, marked breathlessness, new seizures → Same-day; ongoing chest pain or collapse → 999
  • Atomoxetine with jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, abdominal pain or pruritus → stop atomoxetine, Same-day assessment
  • Sudden onset, regression or change after a life event → Same-day safeguarding assessment; focal neurological signs → Same-day
  • Severe weight loss, growth faltering, suspected misuse or diversion → Urgent specialist advice

1Confirm — a specialist diagnosis, never from a rating scale

Symptoms in ≥2 important settings (home, education, work, social) with at least moderate impairment, not better explained by another disorder.

DSM-5Threshold
Inattentive and/or hyperactive-impulsive≥6 of 9 (≥5 if 17+) · ≥6 months · several before age 12

One setting only → find the setting-specific cause. Girls: quiet, inattentive, masking — easily missed. Adults: often after their child's diagnosis.

2Check — before referral

☐ Parent/carer and school (or work) information — do not delay referral for it  ☐ Hearing and vision  ☐ Height, weight, centiles  ☐ BP and pulse  ☐ Cardiac history and examination  ☐ Sleep, snoring  ☐ Comorbidities, safeguarding  ☐ FBC and ferritin if pallor or dietary concern

No routine ECG. Cardiology advice before medication for NICE red flags: congenital heart disease, cardiac death in a first-degree relative under 40, exertional breathlessness or syncope, rapid regular palpitations, cardiac chest pain, murmur, hypertension.

3Decide — start now, without waiting for the specialist

  • Under 5 → ADHD-focused group parent training first-line. No medication without a second specialist opinion.
  • Aged 5+ → information, support, environmental and school changes; refer. Medication only if significant impairment persists after changes are reviewed.
  • Adults → medication if significant impairment persists despite environmental changes. DVLA duties if driving affected; Equality Act adjustments.
  • Everyone: psychoeducation, SENCO, SEND support plan (no diagnosis needed), sleep, activity, routines.
  • Comorbidity → treat the dominant condition first.

5Treat — specialist-initiated; GP continues

Children 5–18Methylphenidate; not enough benefit after 6 weeks at an adequate dose → consider lisdexamfetamine
AdultsLisdexamfetamine or methylphenidate first-line
Non-stimulantBoth stimulants not tolerated or failed 6-week trials → children: atomoxetine or guanfacine; adults: consider atomoxetine
Specialist onlyDexamfetamine; guanfacine in adults (tertiary advice); refractory cases
  • Stimulants contraindicated: MAOI within 14 days, phaeochromocytoma, hyperthyroidism, severe arrhythmia, structural heart disease, severe hypertension.
  • Schedule 2 CD under a current shared-care agreement: valid 28 days, normally ≤30 days' supply, safe storage. No dose change without the specialist. Doses per BNF/BNFc and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • BP and pulse: before and after each dose change and every 6 months. Guanfacine: weekly during titration.
  • Growth: height 6-monthly (children); weight 3-monthly if ≤10, at 3 and 6 months then 6-monthly if >10, adults 6-monthly. Plot on charts.
  • Each review: benefit, appetite, sleep, mood, tics, misuse, diversion. Flat affect → specialist dose review, not stopping. Atomoxetine: suicidality, liver, sexual function.
  • At least annually: ADHD-experienced clinician review. Plan adult transition early.

7Refer — routine

  • Children: local ADHD service (community paediatrics or CAMHS; Greater Manchester criteria). Adults: adult ADHD service.
  • Hearing or vision in doubt → audiology/ophthalmology; snoring or apnoeas → ENT; global delay or autism → developmental paediatrics.

8Never

  • Diagnose ADHD or start medication in primary care, or diagnose from a rating scale alone.
  • Hold a referral back because a school questionnaire has not come back.
  • Medication under 5 without a second specialist opinion.
  • Prescribe a stimulant without a current shared-care agreement.
  • Stop guanfacine abruptly (rebound hypertension) — taper it.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Fainting, chest pain, a racing heart, a fit or talk of self-harm — contact us the same day. Call 999 if chest pain does not settle, they collapse or are in immediate danger.”   Dose: “A flat, switched-off look means the dose is too high — tell us; we review it with the specialist.”   Atomoxetine: “Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or pale stools — stop it and contact us the same day. It takes several weeks to work.”   Controlled drug: “Store it safely; we prescribe about a month at a time.”
