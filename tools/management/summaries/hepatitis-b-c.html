Hepatitis B and C

For: adults at risk of, or found to have, hepatitis B or C in primary care. Not for: hepatitis A (hepatitis A protocol), children (paediatric hepatology; BNFC) or antiviral prescribing (specialist).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE PH43NICE CG165NICE TA430 · TA499Green Book ch. 18NHS England HCV
Core rule: test the people at risk, use HCV RNA (not antibody) to define current infection, and refer every HCV RNA-positive and every HBsAg-positive person — hepatitis C is curable in 8–12 weeks.

0Act now

  • Jaundice with confusion, drowsiness, bleeding, raised INR or persistent vomiting → hospital assessment Same-day; reduced consciousness 999
  • Known cirrhosis with ascites, vomiting blood, melaena or confusion → emergency admission 999
  • Suspected acute hepatitis → notify the health protection team by telephone within 24 hours
  • Needlestick or sexual exposure → PEP via occupational health, sexual health or ED Same-day. Reactivation on immunosuppression → specialist Same-day

1Confirm — read the serology

ResultMeaning
HBsAg +Current hepatitis B (chronic if more than 6 months)
HBsAg −, anti-HBc +, anti-HBs +Past infection, immune
Anti-HBs + onlyVaccinated
Anti-HBc + onlyAsk virology
HCV antibody +, RNA +Current hepatitis C
HCV antibody +, RNA −Cleared or cured

2Check — who to test (PH43)

☐ Ever injected drugs ☐ Born or raised in an intermediate- or high-prevalence country ☐ Prison, homelessness ☐ MSM, sex work ☐ Hepatitis B contacts ☐ HIV ☐ Unexplained abnormal LFTs ☐ Before immunosuppression (HBsAg + anti-HBc). Always offer an HIV test.

3Decide

  • HCV RNA positive → ODN or treatment service for DAAs.
  • HBsAg positive → hepatology; pregnant → urgent, seen within 6 weeks.
  • Past or vaccinated pattern → no referral; tell prescribers of immunosuppressants.

5Treat — primary care role

BloodsLFT, FBC, INR, U&E, HIV, hepatitis A, B, C serology, alcohol, medicines list.
HCVDAAs 8–12 weeks; cure over 95%; check interactions.
HBVSpecialist decides on tenofovir or entecavir.
ContactsTest and vaccinate HBV contacts; babies vaccinated from birth.
  • Hepatitis A and B vaccines if not immune. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Cure: RNA negative 12 weeks after treatment; RNA annually if ongoing risk.
  • Cirrhosis: liver ultrasound every 6 months, even after cure.
  • Baby of HBsAg-positive mother: HBsAg at 12 months.

7Refer

  • Pregnant, acute infection, HIV co-infection, cirrhosis Urgent
  • All other HCV RNA+ and HBsAg+ Routine; check they were seen.

8Never

  • Call a positive HCV antibody “hepatitis C” without an RNA result.
  • Use HCV antibody to look for reinfection.
  • Start chemotherapy or a biologic without knowing hepatitis B status.
  • Leave household and sexual contacts of hepatitis B untested and unvaccinated.
  • Forget the HIV test.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “Yellow skin or eyes with confusion, sleepiness, bleeding or vomiting — get help today; 999 if very drowsy or vomiting blood.”   Treatment: “The tablets cure almost everyone who finishes them — check with us before any new medicine.”   Others: “Your partner and household should be tested and vaccinated; condoms until they are protected.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Liver failure and acute hepatitis excluded or notified Serology interpreted (RNA for HCV) HIV, coinfection and pregnancy checked Referral made and tracked; contacts tested Safety-net and prevention advice documented
