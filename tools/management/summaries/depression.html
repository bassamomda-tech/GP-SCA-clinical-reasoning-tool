Depression in adults

For: adults 18+ in primary care. Not for: under-18s (CAMHS), suspected bipolar (psychiatry), postnatal (postnatal depression protocol), undifferentiated low mood (low-mood algorithm).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG222 (updated Dec 2025)CG192CG185RCPsychNG12
Core rule: ask directly about suicide and past mania before prescribing, choose with the patient by severity, review at 1 or 2 weeks, continue ≥6 months after remission, and stop in slow proportionate steps.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Immediate danger or any overdose (toxicity — especially tricyclics) → 999 / A&E
  • Active intent, feasible plan, recent attempt, can't stay safe, severe intoxication or no support → Same-day crisis assessment (local crisis pathway; NHS 111 option 2).
  • Psychotic features, stupor or severe self-neglect (not eating, drinking or self-caring) → Same-day psychiatry; consider admission.
  • Postpartum psychosis (confusion, hallucinations, shifting mood) → Same-day psychiatry (mother-and-baby service).
  • Parent with thoughts of harming their children → Same-day crisis team; impaired childcare → safeguarding referral.
  • Serotonin syndrome (agitation, sweating, clonus, fever) → stop, Same-day assessment; fever and rigidity 999

1Confirm — clinical diagnosis; the score supports it

ICD-11: ≥2 weeks of low mood and/or anhedonia plus additional symptoms. PHQ-2 ≥3 → PHQ-9. Never diagnose from a questionnaire alone; score GAD-7 too.

PHQ-9 + judgementNG222 fork
<16Less severe — shared choice (block 3)
≥16More severe — antidepressant + therapy

2Check — before prescribing

☐ Suicide plan, intent, means — documented  ☐ Past mania  ☐ AUDIT-C  ☐ Mood-lowering drugs (e.g. steroids, hormonal contraception)  ☐ Domestic abuse  ☐ Pregnancy  ☐ Tests by history: TSH, FBC/ferritin, B12/folate, HbA1c, U&E, LFTs; sodium if higher risk; ECG before (es)citalopram if cardiac or QT risk.

Don't miss: unexplained weight loss or iron-deficiency anaemia → NG12 routes, not the depression label.

3Decide — a shared choice

  • Less severe: self-help, group exercise, CBT, behavioural activation, IPT, counselling, social prescribing. Antidepressant not routinely first-line — offer if preferred, others unsuitable or not helped.
  • More severe: antidepressant plus high-intensity therapy (CBT or IPT); either alone if preferred.
  • NHS Talking Therapies (PHQ-9 5–15): self-referral; chase it.

4Target — treatment goals

Response≥50% PHQ-9 reduction
RemissionClinical recovery and function (PHQ-9 <5 supports)
Continue≥6 months after remission at the dose that worked; often ≥2 years if recurrent, severe or residual

5Treat — match the drug to the patient

ChooseMany adults, cardiac, pregnancy → sertraline. Poor sleep and appetite → mirtazapine.
Judge at 4 weeksPartial → optimise to max tolerated dose; reassess 6–8 weeks.
SwitchAfter checking adherence, dose, alcohol, diagnosis, therapy.
2 adequate trials failedRefer or seek advice.
  • Counsel: 2–4 weeks to start, 4–8 for full effect; early nausea or feeling wired fades.
  • Citalopram max 40 mg (20 mg at 65+). SSRI + NSAID → consider a PPI. High risk → small supplies, no tricyclic (lofepramine excepted).
  • Pregnancy: don't stop an effective drug abruptly; decide with the perinatal team. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Review at 1 week if aged 18–25 or increased suicide risk, else 2 weeks; then 2–4-weekly for 3 months.
  • Sodium if older, diuretic or renal disease: baseline, recheck 1–2 weeks. <130 mmol/L → prompt review; severe or symptomatic → emergency.
  • Venlafaxine, duloxetine: BP at each step. Long-term: annual medication review.
  • Stopping: staged, proportionate cuts, smaller as the dose falls; written plan. Severe withdrawal or relapse → restart the previous effective dose.

7Refer

  • Urgent psychiatry 1–2 weeks suspected bipolar, treatment-resistant or severe recurrent depression, dual-diagnosis substance misuse.
  • Perinatal team: moderate or worse illness in pregnancy or postnatally, or any risk.

8Never

  • An antidepressant alone without asking about past mania. A new “high” on monotherapy is bipolar — stop and refer.
  • “Low risk” without recording what was asked and said.
  • Switch before checking adherence, dose and alcohol; or two serotonergics at full dose.
  • Stop at “feeling better”, abruptly, or “one every other day for a fortnight”.
  • Attribute weight loss, fatigue or anaemia to depression without investigating.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you feel unsafe: 999 or A&E now; NHS 111 option 2; Samaritans 116 123. Save these in your phone now.”   First fortnight: “More agitated, or new thoughts of harming yourself — contact us the same day.”   Never stop dead: “Never stop suddenly — come to us to plan it.”   Mixing: “Ask a pharmacist before adding anything, including St John's Wort.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
