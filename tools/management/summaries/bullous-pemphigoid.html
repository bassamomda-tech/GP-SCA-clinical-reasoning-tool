Bullous pemphigoid (and pemphigus)

For: adults, usually over 70, with suspected or confirmed bullous pemphigoid, in primary and shared care. Not for: suspected pemphigus, SJS/TEN or a systemically unwell patient (block 0: same-day dermatology or hospital). Systemic and steroid-sparing treatment is dermatology-led.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BAD 2012 pemphigoidBAD 2017 pemphigusBLISTER (Lancet 2017)EADV 2022NOGG 2024 · NatPSA steroid card
Core rule: think pemphigoid in an older person with persistent itch and urticated plaques; prominent mucosal erosions or flaccid blisters mean reconsider pemphigus the same day; refer for two biopsies, review the gliptin, and make every prednisolone course bone-, card- and glucose-safe.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Shock, airway concern, severe dehydration, marked confusion, high-risk sepsis or unable to travel safely → 999 acute medical admission; send the drug list with start dates.
  • Suspected SJS/TEN (unwell, fever, mucosal involvement, dusky or targetoid skin, new drug in last 8 weeks) → stop the culprit drug; Immediate hospital assessment
  • Extensive or rapidly progressive blistering/erosions, fever or suspected sepsis (NEWS2 plus clinical concern and frailty), dehydration, AKI, or unable to eat, drink or cope → Same-day dermatology / acute medicine
  • Prominent mouth, eye, genital or laryngeal erosions, flaccid blisters, Nikolsky positive (pemphigus, mucous membrane pemphigoid) → Same-day dermatology discussion; ocular symptoms → urgent ophthalmology, same day if symptomatic.
  • On an immune checkpoint inhibitor → do not stop it yourself; Urgent oncology discussion. Never delay transfer of a deteriorating patient for a biopsy.

1Confirm — dermatology confirms by biopsy

Prodrome: weeks to months of intense itch, urticated or eczematous plaques, no blisters; then large, tense blisters on flexures, trunk, limbs.

FeatureBullous pemphigoidPemphigus vulgaris
BlistersTense, intact; Nikolsky negativeFlaccid → painful erosions; Nikolsky often positive
MucosaSpared or mildOral erosions in most, often first
SymptomItchPain; eating and swallowing

Two biopsies: blister edge in formalin + perilesional skin for DIF (never in formalin). BP180/BP230 serology is supportive only.

2Check — at the first consultation

☐ ABCDE, observations, hydration, U&E  ☐ Mouth, eyes, genitals  ☐ Every drug with start dates (gliptin, loop diuretic, checkpoint inhibitor)  ☐ Who will apply the ointment; coping at home

Don't miss mimics: bullous impetigo (swab), scabies (burrows, contacts), herpes simplex/zoster, oedema blisters.

3Decide — referral and triggers

  • All suspected autoimmune blistering → dermatology for biopsy + DIF.
  • Localised or prodromal, stable → urgent dermatology referral or same-week advice-and-guidance (local Bolton pathway).
  • Gliptin: review and discuss stopping if timing fits and another diabetes drug suits; involve the diabetes team. Never delay dermatology.
  • Document the suspected reaction, Yellow Card, tell dermatology. Loop diuretics and others: possible associations only.

5Treat — on dermatology advice

Localised / mildClobetasol propionate 0.05% to affected skin; can start on advice before the biopsy result.
ExtensiveWhole-body clobetasol (dermatology-supervised, set quantity and taper) or oral prednisolone.
Steroid harm the worryDermatology may consider doxycycline 200 mg daily: slower, may need rescue prednisolone.
Steroid-sparing / refractoryAzathioprine, methotrexate, mycophenolate, biologics: specialist-led; written GM shared care only.
  • Blisters: leave small ones; large tense ones drained aseptically by a trained clinician, keeping the roof. Non-adherent dressings.
  • Emollient and soap substitute; paraffin products are a fire risk. No prophylactic antibiotics — swab and treat only clinical infection. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — steroid safety is the GP's job

  • Bone: assess fracture risk at the start of oral steroid; prior fragility fracture, women ≥70 or prednisolone ≥7.5 mg/day for about 3 months → bone protection without waiting for DXA.
  • Prednisolone ≥5 mg/day for 4 weeks or more → Steroid Emergency Card and sick-day advice. Large-area clobetasol can suppress the adrenals too.
  • Capillary glucose (later in the day) within days of starting or raising prednisolone; BP at baseline and during. PPI only with extra GI risk.

7Refer — not controlled?

  • First check infection, who applies the ointment, an ongoing drug trigger and the diagnosis; taper per dermatology.
  • Active or extensive disease, or steroid or immunosuppressant complications → dermatology, urgent or routine by severity.

8Never

  • Keep calling an older person's refractory itchy urticated rash eczema or scabies — refer for biopsy.
  • Treat persistent oral erosions with repeated mouthwash — think pemphigus.
  • Start prednisolone for suspected pemphigus or extensive pemphigoid without dermatology or acute medical agreement (unless a local pathway).
  • Stop steroids abruptly, or start them without a bone, card and glucose plan.
  • Put the DIF biopsy in formalin, or give one 30 g tube for extensive disease.

9Safety-net — say it, give it in writing to the carer or care home, document it

999 “Confusion or slurred speech, very fast or difficult breathing, or blotchy, grey or blue skin — call 999.”   Same-day “Widespread blistering with fever or shivering, feeling very unwell or large raw areas; spreading redness, pus, pain or smell; sore mouth, eyes or genitals or trouble swallowing — contact us the same day.”   Steroid tablets: “Don't stop suddenly, carry your card; infection, thirst and passing lots of urine, tummy pain or black stools — contact us the same day.”
