Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) in adults

For: unwell adults with known or new diabetes — recognition, 999 handover, prevention. Not for: children (separate paediatric DKA pathway, NG18) or hospital treatment; sick-day doses → unwell on insulin and sick day rules protocols.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
JBDS-IP DKA (2023)NICE NG17NG3NG18TREND-UK · GMMMG SGLT2
Core rule: ketones and acidosis define DKA, not the glucose — check blood ketones in every unwell person with diabetes, call 999 for any suspected DKA, and never stop the basal insulin.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected or confirmed DKA (block 1 triad) → 999 ABCDE, do not delay transfer; hand over ketones, glucose, K⁺ if known and the suspected precipitant.
  • Blood ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L even if glucose is normal, or ketonaemia + acidosis on an SGLT2 inhibitor → 999 euglycaemic DKA; stop the SGLT2 inhibitor.
  • Unable to keep fluids down, drowsy or confused, deep or fast (Kussmaul) breathing, or abdominal pain with ketones → 999
  • Glucose >30, confusion, severe dehydration, minimal ketosis (often older, type 2) = HHS, not DKA → 999
  • DKA in pregnancy, any trimester → 999 urgent obstetric-diabetes care. Occurs at lower glucose — no glucose threshold excludes it.
  • Child or young person; headache, irritability, altered consciousness, bradycardia or abnormal breathing = possible cerebral injury → 999 paediatric emergency; inform the paediatric team.

1Confirm — the JBDS triad; glucose is not required

DKA = blood ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L (or urine ≥2+) + glucose >11 + venous pH <7.3 and/or bicarbonate <15. Ketonaemia + acidosis = possible DKA even if glucose <11.

Precipitants: infection (commonest), new type 1, insulin omission, MI, SGLT2 inhibitor, pregnancy, cocaine.

2Check — while arranging 999; never delay transfer

☐ ABCDE, GCS, temperature  ☐ Glucose and blood ketones  ☐ Hydration, Kussmaul breathing  ☐ Infection source; chest pain → ECG  ☐ Last insulin dose; ask about omission

Ketotic breath is often absent. A hard, peritonitic abdomen may be a true surgical emergency.

3Decide — what primary care can and cannot manage

  • Suspected or confirmed DKA → never managed in primary care: 999.
  • Mild ketonaemia, no acidosis → may be managed on the sick-day plan with urgent diabetes-team advice, only if stable, keeping fluids down and ketones improving.
  • Ketones 1.5–2.9 + vomiting, rising despite correction, pregnancy, pump failure or SGLT2 inhibitor → Same-day hospital

5Prevent — the part primary care owns

Sick-day rulesWritten plan. Never stop basal insulin. Test glucose and ketones; sugar-free fluids; extra insulin per the individual plan.
KitPrescribe a blood ketone meter and strips (local formulary). Offer CGM to adults with type 1, per NICE and local pathway.
SGLT2 inhibitorsStop if ill, vomiting, dehydrated, fasting or before surgery; restart after 24–48 h eating and drinking normally. TREND-UK guidance.
Insulin omissionAsk directly, non-judgementally: eating disorder, distress, depression, self-harm, barriers.
  • Pump failure: check set and cannula; correction insulin by pen if ketones raised; diabetes team urgently.
  • Pre-conception: HbA1c <48, folic acid 5 mg, joint diabetes–obstetric clinic; no SGLT2 inhibitor in pregnancy. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — after DKA and every year

  • After discharge: prompt diabetes-team follow-up and GP review (local post-DKA pathway) — precipitant, regimen, meter, sick-day rules. 3 months: HbA1c, diabetes team.
  • Annual (NG17): HbA1c (≤48 mmol/mol for most), eGFR/ACR, eyes, feet, BP, lipids, thyroid, coeliac if symptoms, PHQ-9/PAID.
  • Every review: 999 triggers known; meter works, strips in date; sick-day rules understood.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Second DKA admission → specialist diabetes team; they decide pump or closed-loop suitability.
  • Suspected insulin omission → joint diabetes psychology / eating-disorder service.
  • Driving on insulin: tell the DVLA; test ≤2 h before and every 2 h; no driving if <4.0.

8Never

  • Exclude DKA on a normal glucose (SGLT2 inhibitor, pregnancy, fasting) or on urine ketones — check blood ketones.
  • Start IV fluids or insulin, or correct potassium, in primary care — or delay the 999 call for tests.
  • Advise stopping basal insulin when unwell or not eating.
  • Manage a child with this adult protocol — separate paediatric pathway.
  • Frame insulin omission simply as non-adherence.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Ketones 3.0 or more, being sick and unable to keep fluids down, deep or fast breathing, or feeling drowsy or confused — call 999, even if your sugar is not high.”   Same-day “Ketones 1.5 to 2.9 — follow your sick-day plan and ring your diabetes team today.”   Insulin: “Never stop your long-acting insulin, even on days you cannot eat.”   SGLT2 inhibitor: “If you are ill, vomiting or not eating, stop it until you are eating and drinking normally again.”
