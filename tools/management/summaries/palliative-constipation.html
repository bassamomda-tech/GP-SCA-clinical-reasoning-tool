Palliative constipation

For: adults with advanced illness or on strong opioids, in primary care with the district nurses. Not for: constipation outside palliative care (adult constipation), children (child constipation), or the last days of life (care of the dying).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG140TA345TA651NG234Scottish Palliative Care Guidelines 2025
Core rule: examine before you prescribe — rule out obstruction, cord compression and hypercalcaemia; then a regular laxative with every strong opioid, clear a loaded rectum from below, and a PAMORA only when laxatives are optimised and obstruction is excluded.

0Act now — rule these out before any laxative

  • Colic, distension, vomiting, no flatus → possible bowel obstruction: stop stimulants, no PAMORA → Same-day assessment (surgical opinion or specialist palliative care, per goals).
  • Cancer + new back or radicular pain with leg weakness, numbness, unsteady gait or new bladder/bowel dysfunction → possible MSCC → Immediately MSCC coordinator; whole-spine MRI within 24 hours; dexamethasone 16 mg orally now if neurological symptoms or signs (unless contraindicated), with gastric protection and glucose monitoring in diabetes. See MSCC.
  • Thirst, confusion, nausea or drowsiness → consider hypercalcaemia: adjusted calcium and U&E if the result would alter management → Urgent local malignant-hypercalcaemia pathway, per goals of care.
  • Severe or persistent abdominal pain on a PAMORA (possible perforation) → stop the drug → Same-day review.
  • Liquid leakage after days without a proper bowel movement → likely impaction with overflow: examine the rectum before any loperamide.

1Confirm — compare with their normal; the rectum decides

Last proper bowel movement, stool, straining, colic, vomiting, distension, flatus. Abdomen, then PR or stoma exam if it alters management and is acceptable.

PR findingFirst → if that fails
Soft stoolBisacodyl suppository → repeat, or small enema
Hard stoolGlycerol ± bisacodyl suppository → arachis oil enema overnight, then phosphate enema
ImpactionEnema → manual evacuation with analgesia, experienced clinician
Empty rectumNo rectal treatment; consider stool higher up or obstruction

Before rectal treatment: exclude obstruction, severe thrombocytopenia or neutropenia, recent rectal/pelvic surgery or radiotherapy. Arachis oil: not in peanut allergy.

2Check — remove what is causing it

☐ Drugs: opioids (incl. codeine), antimuscarinics, 5-HT3 antiemetics, iron, antacids, calcium channel blockers, diuretics — stop or switch  ☐ Intake, mobility, privacy  ☐ Calcium + U&E only if suspected. X-ray rarely needed.

3Decide

  • Starting a strong opioid → prescribe a regular laxative at an effective dose the same day, not “as needed”.
  • Choose stimulant, osmotic or both by stool, fluid intake, swallowing and local formulary; NICE names no class.
  • Non-drug: privacy, commode, footstool; fluid and fibre only as wanted.

4Target

Soft stool passed comfortably at the patient's usual frequency — not a set number of days. Laxative continues as long as the opioid. Last days: comfort, not a bowel target.

5Treat — local practice sequence, not a NICE hierarchy

Step 1 · regularSenna 15–30 mg or bisacodyl 5–10 mg at night; hard stool → add macrogol 1–3 sachets daily or docusate 100 mg BD
Step 2 · every 1–2 daysHard → more macrogol/softener. Soft, not passed → more stimulant. Colic → consider obstruction
Step 3 · rectalLoaded rectum → block 1 table; restart oral laxatives the same day
Step 4 · PAMORAAdequate laxative trial, obstruction excluded → naloxegol 25 mg each morning or naldemedine 200 micrograms daily; methylnaltrexone SC per SPC and local pathway
  • Adequate trial (TA345): ≥1 laxative class for ≥4 days in the previous 2 weeks. Doses per BNF, SPC and local palliative-care formulary.

6Monitor

  • Day 1: laxative with the opioid; bowel chart. Day 3–4: phone check; PR if nothing passed or overflow.
  • Every opioid change: re-titrate; never stop the laxative while the opioid continues.
  • Can't empty rectum (after MSCC, weakness) → planned rectal treatment every 1–3 days.

7Refer

  • Specialist palliative care: no response despite laxatives, rectal measures and an oral PAMORA; opioid switching; manual evacuation needing sedation.
  • District nurses: home suppositories and enemas, bowel charts and plans. Ileostomy → stoma nurse.
  • No known cancer, new persistent change in bowel habit, if fitting goals → FIT ≥10 micrograms Hb/g → Suspected cancer pathway; rectal or anal mass → refer directly.

8Never

  • A stimulant laxative or PAMORA when obstruction is suspected.
  • A strong opioid with laxatives only “if needed”.
  • Loperamide for leakage before a rectal exam.
  • An opioid switch for constipation before laxatives are optimised.
  • A bulk-forming laxative with poor fluid intake, immobility or faecal loading.

9Safety-net — say it to patient and carers, then document it

Same-day “Tummy pain that comes in waves with being sick, a swollen tight tummy or no wind; new back pain, leg weakness or numbness, or loss of bladder or bowel control; new confusion or drowsiness; severe tummy pain after the new bowel medicine — call us straight away, the same day.”   Laxative: “Take it every day from today; it takes a few days. If your bowels haven't moved properly after three or four days, or get uncomfortable, ring us.”   Within a day or two: “Straining, rectal pain or liquid leakage — ring us.”
