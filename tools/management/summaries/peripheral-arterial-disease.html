Peripheral arterial disease

For: adults with suspected or known lower-limb PAD and stable claudication. Not for: a limb threatened today (block 0), an ischaemic diabetic foot ulcer (acute services, NG19) or neurogenic claudication (use the sciatica protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG147NG19NG238TA210 · TA223NG209
Core rule: split stable claudication from limb threat on the history and a look at the foot, not the ABPI; then treat PAD as heart disease in the legs (statin, clopidogrel, stop smoking) and walk before angioplasty.

0Act now — decided clinically, before any ABPI

  • Acute limb ischaemia: sudden pain, pallor, pulselessness, perishing cold, paraesthesia or paralysis (not all needed); any new sensory loss, weakness, a newly cold or pale foot or new pulse loss → 999 / emergency vascular Do not wait for ABPI or imaging.
  • Chronic limb-threatening ischaemia: persistent rest pain (typically >2 weeks, worse lying flat, eased hanging the leg down), non-healing ulcer or gangrene → Urgent vascular per local pathway (often same-day call) Infection, rapidly progressive tissue loss or sepsis → Same-day acute
  • Diabetic foot ulcer with ischaemia, gangrene, deep infection or sepsis → Immediately to acute services and tell the multidisciplinary foot service.
  • Walking distance collapsing over days to weeks → Same-day vascular discussion
  • Sudden back or abdominal pain with collapse or absent femoral pulses, chest pain or stroke symptoms → 999

1Confirm — history first, then ABPI

Claudication: reproducible calf, thigh or buttock pain on walking, relieved by standing still within about 10 minutes, at a similar distance each time. Eased only by sitting or bending forward → neurogenic (sciatica protocol). Record the distance.

Diabetes or CKD with a normal or high ABPI and a classic story → toe pressures, waveforms or duplex; if unavailable or risk is high, seek vascular advice.

2Check — both legs, shoes off

☐ Between toes, heels, pressure points; pulses  ☐ ABPI both legs  ☐ HbA1c, lipids, U&E/eGFR, transaminase; ACR in diabetes  ☐ Monofilament in diabetes  ☐ Men 65+: AAA screening offered?  ☐ ECG only if clinically indicated

Don't miss: neuropathy masks rest pain — look at the foot.

3Decide — PAD is established CVD

  • No QRISK → secondary prevention straight away, for everyone with PAD.
  • Stable claudication → offer a supervised exercise programme before angioplasty.
  • Lifestyle: Mediterranean-style diet, weight, activity, alcohol ≤14 units a week.

5Treat — bundle saves life; exercise treats the leg

Bundle · everyoneAtorvastatin 80 mg · clopidogrel 75 mg daily (aspirin if unsuitable) · stop smoking: varenicline, cytisinicline, combination NRT or e-cigarette + stop-smoking service
Step 1 · exerciseSupervised: about 2 h a week for 3 months, walking to maximal pain. Unavailable or declined → structured home walking with a diary
Step 2 · drugConsider naftidrofuryl only if exercise has not helped enough and no referral wanted; review at 3–6 months, stop if no benefit
Step 3 · referLifestyle-limiting despite exercise and risk-factor treatment, would consider a procedure → vascular; continue the bundle
  • One antiplatelet unless a separate indication. Rivaroxaban 2.5 mg BD + aspirin may be considered in selected high-risk people — usually specialist-guided.
  • Statin muscle symptoms → rechallenge, lower dose or switch, not “intolerant”. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • About 3 months: walking distance and diary, exercise attended, smoking, statin and clopidogrel tolerated, BP and lipids.
  • Annually on the CVD register: non-HDL-C, BP, HbA1c, renal function, smoking.
  • Foot every review (more often in diabetes); repeat ABPI if symptoms change.

7Refer — urgent ones are in block 0

  • Vascular soon: steadily worsening claudication; suspected aorto-iliac disease when younger; popliteal entrapment; classic story with normal or incompressible ABPI; severe; no supervised exercise.
  • Routine vascular: lifestyle-limiting despite exercise and optimised risk factors, wants a procedure.

8Never

  • Manage rest pain, an ulcer or gangrene as claudication.
  • Exclude PAD in diabetes or CKD on a normal or high ABPI alone.
  • Start cilostazol or pentoxifylline for claudication.
  • Withhold a needed beta-blocker because of PAD.
  • Routinely combine clopidogrel with aspirin, or use QRISK in PAD.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If your leg suddenly becomes painful, pale, cold, numb or weak, or you get chest pain or stroke symptoms — call 999; don't wait for an appointment.”   Same-day “Foot pain at night or at rest, any new sore or black or blue area on the foot, or walking distance falling quickly — contact us the same day.”   Tablets: “These protect your heart and brain, not just your leg.”   Walking: “Walk into the ache, rest until it settles, then carry on. Check your feet daily; never walk barefoot.”
