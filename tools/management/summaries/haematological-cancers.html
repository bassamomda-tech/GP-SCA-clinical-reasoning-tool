Haematological cancers

For: possible leukaemia, lymphoma or myeloma at any age; people on anticancer treatment. Not for: lump-led work-up (lymphadenopathy pathway), isolated lymphocytosis (lymphocytosis pathway), iron deficiency (anaemia protocol). Children: local paediatric pathway.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)NG35CG151NG253NG234
Core rule: assemble the constellation, not one symptom; a very urgent FBC (≤48 hours) is only urgent if you chase it; blasts or severe cytopenias mean a same-day phone call; anyone unwell within 30 days of anticancer treatment goes to hospital now.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Unwell, febrile or not, on or ≤30 days after systemic anticancer treatment (or wider local window) → Emergency transfer now, pre-alert acute oncology; don't wait for the count; no oral antibiotics.
  • Back pain + new leg weakness, sensory level, saddle anaesthesia or bladder/bowel change (myeloma/lymphoma) → MSCC coordinator Immediately; MRI ≤24 h; neuro signs → dexamethasone 16 mg unless contraindicated.
  • Hyperviscosity, leukostasis, tumour lysis, symptomatic hypercalcaemia, major bleeding with low platelets → Emergency admission
  • Blasts, film ?acute leukaemia or APL, new severe or progressive cytopenias, grossly abnormal count and unwell → Same-day haematology phone call, not a letter.
  • Child with unexplained petechiae or hepatosplenomegaly → Immediate specialist assessment  Low platelets with bleeding → Same-day haematology advice; no NSAIDs or IM injections.

2Check — examine; send together

☐ Petechiae, bruising, pallor ☐ All nodes incl. supraclavicular, epitrochlear ☐ Liver, spleen, temperature ☐ FBC + film where indicated, U&E, calcium, LFTs, LDH, ESR/PV. Don't miss: a normal FBC does not exclude lymphoma or early myeloma.

3Decide — the constellation, not one thread

  • Ask about all of it — fatigue, bruising, infections, drenching sweats, weight loss, bone pain, lumps, itch; how long?
  • Combination and progression raise concern; NG12 leukaemia criteria need no 6 weeks. A third vague visit is itself a red flag.
  • 60+ with unexplained persistent or night back pain → myeloma screen, not more analgesia.

7Refer — routine haematology

  • Stable isolated lymphocytosis, well (CLL needs immunophenotyping); incidental MGUS; stable mild cytopenias.
  • Myeloma imaging is specialist-led — no skeletal survey.

6Monitor

  • Chase what you ordered: name who owns the result; book follow-up before they leave.
  • On chemotherapy: thermometer and 24-hour acute oncology number; record regimen, last treatment date, central line (local pathways).

8Never

  • Wait for the neutrophil count, or give oral antibiotics, in suspected neutropenic sepsis.
  • Send a letter, or repeat in 2 weeks, for blasts or severe cytopenias.
  • Defer a lymphoma referral because the FBC is normal.
  • Exclude myeloma on one negative test, or offer a tonic or iron for the full constellation.
  • Leave a very urgent FBC unchased in the inbox.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

On anticancer treatment: Hospital now “Feeling unwell or feverish means going straight to hospital — use your 24-hour acute oncology number; don't ring the surgery in the morning or wait to see if it settles. No thermometer? Don't wait.”   Awaiting the FBC: “I'll chase the result myself and ring you. If before then you get new bleeding or bruising, tiny red or purple spots, a high temperature, or feel very unwell — contact us or NHS 111 the same day; 999 if bleeding won't stop or you are seriously unwell.”
