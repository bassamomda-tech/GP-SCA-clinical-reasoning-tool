Neuropathic pain in adults

For: adults with neuropathic pain in primary care, including painful diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia and trigeminal neuralgia. Not for: sciatica or lumbar radicular pain (NICE NG59, use the sciatica page), cancer-related neuropathic pain (oncology or palliative care), or chronic primary pain (use the chronic pain page, NG193).

Core rule: CG173 assumes serious pathology is already excluded — screen for cauda equina and spinal cord compression first, then offer one of four first-line drugs as sequential monotherapy, and never use this ladder for sciatica.

0Act now — before any ladder; document the neurological examination

  • New urinary retention or altered urinary sensation, saddle anaesthesia, loss of bowel control or anal tone, new sexual dysfunction with saddle symptoms, severe/progressive bilateral deficit → 999 / immediate emergency MRI (cauda equina).
  • Current or previous cancer + new severe, progressive, unremitting or nocturnal spinal, radicular or band-like pain, weakness, sensory change or sphincter disturbance → MSCC/oncology pathway Same-day; whole-spine MRI ≤24 h.
  • Rapidly progressive weakness, new foot drop or ascending sensory loss → Same-day emergency assessment (Guillain–Barré, cord lesion, stroke).
  • Fever, weight loss or IV drug use with spinal pain → Same-day emergency assessment (discitis, epidural abscess).
  • Zoster: antivirals, usually within 72 hours. Ophthalmic → Same-day ophthalmology; otic with facial palsy or hearing/vestibular symptoms → Urgent ENT/neurology. New mononeuritis multiplex → Urgent specialist work-up.

1Confirm — a symptom, not a diagnosis

Burning, shooting, electric-shock, stabbing or tingling pain, often worse at night; allodynia or numbness in a neuroanatomical distribution, with examination findings and a cause. DN4, LANSS or painDETECT support, not replace, judgement.

2Check — localise, then investigate by cause

☐ Neurological examination mapped to nerve, root or central pattern  ☐ Diabetic foot check (monofilament, vibration)  ☐ Skin: zoster scars, CRPS signs  ☐ New symmetrical neuropathy: HbA1c, B12 ± folate, renal function, TFTs, FBC

No routine MRI for stable pain. 60+ with persistent bone or back pain, or unexplained fracture → myeloma bloods (NG12).

3Decide — treat the cause and the person

  • Treat the cause in parallel: optimise glycaemia, replace B12. Goal: meaningful relief and function, not abolition.
  • Choose the drug by comorbidity, side effects, cost, misuse risk and patient preference.
  • Non-drug care: explanation, graded activity, CBT/ACT for persistent pain, treat sleep and mood, foot care.

5Treat — CG173, sequential monotherapy

First lineOffer one: amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin or pregabalin. Start low.
Fails or not toleratedSwitch to one of the other three; taper the first, overlap if needed.
ThenCombinations on specialist advice only. Localised pain → consider capsaicin cream.
Trigeminal neuralgiaCarbamazepine 100 mg BD, titrated (max 1.6 g daily); U&E, LFTs, sodium, FBC; rash.
  • Gabapentinoids (Schedule 3): assess misuse; avoid with opioids where possible; caution with benzodiazepines, alcohol; reduce in renal impairment.
  • Amitriptyline: anticholinergic load, QTc, falls. Duloxetine: hepatic cautions. Pregnancy or planning: review every agent with specialist advice. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Early review after each start or change: titration, tolerability, side effects.
  • Regular reviews: pain, sleep, mood, function, side effects, ongoing need, gabapentinoid misuse, sedatives.
  • No benefit after an adequate titrated trial → taper and stop. Neurology changes → re-examine, re-triage (block 0).

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent routes are in block 0)

  • Neurology: diagnostic uncertainty, progressive or atypical neuropathy, suspected central cause or MS, atypical or carbamazepine-resistant trigeminal neuralgia.
  • Pain clinic: refractory pain despite optimised treatment; CRPS (or neurology).
  • Diabetes team: diabetic neuropathy not responding to optimised glycaemia plus first-line drugs.

8Never

  • Start the ladder before the red-flag screen, or let a neuropathic-pain label explain a new deficit.
  • Gabapentin, pregabalin or other antiepileptics for sciatica (NG59).
  • Start strong opioids, cannabis-based products or (outside specialist care) other antiepileptics; or long-term tramadol.
  • Stack drugs: combinations only on specialist advice; stop ineffective ones.
  • Stop long-term gabapentinoid treatment abruptly.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Trouble passing urine, loss of bowel control, numbness around the saddle area, or weakness in both legs — that's an emergency: call 999 or go to A&E straight away.”   Had cancer: “New back pain, weakness or numbness — contact us the same day.”   New tablets: “They take a few weeks to work. If it isn't helping, we'll switch rather than add more.”   Gabapentin or pregabalin: “They can cause drowsiness and dizziness — take care driving; don't stop them suddenly.”
