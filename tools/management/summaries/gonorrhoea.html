Gonorrhoea

For: suspected or confirmed anogenital or pharyngeal gonorrhoea, and contacts, in primary care, including pregnancy. Not for: chlamydia alone (use the chlamydia protocol); disseminated or eye infection and children under 13 leave primary care the same day (block 0).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BASHH gonorrhoea 2025UKHSA resistance surveillance 2026Green Book: gonorrhoea (4CMenB) 2025MHRA fluoroquinolones Jan 2024BASHH PID 2019 · epididymo-orchitis 2020
Core rule: take a culture with the NAAT before antibiotics where possible, treat with ceftriaxone 1 g IM through sexual health — never an oral antibiotic "to cover it" — then trace partners and decide on test of cure.

0Act now — never dismiss a hot joint or a pus-filled eye without a sexual history

  • Fever, hot joint, tenosynovitis or pustular rash (disseminated) → Same-day hospital; treat a hot joint as septic arthritis.
  • Hyperacute purulent conjunctivitis → Same-day ophthalmology. Newborn with purulent eyes → Same-day paediatrics; treat mother and partner(s).
  • PID (pelvic pain + cervical motion/adnexal tenderness) → pregnancy test; treat Same-day; systemically ill, pregnant or abscess → Same-day admission. Positive test → Immediately early pregnancy unit; unstable → A&E
  • Sudden severe testicular pain → torsion → Immediate urology / A&E or 999. Under 13, or at risk of exploitation → Same-day safeguarding; don't delay treatment.

1Confirm — NAAT finds it, culture characterises it

Suspect: discharge, dysuria, intermenstrual bleeding, pelvic pain; often silent, especially rectum and throat.

Culture with the NAAT, before antibiotics; never delay urgent treatment.

2Check — every case

☐ Chlamydia (dual NAAT)  ☐ HIV + syphilis; hepatitis by risk; PrEP  ☐ Pregnancy  ☐ Travel, partners abroad  ☐ Under 16: competence + safeguarding. Don't miss: ceftriaxone resistance is rising, even without travel.

3Decide — sexual health leads

  • Positive NAAT or clinical picture → urgent sexual health; if that delays, a trained GP may culture and give ceftriaxone under local protocol.
  • Partners: symptomatic urethral → 2 weeks before onset; otherwise 3 months. Contacts tested at every site.
  • Chlamydia too → treat per BASHH (doxycycline, not in pregnancy). 4CMenB for high-risk GBMSM → signpost to sexual health.

4Target — test of cure, NAAT ≥2 weeks after treatment

SituationTest of cure?
Anogenital, ceftriaxone 1 g, known susceptibleNot routinely
Pharyngeal · susceptibility unknown · not ceftriaxone · pregnant · persistent symptoms (culture first)Yes

5Treat — BASHH 2025

First lineCeftriaxone 1 g IM, single dose — anogenital and pharyngeal. No azithromycin alongside.
Known susceptible onlyCiprofloxacin 500 mg orally once — not first line; sexual-health decision when susceptibility known before treatment (MHRA).
Ceftriaxone not possibleSpecialist alternative guided by culture.
PIDCeftriaxone 1 g IM once + doxycycline 100 mg twice daily + metronidazole 400 mg twice daily, 14 days.
  • Beta-lactam anaphylaxis or severe immediate reaction → specialist advice.
  • Pregnancy: ceftriaxone alone; avoid quinolones, tetracyclines, gentamicin. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Confirm clinic attendance and partners treated.
  • Positive test of cure or persistent symptoms → sexual health for culture; suspected failure reported to UKHSA.

7Refer — non-urgent routes

  • Sexual health, within days: every positive NAAT or clinical gonorrhoea; flag travel.
  • Maternity: share diagnosis; test of cure before delivery.
  • Won't tell a partner → provider notification; fear of partner → safeguarding.

8Never

  • Give an oral antibiotic "to cover it" on a NAAT alone while waiting — it may fail and select resistance.
  • Use ciprofloxacin first line, or without susceptibility known before treatment.
  • Treat gonococcal conjunctivitis with topical treatment alone.
  • Delay a torsion or ectopic referral for STI tests.
  • Use travel history as the only trigger to suspect resistance.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

A&E / 999 “Sudden severe pain in a testicle, or severe tummy pain if you could be pregnant — go to A&E or call 999 straight away.”   Same-day “A hot swollen joint, a rash with a fever, a red eye with pus, or pain low in your tummy — you need to be seen the same day.”   Sex: “No oral, vaginal or anal sex until 7 days after you and your partners have finished treatment and symptoms have gone; condoms are not a substitute.”   Follow-up: “The clinic may ask you back for a repeat swab in a couple of weeks.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Block 0 excluded: joint, eye, PID, ectopic, torsion, under 13 NAAT from every exposed site incl. throat; culture arranged HIV, syphilis, pregnancy and travel checked Sexual health referral, partners and test-of-cure plan recorded Safety-net and no-sex advice given and documented
