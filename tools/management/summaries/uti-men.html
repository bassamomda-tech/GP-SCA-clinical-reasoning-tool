UTI in men

For: men 16+ with lower UTI, pyelonephritis, acute prostatitis or catheter-associated UTI. Not for: under 16 (children's UTI protocol), urethritis or STI (sexual health), chronic prostatitis (prostatitis protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG109NG110 · NG111NG112 · NG113NG12 (April 2026)
Core rule: send an MSU before the first dose, place the infection (bladder, kidney, prostate, scrotum, urethra) — it decides the drug and the length — and never give a man the women's 3-day course.

0Act now — observations every time; do not delay transfer to get a urine sample

  • Suspected sepsis (altered mental state, raised RR, low BP, tachycardia, mottled skin, not passing urine) → NG253 risk; high risk → 999
  • Acute urinary retention (painful palpable bladder, unable to void) → ED now immediate catheterisation; if febrile, think prostatitis or abscess.
  • Fever and loin pain with colic, stone, single kidney or hydronephrosis → Emergency admission — needs drainage, not just antibiotics.
  • Acute severe testicular pain, especially adolescents and young men → 999 / ED emergency urology: torsion until proven otherwise.
  • Prostatitis with sepsis, retention or suspected abscess, or not improving 48 hours after starting antibiotics → Same-day hospital Fever after prostate biopsy → Same-day urology team that did it.
  • Pyelonephritis: serious illness → Same-day hospital Dehydrated, can't take oral medicines, diabetes, immunosuppression or renal-tract abnormality → consider referral or specialist advice.

1Confirm — symptoms plus culture, not the dipstick

A negative dipstick does not exclude UTI if suspicion remains. Older or catheterised: a positive dipstick alone is not UTI.

PointersTreat as
Dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain; no fever, rigors or loin painLower UTI · 7 days
Fever, rigors, loin pain, vomitingPyelonephritis · 7–14 days
Fever + perineal, rectal or ejaculatory pain, voiding difficultyProstatitis · 14 days, review
Scrotal pain, tender epididymisExclude torsion
Urethral discharge, new partnerSTI — sexual health

2Check — in the room

☐ Temp, pulse, BP, RR, SpO₂, mental state  ☐ Abdomen, bladder, renal angles, genitals  ☐ Gentle DRE only if prostatitis suspected (no massage)  ☐ MSU before antibiotics, labelled  ☐ Previous cultures  ☐ First-void NAAT if STI risk  ☐ U&E/eGFR if pyelonephritis or prostatitis at home

Delirium plus positive dipstick → look for another cause; assess for sepsis.

3Decide

  • Symptomatic UTI → immediate antibiotic; no back-up prescription (NG109).
  • Asymptomatic bacteriuria (any age, catheter or not) → do not treat.
  • Fluoroquinolones only if alternatives are inappropriate (MHRA 2024); counsel and document.
  • Catheter >7 days → consider removing or changing it; don't delay antibiotics.
  • Offer analgesia: paracetamol, or ibuprofen if suitable (avoid NSAIDs with AKI, dehydration or CKD).

5Treat — match the drug to the site

Lower UTI · NG109Trimethoprim 200 mg BD, or nitrofurantoin MR 100 mg BD if eGFR ≥45 — 7 days.
Pyelonephritis · NG111Cefalexin 7–10 days. Culture-susceptible: co-amoxiclav 7–10 days or trimethoprim 14 days. Else ciprofloxacin 7 days.
Prostatitis · NG110Ciprofloxacin 500 mg BD or ofloxacin — 14 days, review. Unsuitable → trimethoprim 200 mg BD (specialist advice).
Catheter · NG113Sample via port or new catheter. Lower, 7 days: nitrofurantoin (eGFR ≥45), trimethoprim, amoxicillin if susceptible. Upper → as pyelonephritis.

Trimethoprim: K⁺, creatinine rise; not with methotrexate. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • 48 hours, or sooner if worse: not improving → reassess; prostatitis → hospital.
  • Culture resistant, still symptomatic → susceptible agent. Prostatitis, 14 days → stop, or 14 more if indicated.
  • After: haematuria cleared? LUTS → assess. PSA only once infection resolved.

7Refer

  • Recurrent UTI (≥2 in 6 months or ≥3 in 12) → treat on culture; refer to urology or seek specialist advice (NG112).
  • ≥45, visible haematuria persisting or recurring after successful UTI treatment, or ≥60 with unexplained non-visible haematuria + dysuria or raised WCC → Suspected cancer pathway (NG12). ≥60, recurrent or persistent unexplained UTI → consider non-urgent referral.
  • Urology: same-organism relapse, stones, obstruction, prostatitis >3 months. Sexual health: urethritis, positive NAAT.

8Never

  • The women's 3-day course, back-up prescription or dipstick-only diagnosis.
  • Antibiotics before an MSU, or a negative dipstick ending the assessment.
  • Nitrofurantoin for a febrile man, the kidney or the prostate.
  • Label scrotal pain epididymo-orchitis without excluding torsion.
  • Long-term prophylaxis without specialist advice; PSA during infection.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Confused, very drowsy, breathless, mottled or blotchy skin, or you stop passing urine — call 999. Can't pass urine with a painful swollen tummy, or sudden severe pain in a testicle — go to A&E now.”   Same-day “High temperature, shivering, back or side pain, pain between your legs or back passage, or being sick and can't keep tablets down — contact us or 111 the same day.”   48 hours: “Not better in two days, or worse — come back. Take every tablet.”   Ciprofloxacin: “Tendon pain, pins and needles, mood or sleep changes — stop, contact us straight away. Sudden severe chest, tummy or back pain, racing heartbeat or sudden breathlessness — call 999 or go to A&E.”
