Chronic kidney disease in adults

For: adults with possible or confirmed CKD in primary care. Not for: children; acute kidney injury (exclude first — block 0); G5, dialysis or transplant care, which is individualised under nephrology.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG203KDIGO 2024TA1075 dapagliflozinTA877 finerenoneNG12
Core rule: exclude AKI first, confirm chronicity over ≥90 days, stage on eGFR and ACR, deploy the big four — and never stop an ACE inhibitor or ARB for the expected eGFR fall of up to 25%.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • AKI (creatinine rise ≥26.5 µmol/L in 48 h or ≥1.5× baseline in 7 days) → Urgent assessment: volume, sepsis, obstruction, drugs; withhold metformin; review NSAIDs, ACEi/ARB, diuretics.
  • Oliguria/anuria, retention, hypotension or sepsis → Same-day hospital   K⁺ ≥6.0 → Same-day ECG, urgent repeat   K⁺ ≥6.5 or ECG change → 999
  • Uraemic encephalopathy, pericarditis (rub) or severe pulmonary oedema → 999   BP ≥180/120 with acute end-organ features → Same-day emergency assessment
  • Stable new eGFR <15, G4 unknown to nephrology, unexplained eGFR fall >15 in 3 months, nephrotic syndrome (ACR >220, low albumin, oedema) or new blood + protein → Urgent renal, within days
  • Unexplained visible haematuria aged 45+ → Urgent suspected-cancer referral (NG12) before CKD work-up. Over 60, anaemia, high calcium → electrophoresis, light chains.

1Confirm — one low eGFR is not CKD

eGFR <60 twice ≥90 days apart, or markers (albuminuria, structural, hereditary) for >3 months. New low eGFR → repeat within 2 weeks (exclude AKI); record “possible CKD”.

GG1–2 ≥60*G3a 45–59G3b 30–44G4 15–29G5 <15
AACR mg/mmol: A1 <3 · A2 3–30 · A3 >30. Risk rises with both.

*CKD only with another marker. Early-morning ACR; repeat if 3–70, not ≥70. Muscle mass distorts eGFR → consider cystatin C.

2Check — baseline, then the cause

☐ U&E ☐ FBC ☐ ACR ☐ HbA1c ☐ Lipids ☐ Urine dip for blood. G3: + bicarbonate, Ca, phosphate, PTH. G4–5: + vitamin D, ferritin, TSAT.

Cause: obstruction, NSAIDs, lithium, PPIs, myeloma, PKD, vasculitis. USS if accelerated decline, visible or persistent haematuria, obstructive symptoms, PKD family history, eGFR <30.

3Decide — who gets an ACEi or ARB

  • Offer ACEi/ARB at maximum tolerated dose: ACR ≥70 any BP; ACR >30 + hypertension; ACR ≥3 + diabetes.
  • ACR ≤30, no diabetes → hypertension protocol (NICE).
  • Everyone: salt <5 g/day, 150 min/week activity, stop smoking, no routine protein restriction; flu, COVID, pneumococcal (G4–5).

4Target — individualise if frail or postural

ACR <70BP <140/90 (systolic 120–139)
ACR ≥70BP <130/80 (systolic 120–129)
DiabetesHbA1c 53–58 mmol/mol; less tight if frail or hypo-prone

5Treat — the big four

1 · ACEi / ARBACEi first (e.g. ramipril); ARB (e.g. losartan) if cough.
2 · SGLT2 inhibitorDapagliflozin 10 mg OD: eGFR 20 to <45; or 45–90 with ACR ≥22.6 or T2DM.
3 · StatinOffer atorvastatin 20 mg unless contraindicated or on lipid therapy.
4 · FinerenoneT2DM, stage 3–4, ACR ≥3, eGFR ≥25, on 1 + 2. Shared care.
  • Before every step: U&E, K⁺, stop NSAIDs. SGLT2i: not below eGFR 20; pause in acute illness, fasting, surgery; rare euglycaemic DKA.
  • Renal gates: metformin reduce at eGFR 30–44, stop <30; nitrofurantoin avoid <30; DOACs by CrCl. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • ACEi/ARB or diuretic change: U&E at 1–2 weeks; eGFR fall ≤25% or creatinine rise ≤30% expected — continue; more → seek a cause.
  • K⁺ 5.5–5.9: diet, review trimethoprim, spironolactone, NSAIDs; recheck. Hb ≤110 g/L → iron, B12, folate, blood loss.
  • Annually, more often as G and A rise: eGFR trend, ACR, BP, KFRE from G3, medicines, sick-day rules.

7Refer — routine nephrology

  • KFRE 5-year risk >5%; ACR ≥70 (unless treated diabetic nephropathy); ACR >30 + persistent haematuria.
  • Sustained eGFR fall ≥25% with category change in 12 months, or ≥15 mL/min/year; BP uncontrolled on ≥4 agents.
  • Keep in primary care: G1–G3a, stable, ACR <70, known cause, BP controlled, no complications.

8Never

  • A CKD diagnosis from one eGFR, or staging without an ACR.
  • Stopping an ACEi/ARB for the expected eGFR fall (≤25%) or creatinine rise (≤30%).
  • An ACE inhibitor and an ARB together.
  • NSAIDs in G3 and above, including over the counter — paracetamol is fine.
  • Metformin, nitrofurantoin or gadolinium contrast at eGFR <30.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden breathlessness or chest pain, confusion or reduced consciousness, no urine for 12+ hours, severe swelling, or muscle weakness with a high potassium — call 999.”   Same-day “Much less urine, very high BP with symptoms, new leg swelling, new blood in the urine, or any dehydrating illness in stage 4–5 — contact us today.”   Sick-day card (in writing): “Vomiting, diarrhoea or can't keep fluids down — ring us; we'll tell you which tablets to pause (usually metformin and the ‘flozin’) and when to restart.”   Painkillers: “No ibuprofen or naproxen-type drugs, including over the counter. Paracetamol is fine.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
