Female pattern hair loss

For: women with gradual thinning over the crown and central parting. Not for: patchy loss (alopecia areata — use the BAD living guideline) or suspected scarring alopecia or tinea capitis (refer, blocks 0 and 7).

Core rule: look at the scalp and check for virilisation before anything else — scarring or virilisation changes the destination, so never start minoxidil and wait.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Scarring signs (lost follicle openings, shiny skin, perifollicular redness or scale, pustules, sore or itchy scalp; receding frontal band with eyebrow loss) → Urgent: expedited dermatology (local pathway, not a suspected cancer referral); don't wait for bloods or a trial.
  • Rapid or late-onset virilisation (deepening voice, clitoromegaly, more muscle bulk, temple balding, fast-worsening hirsutism) or marked androgen excess → Urgent endocrine and/or gynaecology. Examine abdomen and pelvis.
  • Severe virilisation with systemic features, rapidly progressive virilisation, or suspected tumour with a mass or acute symptoms → Same-day medical assessment
  • Ascites and/or pelvic or abdominal mass (not obviously fibroids) → Suspected cancer pathway referral to gynaecological cancer service (NG12 1.5.1).
  • Suspected tinea capitis with kerion, pain, pustules or scarring risk → Urgent specialist advice; send mycology; oral, not topical-only, antifungal.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis, not a blood test

PictureClues
Pattern lossGradual thinning over years; widening central parting, frontal hairline kept, normal scalp
Telogen effluviumShedding over weeks, ~2–4 months after a trigger; can coexist. Beyond 6–9 months → reassess
MimicsAlopecia areata: patches, exclamation-mark hairs, nail pitting. Traction: margins, tight styles

Male-type temple recession → look for androgen excess.

2Check — test selectively

☐ Trigger 2–6 months before, periods, acne, hirsutism, styling, diet, drugs  ☐ Typical, normal scalp: often no bloods  ☐ Diffuse shedding, fatigue, diet risk, heavy periods: FBC, ferritin ± TFTs  ☐ Only if hyperandrogenism, irregular periods or virilisation: testosterone + SHBG (FAI)  ☐ Photos

Combined pill distorts androgens — don't stop it without specialist discussion and other contraception.

3Decide — treat what adds to it, then the hair

  • Confirmed iron deficiency → treat, find the cause (periods, diet, coeliac serology). Thyroid disease → treat.
  • PCOS → manage in its own right.
  • Compatible diagnosis, no red flags → offer topical minoxidil, usually bought OTC.
  • Everyone: name the distress, ask about mood; camouflage. Big impact → talking therapy.

4Target — slow and stabilise, not restore

5% foamRegrowth may take 12–24 weeks; none by 24 → stop
2% solutionNo improvement after a year → stop. Gains last only while used

5Treat

Step 1Correct iron, thyroid, drugs, styling tension
Step 2 · OTCRegaine for Women 2% solution 1 ml twice daily or 5% foam 1 g once daily; licensed 18–65
Step 3 · PCOSCombined pill first line; antiandrogens limited role
Step 4 · specialistSpironolactone, low-dose oral minoxidil: off-label, dermatology-initiated
  • No minoxidil if diagnosis uncertain, sudden or patchy loss, inflamed scalp, possible scarring, after childbirth, unknown cause, untreated severe telogen effluvium.
  • Scalp irritation, facial hair (~14%), early shed. Not in pregnancy or breastfeeding. Supply: solution in Drug Tariff Part XVIIIA — no FP10; GM: foam do-not-prescribe. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Week 0: photos, bloods, plan. 2–6 weeks: results; reassure about early shed.
  • ~6 months: compare photos. 12 months: stable → continue indefinitely; progressing → check use, repeat ferritin if needed, refer.
  • On oral minoxidil: BP, pulse; ask about swelling, palpitations, dizziness. Doses stay with the prescriber.

7Refer — routine dermatology

  • Diagnostic doubt; progressing despite 6–12 months of proper minoxidil; antiandrogens or oral minoxidil considered; major psychological impact.
  • Extensive or treatment-resistant alopecia areata (BAD). PCOS needing fertility or difficult-hyperandrogenism input → gynae/endocrine.

8Never

  • Start minoxidil and wait when there are scarring features or virilisation.
  • A full hormone panel on everyone while skipping the scalp examination.
  • Promise NHS-prescribed minoxidil.
  • Iron to reach an arbitrary ferritin target for hair growth.
  • Start cyproterone, flutamide, bicalutamide, finasteride or dutasteride in primary care (finasteride 1 mg is contraindicated in women).

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Chest pain while using minoxidil — call 999.”   Same-day “Fast heartbeat, faintness, marked dizziness, sudden weight gain or swelling — stop minoxidil and contact us. New scalp pain or redness, patches, eyebrow loss, deepening voice or fast-growing body hair — contact us.”   Pregnancy: “Don't use minoxidil if pregnant or breastfeeding.”   Minoxidil: “It takes six months to a year, can cause extra shedding at first, and only works while you use it.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Scarring and virilisation excluded (scalp seen) Pattern confirmed clinically; mimics named Bloods only as indicated; pregnancy and contraception checked Minoxidil explained; photos and review booked Mood asked; safety-net given and documented
