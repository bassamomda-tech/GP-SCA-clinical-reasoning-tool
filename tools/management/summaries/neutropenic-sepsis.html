Suspected neutropenic sepsis

For: adults and children who are unwell on, or recently off, systemic anticancer treatment or immunosuppressants — GP, telephone and out-of-hours. Not for: hospital management (CG151 is applied by the receiving unit). Pregnant or recently pregnant: assess sepsis under NICE NG255, still refer immediately.

Core rule: unwell + anticancer treatment within 30 days (or immunosuppressants) = suspected neutropenic sepsis → refer immediately. Act on the story, not the temperature or a community FBC; never oral antibiotics and review.

0Act now — every suspected case needs immediate acute assessment; there is no routine referral

  • Shock or hypotension, new confusion or reduced consciousness, severe breathlessness or hypoxia, peri-arrest, rapid deterioration, NEWS2 high, or unable to travel safely → 999 ED with pre-alert; tell the hotline afterwards.
  • Unwell or febrile, stable, on or within 30 days of systemic anticancer treatment → Immediately ring the 24-hour acute oncology / chemotherapy hotline (number on the alert card) → named unit or ED, per the local pathway. Say “suspected neutropenic sepsis”.
  • Child on cancer treatment, any fever or unwellness → Immediately principal treatment centre / shared-care open access; 999 if shocked, drowsy or struggling to breathe.
  • Normal temperature but “feel awful” → still same-hour acute oncology assessment. On the phone → go now via hotline or 999.
  • While waiting: ABCDE, oxygen if hypoxic, full observations (NEWS2 in ≥16s) — do not delay transfer for venous access, bloods or IV treatment.

Beyond 30 days, use judgement if still likely neutropenic (haematology, transplant, CAR-T) — when in doubt, ring the hotline.

2Check — ask in the first minute of every unwell call

☐ Cancer treatment or immunity-lowering drug; date of last dose  ☐ Highest temperature, and when  ☐ Steroids (incl. dexamethasone antiemetics), paracetamol or NSAIDs today  ☐ Rigors or shivering after line use, confusion, breathlessness, fewer wees  ☐ Alert card and hotline number

Don't miss: hypothermia, rigors, lethargy, falls or new confusion may be the only sign; no focus is not reassurance.

3Decide — not only chemotherapy

  • At risk: cytotoxic chemo, some targeted agents, haematological cancers, transplant, CAR-T, extensive marrow radiotherapy. Can occur at any point in a cycle.
  • Immunotherapy: colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, hypophysitis or diabetes can mimic or co-exist with infection, even months after the last dose → same-day hotline (immediate if unstable).
  • Steroids for immune toxicity only on specialist advice — unless adrenal crisis: emergency hydrocortisone now.

5Treat — recognition plus transfer; antibiotics are a hospital treatment

HospitalEmpiric IV antibiotics immediately; oral only after risk scoring
  • Bloods only if immediately available without delaying transfer. Central line: note it; do not access or flush it.
  • Prophylaxis (e.g. ciprofloxacin): don't stop it; fever on it still needs immediate referral. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — after discharge

  • Read the discharge letter: G-CSF or regimen changes, bloods delegated to primary care. Alert card current; hotline number and regimen in the GP record.

7Refer — non-cancer neutropenia: no alert card

  • Carbimazole / propylthiouracil + sore throat, mouth ulcers, fever or malaise → stop it, Same-day FBC with neutrophils; unwell → Immediate ED / medical take (999 if shocked).
  • Clozapine + fever, sore throat or flu-like illness → Same-day FBC, contact the mental health team; don't stop it if stable without advice.
  • csDMARDs + unwell → Same hour ED / medical take; well → Same-day FBC; contact the specialist team.
  • Biologics, JAK inhibitors, high-dose steroids, transplant drugs + unwell → suspect neutropenia (NG253); don't stop transplant drugs yourself.

8Never

  • Oral antibiotics and send home, or “review tomorrow to see how things go”.
  • Delay referral for a community FBC, or send for a “quick blood test” first.
  • Be reassured by a normal temperature or a low NEWS2 score.
  • Say “it's too long since the chemo” without asking the hotline.
  • Treat immunotherapy colitis as gastroenteritis, or prescribe loperamide without oncology input.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you feel feverish, shivery or just unwell — even if your temperature is normal — ring the number on your card straight away, day or night. Don't wait until morning, don't take paracetamol and wait, and don't come to the surgery first. If you are very unwell, dial 999 and tell them you're having cancer treatment.”   When sending in: “It needs antibiotics into a vein at the hospital straight away — that's why I'm not giving you tablets today. I'm calling your cancer team now.”
