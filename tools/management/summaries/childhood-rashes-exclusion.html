Childhood rashes and school exclusion

For: children with common infectious rashes, their settings, and pregnant or vulnerable contacts. Not for: the unwell febrile child without a clear exanthem (fever in children) or neonates (same-day paediatrics). Doses: BNFC.

Core rule: check the rash blanches and the child is well before naming it; then give the UKHSA exclusion period, notify on suspicion, and tell pregnant contacts to call today.

0Act now

  • Non-blanching rash with fever or unwell child → 999; IM or IV benzylpenicillin before transfer if meningococcal disease suspected (BNFC).
  • Chickenpox or scarlet fever getting worse — persistent high fever, spreading painful red skin, lethargy (invasive group A strep) → Same-day
  • Fever 5 days or more with red eyes, red cracked lips, rash, swollen hands or feet (Kawasaki); eczema herpeticum → Same-day
  • Chickenpox in a neonate or immunosuppressed child, or breathing, neurological or hydration problems → Same-day

1Confirm — exclusion periods (UKHSA)

InfectionExclusion
ChickenpoxAt least 5 days from rash and all blisters crusted
Measles*4 days from rash onset
Scarlet fever*24 hours after starting antibiotics
ImpetigoCrusted and healed, or 48 hours after antibiotics
Rubella* · mumps*5 days from rash · from swelling
ScabiesReturn after first treatment
HFMD, slapped cheek, roseola, molluscum, head liceNone

* Notifiable on suspicion.

2Check

☐ Glass test ☐ NG143 traffic light ☐ Rash pattern and prodrome ☐ Vaccine status (MMRV) ☐ Pregnant, neonatal or immunosuppressed contacts ☐ Household itch (scabies). Don't miss: measles in an unvaccinated child.

3Decide

  • Notify measles, rubella, mumps, scarlet fever, meningococcal disease, iGAS — urgent ones by telephone.
  • Well child with no-exclusion rash → back to school.

5Treat

  • Chickenpox: paracetamol, fluids, emollient; avoid ibuprofen; aciclovir not routine in healthy children.
  • Scarlet fever: phenoxymethylpenicillin for 10 days (allergy: clarithromycin or azithromycin).
  • Impetigo: hydrogen peroxide 1% cream if localised; oral flucloxacillin if widespread.
  • Scabies: permethrin 5% for the household, repeat after 7 days. Doses per BNF/BNFC and local formulary.

6Monitor — pregnant contacts

  • Chickenpox: no history → urgent VZV IgG; non-immune → oral antiviral from day 7 to day 14 after exposure.
  • Slapped cheek: parvovirus B19 IgG and IgM; risk greatest before 20 weeks.
  • Measles: health protection team — immunoglobulin within 6 days if susceptible.

7Refer

  • Pregnant contact or pregnant with a rash → GP or midwife Same-day
  • Outbreak in a setting → health protection team.

8Never

  • Name a rash before checking it blanches and the child is well.
  • Give ibuprofen in chickenpox.
  • Wait for lab confirmation before notifying measles.
  • Exclude children for slapped cheek, hand, foot and mouth or head lice.
  • Let a pregnant contact wait for her next antenatal appointment.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A rash that doesn't fade under a glass, or a floppy, drowsy child, or struggling to breathe — call 999.”   Same-day “Getting worse instead of better with chickenpox or scarlet fever — high temperature, red painful skin, not drinking — contact us today or 111.”   Contacts: “Anyone pregnant who has been in contact should ring their GP or midwife today.”
