Acute sinusitis and chronic rhinosinusitis

For: adults and children with acute sinusitis in primary care, and recognising chronic rhinosinusitis (CRS) and nasal polyps. Not for: orbital, intracranial or septic complications (hospital), or children's doses (NG79 table / BNFC). Facial pain without nasal symptoms → think migraine or tension-type headache.

Core rule: look at the eyes, forehead, neck and neurology before you talk about antibiotics — then let the duration decide: around 10 days or less and well → no antibiotic; ≥12 weeks or persistently one-sided is a different disease.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Orbit: periorbital oedema or cellulitis, displaced eyeball, double vision, ophthalmoplegia, reduced acuity (child: painful, red or spreading eyelid swelling) → Immediate hospital
  • Brain: swelling over the frontal bone, severe frontal headache, signs of meningitis or focal neurology → Immediate hospital
  • Severe systemic infection (NEWS2 in adults) → Immediate hospital  Reduced consciousness, seizure, severe sepsis, rapidly progressive visual or neurological loss, or unsafe to transfer → 999
  • Invasive fungal risk (severe immunosuppression, neutropenia, uncontrolled diabetes or ketoacidosis) with facial pain or numbness, black eschar, eye signs or rapid progression → Emergency hospital
  • Persistent one-sided obstruction or discharge, blood, crusting, cacosmia, polyp or mass, facial swelling → Urgent ENT not repeated treatment.

1Confirm — clinical diagnosis; duration drives every decision

Nasal blockage or discharge plus facial pain/pressure and/or reduced smell (children: often cough). Record the duration in days. No swabs, X-ray or CT in uncomplicated acute disease.

The 10-day rule is a prescribing framework, not proof of bacterial infection. ≥12 weeks → suspect CRS; confirmed by endoscopy or CT, usually via ENT.

2Check — every consultation

☐ Temperature, pulse, RR, BP  ☐ Eyes: lids, position, movements, acuity  ☐ Forehead, neck, neurology  ☐ Rhinoscopy: polyps, crusting, mass  ☐ Upper teeth  ☐ Pregnancy, comorbidity

Don't miss: dental source; migraine (“sinus headache”); GCA in older adults (same-day pathway); foreign body in a child with one-sided foul discharge.

3Decide — NICE NG79

  • Around 10 days or less, otherwise well → do not offer an antibiotic; self-care.
  • Around 10 days+, no improvement → consider no antibiotic or a back-up antibiotic; consider a high-dose nasal steroid for 14 days (age ≥12, off-label, record consent).
  • Systemically very unwell, signs of a more serious illness, or high risk of complications → offer an immediate antibiotic (hospital if red flags).
  • High-risk comorbidity (severe immunosuppression, uncontrolled diabetes, cystic fibrosis, major organ disease) → lower threshold to see, treat, investigate or admit.
  • Self-care: paracetamol or ibuprofen; usually lasts 2–3 weeks. Not enough evidence for saline or decongestants (decongestant a few days only).

5Treat — antibiotic, adults ≥18 (NG79, 5 days)

First choicePhenoxymethylpenicillin 500 mg four times a day
Penicillin allergyDoxycycline 200 mg day 1, then 100 mg daily, or clarithromycin 500 mg twice a day. Pregnancy: erythromycin
Very unwell / high riskCo-amoxiclav 500/125 mg three times a day; is hospital better?
Worse after 2–3 daysReassess first (complication? dental?), then co-amoxiclav; already used or allergic → microbiology
  • Nasal steroid, 14 days: mometasone 200 micrograms or fluticasone 110 micrograms twice a day — not the allergic-rhinitis dose; check cumulative steroid exposure.
  • Children: NG79 / BNFC doses. Clarithromycin: check interactions (statins, DOACs, QT). Doses per BNF/BNFC and local GMMMG formulary.

6Monitor — review points

  • Acute, not better by ~3 weeks → review the diagnosis: dental, CRS, one-sided pathology, headache.
  • CRS: daily saline irrigation + steroid spray, technique taught (aim away from septum); reassess at ~6–12 weeks.
  • Polyps: ask about asthma and NSAID sensitivity. Oral steroids, long-term antibiotics and dupilumab are specialist decisions.

7Refer — urgent ones are in block 0

  • Routine ENT: CRS uncontrolled after a proper trial; bilateral polyps; recurrent acute episodes; significant smell loss.
  • Suspected sinonasal tumour: no NG12 criterion → urgent ENT per local pathway.
  • Dental source (upper molar pain, foul taste, recent extraction) → dentist; antibiotics alone will not fix it.

8Never

  • A back-up antibiotic for an eye or neurological red flag.
  • Antibiotics at day 3 for coloured discharge alone.
  • Repeated antibiotics for ≥12-week symptoms instead of saline + steroid.
  • Persistent one-sided, bloody or crusting symptoms labelled as sinusitis.
  • Doxycycline in pregnancy or under 12; a nasal steroid course under 12.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

A&E now “Swelling or redness around your eye, double vision or eyesight changes, a very bad headache, swelling on your forehead, a stiff neck, confusion or feeling very unwell — go to A&E straight away.”   999 “Very drowsy or hard to wake, a fit, or eyesight getting worse quickly — call 999.”   Back-up script: “Only collect it if you're getting significantly worse, or no better after 7 days. If you feel very unwell, don't just start the tablets — get seen.”   Course: “It usually lasts 2–3 weeks; getting rapidly worse — contact us the same day; not better after 3 weeks — come back.”
