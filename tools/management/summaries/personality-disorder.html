Personality disorders (borderline and antisocial)

For: adults with borderline (emotionally unstable) or antisocial personality disorder in primary care. Not for: diagnosis (specialist services) or under-18s (refer to CAMHS); acute self-harm follows block 0.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG78NICE CG77NICE QS88NICE NG225
Core rule: respond to crisis with a risk assessment and a plan, not a new prescription: no drugs for the core features, small quantities of anything you prescribe, and a consistent clinician.

0Act now

  • Recent overdose (any amount) or serious self-injury → 999 or Same-day emergency department
  • Suicidal thoughts with plan or intent, escalating self-harm, psychotic symptoms, credible threat to others → Same-day crisis team; police if immediate danger.
  • Rising distress, loss of support, more frequent self-harm → Urgent care coordinator or community mental health team.
  • Child at risk, domestic abuse, adult at risk → local safeguarding procedures, Same-day if immediate risk.

1Confirm — recognise the pattern

Borderline: unstable relationships and self-image, intense changeable emotions, impulsivity, fear of abandonment, recurrent self-harm. Antisocial: persistent disregard for others' rights, aggression, lack of remorse, conduct disorder before age 15. Diagnosis by specialist services.

2Check — every contact

☐ Suicide and self-harm, including overdose  ☐ Risk to others  ☐ Substance use  ☐ Children in the household  ☐ Crisis plan and care coordinator  ☐ Medicines at home. Don't use risk scales to predict suicide.

3Decide

  • Known to services → follow the crisis plan; contact the care coordinator.
  • High or increasing distress or risk, no improvement, or wants more help → refer to the community mental health team.
  • Coexisting depression, PTSD, ADHD, substance misuse → treat under their own NICE guidance.

4Treat

Core featuresNo drug treatment; no antipsychotics medium or long term
CrisisSedative no longer than 1 week, low overdose toxicity (for example promethazine 25 mg at night)
TherapyDBT (recurrent self-harm), MBT or other structured therapy via CMHT
AntisocialGroup cognitive and behavioural programmes; no routine drugs for aggression
  • Safer prescribing: weekly quantities; avoid tricyclics, opioids, benzodiazepines and Z-drugs; one prescriber. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

5Monitor

  • Planned reviews (for example every 2 to 4 weeks while unstable) with a named clinician.
  • Annually: physical health check, crisis plan, medicines review.

6Refer — non-emergency

  • Community mental health team for assessment and structured therapy.
  • Pregnancy → perinatal mental health. Under 18 → CAMHS.
  • Substance misuse, forensic or probation services as needed.

7Never

  • Start a benzodiazepine or long-term antipsychotic for emotional instability.
  • Give large quantities of any drug, or tricyclics, to someone at risk of overdose.
  • Exclude someone from a service because of the diagnosis or self-harm.
  • Rely on a risk scale to decide who is safe.
  • Forget the children in the household.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you might act on thoughts of ending your life, call 999 or go to the emergency department. Any overdose, go straight away.”   Same day “In a crisis, use your crisis line, NHS 111 (mental health option), or contact us. Samaritans: 116 123.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
