Osteoporosis and fracture risk

For: adults — case-finding, fracture-risk assessment and treatment in primary care. Not for: under 30s or suspected rare bone disease (seek specialist advice), pregnancy-associated or complex glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis (specialist).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NOGG 2024NICE NG259 (July 2026)NICE TA464 · TA204 · TA791NG12
Core rule: every fragility fracture is a treatment opportunity — run FRAX → NOGG, teach the alendronate ritual, run falls work alongside, and never just stop denosumab.

0Act now — the fracture you must not miss

  • Sudden severe back pain, height loss >4 cm or new kyphosis → vertebral fracture: Same-day review, spine X-ray or VFA; may justify treatment without waiting for DXA.
  • Aged ≥60, persistent bone (especially back) pain or unexplained fracture → FBC, calcium, ESR/PV, protein electrophoresis, free light chains together; suggests myeloma → Suspected cancer pathway
  • New dysphagia on a bisphosphonate → stop the drug → Suspected cancer pathway (NG12) — investigate, don't just switch.
  • New thigh or groin ache on long-term treatment → Same-day review, X-ray both femurs; atypical fracture → stop, orthopaedics and endocrine.
  • Fragility fracture aged ≥50 → fracture liaison service (none locally → you assess and treat). Atypical site, focal bone pain, anaemia, high calcium, renal dysfunction, weight loss → investigate for myeloma, malignancy, metabolic bone disease.

1Confirm — case-find, then FRAX → NOGG → DXA

Consider assessing all women ≥65, men ≥75. ≥50 or postmenopausal with fragility fracture or current/frequent steroids → assess.

FRAX without BMD first (NG259: FRAX or QFracture 40–90, QFracture 30–39; one tool throughout; never plot QFracture onto NOGG). Aged ≥30 with hip/vertebral fracture, major fracture in 2 years or ≥2 fractures → DXA directly.

T ≤−2.5 = osteoporosisT −1 to −2.5: treat only if FRAX says so
Young with Z-score ≤−2 → hunt secondary causes and refer

2Check — secondary causes, before or with treatment

☐ FBC, ESR or CRP  ☐ U&E/creatinine, LFTs  ☐ Bone profile  ☐ TFTs  ☐ Vitamin D  ☐ Coeliac serology  ☐ Men: testosterone, LH, FSH  ☐ PTH if calcium abnormal  ☐ Dental review

Don't miss: myeloma, male hypogonadism, coeliac disease, osteomalacia.

3Decide — act by NOGG band

  • Low → lifestyle; reassess in 5 years. Intermediate → DXA, re-enter T-score, recheck NOGG.
  • High → treat (DXA as baseline must not delay it). Very high → specialist for anabolic treatment.
  • Clearly high risk (older adult's fragility fracture, vertebral fracture, prednisolone ≥7.5 mg/day ≥3 months) → may treat without DXA.

4Target — foundations, co-treatment not garnish

FallsAssess; offer strength-and-balance programme
Calcium · vit D700–1200 mg/day, diet first · replace if <25 nmol/L
LifestyleNo smoking; alcohol ≤14 units/week; BMI <18.5 → find cause

5Treat — the ritual is the treatment

1st · almost everyoneAlendronate 70 mg weekly: fasting, full glass of water, upright, nothing for ≥30 min. Or risedronate 35 mg weekly.
2nd · oral unsuitableIV zoledronate 5 mg yearly. eGFR ≥35 and calcium before each; flu-like reaction → paracetamol.
3rd · bisphosphonates unsuitableDenosumab 60 mg SC 6-monthly — not just for eGFR <35. Book ahead; flag like an anticoagulant.
Very high risk · specialistRomosozumab (not after MI or stroke) or teriparatide, then antiresorptive.
  • Before every step: renal function, calcium, vitamin D (correct first), dental check.
  • Reflux: check the ritual → risedronate → zoledronate. Early menopause or <60 with symptoms → HRT. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • 3–6 months, then annually: adherence, technique, side effects.
  • Calcium, vitamin D, renal: annually and before every IV or SC dose.
  • Height annually: new >2 cm loss or thoracic pain → spine imaging; new vertebral fracture → escalate.
  • ~5 years oral, ~3 years zoledronate → continue, pause or switch (prior hip/vertebral fracture or ongoing steroids favour continuing). Pause → reassess at 18 months–3 years.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • T ≤−3.5, multiple vertebral fractures, fracture on verified treatment → anabolic assessment.
  • Osteoporosis <50, Z ≤−2, unexplained in a man → endocrinology.
  • eGFR <30–35, atypical femoral fracture, jaw osteonecrosis, any denosumab stop or switch.

8Never

  • Stop or delay denosumab without a specialist-guided bisphosphonate follow-on — rebound vertebral fractures follow within months.
  • File an incidental vertebral fracture, or delay treatment in clear high risk while waiting for DXA.
  • Give zoledronate or denosumab before correcting calcium and vitamin D.
  • Switch tablets for new dysphagia without investigating it.
  • Call it treatment failure before checking adherence and the ritual.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “Sudden severe back pain, or losing height — that can be a spine fracture; contact us the same day.”   Thigh: “A new ache in the thigh or groin that won't settle — contact us the same day; we X-ray it.”   Swallowing: “Food sticking or pain on swallowing on the weekly tablet — stop it and contact us the same day.”   Denosumab: “Due every 6 months — we book it ahead; if a dose will be missed or stopped, ring us first.” Tell the dentist; jaw pain or a non-healing socket → review.
