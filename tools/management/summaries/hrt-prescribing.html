Menopause and HRT

For: adults with menopausal symptoms; GP-led diagnosis and standard HRT. Not for: POI, breast cancer requesting HRT, previous VTE or thrombophilia — specialist (block 7).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG23 (Apr 2026)NICE NG12 (Apr 2026)BMS bleeding 2024MHRA 2020FSRH 2023
Core rule: diagnose clinically from 45, never start HRT on top of unexplained bleeding, and always give a progestogen to a woman with a uterus on systemic oestrogen.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Unexplained postmenopausal bleeding not attributable to HRT: 55+ → Urgent suspected cancer pathway; under 55 → consider one. No systemic HRT until assessed.
  • Unexplained breast lump aged 30+, or one-nipple change aged 50+ → Urgent suspected cancer pathway; other new breast changes → Urgent breast clinic. No HRT until assessed.
  • On HRT: one painful swollen leg, chest pain with breathlessness, FAST signs → stop HRT, 999
  • Bleeding on HRT starting >6 months after starting or >3 months after a change; heavy, prolonged or persistent; or after amenorrhoea on continuous combined → Urgent TVUSS ≤6 weeks
  • Bleeding on HRT with one major (BMI ≥40, Lynch, Cowden) or three minor (BMI 30–39, diabetes, PCOS) risk factors → Urgent suspected cancer pathway

1Confirm — clinical from 45

Menopause = 12 months' amenorrhoea (average age 51). Perimenopause = symptoms with a cycle change.

FSH unreliable on combined hormonal contraception or high-dose progestogen. Score symptoms (Greene or MRS) at baseline.

2Check — before prescribing

☐ BP, BMI ☐ Bleeding ☐ Breasts, screening ☐ Breast cancer and VTE history ☐ Liver, arterial disease, migraine ☐ Uterus (subtotal hysterectomy, ablation) ☐ Contraception ☐ TSH, FBC/ferritin if features

Don't miss: thyroid, iron deficiency; bone pain, weight loss, focal neurology → investigate first.

3Decide — who, route, counselling

  • Do not start routinely: current or past breast or other oestrogen-dependent cancer, undiagnosed bleeding, untreated hyperplasia, active liver disease, current VTE or recent arterial thrombosis, pregnancy → investigate or refer; non-hormonal options.
  • Route: consider transdermal rather than oral if VTE risk is raised, including BMI >30.
  • Counsel in absolute numbers (MHRA, 5 years from ~50): about 5 extra breast cancers per 1000 oestrogen-only, 14 sequential, 20 continuous combined. Not for CVD prevention.
  • Lifestyle: exercise, alcohol ≤14 units/week, stop smoking; offer CBT alongside or instead of HRT.

5Treat — uterus? peri or post?

Uterus, LMP <12 monthsSequential: + Utrogestan 200 mg nocte days 15–26, or Mirena
Uterus, ≥12 monthsContinuous combined: + Utrogestan 100 mg nocte daily, or Mirena
Vaginal onlyLocal oestrogen, no progestogen routinely; any age, as long as needed
  • Start low: patch 25–50 micrograms twice weekly, Oestrogel 2 pumps or estradiol 1 mg. Raise oestrogen → re-match progestogen. Doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • Mirena 52 mg protects the endometrium up to 5 years. Utrogestan: not in soya or peanut allergy.
  • HRT unsuitable: CBT; fezolinetant 45 mg daily (LFTs baseline, monthly for 3 months). No paroxetine or fluoxetine with tamoxifen.

6Monitor

  • 3 months after any change: symptoms, side effects, BP, bleeding → titrate. Then annually; no arbitrary stop date.
  • Early light bleeding (≤6 months, or ≤3 months after a change): check adherence, regimen, pregnancy, cervical screening → review ~4-weekly, up to 6 months.
  • Not better? Adherence, absorption, dose, route, progestogen, mimics (TFTs, ferritin). Stopping → taper.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Menopause clinic: POI, under 45, previous VTE, failed optimised HRT, breast cancer wanting HRT.
  • Haematology: confirmed or high-risk thrombophilia — not every VTE history.

8Never

  • Unopposed systemic oestrogen with a uterus, or drop the progestogen for intolerance — switch type or route.
  • Start HRT before unexplained bleeding or breast symptoms are assessed.
  • FSH to diagnose menopause aged 45+.
  • Treat HRT as contraception — needed 2 years after LMP if under 50, 1 year if 50+.
  • Refuse HRT outright for previous VTE — refer for specialist assessment.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Pain and swelling in one leg, chest pain with breathlessness, face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech — stop HRT, call 999.” Same day “New breast lump, severe unexplained headache, severe one-sided pelvic pain — contact us today.” Bleeding: “After 6 months, or 3 months after a change, heavy or persistent, or after stopping — tell us promptly.” Side effects: “Don't stop it yourself — we'll adjust it.”
