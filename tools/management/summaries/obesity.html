Obesity in adults

For: adults with overweight or obesity, including after bariatric surgery. Not for: children, or weight management in pregnancy (maternity care).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG246TA875 semaglutideTA1026 tirzepatideGMMMG incretin guidanceBOMSS
Core rule: ask permission, classify with ethnicity-adjusted thresholds, ask about eating behaviour early, and use drugs or surgery only within NICE and local criteria — weight commonly returns when GLP-1 drugs stop.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Post-bariatric: severe or persistent abdominal pain (internal hernia after Roux-en-Y), vomiting, can't keep fluids down, tachycardia, fever, GI bleed → A&E now · 999 if collapse
  • Post-bariatric prolonged vomiting, poor intake or suspected thiamine deficiency → Urgent thiamine before any glucose.
  • GLP-1 drug: severe abdominal pain that does not settle, maybe into the back → Same-day assessment  Pregnant → Stop it today
  • Binge-eating or another eating disorder suspected → no unsupervised restrictive dieting; arrange psychological or eating-disorder support alongside weight care.

1Confirm — ask permission first; say “weight” and “BMI”

BMI below 35: waist-to-height ratio ≥0.5 = increased health risk; do not use it to classify further at BMI ≥35. BMI class alone does not establish drug eligibility.

2Check — comorbidity is the reason to treat

☐ HbA1c  ☐ BP  ☐ Lipids, QRISK3  ☐ FIB-4 (MASLD) where indicated  ☐ OSA: Epworth or STOP-BANG → local sleep pathway  ☐ Eating behaviour, loss of control, mood  ☐ Weight-promoting medicines

TFTs, Cushing's, PCOS, hypothalamic causes only if history or examination suggests.

3Decide — the foundation for everyone

  • Diet: deficit of about 600 kcal/day; total diet replacement only via an NHS or specialist pathway.
  • Activity: ≥150 min/week moderate plus resistance work. Behaviour: refer to a structured Tier 2 programme.
  • Medicines only after diet, activity and behavioural approaches have been started and evaluated.

5Treat — adjuncts to lifestyle, never replacements

Semaglutide 2.4 mg weeklyTA875, in a specialist weight service: BMI ≥35, or 30–34.9 meeting referral criteria, + ≥1 weight-related comorbidity
TirzepatideTA1026: BMI ≥35 + ≥1 comorbidity. Only within the NHS England / GM pathway — check GMMMG criteria first
Orlistat 120 mg TDSWith meals; omit if meal missed or fat-free. Multivitamin ≥2 h apart
Bariatric surgeryBMI ≥40, or ≥35 + significant comorbidity. T2DM <10 years: offer expedited assessment at ≥35; consider at 30–34.9
  • GLP-1: nausea (titrate slowly), gallstones, rare pancreatitis, pause peri-operatively; weight returns on stopping → offer maintenance support.
  • Pregnancy: stop semaglutide ≥2 months, tirzepatide ≥1 month before a planned pregnancy. Tirzepatide may reduce oral contraception after starting and dose rises → non-oral or barrier method.
  • Lower BMI thresholds for high-risk ethnic groups. Orlistat: ciclosporin, levothyroxine, warfarin interactions. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Annually (more often if abnormal): HbA1c, BP, lipids/QRISK3, FIB-4 — each to its own target.
  • Drugs: 5% rule, side effects, contraception, funding, maintenance plan.
  • Post-bariatric: bariatric service ≥2 years, then lifelong annual shared care; micronutrients per BOMSS/specialist protocol. Watch for dumping, hypoglycaemia.

7Refer — routine

  • Tier 3 MDT: complex obesity, drug initiation, pre-bariatric optimisation.
  • Endocrinology: suspected Cushing's or hypothalamic cause.
  • Mental health / eating-disorder service: binge-eating, suspected eating disorder, significant depression.

8Never

  • Stop or switch antipsychotics, insulin or antiseizure drugs without the prescriber.
  • Unsupervised restrictive dieting when an eating disorder is suspected.
  • Promise a GLP-1 drug — NICE eligibility does not mean immediate local access.
  • Improvise post-bariatric micronutrient regimens.
  • Frame weight regain as failure — re-engage every time.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

A&E · 999 After surgery: “Severe or persistent tummy pain, vomiting or can't keep fluids down, fast heartbeat, fever or bleeding — A&E straight away, 999 if you collapse; tell them about your surgery.”   Same-day GLP-1: “Tummy pain that doesn't go away, maybe into your back — urgent help the same day.”   Pregnancy: “If you become pregnant, stop and tell us.”   Online supply: “Please don't buy these injections from unregulated websites.”
