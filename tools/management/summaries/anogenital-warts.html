Anogenital warts

For: adults (16 and over) with suspected uncomplicated external anogenital warts. Not for: a child (safeguarding pathway, block 0), internal vaginal, cervical, anal or urethral lesions (sexual health), or atypical lesions (specialist assessment and biopsy).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BASHH 2024UKHSA Green Book ch.18aNICE NG12 (Apr 2026)NICE CG89SPS podophyllotoxin PGD
Core rule: treat the warts but screen the patient — exclude the lesion that is not a wart, offer STI testing by risk, and treat warts in a child as a safeguarding question first.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • A child with anogenital warts → safeguarding assessment; consider sexual abuse, particularly under 13 → Same-day discussion with the safeguarding lead, at the visit. Do not examine or treat outside your competence.
  • Unexplained anal mass or anal ulceration (incl. suspected Buschke–Löwenstein: large, rapidly growing, treatment-resistant) → consider an anal Urgent suspected cancer pathway referral — do not wait for FIT.
  • Unexplained vulval lump, ulceration or bleeding, or penile mass or ulcerated lesion with STI excluded or persisting after STI treatment → consider a vulval or penile Urgent suspected cancer pathway referral.
  • Pigmented, ulcerated, indurated, fixed, bleeding or rapidly growing → not a simple wart: specialist biopsy; ulcerated, indurated or bleeding, especially if older or immunocompromised → consider a suspected cancer pathway referral by site.
  • Rectal bleeding → never put it down to warts without assessment; offer FIT where indicated for possible colorectal cancer.
  • New extensive or rapidly progressing warts on immunosuppressants → Urgent HIV test, FBC; GUM/dermatology management.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis

Soft flesh-coloured, grey or pink papules, cauliflower-like. Typical → no biopsy, swab or HPV typing. Chaperone offered; examine every site: penis incl. meatus, vulva and introitus, perianal skin.

2Check — the tests are for the patient, not the warts

☐ STI tests by risk: commonly HIV, syphilis, chlamydia/gonorrhoea NAAT from exposure sites; hepatitis B/C if indicated  ☐ Immunosuppression  ☐ Cervical screening  ☐ Pregnancy test  ☐ Vaccination, smoking

Meatal warts or urethral symptoms → specialist (intraurethral warts). Intra-anal symptoms → GUM.

3Decide — who is treated where

  • Offer sexual-health assessment to all new presentations; confident, uncomplicated external warts may be managed in primary care.
  • Choose with the patient by site, size, morphology, preference, pregnancy; observation is an option.
  • Everyone: condoms reduce but do not eliminate spread; offer stop-smoking support.

<~50% improvement, poor tolerance or doubt → switch or refer.

5Treat — external warts only

Podophyllotoxin0.5% solution (0.15% cream for perianal or hard-to-reach sites): twice daily for 3 days, then 4 days off = 1 cycle; local protocol sets the maximum
Imiquimod 5% creamThree nights a week at bedtime, washed off after 6–10 hours, up to 16 weeks; any morphology
CryotherapyTrained clinician, usually weekly; up to four sessions before reassessing
Pregnancy · breastfeedingNo self-applied agents. Cryotherapy, TCA or specialist ablation, or defer until after delivery
  • Every review: still typical? Technique right? No podophyllotoxin on inflamed, eroded or bleeding skin; no OTC wart products on genital skin. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Severe imiquimod reaction (severe ulceration, systemic symptoms) → stop, same-day review, consider reducing frequency.
  • On clearance: recurrence talk (no treatment eradicates HPV); STI results actioned, partner advice given. No test-of-cure.
  • Repeated recurrences → reassess the diagnosis; HIV test if not done; immunosuppression screen; biopsy atypical lesions.

7Refer — routine, by criteria

  • Sexual health/GUM: internal lesions, uncertain diagnosis, extensive or resistant disease, HIV or immunosuppression, pregnancy.
  • Colposcopy/gynaecology: warts with an abnormal cervical screening result. Obstetrics: extensive lesions near delivery.
  • Gardasil 9 by national eligibility: protects against types not yet acquired; does not treat warts.

8Never

  • Treat a pigmented, flat, ulcerated, indurated or fixed lesion as a simple wart.
  • Self-applied podophyllotoxin or imiquimod in pregnancy or breastfeeding.
  • Wait for FIT before referring an unexplained anal mass or ulceration.
  • Let a child's warts wait for a later safeguarding discussion.
  • Imply that warts now prove a new partner or infidelity — HPV may be months or years old.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Return immediately “Rapid growth, bleeding, an ulcer or severe pain, a hard or fixed lump, bleeding from the back passage or a change in bowel habit, trouble passing urine, blood in the urine or a discharge — come back straight away.”   Imiquimod: “Some redness and soreness is common; wash it off after 6–10 hours. Very sore, ulcerated or feeling unwell — stop and contact us the same day.”   Recurrence: “The virus can sit quietly for months or years; warts coming back is the virus waking up, not a new infection.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
