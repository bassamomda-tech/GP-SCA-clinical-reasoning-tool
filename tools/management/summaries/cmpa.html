Cow's milk protein allergy (CMPA)

For: infants and young children with suspected CMPA. Not for: lactose intolerance; unexplained pallor, bruising, petechiae, hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy or a mass (paediatric pathway, NICE NG12).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG116BSACI 2014iMAP / MAP 2019Resuscitation Council UKNICE NG75
Core rule: classify from the allergy-focused history, prove non-IgE CMPA by a 2–6-week elimination and a planned reintroduction, plot the weight before discussing formula, and never leave a child milk-free without review.

0Act now — two shock presentations, plus the growth and bleeding emergencies

  • Anaphylaxis (lip or facial swelling, wheeze, stridor, hypotension, collapse after milk) → IM adrenaline now (100–150 micrograms <6 months; 150 micrograms 6 months–6 years), 999; repeat after 5 minutes if no improvement. No delay for antihistamines or steroids.
  • Possible acute FPIES: repetitive, profuse vomiting 1–4 hours after milk with pallor, lethargy, hypotonia or shock-like state → 999   Respiratory distress after a feed → 999
  • Suspected intussusception or acute abdomen → Same-day emergency assessment
  • Dehydration, inability to feed or persistent vomiting, haematemesis, melaena or significant rectal bleeding, faltering growth with acute illness, or infected eczema with fever and systemic upset → Same-day paediatric assessment
  • Visible blood in stools >2 weeks despite a milk-free diet → Urgent (2-week) paediatric gastroenterology — a referral, not a wait.

Over-diagnosed: eczema, colic or reflux alone do not diagnose CMPA. Only recurrence on reintroduction confirms it.

2Check — the weight matters more than any test

☐ Allergy-focused history (timing, reproducibility, feeding, maternal diet, atopy)  ☐ Weight, length, head circumference on UK-WHO charts  ☐ Eczema and infection  ☐ Hydration  ☐ Nappy seen or photographed

No routine bloods, and no IgE tests for isolated non-IgE disease.

3Decide — by mechanism

  • Suspected mild–moderate non-IgE → complete elimination 2–6 weeks with dietetic advice, then planned reintroduction.
  • Suspected IgE or mixed → strict avoidance, allergy assessment; assess auto-injector need. If prescribed: two, carried at all times, written emergency plan.
  • Eczema → treat it first; consider elimination only if moderate–severe and poorly controlled, or convincing food history.
  • No improvement after a full trial → check adherence; reconsider the diagnosis; stop the diet.

5Treat — eHF first, AAF for a reason

BreastfedKeep breastfeeding; consider time-limited maternal exclusion with maternal calcium and vitamin D
Step 1 · formula-fedeHF, local formulary (ACBS). Refused for taste → transition advice, not AAF
Step 2 · specialist adviceConsider AAF: severe reaction, FPIES, faltering growth, multiple or eczema-linked food allergy, failed eHF
Soya · conditionalNot under 6 months without specialist/dietetic advice
  • No plant drink as main milk under 12 months; no rice drink under 5. Vitamin D per UK advice, not duplicating the formula. Doses per BNFc and local formulary.

6Monitor — the commonest long-term error is drift

  • Trial: judge over 2–6 weeks; plot growth every review. Improved → reintroduce to confirm.
  • Reintroduction often around 9–12 months. Home milk ladder only for mild–moderate non-IgE, controlled, growing well: baked → yoghurt → cheese → fresh milk, a step every 1–2 weeks.
  • Supervised if IgE, mixed, FPIES, previous anaphylaxis or severe reaction, faltering growth, multiple allergy. Tolerance review every 6–12 months.

7Refer — emergencies are in block 0

  • Allergy: suspected IgE or mixed, previous systemic reaction, multiple food allergies, severe eczema with suspected food allergy. FPIES → Urgent paediatric/allergy.
  • Paediatrics / dietitian: faltering growth with GI symptoms; symptoms despite correct elimination; uncertainty. Dietitian input wherever possible.

8Never

  • IgG “food intolerance” tests, patch tests or broad food-allergy panels.
  • A home milk ladder in IgE, mixed or FPIES disease unless the allergy service advises it.
  • AAF first-line, or escalation from eHF to AAF without an indication and review.
  • “Lactose-free”, goat's or sheep's milk as treatment; soya under 6 months without specialist advice.
  • A milk-free diet left running without reintroduction and a tolerance review.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Swelling of the lips, face or tongue, difficulty breathing, wheeze, floppiness or collapse after milk — use the adrenaline pen straight away if you have one and call 999. Repeated heavy vomiting 1 to 4 hours after milk with floppiness or paleness — 999.”   Same-day “Not feeding, keeps vomiting, fewer wet nappies, unusually sleepy or not gaining weight; black or tarry poo, blood in the vomit, or more than small streaks of blood — contact us the same day.”   Blood streaks: “Streaks of blood or mucus still there after 2 weeks on the milk-free diet — contact us then, so we can refer.”   Diet: “No improvement after 6 weeks off milk — come back; we should stop the diet.”
