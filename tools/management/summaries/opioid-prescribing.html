Opioids in non-cancer pain

For: adults with chronic non-cancer pain, starting or already on opioids. Not for: acute, postoperative, trauma, renal colic or sickle-cell pain; cancer pain or palliative care (use palliative pain); opioid dependence treatment (use opioid dependence).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
Opioids Aware (FPM 2026)NICE NG193NG215NG59MHRA
Core rule: don't start opioids for chronic primary pain; any trial has written goals and an exit; for someone still in pain the answer is rarely a higher dose — review benefit and harm, and never stop abruptly.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected opioid toxicity (cannot be woken, slow or gurgling breathing, blue lips) or respiratory depression → 999 and give naloxone.
  • Recurrent overdose or severe sedation; suicidality, severe psychiatric risk or psychosocial crisis → Same-day advice (999 if in immediate danger)
  • Opioid plus benzodiazepine, Z-drug, gabapentinoid or alcohol → Urgent review: agree which to reduce first, taper gradually, consider naloxone.
  • Changed pain, red flags, weight loss → re-examine for new pathology; refer by the relevant pathway.
  • Restart after a gap (e.g. hospital discharge without the opioid) → tolerance is lost: restart low.

2Check — risk screen before starting

☐ Misuse, alcohol, overdose history  ☐ Mental health  ☐ CNS depressants  ☐ Lung disease, sleep apnoea  ☐ eGFR, liver  ☐ Frailty, falls  ☐ Pregnancy, breastfeeding  ☐ Driving  ☐ Total daily dose incl. PRN. Low eGFR → renal-safe plan with pharmacy or specialist advice.

3Decide — the trial, with the exit named at the start

  • Written functional goals, duration, maximum daily dose, exit plan. Never "as directed".
  • Lowest appropriate immediate-release dose; avoid routine MR initiation (NG215).
  • Review ≤2–4 weeks: functional gain → lowest effective dose; no benefit → taper and stop.
  • Established users, limited benefit or harm → offer an individualised taper; invite, don't ambush.

4Target — OME review thresholds (not safe limits or ceilings)

Total daily OMEAction
About 50 mg/dayHeightened review; escalate only with documented benefit

SwitchingConvert, then reduce 25–50%; experienced prescriber

5Treat — choice, harms, taper

ChooseMorphine IR, lowest dose. Buprenorphine patch by brand, per SPC (e.g. renal impairment, poor swallow). Oxycodone, fentanyl, tapentadol → specialist only.
Weak-opioid trapsTramadol: serotonin syndrome with SSRIs, seizures, dependence. Codeine: CYP2D6 — little effect or toxicity; not in breastfeeding.
Constipation from day 1Regular stimulant (senna or bisacodyl) ± macrogol. Exclude obstruction or impaction; PAMORA per formulary if inadequate.
TaperOne opioid at a time; slow steps proportionate to the dose, smaller as it falls. Pause or slow for withdrawal. Months is success.
  • Still in pain? Rarely a higher dose: check function, hyperalgesia (diffuse, more sensitive pain → taper), pain type, combinations, mood, sleep, new pathology. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Monthly until stable, then at least 6-monthly while prescribed.
  • Each review: OME, function, sedation, constipation, falls, endocrine symptoms, other prescribers, readiness to taper.
  • Naloxone with training: high dose, sedative combination, previous overdose, lung disease or OSA, lost tolerance.
  • Early requests, multiple prescribers, crushing MR → name it; no early scripts.

7Refer

  • Pain service: approaching 90 mg/day OME, complex rotation, refractory pain, complex or failed taper.
  • Drug and alcohol service: suspected opioid use disorder, diversion, illicit top-ups — alongside primary care.
  • Pregnant on regular opioids → joint obstetric and specialist plan; neonatal team aware.
  • Definitive fix: the joint to replace, the radiculopathy to image.

8Never

  • Start opioids for chronic primary pain, fibromyalgia or chronic low back pain.
  • Stop abruptly after regular use — including in pregnancy (fetal withdrawal).
  • Start an avoidable opioid + benzodiazepine, Z-drug or gabapentinoid combination.
  • Raise the dose for ongoing pain before checking benefit, hyperalgesia and new pathology.
  • Switch opioids at the full "equivalent" dose — incomplete cross-tolerance can overdose.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Can't be woken, slow or gurgling breathing, blue lips — whoever is with you calls 999, says 'opioid overdose' and uses naloxone if you have it.”   Mixing: “No alcohol, sleeping or 'nerve' tablets on top — it can stop your breathing.”   Driving: “Don't drive if drowsy or dizzy, or after a dose change until you know how it affects you.”   Storage: “Lock them away — one MR tablet can kill a child.”   Never stop dead: “Stopping suddenly makes you ill — we reduce together, in small steps.”
