Sick day rules — medicines, insulin and ketones

For: adults on regular medicines, including insulin, with an acute illness. Not for: treating DKA (DKA protocol), Addison's long-term care (Addison's protocol) or children (paediatric diabetes team). Insulin dose tables → full protocol.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
Think Kidneys 2018JBDS 02 (2023)MHRA SGLT2iNICE NG243TREND-UK (consensus)
Core rule: screen for DKA, AKI and adrenal crisis first; never stop basal insulin — test glucose AND ketones; steroids go up, never down; hold SADMAN drugs only in significant dehydration, and write down when each restarts.

0Act now — screen before deciding home is safe

  • Blood ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L (or urine 2+ with deterioration), any ketones with vomiting, drowsy, Kussmaul breathing, abdominal pain (DKA) → 999 Do not stop insulin, including en route.
  • Glucose ≥30 mmol/L, marked dehydration, drowsy, little or no ketones (HHS, usually type 2) → 999
  • Steroid-dependent with vomiting, drowsiness, hypotension, collapse or hyponatraemia (adrenal crisis) → hydrocortisone 100 mg IM/IV now, then 999 — do not wait for bloods.
  • Persistent vomiting or unable to keep fluids down → Admit now for IV fluids ± insulin ± hydrocortisone.
  • Fever, sore throat or mouth ulcers on carbimazole (stop it), clozapine (don't stop; clozapine service) or a DMARD → assessment and FBC Same-day. Recent anticancer treatment or neutropenia → Immediate acute oncology
  • Ketones >1.5 to <3.0 → Same-day urgent diabetes-team advice; hospital if rising or not falling at the 2-hour recheck, vomiting or unwell. Oliguria or postural drop on SADMAN → U&E, same-day if unwell. NEWS2 ≥5 → urgent assessment (sepsis pathway).

1Confirm — is it a sick day? map the drug list

Sick day = vomiting, diarrhoea, or fever with sweats and shaking, with poor intake. A cold without fever → no hold; other drugs continue.

3Decide — home or admit

  • Fluids kept down, able to follow the plan (not the glucose) → home, written plan. Lower threshold: recent DKA, hypo unawareness, pregnancy, frailty, CKD, pump, alone and worsening.

  • Heart failure: no automatic diuretic or ACEi/ARB hold — advice early.
  • Steroid risk: adrenal insufficiency; prednisolone ≥5 mg/day ≥4 weeks (any route); high-dose inhaled; recently stopped.
  • Lithium + significant dehydration or toxicity → stop, urgent advice. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — restart drug by drug

  • SGLT2i: recovered, eating and drinking, ketones normal. Metformin: eating and drinking, renal function satisfactory.
  • ACEi, ARB, diuretic: by BP, volume, K⁺, renal function; recheck U&E after AKI. Sulfonylurea: intake reliable. NSAID: not routinely.
  • Reconcile after every illness or admission so held drugs are not lost.

7Refer — discuss

  • Diabetes team: early if high insulin doses or glucose/ketones not settling; insulin doubt; recurrent decompensation.
  • Specialist: lithium toxicity, Parkinson's nil by mouth, DMARD in serious infection, transplant/HIV drugs with vomiting, steroid doubt.

8Never

  • Stop or halve basal insulin — even if not eating, even en route to hospital.
  • Accept a normal glucose as reassurance on an SGLT2 inhibitor — check ketones.
  • Hold or skip steroids in the steroid-dependent — increase them; when in doubt, give hydrocortisone.
  • Metoclopramide or prochlorperazine in Parkinson's disease.
  • Hold without a written restart plan, or a blanket hold list for a cold.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Vomiting with any ketones, can't keep fluids down, ketones 3 or more, drowsy or confused, deep fast breathing, tummy pain or collapse — call 999. On steroids and vomiting or very unwell — use your injection first.”   Same-day “Starting to vomit, ketones up (recheck in 2 hours), sugars very high or very low despite extra insulin, passing less urine, or getting worse — ring us today; out of hours, 111.”   Agranulocytosis drugs: “Sore throat, mouth ulcers or a temperature — contact us the same day for a blood test.”   Restart: “I've written down when to restart each paused tablet — some need a blood test first.”
