Lyme disease

For: adults and young people aged 12 and over with suspected Lyme disease or a tick bite in primary care. Not for: under-18s with anything other than a single EM lesion and no other symptoms (discuss with paediatrics); focal or disseminated disease in pregnancy (infection/obstetric specialists).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG95 (updated Oct 2018)NICE QS186 (2019)UKHSA RIPLNICE NG182GM antimicrobial guidance
Core rule: erythema migrans is a clinical diagnosis — treat it the same day without testing; and suspected Lyme is never a reason to delay usual emergency care for heart block, CNS infection or uveitis.

0Act now — do not wait for Lyme serology

  • Palpitations, syncope or presyncope, breathlessness or chest pain → ECG. Second/third-degree AV block, syncope or haemodynamic compromise → 999 / same-day admission (may need pacing). One normal ECG does not exclude intermittent block.
  • Headache with neck stiffness, photophobia, confusion, reduced consciousness or seizures → Same-day emergency assessment (ED / acute medicine) as for any CNS infection.
  • Red painful eye, photophobia or visual change (uveitis, keratitis) → Same-day ophthalmology
  • Facial palsy (can be bilateral), other cranial neuropathy or radiculopathy → Urgent specialist discussion, serology, and start the oral regimen without delay. Exclude stroke as usual.

1Confirm — EM is diagnosed on sight

PictureFeaturesNext
Erythema migransRed rash expanding over days, usually not itchy, hot or painful; central clearing variableDiagnose clinically and treat; no test
Tick-bite reactionDevelops and recedes within 48 hours of the bite; often hot, itchy or painfulAdvise and review; do not treat
No typical EMNon-focal (fever, nodes, fatigue, migratory aches) or focal features, plausible exposureTest (block 2)

Do not exclude EM because it is smaller than 5 cm, lacks central clearing, or no tick bite is recalled.

2Check

☐ Exposure (woodland, parks, gardens, travel)  ☐ Skin: size, site, photo  ☐ Neuro, cardiac (ECG), joint, eye  ☐ Pregnancy, age

Tests (no typical EM): ELISA; positive or equivocal → immunoblot. Still suspected: negative ELISA within 4 weeks of onset → repeat 4–6 weeks later; symptoms ≥12 weeks → immunoblot. Accredited labs; UKHSA RIPL.

Don't miss: an early negative does not rule Lyme out; if suspicion is high, consider treating while awaiting results.

3Decide

  • EM → start antibiotics the same day.
  • Well after a tick bite → no antibiotics; advise and safety-net.
  • Adults, focal symptoms → consider specialist discussion or referral, without delaying treatment.
  • Under 18 → discuss with a specialist unless a single EM lesion and no other symptoms. Under 9: amoxicillin first choice, no doxycycline.
  • Pregnancy → antibiotic suitable in pregnancy (commonly amoxicillin for EM); no doxycycline unless a specialist advises.

5Treat — by presentation, adults and ≥12 years

EM / non-focalDoxycycline 100 mg BD or 200 mg OD, 21 days. 1st alternative amoxicillin 1 g TDS 21 days; 2nd azithromycin 500 mg OD 17 days
Cranial / peripheral nerveDoxycycline (as above) or amoxicillin 1 g TDS, 21 days, plus specialist discussion
Arthritis / ACADoxycycline or amoxicillin, 28 days
CNS · carditisSpecialist-led. Stable carditis: oral doxycycline 21 days. CNS or unstable carditis: IV ceftriaxone
  • Never azithromycin with cardiac involvement (QT). Doxycycline: full glass of water, upright; sun protection.
  • Jarisch–Herxheimer: fever, aches in the first 24 h usually settle; keep taking. Doses per BNF/BNFc and local (GM) formulary.

6Monitor

  • End of course (3–4 weeks): confirm improvement; fatigue can lag by weeks. Persisting → reassess diagnosis, course taken, new focal features, reinfection.
  • Treatment may have failed → consider one second course of an alternative antibiotic (e.g. amoxicillin after doxycycline).
  • After 2 courses → do not routinely offer more antibiotics. Serology can stay positive after cure.

7Refer — emergencies are in block 0

  • Lyme arthritis, ACA → rheumatology / dermatology / infection Routine/soon
  • Under 18, beyond a single EM lesion → paediatrics / paediatric infection; Urgent if focal.
  • Diagnostic doubt, or symptoms after 2 courses → infection specialist or UKHSA RIPL advice Routine

8Never

  • Test an EM rash, or wait for serology before treating it.
  • Antibiotics or Lyme tests for a well person after a tick bite.
  • Azithromycin with cardiac involvement; doxycycline under 9 or in pregnancy without specialist advice.
  • Delay emergency care for heart block, CNS infection or uveitis to get Lyme serology.
  • Endless antibiotic courses, re-treating on a positive antibody test, or unvalidated private tests.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A racing or very slow heartbeat, fainting or nearly fainting, breathlessness or chest pain; a severe headache with a stiff neck or dislike of light, or confusion; a face droop with arm weakness or difficulty speaking; trouble breathing or facial swelling on the antibiotic — call 999 or go to A&E.”   Same-day “One side of your face drooping on its own, new shooting nerve pains or weakness, a painful red eye or blurred vision, or feeling very unwell or a fever that doesn’t settle on the antibiotic — contact us the same day (111 out of hours).”   After a bite: “A new spreading red rash or flu-like illness in the weeks after a tick bite — come back and we’ll treat it.”
