UK screening programmes (England)

For: asymptomatic people eligible for NHS population, targeted or opportunistic screening in England. Not for: anyone with symptoms (use the diagnostic or suspected cancer pathway, NICE NG12), or specialist enhanced surveillance (BRCA, Lynch, FH — clinical genetics or lipid service). Core rule: screening is for people without symptoms — symptoms override any screening result, abnormal screens are actioned by the programme, and the practice closes the loop on every one.

0Act now — symptoms override a screening result

  • Symptomatic or tender AAA → emergency: 999 immediate transfer to the regional vascular service (not the 2-week pathway).
  • New visual symptoms in diabetes → Same-day clinical assessment (sudden loss of vision → same-day eye service); do not wait for routine DES. Act on urgent DESP advice.
  • Symptoms despite a normal or recent screen (breast lump, rectal bleeding, suspicious cervix, postmenopausal bleeding meeting NG12 criteria, symptomatic FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g) → Suspected cancer pathway. Postcoital bleeding → assess and refer per local gynaecology pathway.
  • Pregnant and not booked for antenatal screening by 10 weeks → Urgent maternity referral (sickle cell/thalassaemia ideally by 10 weeks; combined test 11+2 to 14+1 weeks).

2Check — when the record or consultation allows

☐ Symptoms asked  ☐ Eligible and up to date  ☐ Diabetes registered with DESP  ☐ CIN treatment coded  ☐ Learning disability health check: screening status

3Decide — informed choice, equitable uptake

  • Explain benefits, limits, false results and the right to decline; record a decline without coercion.
  • Offer opportunistically; interpreters, reasonable adjustments.
  • NHS Health Check 40–74, 5-yearly: QRISK3 ≥10% → shared discussion, then offer atorvastatin 20 mg (NG238).
  • TLHC via local case-finding only · HIV opt-out where commissioned · hepatitis B/C in UKHSA risk groups.

5Act on results — the programme owns abnormal screens

  • HPV+, cytology negative → retest at 12 months (not routine recall); persistent HPV → colposcopy per national pathway.
  • HPV+, cytology abnormal, or two consecutive inadequate samples → colposcopy via NHSCSP.
  • After CIN treatment → test of cure about 6 months: HPV-negative → recall in 3 years; HPV-positive → back to colposcopy.
  • Abnormal screening FIT → hub arranges specialist screening practitioner and colonoscopy; don't duplicate referral unless symptomatic (helpline 0800 707 6060).
  • Breast recall → breast screening service; GP referral only if symptomatic or asked.
  • DESP R1 → DESP recall, no ophthalmology referral; optimise HbA1c, BP, lipids, smoking.

6Close the loop

  • Every abnormal result, record: result received, action, who is responsible, patient informed, referral receipt confirmed, outcome, follow-up plan.
  • Electronic recall to check the outcome; fail-safe for non-attenders. A referral sent but not received is a patient safety event.

7Refer — specialist-led, not population screening

  • Family history of breast/ovarian, pancreatic or prostate cancer meeting NICE CG164 criteria → clinical genetics or family-history service.
  • MMR-deficient tumour, suspected or confirmed Lynch, or FAP → clinical genetics. Lynch aspirin only after specialist discussion.
  • Suspected FH (total cholesterol >7.5 mmol/L or LDL-C >4.9 mmol/L, secondary causes excluded; Simon Broome or DLCN) → lipid/genetics service.

8Never

  • Reassure a symptomatic patient with a normal or recent screen.
  • Confuse screening FIT with symptomatic FIT (≥10 µg Hb/g, NG12).
  • Duplicate a referral the programme (hub, breast service, colposcopy, DESP) already owns, unless symptomatic or asked.
  • Discharge to routine recall straight after CIN treatment or a previous HPV-positive result.
  • Start aspirin 600 mg daily for Lynch syndrome in primary care, or set up ad hoc TLHC referral routes.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Everyone: “Screening is for people without symptoms. A new breast lump, bleeding from the bottom, or bleeding after sex or the menopause — see us now; don't wait for your next screen.”   AAA: “New pain or tenderness in your tummy — call 999.” 999   Diabetes: “Any new change in your sight — contact us the same day.”   Abnormal result: “A positive result means a closer look, not cancer.”   After colonoscopy: “Significant bleeding, fever or severe tummy pain — come back immediately.”
