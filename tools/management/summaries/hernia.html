Hernia

For: adults and children with a suspected groin or abdominal wall hernia (inguinal, femoral, umbilical, paraumbilical, epigastric, incisional, Spigelian), before and after repair. Not for: acute severe scrotal pain — treat as torsion (block 0); a testicular lump or unexplained lymphadenopathy — NICE NG12 routes (block 7).

Core rule: locate the lump against the pubic tubercle; never push back a hernia that may be strangulated — hard, tense, constantly painful, red or febrile means 999 with your hands off — and never watch and wait a femoral hernia, a woman's groin hernia or a child's inguinal hernia.

0Act now — the character of the pain separates obstruction (colicky) from strangulation (constant)

  • Strangulation: irreducible, tense, tender; constant severe pain; red skin; fever, vomiting, peritonism or shock → 999 emergency surgery within hours; nil by mouth, no attempt at reduction.
  • Obstruction: colicky pain, bilious vomiting, distension, absolute constipation → Emergency hospital (999 if unwell). Peritonism + repair scar + irreducible midline bulge → 999
  • Femoral painful, irreducible, tender, enlarging or obstructed → 999 or Same-day emergency by severity.
  • Recently irreducible, new pain with enlargement, or painful ventral hernia, systemically well → Same-day surgical.
  • Child: irreducible, painful or tense inguinal hernia, vomiting or unwell → Emergency assessment.
  • Acute severe scrotal pain → torsion until excluded → Immediate A&E or 999; no ultrasound first; viability falls after 6 hours.

1Confirm — mostly clinical; examine standing and supine

InguinalAbove and medial to the pubic tubercle
FemoralBelow and lateral; weak cough impulse; hard; mistaken for a node

Less reliable in obesity, large hernias, women. Image only if uncertain: dynamic ultrasound; MRI if occult; CT if complex.

2Check — every case

☐ Pain: at rest = incarceration, constant = strangulation  ☐ Testes, other side  ☐ Cough, constipation, prostatism, weight  ☐ Occupation. Don't miss: firm lump below the ligament, older woman → image or refer; not "a node".

3Decide — the type sets the urgency

  • Femoral (symptoms or not), non-pregnant woman's groin hernia → timely repair; never watch and wait.
  • Child inguinal → paediatric surgery, promptly (infants, premature). Child umbilical: most close; assess if persistent or symptomatic.
  • Man, reducible inguinal: symptomatic → elective repair; minimal → discuss repair vs waiting.
  • Adult ventral → elective if symptomatic, enlarging or limiting.

5Treat — repair is surgical; the GP prepares and counsels

Usual first choiceMesh repair; technique chosen by the surgeon.
LaparoscopicBilateral or recurrent → flag it in the letter.
OptimiseWeight, smoking, cough, constipation — on own merits, not a referral condition.
TrussUnfit or declining; fitted lying, reduced. Never if irreducible.
  • Mesh: 2018 MHRA alert = transvaginal pelvic mesh, not inguinal; discuss pain, infection, recurrence at consent.
  • ACE inhibitor: change only for a probable ACE-inhibitor cough, on cardiovascular/renal merits. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — after repair, per the surgeon's pathway

  • Work by pain and function; drive when an emergency stop is pain-free.
  • Wound: dry 48 h, then shower; red, warm, pus, fever → Same-day
  • Pain >~3 months → check recurrence, infection, testes, hip/spine. New bulge → ultrasound, re-refer.

7Refer — non-urgent routes

  • Urgent reducible femoral; possible femoral; woman's groin hernia; recurrent incarceration; paediatric inguinal.
  • Routine symptomatic inguinal or ventral; uncertain lump → dynamic ultrasound. Specialist clinic: disabling post-repair pain.
  • NG12 — consider suspected cancer referral: non-painful testis change; unexplained adult lymphadenopathy.

8Never

  • Push back a possibly strangulated hernia — the lump vanishes while ischaemic bowel perforates.
  • Watch and wait a femoral hernia, a child's inguinal hernia or a woman's groin hernia.
  • Call a firm groin lump in an older woman "reactive" and review in six weeks.
  • Call acute scrotal pain a stuck hernia, or delay torsion referral for ultrasound.
  • Fit a truss over an irreducible hernia, or let the patient think it prevents incarceration.

9Safety-net — say it to everyone with a hernia, repaired or not; written if waiting

A&E “If the lump becomes painful, won't push back in, or the skin over it goes red or hot — or you start vomiting, get bad tummy pain, or sudden severe pain in a testicle — go straight to A&E. Don't ring the surgery first.”   Parents: A&E “Bring your child to A&E immediately if the lump becomes hard, they are inconsolable, or they start vomiting.”   After repair: Same-day “Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus or a fever from the wound — be seen the same day.”
