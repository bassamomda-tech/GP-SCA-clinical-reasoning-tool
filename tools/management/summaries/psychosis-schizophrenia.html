Psychosis and schizophrenia

For: adults with suspected first-episode psychosis or known psychotic illness in primary care. Not for: under 18 (CAMHS/EIP, CG155) or the undifferentiated acute presentation (psychosis pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG178CG155CG185TA136NHS England EIP standard
Core rule: assess risk and exclude organic causes first, then refer a first episode urgently to Early Intervention in Psychosis (EIP) without waiting for diagnostic certainty — antipsychotics are specialist-led.

0Act now — risk and organic causes before any psychiatric label

  • Imminent or unmanageable risk, severe agitation or violence, or unable to assess safely → 999 (police if needed).
  • Command hallucinations or suicidal thoughts with a plan → assess content, intent, means and immediacy now. Imminent → 999, do not leave alone; otherwise Same-day crisis team
  • Fever with confusion (encephalitis, meningitis), new seizures, suspected overdose or severe intoxication → 999
  • Focal neurological signs (999 if stroke suspected), delirium, rapid cognitive decline, rapidly progressive psychosis, catatonia, autonomic instability, papilloedema → Same-day medical/neurology
  • Postpartum psychosis (sudden onset weeks after birth) → secondary mental health, preferably perinatal: Assessed ≤4 hours
  • Severe self-neglect → emergency hospital if immediate medical risk, else Same-day crisis team. Refuses help with significant risk → crisis/AMHP service about a Mental Health Act assessment.

1Confirm — characterise; EIP/psychiatry makes the diagnosis

Positive, negative (blunted affect, avolition, anhedonia, withdrawal — often missed, may come first) and disorganised symptoms; duration, function, insight, MSE. Collateral history with consent.

Mania or psychotic depressionUrgent psychiatry (mania: CG185)
Substance-inducedAsk about all; toxicology neither proves nor excludes
OrganicTargeted bloods; medical/neurology per block 0
At-risk mental state + distress or declineRefer without delay to EIP; no preventive antipsychotics

2Check — full physical exam in every new presentation

☐ Temperature, pulse, BP, SpO₂  ☐ Neuro exam, cognitive screen  ☐ Bloods as indicated: FBC, U&E, LFT, glucose/HbA1c, TFT, calcium  ☐ HIV/syphilis if indicated  ☐ Pregnancy test if possible

Don't miss: never delay referral for results; no routine brain imaging (TA136).

3Decide — who goes where

  • First episode, any age or duration → Urgent EIP; do not wait for certainty.
  • Relapse → CMHT/EIP team and crisis plan. Under 18 → CAMHS/EIP.
  • Offer healthy eating + activity programme and smoking cessation; cannabis and substance support.

4Target — antipsychotic physical-health schedule (CG178)

Weight (plotted)Baseline, weekly to 6 wk, 12 wk, 1 yr, annually
Pulse, BP, fasting glucose or HbA1c, lipidsBaseline, 12 wk, 1 yr, annually (waist: baseline, annually)
Prolactin, movement, diet, activityBaseline; ECG if SPC, CV risk, personal/family cardiac history, admission or QT drugs

5Treat — specialist-led; GP role is safety and referral

Specialist-ledOne antipsychotic chosen with the person; no preferred drug; titrate cautiously
Alongside, first lineCBTp (≥16 sessions) and family intervention (≥10 sessions, 3 months–1 year)
Treatment-resistantClozapine after ≥2 antipsychotics (one a non-clozapine second-generation) — specialist only
  • No routine antipsychotic polypharmacy. Acute agitation → rapid-tranquillisation pathway via emergency/crisis services.
  • Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — a shared, long-term role

  • Secondary care monitors ≥12 months or until stable, whichever is longer; then explicit transfer with a shared-care plan.
  • Titration: akathisia (restlessness) is often mistaken for worsening psychosis.
  • Annual SMI review: weight, waist, BP, glucose/HbA1c, lipids, smoking, substances, adherence.
  • Relapse signature with patient and carer: sleep change, withdrawal, suspiciousness, stopping medicines.

7Refer — back to the responsible team

Deterioration, suspected relapse, significant side effects, pregnancy or medication changes → contact secondary care promptly.

8Never

  • Wait for diagnostic certainty or routine results before an EIP referral.
  • Assume primary psychosis before risk, organic and substance causes are screened.
  • Start an antipsychotic for a first presentation in primary care without a consultant psychiatrist.
  • Give antipsychotics to prevent psychosis in an at-risk mental state.
  • Manage substantial risk alone, or read akathisia as worsening psychosis.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Anyone at immediate risk of harm, severe agitation, or fever with confusion or a fit — call 999.”   999 On an antipsychotic: “High temperature, stiff muscles and confusion — call 999.”   Same-day “Voices telling you to harm, or things getting worse — contact the crisis team today.”   Crisis plan: written crisis contacts and safety plan — the carer has them too.
