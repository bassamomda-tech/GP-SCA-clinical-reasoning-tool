Bronchiectasis in adults

For: adults with established non-cystic-fibrosis bronchiectasis under shared care. Not for: cystic fibrosis or children. Undiagnosed chronic cough → chronic cough pathway; other chest infections → chest infections; see also asthma · COPD.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BTS bronchiectasis (2019)NICE NG117BTS long-term macrolides (2020)MHRA fluoroquinolones (2024)GM antimicrobial guidelines
Core rule: daily airway clearance is the cornerstone; treat an exacerbation with sputum sent first and a 7–14-day course, never like a simple chest infection; and no long-term macrolide before active NTM is excluded — a specialist decision.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Massive haemoptysis → 999
  • Severe breathlessness, hypoxia or respiratory failure, suspected sepsis, haemodynamic instability, confusion, cannot maintain oral intake, significant or ongoing haemoptysis, or outpatient treatment failing with deterioration → Same-day hospital assessment
  • Smaller-volume recurrent haemoptysis, particularly if increasing or with breathlessness → Same-day clinical assessment
  • Significantly unwell with an exacerbation → start antibiotics now; do not wait for a sputum sample.

1Confirm — diagnosis, cause and what an exacerbation is

Chronic productive cough with recurrent infections → chest X-ray, then thin-section CT showing airway dilatation.

Find the cause (“idiopathic” is a conclusion, not a default): post-infective, immunodeficiency, ABPA, aspiration/reflux, RA or IBD. CF testing if early onset, upper-lobe disease, malabsorption, infertility or family history; PCD assessment if chronic sinusitis, otitis, infertility or childhood respiratory disease.

Exacerbation = sustained worsening from stable state: cough, sputum volume, purulence or viscosity, breathlessness, wheeze, haemoptysis, pleurisy, systemic upset. Colour alone does not prove bacterial infection.

2Check — baseline work-up

☐ Immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM)  ☐ ABPA screen (total IgE, Aspergillus sensitisation)  ☐ Sputum culture and susceptibility, incl. Pseudomonas  ☐ Mycobacterial cultures per respiratory pathway  ☐ Spirometry  ☐ FBC, renal, liver function where indicated

Don't miss: one NTM isolate ≠ active NTM disease; colonisation ≠ infection.

3Decide — stable disease, everyone

  • Airway clearance → respiratory physiotherapist for an individualised technique (e.g. active cycle of breathing, oscillating PEP); daily, even when well.
  • Vaccines: annual influenza; pneumococcal per the Green Book; COVID-19 if currently eligible. Pulmonary rehab if breathlessness limits activity.
  • Stop smoking, avoid irritants, hydration, nutrition; treat the cause and comorbidities. Mucoactives only on specialist advice. Self-management or rescue plan for known patients.

4Target — exacerbation course length

7–14 days by severity, previous microbiology, exacerbation history and response. 14 days if more severe disease, poor response or Pseudomonas; shorter may suffice in mild disease.

5Treat — an exacerbation

Step 1 · assessSeverity, oxygenation, sepsis risk, pneumonia
Step 2 · sputumBefore the first dose where possible — never delay if significantly unwell
Step 3 · empiricalPer last culture and GM antimicrobial guideline
Step 4 · result backChange only if resistant and not improving; narrow spectrum where possible
  • Not improving → reassess diagnosis, adherence, clearance, complications, need for hospital or specialist.
  • Pseudomonas: susceptibility-based, with specialist/microbiology advice. Fluoroquinolones (MHRA) only when others are inappropriate; counsel on tendon, nerve and psychiatric effects. Check renal function and interactions. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Each review: exacerbations, antibiotic courses, admissions; sputum, haemoptysis, breathlessness; smoking, vaccines, weight; clearance adherence first when control slips.
  • Sputum culture with exacerbations and at least annually if able to expectorate.
  • Spirometry, SpO₂, function at least annually, if available.
  • Long-term macrolide (specialist): ECG at 1 month; LFTs at 1 month then 6-monthly; review at least 6-monthly.

7Refer — respiratory specialist

  • ≥3 antibiotic-treated exacerbations a year, recurrent admissions or IV antibiotics → optimise the basics first; prophylaxis only on specialist advice.
  • New Pseudomonas (eradication offered if deteriorating; if stable, eradication vs observation) or NTM isolated or suspected.
  • Suspected immunodeficiency, ABPA, CF or PCD; diagnostic uncertainty; progressive decline.

8Never

  • Treat an exacerbation like a chest infection — 5 days, empirical, no sputum.
  • Start a long-term macrolide in primary care, or before active NTM, ECG/QTc, LFTs, interactions and hearing/balance are checked.
  • Escalate drugs before checking daily airway clearance is being done.
  • Prescribe inhaled or oral corticosteroids, or DNase, solely for bronchiectasis.
  • Offer prophylactic antibiotics routinely without specialist advice.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Coughing up a large amount of blood, or very breathless, confused or very unwell — call 999.”   Same day “More or discoloured phlegm, more breathless, feeling unwell, or getting worse quickly at any time — contact us the same day; you will likely need a phlegm sample and a 7–14-day course.”   Clearance: “Keep up your daily exercises even when you feel well.”   Fluoroquinolone: “Tendon pain, numbness or mood change — stop it and seek advice immediately.”
