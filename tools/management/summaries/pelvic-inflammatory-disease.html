Pelvic inflammatory disease

For: suspected PID in sexually active patients in general practice, including those with a coil, living with HIV or breastfeeding. Not for: pregnancy (admit — no outpatient regimen is established as safe), atypical PID after the menopause or with no sexual risk (gynaecology advice; NG12), or a positive single STI without PID (use the chlamydia or gonorrhoea protocol).

Core rule: pregnancy test first; then start the 14-day triple regimen on the day you suspect PID — never wait for swabs — and review at 48–72 hours.

0Act now — rule these out before, or alongside, PID treatment

  • Positive pregnancy test + pain, bleeding, syncope, shoulder-tip pain or cervical motion / adnexal tenderness → Immediate EPAS / out-of-hours gynae (pregnancy of unknown location, not PID alone). Haemodynamically unstable, or significant pain or bleeding → 999 / A&E
  • Peritonism, sudden one-sided pain, vomiting or a tender mass (appendicitis, ovarian torsion, cyst accident) → Same-day surgical / gynae assessment
  • High fever, tachycardia, hypotension, severe pain or tender adnexal mass (sepsis, tubo-ovarian abscess) → Same-day admission for parenteral antibiotics and ultrasound.
  • Also admit: severe disease, PID in pregnancy, can't tolerate oral treatment, no improvement at 48–72 h, diagnostic uncertainty → Same-day gynae
  • Ascites, or a pelvic or abdominal mass not obviously fibroids → Suspected cancer pathway. Cervix looks like cancer → consider the same.
  • Child under 13, or concern about abuse or exploitation → Same-day safeguarding lead, local procedures.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis; no test rules it in

Sexually active, recent-onset pelvic or lower abdominal pain + cervical motion, uterine or adnexal tenderness (one is enough) + no better alternative → treat. Discharge, deep dyspareunia, abnormal bleeding or fever >38 °C support but are not required.

2Check — test, but do not wait

☐ Pregnancy test (negative urine test doesn't exclude very early pregnancy — repeat or serum if suspicion) ☐ Temp, pulse, BP; sepsis ☐ Speculum + bimanual (consent, chaperone offered) ☐ NAAT chlamydia + gonorrhoea; M. genitalium if offered ☐ HIV + syphilis

Don't miss: after the menopause, a first PID diagnosis → think ovarian cancer (NG12).

3Decide — where she is treated

  • Mild–moderate, well, not pregnant, can take oral treatment and attend review → general practice; sexual health for partners.
  • Coil: leave in initially; no improvement at 48–72 h → discuss removal with her and specialist. If removed: pregnancy risk, consider emergency contraception (sex in previous 7 days), new method now.
  • Under 16: if competent, can be treated confidentially; assess coercion and age gap; document capacity.
  • Breastfeeding: seek advice; a short doxycycline course is often acceptable.

5Treat — start today, 14 days

First lineCeftriaxone 1 g IM once, then doxycycline 100 mg BD + metronidazole 400 mg BD for 14 days
No ceftriaxone in practiceDon't drop it → sexual health, UTC or gynae Same-day
AlternativeOfloxacin 400 mg BD + metronidazole, or moxifloxacin 400 mg OD, 14 days — only if first line inappropriate; not if gonorrhoea likely.
M. genitaliumMoxifloxacin 400 mg OD 14 days via sexual health — not GP-initiated.
  • Ask about severe penicillin or cephalosporin allergy. Quinolones (MHRA): warn about tendon, nerve, psychiatric, QT and aortic effects. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Follow up — two reviews

  • 48–72 h: not improving, worse pain, fever, vomiting or doubt → Same-day hospital / gynae
  • Gonorrhoea found: confirm ceftriaxone given; test of cure agreed with sexual health (persistent symptoms, throat, unknown susceptibility, other regimen, pregnancy).
  • 2–4 weeks: course done, partners treated, results acted on, contraception, fertility.

7Refer — non-urgent routes

  • Sexual health Urgent, within days: partners (no organism → all in the 6 months before onset); recurrent PID.
  • Gynaecology (routine): chronic pain or subfertility after PID.
  • Persistent bloating, early satiety, pelvic pain → reconsider PID; NG12 ovarian pathway, never CA125 alone.

8Never

  • Treat for PID without a pregnancy test.
  • Wait for swab results to treat — a negative STI test does not exclude PID.
  • Give doxycycline + metronidazole without ceftriaxone as the plan.
  • Use a quinolone first line, or when gonorrhoea is likely.
  • Repeat antibiotics for persistent symptoms without reassessing.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 / A&E “If you faint or collapse, or get sudden severe tummy pain — call 999 or go to A&E now.”   Same-day “Worse pain, a fever, being sick so you can't keep the tablets down, or a missed period or positive pregnancy test with pain — contact us the same day, or 111.”   Course: “Finish all 14 days. No sex, even with condoms, until you and your partner have finished treatment and the symptoms have settled.”   Metronidazole: “No alcohol until 48 hours after the last tablet.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Pregnancy test; ectopic, surgical abdomen, sepsis excluded Bimanual signs found (chaperone offered) NAATs, HIV + syphilis sent; safeguarding checked Triple regimen today; partners, coil, 48–72 h review booked Safety-net given and documented
