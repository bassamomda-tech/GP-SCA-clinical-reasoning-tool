Urinary incontinence in women

For: women with urinary incontinence in primary care. Not for: men (use the male LUTS protocol) or children. Pregnant or postnatal: PFMT first-line, avoid drugs.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG123 (updated 2023)NG12 (updated Apr 2026)NG23NG210MHRA Jul 2026
Core rule: dipstick and examine every woman before labelling it incontinence, define the type from the history and a 3-day diary, clear the reversibles, and give conservative therapy a real trial before drugs or surgery.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • New leg weakness, saddle anaesthesia or severe perineal pain with new bladder or bowel dysfunction → 999 / emergency (cauda equina).
  • Acute retention (cannot pass urine, suprapubic pain, distended bladder) or suspected urosepsis → Same-day assessment. Chronic incomplete emptying → prompt PVR; no antimuscarinics.
  • Unexplained visible haematuria aged ≥45 without UTI, or persisting/recurring after successful UTI treatment; non-visible haematuria aged ≥60 with dysuria or raised WCC → Urology suspected cancer pathway. Under 45: still needs urological/renal assessment.
  • Pelvic or abdominal mass not obviously fibroids, or unexplained post-menopausal bleeding aged ≥55 not attributable to HRT (under 55: consider) → Gynaecology suspected cancer pathway
  • Persistent urgency/frequency with bloating, early satiety, appetite loss or pelvic pain, aged ≥40 → CA125 against NG12 age thresholds; at or above → Urgent direct-access ultrasound. Aged ≤39: consider ultrasound, not CA125 alone.

1Confirm — the type dictates the plan

History usually classifies it; add examination and a 3-day bladder diary if uncertain. Mixed → treat the predominant component first.

TypePattern
StressLeak on cough, sneeze, exercise, lifting; no urge first
Urgency / OABSudden compelling urge precedes the leak
OverflowDribble, incomplete emptying → refer; no antimuscarinics
FunctionalMobility or cognition → timed voiding, carers

2Check — every new presentation

☐ Dipstick (MSU if UTI symptoms)  ☐ Abdomen  ☐ Chaperoned vaginal exam: atrophy, prolapse  ☐ BMI  ☐ Diary + ICIQ-UI-SF  ☐ DIAPPERS (HbA1c if new)  ☐ PVR, neuro screen if indicated

Blood on dipstick → haematuria pathway regardless. No routine urodynamics in primary care.

3Decide — conservative first, always

  • Reversibles first: symptomatic UTI, constipation, cough, diuretics; ACE-inhibitor cough → switch to an ARB.
  • Vaginal atrophy → offer vaginal oestrogen, at any rung.
  • Lifestyle: BMI ≥30 → offer weight management (do not delay PFMT); trial of caffeine reduction; modify fluids only if excessive or insufficient.

4Target — adequate trial; measure with ICIQ-UI-SF

TreatmentMinimum trial
Supervised PFMT (stress/mixed)≥8 contractions 3× daily, ≥3 months
Bladder training (urgency/mixed)≥6 weeks
OAB drugReview at 4 weeks

5Treat — OAB = overactive bladder

Step 1 · allSupervised PFMT (stress) or bladder training (urgency)
Step 2 · OABStill troublesome → offer an antimuscarinic by local formulary
Step 3 · OABUnsuitable, ineffective or not tolerated → consider mirabegron (or vibegron)
Step 4 · refractorySpecialist: botulinum toxin A, neuromodulation; stress → surgery
  • Before every step: right type, technique, adherence, reversibles, no overflow. Stress UI: no first-line drug; duloxetine not routine, may offer if she prefers drugs or surgery unsuitable.
  • Avoid oxybutynin in frail or older women. Vaginal estradiol 10 microgram daily 2 weeks, then twice weekly — doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • 4–6 weeks: PFMT technique (lift, not bear down), ICIQ-UI-SF.
  • OAB drug, 4 weeks: effect, side effects, cognition; decline → stop.
  • Mirabegron: BP before and regularly; ≥180 systolic or ≥110 diastolic → stop. 3 months: full response. Long-term OAB drug: annually, 6-monthly if over 75.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Urogynaecology: stress UI refractory after ≥3 months supervised PFMT; visible or symptomatic prolapse; previous continence surgery or pelvic radiotherapy.
  • Continence service: OAB refractory to bladder training + ≥2 drugs. Neurological disease: specialist; residual before any antimuscarinic.
  • Urology: suspected fistula (constant wetness); persistent significant residual; recurrent UTI, no clear cause (≥60: consider non-urgent cancer referral).

8Never

  • Let a presumed UTI or incontinence explain visible haematuria.
  • An antimuscarinic in overflow or retention.
  • Immediate-release oxybutynin in older women at risk of sudden deterioration.
  • Drugs before an adequate conservative trial (unless block 0 or 7 applies).
  • Antibiotics for asymptomatic bacteriuria in non-pregnant women.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “New weakness in both legs, numbness around the saddle area, or severe perineal pain, with new bladder or bowel problems — call 999.”   Same-day “If you cannot pass urine and have tummy pain, or see fresh blood in the urine — contact us the same day.”   Bladder tablet: “Confusion, constipation or a very dry mouth — tell us and we will switch it.”   Exercises: “No better after three proper months — come back; that is when we change the plan.”
