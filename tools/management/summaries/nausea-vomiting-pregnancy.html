Nausea and vomiting in pregnancy and hyperemesis gravidarum

For: pregnant women with nausea and vomiting assessed and treated in primary care. Not for: inpatient care (IV fluids, VTE prophylaxis, steroids, parenteral nutrition — specialist decisions) or vomiting from another cause.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
RCOG Green-top 69 (2024)NICE NG201RCOG Green-top 37aMHRA
Core rule: score with PUQE and judge hydration clinically (never by urinary ketones), treat on the score with pregnancy-safe antiemetics, review at 24–48 hours, and treat any neurological sign with vomiting as Wernicke's — 999 for parenteral thiamine.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Confusion, diplopia, nystagmus or ataxia with vomiting (eye signs alone suffice) → 999 for parenteral thiamine (Wernicke's); don't wait for a level.
  • Can't keep fluids or oral antiemetics down, clinical dehydration, postural hypotension, weight loss >5% of pre-pregnancy weight, PUQE 13–15, electrolyte disturbance or comorbidity → Same-day maternity
  • Abdominal pain or fever (appendicitis, gallbladder, pancreatitis, pyelonephritis; after 20 weeks pre-eclampsia/HELLP) → Same-day; severe pain or sepsis → 999
  • Haematemesis (often Mallory-Weiss) → Same-day; haemodynamically unstable → 999
  • Vomiting out of proportion, vaginal bleeding, uterus larger than dates (molar pregnancy) → Same-day EPU/maternity for ultrasound and quantitative hCG.
  • Suicidal ideation → Same-day mental health assessment; suicidal intent or immediate risk → 999

1Confirm — score it, don't eyeball it

PUQE: hours of nausea, vomits and retches a day, each 1–5 (total 3–15); record with weight and hydration every visit.

PUQESeveritySetting
≤6MildPrimary care
7–12ModeratePrimary care; low threshold to refer
13–15SevereSame-day maternity (block 0)

Hyperemesis (Windsor): onset before 16 weeks, severe nausea and/or vomiting, unable to eat and/or drink normally, daily life strongly limited. New onset after 16 weeks or marked change → Same-day look for another cause.

2Check — proportionate to the score

☐ Weight, pulse, BP, postural drop  ☐ Urine only if indicated (infection; protein after 20 weeks)  ☐ Moderate/severe: U&E, FBC, LFTs, TFTs  ☐ Ultrasound if viability uncertain, severe/atypical, or multiple/molar suspected  ☐ PHQ-9, GAD-7, suicidal thoughts

Mild typical NVP: no bloods, no repeat ultrasound.

3Decide — treat the score

  • Mild → lifestyle ± first-line antiemetic. Moderate → first-line antiemetic, close monitoring.
  • Community treatment failed, PUQE <13 → consider ambulatory day care (IV fluids, antiemetics); PUQE <13 alone does not make it safe.
  • Thiamine: consider oral 100 mg TDS if vomiting is prolonged or intake substantially reduced.
  • Lifestyle: small, frequent carbohydrate meals, cold food, avoid triggers, rest. Continue folic acid.

5Treat — review every step at 24–48 hours

Step 1 · mildLifestyle, ginger 250 mg QDS (not for hyperemesis), P6 acupressure
Step 2 · first lineDoxylamine/pyridoxine (per SPC), prochlorperazine 5–10 mg TDS, cyclizine 50 mg TDS, promethazine 25 mg TDS
Step 3 · second lineMetoclopramide 10 mg TDS (max 5 days, 30 mg/24 h) or ondansetron 4–8 mg BD–TDS
Step 4 · refractoryTwo classes + famotidine 20 mg BD or omeprazole 20 mg OD; steroids only by obstetrics
  • Before every step: full dose; buccal, IM or IV if she vomits tablets; another cause?
  • Ondansetron: discuss the small first-trimester oral cleft risk. Metoclopramide: stop for dystonia. Doses per BNF, SPC and local maternity formulary.

6Monitor

  • 24–48 hours after starting: not better → step up, don't repeat a failing drug.
  • Weekly in hyperemesis: PUQE, weight, hydration. Dehydrated or weight falling → Same-day maternity
  • Settles by 16–20 weeks in most → review medicines. Mood again postnatally.

7Refer — early, not after repeat GP visits

  • Moderate/severe hyperemesis, weight loss, treatment failure or mental-health concern → obstetric team or EPAU; tell the midwife.
  • Confirmed molar pregnancy → trophoblastic disease centre (not NG12).
  • Perinatal mental health when indicated; Pregnancy Sickness Support 0800 055 4361.

8Never

  • Grade severity or dehydration on urinary ketones.
  • Wait for a thiamine level before giving thiamine.
  • Withhold antiemetics over teratogenicity fears, or dismiss it as "morning sickness".
  • Metoclopramide beyond 5 days or above 30 mg in 24 hours; ginger for hyperemesis.
  • Tell her to stop folic acid.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you become confused, get double vision or feel unsteady on your feet — call 999.” Also collapse or vomiting blood.   Same day “If you can't keep any fluids down for a day, your urine goes dark or you pass very little, you feel faint on standing, you get tummy pain or a fever, or you are losing weight — contact us or maternity the same day.”   After 16 weeks: “Sickness that starts or worsens — contact us the same day.”   Mood: “Thoughts of harming yourself — tell us the same day; if you might act on them, call 999.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Block 0 red flags excluded, incl. neurological signs PUQE, weight and clinical hydration documented Proportionate work-up; mood and suicidal thoughts asked Antiemetic ± thiamine; 24–48 h review booked Safety-net given and documented; fit note offered
