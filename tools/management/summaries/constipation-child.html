Constipation in children

For: children and young people with idiopathic (functional) constipation, including faecal impaction and overflow soiling. Not for: adults (use the adult constipation page); any red flag → refer, do not treat as idiopathic.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG99NICE QS62NICE NG12 (children)Rome IVGMMMG formulary
Core rule: exclude the red flags, disimpact before maintenance, then keep the macrogol going for months — stopping too soon is the commonest cause of relapse.

0Act now — any red flag: do not treat as idiopathic constipation

  • Bilious vomiting, severe distension, no stool or flatus (obstruction); acutely unwell child; neonate with vomiting → 999 / same-day ED
  • Delayed meconium (>48 h in a term baby), constipation from birth or the first few weeks, ribbon stools from birth, family history of Hirschsprung's → Urgent paediatrics .
  • Neurological or spinal signs: weak legs, abnormal tone, reflexes or gait; deep or atypical sacral dimple, hair tuft, abnormal gluteal cleft → Urgent paediatric assessment; imaging via the specialist pathway.
  • Abnormal anus, blood not explained by a fissure, abnormal thyroid gland; under 1 year with no response to optimal treatment within 4 weeks → Urgent paediatrics
  • Unexplained abdominal mass not clearly faecal, or not resolving with treatment → NG12 very urgent ≤48 h (neuroblastoma, Wilms') — not a laxative trial.

1Confirm — a positive diagnosis, not "not going enough"

NICE CG99 features (Rome IV only with its exact criteria) plus the Bristol Stool Chart. Find the trigger: toilet training, school, a fissure.

BristolTypes 1–2 constipated · 3–4 normal · 5–7 loose
CG99 feature<3 complete stools a week (not exclusively breastfed babies after 6 weeks) — with other features, not alone
SoilingWith infrequent, large or painful stools → usually overflow; confirm — true diarrhoea also occurs

2Check

☐ Plot height and weight  ☐ Abdomen: indentable faecal mass?  ☐ Perianal inspection: fissure, anal position  ☐ Spine, gluteal cleft, leg power, tone, reflexes  ☐ Thyroid

No routine rectal examination, abdominal X-ray or bloods. TFTs, coeliac serology (tTG IgA + total IgA) only selectively. Don't miss: faltering growth → refer, investigate selectively.

3Decide

  • Impacted (overflow soiling or palpable faecal mass) → disimpact first, then maintain.
  • Not impacted → maintenance macrogol straight away.
  • Behaviour alongside: sit after meals, feet on a footstool; reward sitting, not stools; no blame; school toilet access; ERIC. Fluid, less excess milk, fibre — never diet alone.

4Target

GoalRegular, soft, painless stools without soiling or withholding; no set daily frequency if well
DurationSeveral weeks after a regular habit (and toilet training established), then taper over months
RelapseRestart the previous dose immediately

5Treat — disimpaction, then maintenance

Phase 1 · disimpactEscalating oral macrogol; warn soiling may briefly worsen. Review ≤1 week; not cleared after 2 weeks → add a stimulant
Step 1 · maintainMacrogol as disimpaction completes; titrate to the goal. Do not stop after the clear-out
Step 2 · add stimulantStill not soft and painless → senna or sodium picosulfate at night. Macrogol not tolerated → lactulose
Step 4 · rectal, last resortOral failed → glycerol suppositories. Phosphate enema and manual evacuation: hospital only
  • Macrogol products are not interchangeable by sachet; check licensed age (off-label in young children). Doses per BNFC, product information and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Within 1 week: disimpaction complete (no soiling, mass gone) → maintenance.
  • Within 6 weeks: first maintenance review → adjust dose; reinforce routine, school, diet.
  • Not responding? Still impacted? Dose, adherence, behaviour, new red flags.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • No response within 3 months despite adequate disimpaction and maintenance, or recurrent impaction → paediatric constipation specialist.
  • CAMHS / psychology: toileting anxiety, school refusal, trauma, ASD with a severe behavioural component — alongside laxatives.
  • Safeguarding: soiling punished harshly, distress beyond expectation, inconsistent accounts → consult the named GP.

8Never

  • Treat constipation with a red flag as idiopathic.
  • Start maintenance in a loaded child — disimpact first.
  • Treat overflow soiling as diarrhoea, or blame the child for it.
  • Stop maintenance abruptly, or as soon as stools normalise.
  • Do a routine rectal examination, abdominal X-ray or blood tests, or put an unexplained non-faecal mass into a laxative trial.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Green (bile) vomit, tummy pain with a swollen tummy and vomiting, or your child becoming very unwell — call 999 or go to A&E.”   Same-day “Refusing all fluids, significant bleeding from the bottom, or new leg weakness or loss of bladder control — contact us or NHS 111 the same day.”
Within a week “Soiling getting much worse on the daily laxative — contact us within a week.”   Expect and persist: “Things get messier during the clear-out — that's expected. The leaking isn't naughtiness. Keep the laxative going for months; stopping early is the main reason it comes back.”
