Venous leg ulcer

For: adults with a leg ulcer in primary care and community nursing. Not for: foot wounds in diabetes (diabetic foot pathway, NICE NG19), children (specialist assessment), or atypical ulcers (block 0).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NWCSP lower limbNICE CG168NICE NG152NICE NG12SIGN 120
Core rule: exclude ischaemia, infection, DVT and cancer; start mild compression at the first visit; measure the ABPI within 14 days; then strong compression and a vascular referral for the veins.

0Act now — no compression until these are excluded

  • Acute limb ischaemia (sudden pain, pallor, pulseless, cold, paraesthesia) → 999 or Same-day vascular
  • Rest pain, gangrene, tissue loss or ABPI <0.5 → Urgent vascular referral; no compression.
  • Sepsis or rapidly spreading infection → Same-day hospital; 999 if any sepsis high-risk criterion
  • Suspected DVT → Wells score; ultrasound within 4 hours or interim anticoagulation (NICE NG158).
  • Rolled edge, overgranulation, pigmentation, bleeding, non-healing → Suspected cancer pathway referral (SCC or melanoma).
  • Diabetic foot wound → foot care service within 1 working day; Same-day if ischaemic or severely infected.

1Confirm — full assessment within 14 days

History, both legs, pulses, wound size and photograph, and ABPI (toe pressures in diabetes or calcified arteries). Venous: gaiter area, shallow, oedema, haemosiderin, eczema. Arterial: foot, punched out, rest pain.

2Check

☐ Red flags  ☐ Pulses and ABPI  ☐ FBC, HbA1c or glucose, renal function, albumin as indicated  ☐ Nutrition  ☐ Pain  ☐ Signs of infection. Do not swab without signs of infection.

3Decide

  • First visit, no red flags → immediate and necessary care: clean, simple non-adherent dressing, emollient, mild compression (up to 20 mmHg).
  • Venous, ABPI 0.8 to 1.3 → offer strong compression, applied by trained staff.
  • Lifestyle: walk, ankle exercises, elevate legs, weight, stop smoking, eat well.

4Treat

First visitMild compression (up to 20 mmHg) and skin care
By 14 daysFull assessment with ABPI
VenousStrong compression (40 mmHg or more): bandages, wraps or hosiery kit
HealedCompression hosiery for life
  • Infection only (spreading redness or swelling, warmth, increased pain, fever) → flucloxacillin 500 mg to 1 g four times daily for 7 days; allergy: doxycycline or clarithromycin; pregnancy: erythromycin. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.
  • Venous eczema → short course of moderately potent steroid on surrounding skin, not the ulcer bed.

5Monitor

  • Every 4 weeks: measure; less than about 40% smaller → review diagnosis and refer.
  • Antibiotics: reassess if worse, or not improved in 2 to 3 days.
  • Not healed at 12 weeks → specialist review; consider biopsy.

6Refer — non-emergency

  • Every active or healed venous ulcer → vascular service for duplex and endovenous ablation (NICE CG168, EVRA).
  • ABPI 0.5 to less than 0.8, or above 1.3 → vascular assessment before strong compression.
  • Atypical ulcer or suspected contact dermatitis → dermatology or tissue viability.

7Never

  • Strong compression without an ABPI or arterial assessment.
  • Compression with an ABPI below 0.5 or rest pain.
  • Antibiotics for a positive swab without signs of infection.
  • Months of dressings without a vascular referral.
  • Miss a malignant ulcer that is not healing.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Leg suddenly cold, pale and very painful — call 999.”   Same day “Toes numb, cold, pale or blue, or much worse pain — take the bandage off and contact us. Redness spreading up the leg, fever or feeling unwell — contact us or NHS 111.”
