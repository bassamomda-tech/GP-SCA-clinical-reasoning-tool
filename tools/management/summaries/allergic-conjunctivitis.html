Allergic conjunctivitis

For: adults and children with itchy red eyes in primary care. Not for: a painful or sight-threatening red eye (same-day eye pathway, block 0) or established VKC/AKC (ophthalmology-led). See also the allergic rhinitis, dry eye, blepharitis and uveitis pages.

Core rule: allergic conjunctivitis is a diagnosis of exclusion — check acuity, pupils, cornea, pain, photophobia and contact lenses first, and never start ocular steroids for an undifferentiated red eye in primary care.

0Act now — local urgent eye pathway, eye casualty or ED

  • Any reduction or change in vision, moderate or severe pain or photophobia, ciliary flush, or a fixed, irregular or abnormal pupil → Same-day eye assessment
  • Corneal opacity, infiltrate, ulcer or significant fluorescein staining, or suspected herpetic keratitis → Same-day eye assessment
  • Marked unilateral redness, recent ocular surgery or trauma, suspected scleritis, uveitis, acute angle-closure glaucoma or orbital/periorbital cellulitis → Same-day eye assessment
  • Contact-lens wearer with pain, photophobia, visual change, corneal staining or marked redness (possible microbial keratitis) → Same-day eye assessment; lenses out, bring lenses and case.
  • Suspected VKC or AKC with photophobia or corneal involvement, or photophobia in a child → Same-day ophthalmology advice

1Confirm — a diagnosis of exclusion

Prominent itch, usually both eyes; watery or stringy discharge, chemosis, lid swelling; often hay fever or atopy. Itch alone is not diagnostic — blepharitis, dry eye and lens problems itch too.

Marked unilateral, photophobia, visual reduction, ciliary injection, corneal opacity or significant pain → reconsider (block 0): keratitis, uveitis, herpes simplex, angle closure.

2Check — document before calling it allergy

☐ Acuity, each eye  ☐ Pupils  ☐ Cornea  ☐ Redness pattern, discharge, lids  ☐ Pain, photophobia  ☐ Contact lenses  ☐ Fluorescein if pain, foreign body, photophobia, lenses or trauma

Don't miss: a normal-looking cornea does not exclude keratitis. Can't exclude corneal or intraocular disease → optometry/ophthalmology, not empirical allergy treatment.

3Decide — self-care first

  • Everyone: trigger reduction (windows shut in high pollen, shower after outdoors, wraparound glasses), cool compresses, no rubbing; lenses out until resolved.
  • Mild seasonal symptoms → consider pharmacy self-care.
  • Rhinitis coexists → oral antihistamine and/or intranasal treatment; assess asthma.

4Target — choose the drop safely

PregnancyNon-drug first; cromoglicate if needed. Avoid olopatadine
BreastfeedingAvoid olopatadine; cromoglicate: product advice
LensesBenzalkonium in both: soft lenses out, wait ≥15 min
Frequent use, dry eyeConsider preservative-free

5Treat — Greater Manchester formulary

Step 1 · self-careTriggers, cool compresses, preservative-free lubricants
Step 2 · first choiceSodium cromoglicate 2% 1–2 drops each affected eye up to 4 times daily; seasonal → start before or at the beginning of the season
Step 3 · alternativeOlopatadine 1 mg/mL 1 drop twice daily; seasonal licence, 3+ years, ≤4 months
Persistent · severe · atypicalReconsider diagnosis, refer
  • Antazoline/xylometazoline: short term only, 12+, ≤7 consecutive days; avoid with lenses, narrow-angle glaucoma, MAOIs; caution in CVD, hypertension.
  • Rhinitis: adults cetirizine or loratadine 10 mg once daily; cetirizine may sedate (driving). Doses per BNF/BNFC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • No improvement or worsening → reassess diagnosis, adherence, lenses, preservatives.
  • Isolated eye symptoms despite drops → consider referral, not an oral antihistamine by default.

7Refer — same-day features → block 0

  • Ophthalmology (local pathway): suspected VKC (young people, ropy mucus) or AKC (atopic dermatitis); steroid consideration; persistent disease; uncertainty.
  • Allergy: troublesome despite optimised treatment, with a confirmed relevant allergen. No total IgE or broad panels.

8Never

  • Ocular corticosteroids for an undifferentiated red eye — they mask or worsen keratitis.
  • Allergy diagnosed without acuity, pupils, cornea and lens history.
  • Antibiotics for a lens wearer before assessment if keratitis is possible.
  • Antazoline/xylometazoline for more than 7 days, under 12, or with contact lenses.
  • Olopatadine in pregnancy or breastfeeding without specialist advice.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “If the eye becomes painful or sensitive to light, your vision changes, the redness becomes marked or one-sided, or you wear contact lenses and develop pain, light sensitivity, vision changes or marked redness — get same-day eye assessment.”   Lenses: “Take contact lenses out while it's flared.”   Drops: “Use them regularly — before and throughout the pollen season if it's hay fever.”   Cetirizine: “It can make you drowsy — take care with driving and alcohol.”
