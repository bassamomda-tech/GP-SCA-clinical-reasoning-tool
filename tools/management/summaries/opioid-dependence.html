Opioid dependence

For: adults dependent on heroin or other illicit opioids, including GP prescribing under shared care. Not for: under-18s (specialist children and young people's substance-misuse service) or analgesic opioid tapering in chronic pain (use the opioid prescribing protocol). Pregnancy → urgent joint drug-service and maternity care.

Core rule: people are far safer in treatment than out of it — never prescribe into an untolerant person, never restart an old dose after a gap, and give take-home naloxone to everyone.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Overdose: pinpoint pupils, slow or shallow breathing, reduced consciousness, cyanosis, snoring or gurgling → 999 + naloxone per product and local protocol, repeat as required; airway, recovery position; don't wait for testing. Never leave them alone — naloxone wears off first; hospital assessment even if they wake.
  • Lost tolerance (prison release, hospital discharge, detox, any break) → do not restart the old dose; take-home naloxone at this consultation.
  • Suspected infective endocarditis (fever + new murmur), serious injecting-related infection, or acute suicide risk → 999 / emergency
  • Pregnancy → Urgent joint drug service + maternity; do not abruptly stop methadone or buprenorphine.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis; testing supports

Dependence = ≥3 in the past year: craving, impaired control, withdrawal, tolerance, salience, use despite harm — from history, examination and withdrawal signs (yawning, sweating, dilated pupils, piloerection).

Urine or oral-fluid test supports, never replaces: negative doesn't exclude use; positive doesn't prove tolerance. Uncertain → confirmatory lab test + specialist advice before prescribing. Don't demand injection marks or a positive test if risk is high.

2Check — whole person, at assessment

☐ Drug history: opioids, route, last use, overdoses, alcohol, benzodiazepines, pregabalin  ☐ HIV, hepatitis B and C if ever injected; hepatitis B vaccine  ☐ Injection sites  ☐ Mood, self-harm, suicide risk  ☐ Safeguarding: children, pregnancy, domestic abuse  ☐ ECG before methadone if cardiac risk, QT drugs or higher doses

Don't miss: new pain, weight loss or bleeding — assess it, don't blame drug use.

3Decide — who starts what

  • Everyone dependent → refer promptly to the specialist drug service; OST starts there or under agreed shared care only.
  • Methadone or buprenorphine: both first line, chosen with the patient. Maintenance is valid long term; detox only if stable, voluntary and planned.
  • Everyone: naloxone, needle programme, keyworker, contingency management, mutual aid, stop smoking.

4Target — retention, not opioid-free

AimWhat it means
DoseIndividual: stops withdrawal and craving, cuts illicit use, no sedation. No universal dose
SupervisionUsually daily, at least 3 months; then take-home if stable
Ongoing illicit useUnder-dosing? Other drugs, diversion, housing?
After detoxRelapse-prevention support for at least 6 months

5Treat — specialist-initiated or shared care

MethadoneTypical start 10–30 mg (lower if tolerance uncertain); titrate slowly — accumulates. Highest risk first 2 weeks; QT
BuprenorphineSublingual 2–4 mg, only in objective withdrawal. Safer in overdose
Detox (specialist)Gradual taper, community up to 12 weeks; lofexidine may be considered
Naltrexone · relapse preventionOpioid-free, motivated, after detox. Blocks opioid analgesia; relapse after it is dangerous
  • Before any restart or change: verify prescription, last dose and tolerance with the service or pharmacy.
  • Avoid co-prescribing benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, pregabalin, gabapentin; if essential, lowest dose + naloxone, documented. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • ≥3 consecutive missed methadone doses or any gap → no automatic dose; contact service, reassess tolerance, re-titrate per local protocol.
  • Reviews + drug testing; ECG if higher methadone dose or new QT drug. Diversion → adjust supervision, don't stop abruptly.
  • Transitions (admission, prison, detox, new pharmacy) → verify and continue dose; reissue naloxone.
  • Driving: not if impaired. Dependence must be notified to the DVLA; stable supervised OST may be licensed, annual review.

7Refer

  • Drug service, urgently: severe mental illness, complex polydrug use, recurrent failed treatment, uncertain dependence or tolerance.
  • Mental health services: serious mental illness or complex dual diagnosis — “no wrong door”.
  • Hepatitis C → curative antiviral treatment. Under 18 → young people's substance-misuse service.

8Never

  • Methadone for someone not shown to be opioid-tolerant — it can be fatal.
  • Reinstate a previous dose on history alone after prison, hospital or a gap.
  • Stop OST abruptly — in pregnancy, on admission or in custody.
  • Treat maintenance as failure or push detox before the person is ready.
  • Leave someone alone after naloxone, or let a test result delay treatment when risk is high.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Hard to wake, breathing slowly or tiny pupils — give the naloxone, put them on their side, call 999. Don't leave them, even if they wake.”   After any break: “Tolerance drops — never go back to your old amount; tiny tester, never alone.”   Missed doses: “Miss 3 days or more of methadone and the pharmacy won't give the usual dose until we've checked.”   Kit: “Come back for another naloxone kit any time — used, lost or out of date.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Overdose, suicide and infection red flags excluded Dependence and tolerance assessed clinically, test in support BBV, mental health, safeguarding and pregnancy checked Drug service referral or shared-care plan; dose verified Naloxone supplied; safety-net given and documented
