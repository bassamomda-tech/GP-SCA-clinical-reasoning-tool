Raynaud's phenomenon in adults

For: adults with suspected Raynaud's in primary care. Not for: children (paediatric Raynaud's), established systemic sclerosis, critical limb ischaemia or specialist digital-ulcer treatment (specialist-led).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BSR systemic sclerosis 2024EULAR SSc 2023PCDSNifedipine SPCsHSE (HAVS)
Core rule: critical digital ischaemia decides the urgency — a digit that stays painful, pale or blue after warming is a same-day emergency; then weigh all features together to classify primary versus secondary, because no single age, ANA result or pattern decides it.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Digit stays painful and pale or blue despite gentle warming, new necrosis, gangrene, rapidly progressive ischaemia, or absent or markedly reduced pulses → Same-day emergency — do not wait for ANA or bloods.
  • Suspected acute arterial occlusion → Same-day emergency vascular
  • New digital ulcer → Urgent specialist; infected, deep, non-healing or with systemic sclerosis → same-day or rapid per local pathway; stable and superficial → urgent rheumatology plus wound care.
  • Asymmetric or single-digit attacks, abnormal pulses or Allen test → Vascular assessment or imaging, urgency by severity.
  • Connective tissue disease features (puffy fingers, sclerodactyly, dysphagia, breathlessness, arthritis, rash, weakness) → Rheumatology, urgency by severity and local pathway.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis

Recurrent, clearly demarcated pallor and/or cyanosis of fingers or toes, provoked by cold or emotional stress, improving with rewarming. Triphasic change is not required.

FeaturePrimary more likelySecondary more likely
OnsetYoung or long-standingLater onset — a risk feature, not a cut-off
PatternSymmetrical, no tissue damageAsymmetrical, severe, ulcers or pitting scars
Nailfolds, ANANormal; usually negativeAbnormal capillaries; clinically relevant ANA

Also later onset, male sex, puffy fingers, systemic symptoms → think secondary. Persistent, unilateral or not cold-related → consider mimics.

2Check — examine everyone; test selectively

☐ Radial and ulnar pulses; both-arm BP if vascular disease suspected  ☐ Hands: ulcers, pitting scars, sclerodactyly, puffy fingers, calcinosis  ☐ Nailfolds  ☐ Drugs: non-selective beta-blockers, ergot/triptans, decongestants, stimulants, nicotine  ☐ Vibrating tools

Typical primary → no routine tests. Atypical or secondary features → FBC, ESR/CRP, ANA, renal function and urinalysis; TFTs or CK if indicated.

Don't miss: low-titre ANA alone is not diagnostic; negative ANA does not exclude.

3Decide — who stays in primary care

  • Typical primary → primary care: lifestyle, diary, a CCB if troublesome.
  • Secondary features, no tissue loss → directed investigation; refer by findings. Drug cause → change it where safe.
  • Lifestyle for everyone: whole-body warmth, gloves, avoid sudden cold, rewarm with warm not scalding water, stop smoking, reduce vibration.

5Treat — lifestyle first, then nifedipine

Step 1Lifestyle only for mild primary; review if it worsens
Step 2 · troublesomeNifedipine prolonged-release, start low, titrate; trial for several weeks
Step 3 · not toleratedConsider amlodipine; seek advice in cardiac disease
Step 4 · refractory / secondaryRefer: PDE-5 inhibitor (off-label, specialist-initiated), IV iloprost
  • Prescribe the exact MR product (not interchangeable); seasonal use optional. Doses per SPC, BNF and local formulary.
  • Cautions: hypotension, aortic stenosis, acute coronary instability, hepatic impairment; clarithromycin and azoles raise levels.
  • Pregnancy: don't automatically withhold (BSR: compatible <90 mg/day); check SPC, seek maternal-medicine advice.
  • Ulcer: dressings, analgesia, antibiotics if infected, osteomyelitis check if deep, plus referral.

6Monitor

  • After starting or changing a vasodilator: review within a few weeks: BP, side effects, diary.
  • Stable primary: periodic review; do not repeat the ANA in an unchanged patient.
  • Re-assess for secondary disease if progressive, tissue damage, asymmetry or systemic features.

7Refer — non-emergency

  • Rheumatology, by severity: pitting scars, puffy fingers, sclerodactyly, abnormal nailfolds, relevant positive ANA with compatible features, later-onset unexplained disease.
  • Routine rheumatology: later onset under investigation without those features; refractory to lifestyle and a CCB; major functional impact.
  • Occupational health: suspected hand–arm vibration syndrome (HSE).

8Never

  • Delay same-day assessment of critical ischaemia while awaiting ANA or routine bloods.
  • Label persistent, unilateral or non-cold colour change as Raynaud's without considering mimics.
  • A PDE-5 inhibitor with nitrates or nicorandil.
  • Swap between modified-release nifedipine products as if interchangeable.
  • Stop a beta-blocker abruptly, or withdraw one needed for a cardiac indication without an alternative plan.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “A finger or toe that stays painful, pale or blue after warming, turns black, loses feeling or develops a sore — get seen the same day.”   Same-day “A new ulcer, non-healing wound, spreading redness, discharge or fever, rapidly worsening attacks, or joint pains, rashes, trouble swallowing, breathlessness or tight skin on the fingers — contact us the same day.”   Everyday: “Whole-body warmth and not smoking are the most effective measures.”
