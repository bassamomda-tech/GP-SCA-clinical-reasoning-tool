Chronic primary pain

For: aged 16+ with pain >3 months. Not for: chronic secondary pain; use its own guideline (neuropathic, sciatica, osteoarthritis, cancer).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG193NG215CG173NG12 (2026)NG234
Core rule: name it, re-screen red flags every visit, treat with exercise and ACT/CBT rather than do-not-start drugs, and reduce existing ones gradually, never abruptly.

0Act now — re-screen at every consultation; an established pain label hides new disease

  • Saddle anaesthesia, bilateral leg weakness or numbness, bladder or bowel dysfunction → 999 / emergency same-day (cauda equina).
  • Cancer history + new limb weakness, sensory loss or difficulty walking → MSCC coordinator Immediately; MRI ≤24 h; dexamethasone 16 mg if neuro signs.
  • Cancer history + new or changed spinal pain, no neurological signs → MSCC coordinator advice Within 24 h; MRI ≤1 week.
  • Back pain + fever, systemic upset or focal deficit; IV drugs or immunosuppressed → Same-day emergency assessment; 999 if septic (spinal infection).
  • Suicidal intent or plan, recent attempt, or unable to stay safe → Same-day mental-health assessment; stockpiling → shorter scripts, naloxone.
  • 60+, persistent bone or back pain or unexplained fracture → FBC, calcium, ESR or PV, protein electrophoresis + free light chains (myeloma). 60+, weight loss + back pain → consider CT Within 2 weeks (pancreas).

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis, not a failed search

Pain >3 months with no clear underlying condition, or pain or impact out of proportion to observable injury or disease, particularly with significant distress or disability. Real, not "psychological".

2Check — the whole person

☐ One countable function baseline  ☐ Mood, sleep, suicide risk (PHQ-9 or GAD-7 as aids only)  ☐ Every analgesic: dose, oral morphine equivalent, the function it buys; OTC codeine; alcohol  ☐ Work, social context, domestic abuse, safeguarding  ☐ Focused neurological exam. Targeted tests only; no routine imaging or broad panels.

3Decide — which guideline applies

  • Primary component → NG193; the do-not-start list applies. Secondary → its own guideline; the prohibitions do not transfer.
  • Explain first: "not on a scan" is not "not real". Agree one named activity today.

4Target — function, not the pain score

Function and quality of life against the baseline (activities, work, sleep, mood). Read the pain score last.

5Treat — the treatment is not a prescription

OfferSupervised group exercise: any modality, graded, patient-chosen
ConsiderACT or pain-focused CBT (Talking Therapies pain pathway, not a depression referral)
Consider · 18+Antidepressant, even without depression (off-label, say so): amitriptyline, citalopram, duloxetine, fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline
ConsiderOne acupuncture course within NG193 limits; sleep; social prescribing
  • Already on an opioid or gabapentinoid: review benefit, harms, withdrawal risk; continue only with a documented rationale, or agree a gradual, individualised NG215 reduction.
  • No TENS, ultrasound, interferential or biofeedback. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — suggested local framework, individualised

  • About 4 weeks: exercise started, therapy booked, reduction step, red flags. Antidepressant: benefit at about 4–6 weeks; stop if none.
  • 8–12 weeks, 3–6 months, yearly: function v baseline, mood, suicide risk, each medicine's benefit, harm, dependence.
  • Sedating medicines: falls, driving (not if impaired), safe storage.

7Refer — routine

  • Pain clinic: consider early advice for severe disability, high-dose or complex opioids, opioid use disorder, failed reductions, diagnostic doubt; not for escalation.
  • Physiotherapy: supervised graded exercise, not passive treatment alone.
  • Rheumatology: inflammatory pattern (waking in the second half of the night, better with exercise), synovitis, raised CRP or ESR.

8Never

  • Start opioids, gabapentinoids or other antiepileptics, paracetamol, NSAIDs, benzodiazepines, antipsychotics, ketamine, trigger-point injections or local anaesthetics for chronic primary pain (CRPS trial exceptions).
  • Stop an opioid, gabapentinoid or benzodiazepine abruptly, or impose a reduction without agreement and support.
  • Say "your scan is normal, so nothing is wrong", or imply the pain is psychological.
  • Blame new symptoms on the pain label without re-screening, or re-image a stable pattern without red flags.
  • Apply the do-not-start list to chronic secondary pain.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Numbness around your bottom or genitals, weakness in both legs, or new bladder or bowel problems: call 999, do not wait for an appointment.”   Same-day “New or worsening weakness, fever with back pain, night pain with weight loss, or new or changed back pain if you have had cancer: contact us the same day.”   Reduction: “Withdrawal, poor sleep or more pain after a reduction: tell us, and we can pause or slow down.”   Crisis: “If you feel you cannot go on: contact us the same day, call Samaritans on 116 123, or go to A&E.”
