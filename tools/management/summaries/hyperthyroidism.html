Hyperthyroidism / thyrotoxicosis

For: adults with a suppressed TSH or suspected thyrotoxicosis in primary care. Not for: pregnancy (joint endocrine–obstetric care, RCOG Green-top No. 76), children and young people (urgent endocrinology), or suspected thyroid storm (999).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG145 (updated Oct 2023)MHRA DSU Feb 2019BNFRCOG GTG 76 (2025)NG12 (Apr 2026)
Core rule: confirm with TSH, FT4 and FT3, ask “overproduction or leak?” (TRAb) before any antithyroid drug, and tell everyone on carbimazole or PTU, verbally and in writing: sore throat, mouth ulcers or fever → stop the tablet, same-day FBC.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected thyroid storm: fever with marked tachycardia or AF, plus any of confusion or agitation, heart failure, or vomiting, diarrhoea or jaundice → 999 — never wait for TFTs.
  • Rapid AF with hypotension, breathlessness or chest pain, acute heart failure, or adrenal crisis (collapse, vomiting, postural hypotension) → 999
  • On carbimazole or PTU: sore throat, mouth ulcers, fever or any infection → stop it, Same-day FBC; septic → neutropenic-sepsis pathway now.
  • Reduced acuity, colour-vision loss, globe subluxation or corneal exposure → Same-day ophthalmology
  • Uncontrolled hyperthyroidism in pregnancy → Same-day obstetric and endocrine review
  • Unexplained thyroid lump, any TSH → consider Suspected cancer pathway (2 weeks); with hoarseness, nodes, rapid growth, fixed mass, a child or neck irradiation → refer on it; stridor → Same-day

1Confirm — never treat a low TSH alone

PatternTSHFT4 / FT3

Always FT3 too. Borderline or acutely unwell → repeat first. Ask about biotin.

2Check — order with the TFTs

☐ TRAb (negative → technetium scan via secondary care) ☐ Baseline FBC, LFTs before any antithyroid drug ☐ ECG if palpitations or irregular pulse ☐ Gland, nodes, eyes ☐ Drugs ☐ Pregnancy plans. Don’t miss: older people — check TFTs in new AF.

3Decide — overproduction or leak?

  • Graves’ or toxic nodular → refer; antithyroid drug by, or on advice of, specialist.
  • Thyroiditis → supportive: beta-blocker if needed; NSAID if painful, where safe. No antithyroid drug.
  • Amiodarone → endocrinology; never stop it unilaterally.
  • Subclinical: two TSH <0.1 ≥3 months apart + thyroid disease or symptoms → consider specialist advice.

Lifestyle: stop smoking (eyes), no kelp/iodine, less caffeine.

5Treat — doses per BNF and local formulary

Symptom controlBy severity, if no contraindication: propranolol 10–40 mg three or four times daily.
Carbimazole · first choiceShared care or specialist advice: 15–40 mg daily → 5–15 mg. Graves’: 12–18 months.
Block-and-replaceSpecialist, Graves’ only: 40–60 mg + levothyroxine. Not in pregnancy.
DefinitiveRadioiodine (first-line for Graves’) or surgery. Expect hypothyroidism.
  • Beta-blocker: check asthma, bradycardia, heart block, hypotension, PVD; decompensated heart failure → specialist.
  • Pancreatitis history → consider PTU. Stable new AF: beta-blocker; assess anticoagulation now.

6Monitor — NG145: consider

  • On a drug: TSH, FT4, FT3 6-weekly until TSH in range, then TSH 3-monthly. Repeat the agranulocytosis warning.
  • FBC, LFTs: baseline, then only if symptoms.
  • After stopping: TSH within 8 weeks, 3-monthly for a year, then yearly.
  • After radioiodine: TSH, FT4, FT3 6-weekly for 6 months. After surgery: early calcium.

7Refer — endocrinology

  • Urgent new Graves’, pregnancy or planning, under 18, active eye disease, severe disease.
  • Routine toxic nodular, relapsed Graves’, amiodarone-induced.
  • Radioiodine unsuitable in pregnancy, breastfeeding, active eye disease; avoid pregnancy ≥6 months after.

8Never

  • Carbimazole for thyroiditis, or for a low TSH with normal FT4/FT3 before cause and persistence are known.
  • Titrate on TSH alone — it stays suppressed for weeks to months.
  • Carbimazole without contraception advice; block-and-replace or radioiodine in pregnancy; a self-directed switch to or from PTU.
  • Re-expose after carbimazole pancreatitis, or re-challenge after agranulocytosis without specialist direction.
  • Take a suppressed TSH as reassurance about a thyroid lump.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “High fever with a racing or irregular heart, sudden confusion or drowsiness, severe palpitations with chest pain or collapse, or vomiting and unable to keep fluids down — call 999.”   Same-day “Sore throat, mouth ulcers, fever or any infection — stop the tablets and contact us the same day for a blood test. The same for yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, tummy pain or severe upper abdominal pain.”   Eyes: “Eye pain, double vision, blurred vision or washed-out colours — same-day eye assessment.”   Pregnancy: “Use reliable contraception, tell us before trying for a baby, and don’t change or stop your tablets yourself.”
