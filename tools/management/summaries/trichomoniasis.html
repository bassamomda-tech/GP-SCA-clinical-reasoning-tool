Trichomoniasis

For: suspected or confirmed Trichomonas vaginalis in general practice, women and men, including pregnancy, breastfeeding and partners. Not for: pelvic pain or fever (use the PID protocol), a suspicious cervix or vaginal mass (NG12, block 0), or a child or young person with safeguarding concerns (block 0).

BASHH TV 2021BASHH STI testing 2023BHIVA/BASHH/BIA HIV testing 2020NICE NG12 (Apr 2026)UKTIS
Core rule: confirm with a TV NAAT, not a wet mount; treat women with 7 days of oral metronidazole; treat current partners at the same time, whatever their own result.

0Act now — any of these moves the patient off this page

  • Positive pregnancy test with pain and abdominal, pelvic or cervical motion tenderness (possible ectopic) → Immediate EPAU / out-of-hours gynaecology; collapsed or haemodynamically unstable → 999
  • Severe pelvic pain, peritonism, or severe PID with systemic illness → Same-day hospital assessment. Pelvic pain with cervical motion tenderness or fever → PID assessment (PID protocol).
  • Cervix looks consistent with cervical cancer, or unexplained palpable mass in or at the entrance to the vagina → consider a Suspected cancer pathway referral.
  • Sexual activity under 13 → Same-day referral through local safeguarding procedures. Any STI under 16 or signs of exploitation → age-appropriate safeguarding assessment (competence, consent, partner age, coercion).

1Confirm — TV NAAT is the test of choice

Women: discharge (frothy yellow-green only sometimes), vulval itch, soreness, dysuria, odour, dyspareunia; many have none. Men: most have none. Frothy discharge or pH >4.5 is suggestive, not diagnostic.

TestUse
TV NAATVulvovaginal swab (self-taken fine) in women; first-void urine or urethral swab in men. Request it specifically if not included with chlamydia/gonorrhoea.
Wet mountMisses many; a negative does not exclude TV
Vaginal pHSupportive only: >4.5 in TV and BV, normal in candida
CytologyIncidental TV → confirm with NAAT before treating where possible

2Check — it is an STI: screen for the rest

☐ Sexual history, partners, contraception, pregnancy ☐ Speculum: discharge, vulvitis, cervix ☐ Chlamydia + gonorrhoea NAAT, syphilis, HIV; hepatitis B/C by risk; pregnancy test if relevant ☐ BV and candida (mixed infection common)

Don't miss: recurrent “BV” may be TV kept alive by an untreated partner.

3Decide — patient and partners

  • Every new diagnosis → treat, and involve sexual health for partner notification and a full STI screen.
  • Current partners → treat at the same time, without waiting for their result; a negative male NAAT does not exclude TV.
  • Partners in the 4 weeks before presentation (or the last partner) → offer testing and treatment via sexual health.
  • Pregnancy: treat symptomatic infection promptly with the 7-day course; no routine screening if asymptomatic. Prevention: condoms.

5Treat — oral nitroimidazoles only

Women (preferred)Metronidazole 400–500 mg twice daily for 7 days, including pregnancy and breastfeeding.
MenCurrent BASHH/local sexual-health pathway (2 g once in some cases; 7 days may be preferred after failure).
AlternativeMetronidazole 2 g once: less effective in women; selected cases, after sexual-health advice.
Not clearedAdherent, reinfection unlikely → repeat 7-day course. Fails again → specialist-led (tinidazole, higher doses).
  • No vaginal metronidazole gel — it does not reach urethral and paraurethral reservoirs.
  • Interactions: warfarin (check INR), lithium, busulfan. Breastfeeding: generally compatible; caution after 2 g. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — no routine test of cure

  • Symptoms settle, partner treated → no test of cure.
  • Persist or recur, or partner treatment uncertain → NAAT no earlier than about 4 weeks after treatment (sooner only after specialist discussion).
  • Work through: missed doses → untreated or new partner → co-infection → re-examine the cervix.

7Refer — soon, non-urgent

  • Sexual health: every new diagnosis, men for treatment, failure after a repeat course, metronidazole allergy (no non-nitroimidazole alternative), HIV co-infection, safeguarding in a young person.
  • Maternity team: symptomatic TV in pregnancy.
  • Won't attend: document, written partner information, treat partners registered with you.

8Never

  • Treat recurrent “BV” with repeated metronidazole without a TV NAAT and asking about partners.
  • Use vaginal metronidazole gel for TV, or substitute an unrelated antimicrobial in metronidazole allergy.
  • Wait for a partner's result before treating them, or accept a negative male NAAT as all-clear.
  • Give a high single 2 g dose as the preferred regimen in pregnancy.
  • Prescribe again for persisting discharge without examining the cervix: abnormal cervix or vaginal mass → NG12, not another antibiotic.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you have severe tummy pain and feel faint or collapse, call 999.”   Same-day “Tummy or pelvic pain, a fever, or pain or bleeding while pregnant — contact us the same day.”   Metronidazole: “Twice a day for a week; finish the course. No alcohol while taking it and for the time the leaflet advises afterwards, usually two days. No sex — vaginal, anal or oral, including with anyone new — until you've both finished and for at least a week.”   Not settled: “Come back for a repeat test.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Block 0 excluded: ectopic, PID, cervix, safeguarding TV confirmed by NAAT; cervix examined Pregnancy status; chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis, HIV offered 7-day course, partner treatment and abstinence agreed Safety-net given and documented
