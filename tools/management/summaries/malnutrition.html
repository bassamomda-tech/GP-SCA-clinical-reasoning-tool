Malnutrition in adults

For: adults in general practice, the housebound and care homes. Not for: children or young people (safeguarding, paediatrics or CAMHS) or a suspected eating disorder (MEED CR233 and NICE NG69 — adult eating disorder service).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG32BAPEN MUSTNICE NG97RCPsych MEED CR233NICE NG12
Core rule: score MUST on anyone at risk, check refeeding risk and potassium, phosphate and magnesium before feeding, and give thiamine before or alongside feeding — but never delay emergency glucose for severe hypoglycaemia.

0Act now — the two killers are refeeding syndrome and missed Wernicke encephalopathy

  • Confusion in a malnourished patient (suspected Wernicke) → Emergency transfer; parenteral thiamine first if available, without delaying transfer.
  • Severe dehydration, instability, delirium, arrhythmia or severe electrolyte derangement, or feeding unsafe at home → Same-day hospital
  • Eating disorder, MEED red or 2+ amber (e.g. BMI <13, HR <40, QTc >450) → Same-day specialist
  • Unexplained malnutrition in an adult with capacity concerns → Safeguarding referral immediately (MASH or adult social care).
  • Unexplained weight loss, cancer not excluded → NG12 route (block 7) Urgent. MUST ≥2 alone is not a same-day trigger → care plan and dietitian Within 2 weeks

2Check — the cause, on three axes

☐ PHQ-9  ☐ Mouth and dentures  ☐ Medicines (opioids, metformin, digoxin, SSRIs)  ☐ Swallow, alcohol  ☐ FBC, U&E/eGFR; others as the history directs

“In the last twelve months, have you been worried whether your food would run out before you had money to buy more?”

Don't miss: albumin never diagnoses or excludes malnutrition.

5Treat — food first, then ONS, then tube

Step 1 · food firstFortify; small meals every 2–3 hours; written advice. Reassess 4–8 weeks
Step 2 · ONSIndication + ACBS criteria + local formulary; typically 1–3 a day, not a meal
Step 3 · enteralOral unsafe or inadequate, gut works, realistic benefit
Step 4 · parenteralGut not usable — specialist team only
  • Don't delay ONS if malnutrition is significant, deteriorating or high risk.
  • Depression → standard guidance; mirtazapine may be considered with poor appetite (not for appetite alone).
  • Alcohol: oral thiamine 100 mg TDS prophylaxis — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Weight weekly if high risk, then monthly; MUST at every review.
  • High refeeding risk: K⁺, PO₄, Mg²⁺ daily initially, then 3× weekly until stable, then weekly; fluid balance, glucose.
  • ONS review within about 4–6 weeks → continue, modify or stop; stop when normal food is enough.

7Refer

  • NG12: 60+, unexplained loss >5% in 6 months → assess, then urgent investigation or suspected cancer / non-specific pathway. 55+ with loss + dyspepsia, reflux or upper abdominal pain → upper GI pathway. 40+ with loss + abdominal pain → FIT.
  • Dietitian: MUST ≥2 (local pathway). SALT: dysphagia.
  • Social prescribing for food insecurity or isolation; gastroenterology for malabsorption.

8Never

  • Start feeding a high-refeeding-risk patient without electrolytes, thiamine and a slow start.
  • Delay emergency glucose for severe hypoglycaemia while waiting for thiamine.
  • Treat suspected Wernicke with oral thiamine, or wait for the full triad.
  • Skip MUST because the patient cannot be weighed.
  • Routinely tube-feed severe dementia unless reversible; ONS without indication or review date.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 / Same-day “If you become confused or unsteady on your feet, your heart races or skips, or you get breathless or new ankle swelling, you need to be seen in hospital the same day — ring us straight away, or NHS 111 if we are closed; call 999 if you collapse or have chest pain.”   Refeeding: “We will build your intake up gradually and check your blood salts closely at first.”   Progress: “Strength and energy often come back before the weight does.”
