HIV in primary care (adults)

For: adults — testing, PEP/PrEP routes, giving results and shared care. Not for: ART choice or monitoring (HIV team), pregnancy care (urgent HIV and antenatal team), or children (HIV or paediatric service).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BHIVA/BASHH/BIA testing 2020NICE NG60BHIVA ART 2022 (2025 update)UK PEP 2021BASHH/BHIVA PrEP 2025
Core rule: offer a test with every indicator condition, send any exposure within 72 hours for PEP today, and check every new prescription against the recorded ART — never stop or alter ART without HIV-team advice.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Meningism or headache with fever, focal neurology, seizure or new confusion (cryptococcus, toxoplasmosis) → 999 emergency admission
  • Exertional breathlessness, dry cough, desaturation on exertion (PCP); severe oral/oesophageal candidiasis with weight loss; suspected TB, systemically ill → Same-day admission
  • Visual loss or floaters (CMV retinitis) → Same-day ophthalmology and inform the HIV team.
  • Exposure ≤72 h (condom failure, assault, shared injecting kit) → Same-day PEP at sexual health, or ED out of hours. Never a routine appointment; don't decide eligibility or let testing delay it. Assault → also SARC.
  • New confirmed diagnosis → phone the HIV service now; seen ≤2 weeks, but Same day / urgent if unwell, pregnant, seroconverting, opportunistic infection, very low CD4 or high psychosocial risk.
  • Can't take ART (vomiting, nil by mouth, admitted) or a missed long-acting injection → Urgent HIV team for uninterrupted ART or a bridging plan.

1Confirm — offer the test as routine; no lengthy pre-test counselling

Anywhere: indicator condition, seroconversion symptoms, any STI, partner with HIV, injecting, sex between men (and their female partners), high-prevalence country links, sex work, anyone who asks. High-prevalence areas (≥2 per 1,000, UKHSA data): offer at GP registration and with bloods if untested in 12 months.

2Check — indicator conditions: offer a test

☐ Shingles, younger adult or several dermatomes  ☐ Severe seborrhoeic dermatitis or psoriasis  ☐ Oral candida, recurrent thrush or herpes  ☐ Glandular-fever-like illness  ☐ Unexplained lymphadenopathy, weight loss, cytopenia or chronic diarrhoea  ☐ Hepatitis B/C, any STI

Don't miss: a negative test in seroconversion does not exclude HIV — tell the lab “query seroconversion”, repeat at ≥45 days, condoms and no blood donation meanwhile.

3Decide — result, linkage and record

  • Agree how the result is given; reactive or positive → face to face.
  • Confirmed: treatable, near-normal lifespan; sustained undetectable on ART = untransmittable sexually (U=U). Check safety and suicide risk.
  • ART for everyone, any CD4 — specialist-initiated, lifelong. Confirm the patient attended.
  • Partner notification is led by HIV/sexual health. Ask about children — test however well they appear.
  • Code HIV; record the full ART regimen, incl. injectables, so alerts fire.

5Treat — prevention and every new prescription

PEPIdeally ≤24 h, no later than 72 h; 28-day course via sexual health or ED
PrEPSexual health prescribes and monitors. Need (STI, esp. rectal or syphilis; after PEP; partner not suppressed) → refer
BoostersRitonavir/cobicistat: no simvastatin; avoid fluticasone, budesonide, steroid injections
OtherNo PPI with oral rilpivirine; space cations from integrase inhibitors; enzyme inducers
  • Before every script: Liverpool checker; unsure → HIV pharmacist. Doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • Enzyme-inducing ART (e.g. efavirenz) → IUD or depot; EC: copper IUD, else levonorgestrel 3 mg, no ulipristal.

6Monitor — shared care per the clinic letter

  • Viral load, CD4: HIV clinic. Detectable → check interactions, vomiting/diarrhoea, adherence.
  • Creatinine: benign rise with dolutegravir, bictegravir, cobicistat, rilpivirine; true toxicity with tenofovir disoproxil.
  • CVD at least annually from 40: QRISK3 underestimates risk; review statin eligibility (BHIVA) with an interaction check.
  • Vaccines: flu yearly, pneumococcal, hepatitis B. No live vaccine if CD4 <200, ask HIV team first; never BCG.
  • Cervical screening: HIV-specific annual recall where applicable — set at diagnosis.

7Refer

  • Pregnant with HIV → Urgent HIV and antenatal team; continue ART.
  • Anal pain, bleeding or mass → Urgent pathway.
  • Sexual health: PEP, PrEP, STIs, partners, HPV vaccine (GBMSM ≤45).

8Never

  • Book a routine appointment for PEP, or let an HIV test delay it.
  • Call a reactive screen “HIV positive” before laboratory confirmation.
  • Stop or alter ART yourself for an interaction, admission or vomiting.
  • Prescribe fluticasone, budesonide or simvastatin with a booster.
  • Reassure on a negative test inside the window (45 days lab; up to 90 days point-of-care), or default to 3- or 5-year cervical recall.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A fit, new weakness or confusion, or a severe headache with a fever or stiff neck — call 999.”   Same-day “Breathless when you walk, changes in your vision, or you can't keep your HIV tablets down — contact us or the HIV clinic the same day.”   Medicines: “Before any new medicine, even indigestion remedies, supplements or nasal sprays, check with us or your pharmacist.”   Window: “This negative test isn't final — repeat it at 45 days; use condoms and don't give blood until then.”
