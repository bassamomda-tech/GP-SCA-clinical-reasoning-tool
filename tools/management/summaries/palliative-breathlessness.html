Palliative breathlessness

For: adults with breathlessness in advanced cancer, COPD, heart failure or ILD. Not for: breathlessness with no established diagnosis (NG12 route, block 7), or full last-days and SVCO care (use the care of the dying and SVCO protocols).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG31NG115NG106BTS home oxygenMHRA DSU 2020
Core rule: look for a new, treatable cause first; then the fan and breathing control for everyone, a monitored low-dose opioid trial for refractory breathlessness, and no routine oxygen without hypoxaemia.

0Act now — a new emergency on top of advanced illness; act if it fits the patient's goals and care plan

  • Stridor, suspected tension pneumothorax, massive haemoptysis or acute pulmonary oedema → 999 where hospital treatment fits the patient's goals.
  • Swelling of the face, neck and arms (SVCO) without stridor → Same-day immediate admission via acute oncology or emergency pathway.
  • Sudden breathlessness with pleuritic pain, haemoptysis or a swollen leg → possible PE; investigate and anticoagulate by prognosis, bleeding risk and wishes — a deliberate decision, not a default.
  • Drowsy, pinpoint pupils or slow breathing on an opioid ± benzodiazepine → review now: doses, renal function, co-prescribed sedatives; monitor closely.
  • Severe COPD, hypercapnia or NIV → local respiratory/palliative advice; avoid excess oxygen. Escalating last-days distress → Same-day specialist palliative advice.

1Assess — usually more than one cause; fear makes it worse

  • Name the goals first (home, avoiding hospital, comfort only); match investigation to prognosis and goals.
  • SpO₂ (incl. on exertion) for a new or worsening symptom; not routinely in a comfortable dying person — treat distress, not the number.

2Check — reversible causes, at first visit and each deterioration

☐ Effusion ☐ Infection ☐ Bronchospasm ☐ Heart failure / fast AF ☐ Anaemia ☐ PE ☐ SVCO / lymphangitis ☐ Ascites ☐ Sedatives ☐ Secretions ☐ Constipation ☐ Anxiety

Don't miss: a new cause on top of "progression". Steroids only for selected causes (airway obstruction, SVCO, lymphangitis, bronchospasm).

3Non-drug measures — everyone, before any drug

  • Handheld fan on the face; sit upright, lean forward; open a window.
  • Pursed-lip and relaxed breathing, practised when calm; pacing, sit to wash, OT equipment.
  • Written plan for breathless episodes; teach carers the same; continue in the last days.

4Oxygen — not routinely for breathlessness alone

Not hypoxaemic, or mild above LTOT thresholdsNo palliative oxygen
Symptomatic hypoxaemia, known or suspectedConsider (NG31: offer only to these); long-term → home-oxygen pathway
Advanced heart failureNo long-term home oxygen (unless COPD)
If givenTrial with a goal; fire risk; stop if no benefit

5Drugs — after reversible causes and non-drug measures

1 · Opioid-naive or frailConsider a monitored low-dose oral IR morphine trial for refractory breathlessness + laxative. Check cognition, sedation, RR, renal.
2 · Benefit, regular needLowest effective dose; consider low-dose MR. Little extra gain above ~30 mg/24 h (not a ceiling) — advice first.
3 · Already on an opioidConsider the existing breakthrough dose; high, escalating or unclear → specialist advice.
4 · Renal impairmentNo routine morphine escalation; specialist advice, especially eGFR <30 or changing.
  • Benzodiazepines: not routine for chronic breathlessness; consider for panic (lorazepam 500 micrograms SL PRN) or last-days distress. With an opioid (MHRA): lowest doses, document, monitor — never withhold end-of-life symptom control.
  • Plan ahead: individualised anticipatory medicines (not automatic opioid + midazolam); OOH told. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Review

  • Opioid trial: review within a few days; continue only if clear benefit outweighs harm, else stop or reduce.
  • Repeated PRN doses → review cause; consider a syringe pump.

7Refer

  • Urgent pleural drainage or catheter; stenting or radiotherapy.
  • Specialist palliative care: refractory symptoms; opioid intolerance, renal impairment, frailty, uncertainty; opioid + benzodiazepine; hypercapnia or NIV.
  • No established diagnosis, ≥40: offer urgent direct-access chest X-ray for ≥2 unexplained symptoms, or ≥1 if ever smoked (NG12).

8Never

  • Treat every change as "the disease progressing" — PE, effusion, SVCO and pulmonary oedema are treatable.
  • Routine oxygen for breathlessness without hypoxaemia; excessive oxygen in hypercapnia.
  • Routine benzodiazepines for chronic breathlessness itself.
  • An opioid with no laxative, no review date, or escalation in renal impairment without advice.
  • Apply last-days (NG31) recommendations automatically to everyone with advanced disease.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Attack plan: “Sit forward, fan on your face, breathe out slowly through pursed lips, and take the morphine dose on your plan if we've agreed it.”   Now, day or night “Not settling → ring the 24-hour number on your care plan. Sudden breathlessness, chest pain, coughing blood, noisy breathing, a swollen face or arms, or very sleepy with slow breathing after a dose → ring 999, or the 24-hour number if you've chosen not to go to hospital.”   Within a day “Steadily worse despite the plan, new fever or cough, or morphine side effects.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
