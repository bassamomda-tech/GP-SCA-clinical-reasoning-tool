Shingles (herpes zoster)

For: adults with shingles in primary care, including pregnancy and immunosuppression. Not for: children (seek paediatric advice rather than treating as routine) or chickenpox; exposed newborns go to paediatrics and virology (block 0).

Core rule: check the eye, the ear and the immune system first; give an antiviral by timing and risk, not to everyone, adjusted for eGFR; and treat pain early, because post-herpetic neuralgia is what patients live with.

0Act now — whatever the day of the rash

  • Ophthalmic zoster (Hutchinson's sign lowers the threshold) → oral antiviral today. Red or painful eye, reduced vision, photophobia, corneal or uveal signs, marked lid swelling, hard to examine, significant immunosuppression or doubt → Same-day ophthalmology Quiet eye → urgent eye advice (local pathway).
  • Ramsay Hunt: ear or palate vesicles with a lower motor neurone facial palsy → antiviral and Same-day ENT / acute team; protect the eye if it does not close.
  • Disseminated or multidermatomal rash, new lesions despite oral treatment, systemic or visceral symptoms, severe immunosuppression with extensive disease, cannot take oral treatment → Same-day hospital
  • Confusion, drowsiness, severe headache, neck stiffness, focal signs → Same-day hospital Seizure, stroke or reduced consciousness → 999
  • Newborn: mother with chickenpox 7 days either side of delivery, or exposed to chickenpox or shingles in week 1 → Same-day paediatrics + virology
  • Susceptible pregnant or immunosuppressed contact → Urgent VZV IgG and prophylaxis assessment (virology, health protection, specialist or obstetric team).

1Confirm — the diagnosis is clinical

One-sided pain, then grouped vesicles on a red base in a dermatome, stopping at the midline. Time runs from rash onset. PCR swab (fresh vesicle base) only if atypical, several dermatomes, immunosuppressed or possible herpes simplex; do not wait for it to treat.

2Check — every patient

☐ Nose, eye, vision  ☐ Ear, facial nerve, hearing  ☐ Immune status  ☐ Rash day  ☐ Pregnancy  ☐ eGFR if CKD, frail, dehydrated or older  ☐ Vulnerable contacts. Multidermatomal or recurrent zoster → offer an HIV test.

3Decide — who gets an antiviral

  • ≤72 h from rash onset → offer if over 50, immunosuppressed, non-truncal, moderate or severe pain or rash, or high-risk site or risk factor.
  • 3–7 days → consider if new vesicles, severe pain, significant rash, immunosuppressed, or high complication risk (70+ supports this).
  • Eye and ear zoster → treat whenever active. Otherwise → analgesia, skin care, explain why not.

4Contacts and Shingrix

  • Gives chickenpox to the non-immune until all lesions crusted. Keep covered; avoid pregnant, newborn and immunosuppressed people.

5Treat — 7 days; start today

Aciclovir800 mg five times daily (4-hourly, omit night dose). CrCl 10–25: 800 mg TDS; <10: 800 mg BD
Valaciclovir1 g three times daily. Immunosuppressed: at least 7 days and 2 days after crusting
Famciclovir500 mg three times daily. Not routinely in pregnancy (use aciclovir or valaciclovir)
PainRegular paracetamol ± NSAID; short codeine + laxative; tramadol rescue only
  • Renal: dose-adjust all three antivirals (SPC); advise fluids.
  • Neuropathic pain → consider amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin or pregabalin; check renal function, falls, sedation, interactions.
  • No NSAID in CKD, frailty, ulcer, anticoagulation or heart failure. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — and post-herpetic neuralgia

  • New confusion, hallucinations, tremor or drowsiness on aciclovir or valaciclovir → stop and review (can mimic encephalitis).
  • Older people: book an early review (falls, sedation, renal toxicity).
  • PHN = pain ≥90 days → offer a choice of the four neuropathic drugs; switch if not effective. Consider capsaicin cream on healed skin if oral drugs unwanted. Gabapentinoids: Class C, dependence.

7Refer — non-emergency

  • Pregnant with severe, disseminated, ophthalmic or complicated shingles → Urgent obstetric advice; start treatment, do not wait.
  • Immunosuppressed but well, localised → oral antiviral, contact the specialist team, close review.
  • PHN uncontrolled on first-line options → routine pain service.

8Never

  • Withhold an antiviral in active eye or ear zoster because it is past 72 hours.
  • Aciclovir or valaciclovir without eGFR in CKD, frailty, dehydration or old age.
  • Routine systemic steroids to prevent PHN; topical aciclovir for shingles.
  • Long-term strong opioids for PHN started in primary care.
  • An exposed newborn or susceptible pregnant contact left to routine care.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A fit, sudden weakness in an arm or leg, or trouble speaking — call 999.”   Same day “Red or painful eye, sight change, face drooping, rash spreading, feeling very unwell, bad headache or confusion — be seen the same day.”   Contacts: “Keep it covered until scabbed; avoid pregnant people, newborns and weak immune systems. If you've been close to one, tell us the same day.”
