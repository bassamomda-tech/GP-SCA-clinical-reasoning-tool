Benzodiazepines and z-drugs

For: adults when a benzodiazepine or z-hypnotic is considered, and every long-term user. Not for: epilepsy, alcohol withdrawal, very high-dose, illicit or multiple-source use, palliative care, catatonia, spasticity → specialist advice before withdrawal (block 0). See also insomnia, anxiety.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG215TA77CG113CG192MHRA
Core rule: lowest dose, shortest course, acute script, with the stop plan agreed before the first prescription — and never stop abruptly after prolonged use: taper slowly, at the patient's pace.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • A fit, new confusion or hallucinations during a reduction, or very slow or shallow breathing or hard to wake (especially with opioids, gabapentinoids or alcohol) → 999
  • Abrupt stop after prolonged use — including a hospital discharge or care-home transfer that drops the drug → same hazard: seizures, delirium, severe rebound. Taper over weeks to months instead.
  • Opioid or gabapentinoid co-prescribing inherited → review as a priority. MHRA: with an opioid only if no alternative; lowest doses, shortest time, monitor breathing.
  • Epilepsy, previous withdrawal seizures, alcohol withdrawal, polysubstance dependence, severe psychiatric illness, pregnancy needing rapid review → specialist advice before any reduction.

1Confirm — is it genuinely short-term and severe?

Only short-term, severe, disabling anxiety (crisis) or insomnia. Not for mild or chronic symptoms, distress alone, GAD, or panic disorder (CG113: should not be prescribed).

LimitDuration
PracticeAcute script ≤2 weeks; hypnotic preferably <1 week
MHRA / TA77≤2–4 weeks in total incl. taper (zaleplon ≤2 weeks)

Exclude: intoxication, withdrawal, delirium, mania, psychosis, severe depression, sleep apnoea, respiratory compromise, misuse.

2Check — before a script and at every review

☐ Alcohol, opioids, gabapentinoids, illicit drugs  ☐ Falls, cognition, sedation, driving  ☐ Respiratory disease, sleep apnoea  ☐ Hepatic and renal function  ☐ Pregnancy or breastfeeding  ☐ Previous withdrawal attempts, goals

Don't miss: restless legs, OSA or another sleep disorder posing as insomnia → treat that, not a hypnotic.

3Decide

  • Non-drug first: CBT-I, sleep hygiene; Talking Therapies for anxiety. Treat the driver.
  • If prescribing: agree dose, stop date and review first; further scripts not usually given.
  • Review all long-term users; prioritise 65+, falls, cognitive impairment, sedation, respiratory disease, opioid/gabapentinoid use. Gains: alertness, cognition, fewer falls.
  • Decision aid; dose reduction may be the first goal. Declines → no abrupt stop or pressure; document, keep reviewing.
  • Pregnancy (CG192): do not offer, except short-term for severe anxiety and agitation; established use → don't stop abruptly, seek perinatal advice.

5Treat — doses are examples; per BNF and local formulary

If you mustInsomnia: zopiclone 3.75–7.5 mg (3.75 mg elderly) or zolpidem 5–10 mg (5 mg elderly) at night. Crisis anxiety: diazepam 2 mg three times a day for a few days (half dose if elderly).
DeprescribeReduce the current drug directly first. Or switch to diazepam selectively: short half-life, high potency, interdose withdrawal, coarse steps.
TaperSlow, stepwise, patient-led: often about 5–10% of the current dose every 2–4 weeks; smaller steps, longer gaps near the end.
SetbackWithdrawal, relapse or new illness? Delay or shrink the next step; severe → previous tolerated dose.
  • ≈ Diazepam 5 mg (approximate only): temazepam 10 mg, lorazepam 0.5 mg, nitrazepam 5 mg, zopiclone 7.5 mg, zolpidem 10 mg.
  • Hepatic impairment → avoid diazepam switch or seek advice (oxazepam). Frail → lower doses.

6Monitor

  • During reductions: review at an interval matched to risk; face to face if over-sedation, falls, confusion, misuse, diversion or escalation.
  • Withdrawal (insomnia, anxiety, sweating, GI upset): often mild, but can last months.
  • Stalling? Too fast, untreated cause, no CBT-I, multiple prescribers.

7Refer — routine, local GM routes

  • Talking Therapies (CBT-I, anxiety). CMHT: severe or refractory anxiety or depression.
  • Substance-misuse service: high-dose, illicit or multiple-source use, diversion. Specialist taper: failed tapers.

8Never

  • Stop abruptly after prolonged use, or continue a hospital script by default without a duration and review plan.
  • An automatic repeat, a script simply on request, or any further script without a documented review.
  • Try another z-hypnotic for non-response — switch only for an agent-specific adverse effect.
  • Substitute promethazine or a sedating antidepressant, or treat withdrawal with another dependence-forming drug.
  • Co-prescribe with an opioid unless there is no alternative; advise avoiding alcohol.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A fit, new confusion or hallucinations, or very slow breathing or being hard to wake — call 999.”   Reducing: “Never stop suddenly. If withdrawal is troublesome, contact us before the next reduction.”   Driving: “Don't drive if drowsy, slowed or dizzy, especially when starting or changing dose. Carry evidence of your prescription.”   Alcohol: “Avoid alcohol and other sedatives.”
