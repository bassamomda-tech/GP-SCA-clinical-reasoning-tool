Bacterial vaginosis

For: women with a vaginal discharge in general practice, including pregnancy and breastfeeding. Not for: suspected pelvic infection (use the PID protocol), new discharge or bleeding at 55 and over (NG12 routes in block 0), or a child where abuse is possible (safeguarding).

Core rule: exclude pelvic infection, a pregnancy problem and the NG12 cancer criteria first; a pH above 4.5 supports BV but is not diagnostic; treat symptomatic BV and plan for recurrence at the first visit.

0Act now — any of these moves the patient off this page

  • Pelvic pain, deep dyspareunia, fever, adnexal or cervical motion tenderness (PID) → pregnancy + STI tests; treat promptly. Unwell, pregnant, severe pain, oral treatment not tolerated or failing, or abscess/surgical cause possible → Same-day hospital / gynaecology
  • Positive pregnancy test with pain and abdominal, pelvic or cervical motion tenderness → Immediate EPAU. Bleeding with pain, or at ≥6 weeks or uncertain gestation → EPAU (immediately or within 24 hours). Haemodynamically unstable → 999
  • Retained tampon or object with fever, rash, vomiting, diarrhoea or hypotension (toxic shock) → remove it; ED immediately; 999 if hypotensive, confused or very unwell.
  • Post-menopausal bleeding aged 55+, not attributable to HRT → Suspected cancer pathway (under 55: consider). Cervix looks malignant or unexplained vaginal mass → consider a suspected cancer pathway referral.
  • Aged 55+, unexplained discharge presenting for the first time, or with thrombocytosis or haematuria → consider an Urgent direct access ultrasound (endometrium).
  • Discharge in a pre-pubertal girl, any abuse or exploitation concern, or sexual activity under 13 → Same-day safeguarding lead discussion

1Confirm — clinical pattern, pH supportive

Thin grey-white discharge + fishy odour (worse after sex or a period) + pH >4.5 + no itch or inflammation. pH ≤4.5 makes BV unlikely. Lab: Amsel 3 of 4, or Hay–Ison grade 3.

2Check — history, look, then decide on a swab

☐ Bleeding, pain, sexual risk, pregnancy, douching, contraception ☐ Vulva and cervix ☐ pH ☐ HVS Gram stain if atypical, failed, recurrent or uncertain in pregnancy ☐ STI/BBV tests by risk; TV NAAT if frothy, yellow-green, inflamed or persistent

Don't miss: after menopause, pH cannot separate BV from atrophy or cancer.

3Decide — who to treat

  • Symptomatic (pregnant or not) → treat. Asymptomatic → usually no; no routine screening in pregnancy, or before IUD insertion unless local guidance requires.
  • Before termination or invasive gynae procedure → treat confirmed BV if the local pathway requires.
  • Partners: male partners not routinely treated; treat a symptomatic female partner in her own right.
  • Self-care: no douching; water or plain emollient; stop smoking; consider condoms.

5Treat — choose route with the patient

First choiceMetronidazole 400 mg twice daily 5–7 days; 2 g once if a course is unlikely to be completed (not in pregnancy).
VaginalMetronidazole 0.75% gel 5 g nightly × 5, or clindamycin 2% cream 5 g nightly × 7.
AlternativesTinidazole 2 g once, or clindamycin 300 mg twice daily × 7 days (C. difficile risk).
Confirmed recurrentConsider sexual-health referral for suppression, e.g. metronidazole gel twice weekly × 16 wk.
  • Pregnancy: 5–7 day oral or vaginal course; no 2 g unless a specialist advises. Breastfeeding: these may be preferred.
  • Interactions: warfarin (INR), lithium, disulfiram, busulfan. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — recurrence plan

  • No test of cure if symptoms settle. Warn at first visit: recurrence is common. First recurrence: repeat first-line (may switch route); review triggers, STI risk.
  • Frequent: HVS Gram stain when symptomatic + TV NAAT. Copper coil → review contraception, no forced removal.

7Refer — routine

  • Sexual health: confirmed recurrent BV or recurrence despite suppression, doubt after a swab, STI partner notification, partner-treatment request.
  • Maternity team: symptomatic BV with previous preterm birth, second-trimester loss or other preterm risk factors.

8Never

  • Label new discharge as BV at 55+ without asking about bleeding and the NG12 ultrasound criterion.
  • Diagnose or treat on pH alone — blood, semen, trichomonas and atrophy also raise it.
  • A single 2 g metronidazole dose in pregnancy unless advised by a specialist.
  • Miss trichomonas: frothy, yellow-green, inflamed or persistent discharge → TV NAAT (an STI needing partner treatment).
  • Routinely prescribe partner treatment, or screen asymptomatic pregnant women.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Very unwell with a high temperature, rash, vomiting or diarrhoea, dizziness or fainting while using a tampon — take it out and call 999.”   Same-day “Tummy or pelvic pain, fever, bleeding between periods, after sex or after the menopause, or pain or bleeding in pregnancy — contact us the same day.”   Metronidazole tablets: “No alcohol during the course and for 48 hours after; finish the course.”   Clindamycin cream: “Condoms and diaphragms are unreliable during treatment and for 72 hours after.”
