Rheumatoid arthritis in adults

For: adults with suspected persistent synovitis, or established RA on shared-care DMARDs. Not for: a hot single joint or a febrile patient on immunosuppression (same-day pathways, block 0); DMARD choice and doses (rheumatology).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG100QS33BSR 2025 csDMARDMHRA DSUGreen Book
Core rule: refer suspected persistent synovitis urgently without waiting for blood tests, prescribe methotrexate once weekly only, and treat fever, sore throat or new cough on a DMARD as same-day toxicity or infection.

0Act now — the time-critical harms

  • Suspected persistent synovitis: small joints of hands/feet, >1 joint, or ≥3 months since onset → Urgent rheumatology, ideally ≤3 working days, even if CRP normal or RF/anti-CCP negative.
  • Hot, swollen single joint = septic arthritis until proven otherwise → Same-day hospital assessment; septic or unstable → don't delay antibiotics.
  • Fever, rigors, hypotension or confusion on a DMARD, biologic or JAK inhibitor → Same-day hospital assessment; hold the DMARD, inform rheumatology.
  • Sore throat, mouth ulcers, bruising or bleeding, fever with systemic illness on a DMARD → Same-day assessment + FBC; withhold next methotrexate dose. Overdose → hospital.
  • New cough, breathlessness or hypoxia on methotrexate (pneumonitis, infection, ILD, PE) → Same-day assessment; withhold methotrexate; hypoxia → hospital.
  • Neck pain + myelopathic signs → Urgent MRI + surgical opinion. Painful red eye, reduced vision (scleritis) → Same-day ophthalmology

1Confirm — the GP diagnosis is "suspected persistent synovitis"

Need not be symmetrical; any adult age; palindromic still refer. Squeeze test MCPs/MTPs.

2Check — in parallel, never before referring

☐ Swollen and tender joints separately  ☐ Psoriasis, eyes, bowel, lungs  ☐ Smoking, pregnancy plans  ☐ RF; anti-CCP if RF negative  ☐ FBC, CRP/ESR, U&E, LFT  ☐ X-ray hands and feet. Don't miss: normal tests do not exclude RA.

3Decide — bridging the wait for rheumatology

  • Never delay referral for tests or treatment. Analgesia; NSAID if suitable, lowest dose, with gastroprotection.
  • Severe or function substantially impaired → discuss a steroid with rheumatology; exclude infection; dose and date in the referral.
  • Smoking cessation: strongest modifiable risk factor.

5Treat — rheumatology starts every DMARD

Step 1 · csDMARDOffer methotrexate, leflunomide or sulfasalazine; consider HCQ if mild/palindromic; consider bridging steroid
Step 2 · combinationTarget missed despite dose escalation → offer an added csDMARD
Step 3 · biologic / JAKHospital-supplied → record on GP system. JAK: MHRA limits ≥65, smokers, CV/cancer risk
FlareAdvice line; offer short-term steroid as agreed; consider NSAID + PPI
  • Methotrexate: weekly, day on the script, patient card, 2.5 mg tablets; folic acid another day.
  • Each repeat: shared care, bloods current, no infection, interactions. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — BSR 2025, per local shared-care protocol

  • FBC, U&E, ALT/AST, albumin: week 2, monthly for 3–6 months, then 3-monthly if no risk factors (more often if at risk; review yearly). Leflunomide: BP, weight.
  • Count, LFT, albumin, creatinine or MCV beyond protocol thresholds → withhold, discuss with rheumatology.
  • Hydroxychloroquine: no routine bloods; eye check in year 1, annual after 5 years (1 year if high risk).

7Plan — annual review, pregnancy, vaccines

  • Annual review: activity, function, QRISK3, FRAX + NOGG, mood, lung, eyes, neck; MDT.
  • Pregnancy: contraception on methotrexate/leflunomide; plan with rheumatology (methotrexate off ≥1 month).
  • Vaccines incl. Shingrix per Green Book; check before any live vaccine.

8Never

  • Methotrexate daily, or mixed tablet strengths / 10 mg tablets without a specialist reason.
  • Trimethoprim or co-trimoxazole with methotrexate (marrow suppression).
  • Wait for RF, anti-CCP, CRP or X-ray before referring persistent synovitis.
  • Call a new hot single joint an "RA flare" before sepsis and crystals are excluded.
  • Issue a DMARD without written shared care and current monitoring; or leave hospital-only drugs off the GP record.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “Fever or feeling unwell, severe sore throat, mouth ulcers, unexplained bruising or bleeding, or a new cough or breathlessness — don't take your next dose and contact us the same day.”   Weekly: “Methotrexate is once a week, on the same day — taking it every day is dangerous. Show your card to any doctor, nurse or pharmacist.”   Vaccines: “Check with us before any travel or live vaccine.”   Flares: “Ring the rheumatology advice line — they should get back to you within a working day.”
