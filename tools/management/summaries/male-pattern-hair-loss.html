Male pattern hair loss

For: men with gradual thinning at the temples and crown, and men asking about finasteride or minoxidil. Not for: patchy loss (alopecia areata — BAD living guideline), scarring alopecia or tinea capitis (blocks 0 and 7). Written for cis men; for transgender men, adapt to anatomy, hormones and the gender service pathway.

Core rule: look at the scalp before you call it balding, and ask about depression and suicidal thoughts before any finasteride — scarring loses its window, and finasteride can harm mood.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Scarring signs (shiny skin, lost follicle openings, perifollicular redness or scale, pustules, pain, burning, itch) → Urgent: expedited dermatology via the local pathway (not a suspected cancer referral); state the signs. Same-day only if acutely unwell or severe infection.
  • Suicidal thoughts on finasteride → stop finasteride 1 mg; Same-day suicide-risk assessment; crisis services or 999 by intent, plan, means, immediate risk and safeguarding. Yellow Card.
  • Depression on finasteride 1 mg → stop it and see a clinician promptly (MHRA). 5 mg or dutasteride → contact promptly; no stop instruction.
  • Tinea capitis with kerion, marked inflammation, pustules, pain or scarring risk → Urgent specialist or paediatric dermatology advice; mycology; oral antifungal, not topical alone.

1Confirm — made by looking, not by a blood test

PictureClues
Pattern lossTemples recede, crown thins, back and sides kept; gradual over years; normal scalp. Change over weeks → think again
Alopecia areataRound or oval patches, exclamation-mark hairs; beard, eyebrows; nail pits
Telogen effluviumDiffuse shedding weeks to months after fever, stress, a new drug or weight loss
Tinea capitisScaly patches, broken hairs, black dots, pustules, kerion; mostly children

2Check — test only when the story does not fit

☐ What he wants: reassurance, treatment or transplant  ☐ Scalp, beard, eyebrows, nails  ☐ Typical, well man: no bloods  ☐ Diffuse or unclear: FBC, ferritin, TFTs  ☐ Scaly patch in a child or teenager: scrapings and plucked hairs  ☐ Mood, body-image preoccupation, plans to buy tablets online. Don't miss: lichen planopilaris or discoid lupus labelled as balding.

3Decide — his choice, not a need

  • Doing nothing is a reasonable choice; camouflage or a shorter cut.
  • Topical minoxidil from a pharmacy · finasteride 1 mg on private prescription · transplant (private: check GMC registration, regulation, written quote).
  • Not routinely NHS-prescribable: finasteride 1 mg and minoxidil solution, cream, lotion, ointment are in Drug Tariff Part XVIIIA — no FP10. GM: foam do-not-prescribe.

4Target — slow the loss; works only while used

MinoxidilResults ≥2–4 months; judge at 6–12 months; no benefit at 12 → reasonable to stop
FinasterideStabilises in 3–6 months; benefit may take over a year. Stopped → reverses by 6 months, baseline by 9–12

5Treat

Step 1Explain, baseline photos of crown and temples
Step 2 · OTCRegaine Extra Strength 5% solution 1 ml twice daily to dry scalp (max 2 ml/day); licensed 18–49. Foam per its SPC
Step 3 · privateFinasteride 1 mg once daily; licensed 18–41, outside this off-label
Step 4 · specialistLow-dose oral minoxidil: off-label, dermatology; not a GP start
  • No minoxidil if loss is sudden or patchy, no family history, unknown cause, or scalp inflamed, infected or painful. Irritation, facial hair (~14%), shedding in weeks 2–6.
  • Before finasteride: ask about depression, suicidal thoughts, self-harm. Active suicidal ideation or significant untreated depression → specialist or mental-health advice first (past history not an absolute bar). Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Finasteride: mood and sexual function at every contact; record private supply in the medication record; don't take over prescribing.
  • PSA: finasteride lowers it; SPC suggests considering doubling, but weigh duration, symptoms, DRE, age, trend. New urinary symptoms, haematuria, abnormal DRE or concerning PSA → usual prostate-cancer pathway.
  • Private oral minoxidil: BP, pulse, ankle swelling, palpitations, dizziness.

7Refer — routine dermatology; most men never need it

  • Diagnosis uncertain; extensive or unresponsive alopecia areata (BAD); tinea capitis not responding.
  • Suspicious scalp lesion → relevant skin-cancer pathway. Typical pattern loss: no referral for treatment.

8Never

  • Call it balding without examining the scalp.
  • Support finasteride without asking about depression and suicidal thoughts.
  • Prescribe finasteride 5 mg on the NHS to be split for hair loss.
  • Promise NHS treatment, or refer to get it, for typical pattern loss.
  • Rely on an isolated “double the PSA” rule.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you are at immediate risk of harming yourself, call 999 or go to A&E. Chest pain on minoxidil — call 999.”   Same-day “Suicidal thoughts on the 1 mg tablet — stop it and contact us the same day. Fast heartbeat, faintness, dizziness, sudden weight gain or swollen hands or feet on minoxidil — stop it and contact us the same day.”   Promptly: “Depression on the 1 mg tablet — stop it and contact us. Tell us about sexual problems (they can persist) or a breast lump, pain or nipple discharge.”   Finasteride: “Read the alert card. Tell any doctor doing a PSA test. A woman who is or may be pregnant must not handle broken tablets.”   Both drugs: “Six months to a year to judge; extra shedding at first on minoxidil; works only while used.”
