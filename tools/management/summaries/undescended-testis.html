Undescended testis

For: infants, boys and men with a testis that is not in the scrotum, from the newborn check to adulthood, and those with a history of undescended testis. Not for: DSD/CAH work-up or adult orchidopexy-versus-orchidectomy decisions — these are specialist-led (blocks 0 and 5); the newborn screen-positive pathway is NIPE's.

Core rule: the mobility test decides the diagnosis and the NIPE timetable decides the referral — specialist review by 6 months, treatment by 12 months where possible (18 at the latest); no imaging before referral, and if unsure, refer.

0Act now — most undescended testes are not emergencies; these are

  • Newborn check: bilateral impalpable testes, a unilateral impalpable testis, or a palpable undescended testis with hypospadias → Senior paediatric review ≤24 h (NIPE) — exclude DSD, including CAH.
  • Atypical genitalia (hypospadias, micropenis, bifid scrotum, scrotal hyperpigmentation, ambiguous) → Same-day paediatric/DSD. Hyperpigmentation + bilateral impalpable testes = possible salt-wasting CAH, a medical emergency. Don't delay for a primary-care endocrine panel.
  • Acute groin, abdominal, scrotal or testicular pain, or swelling or tenderness where the testis lies = possible torsion → Immediate ED or surgical centre, ambulance if needed; no ultrasound first. A normal scrotum does not exclude it — examine the testes in every boy with acute abdominal pain.
  • Bilateral undescended at the 6–8-week check, or bilateral non-palpable testes found later → Urgent paediatrics, seen ≤2 weeks
  • Man with a history: non-painful enlargement or change in shape or texture → consider Suspected cancer pathway (NG12); unexplained or persistent testicular symptoms → consider a direct-access ultrasound.

6Monitor — follow up for life

  • Retractile: yearly until puberty; no longer reaches or stays, or uncertain → refer.
  • After orchidopexy: per the surgical team; re-ascent or atrophy → re-refer.
  • Puberty: bilateral, anorchia, atrophic testes or no progression → paediatric endocrinology.
  • Adulthood: testicular awareness — orchidopexy reduces but does not remove the cancer risk; no routine ultrasound surveillance.

7Refer — paediatric surgery or urology (urgent routes in block 0)

  • Prompt still undescended at 4–5 months; becomes impalpable; found later; ascending — Bolton/GM pathway.
  • Paediatric (not adult) surgeons for infants, bilateral or non-palpable testes, and recurrence.
  • Adult urology untreated testis in an adolescent or man; fertility concerns.

8Never

  • Request ultrasound or MRI to locate the testis, or let a scan delay referral.
  • Let ultrasound delay surgical assessment of possible torsion, or be reassured by a normal scrotum.
  • Delay a DSD referral to arrange a primary-care endocrine panel.
  • Label a testis retractile when unsure, or wait beyond the timetable for it to descend.
  • Offer hormonal treatment in primary care to bring a testis down.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 / A&E “Sudden pain in his groin, tummy or testicle, or swelling or tenderness where the testis sits, could be a twisted testis — go to A&E straight away, by ambulance if needed.”   After surgery: Same-day “A fever, a red or weeping wound, or a swollen bruised scrotum — be seen the same day.”   From puberty: “Check your testicles; a new lump, swelling, change in shape or feel, ongoing discomfort, or a testis that seems to have gone up — see your GP promptly.”
