Subconjunctival haemorrhage (SCH)

For: adults and children with a red patch on the white of the eye in primary care. Not for: a painful red eye or reduced vision (use the red eye pathway) or a suspected open globe (block 0).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
College of Optometrists CMG (2025)NICE NG12 (Apr 2026)NG240NG136CG89
Core rule: measure the acuity in each eye, see the posterior border and swing the torch before you reassure — then check the BP, and never stop anticoagulation for an uncomplicated SCH without advice.

0Act now — use the local Greater Manchester urgent eye-care or on-call ophthalmology route

  • Suspected open globe (trauma + reduced acuity, RAPD, abnormal pupil or globe, flat chamber) → rigid shield, no pressure, IOP, drops or eversion; nil by mouth → Immediate ophthalmology or ED; 999 if major trauma or unsafe to transfer.
  • Reduced or changed acuity, new RAPD, severe pain, or posterior limit not visible (extensive, circumferential), especially after trauma → no pressure → Same-day eye emergency
  • Bilateral SCH after head or neck compression or trauma, or with neurological signs, reduced consciousness, facial petechiae, periorbital/mastoid bruising, CSF leak → 999
  • Fever with a non-blanching rash, meningism, shock or altered consciousness → 999; benzylpenicillin (adult and 10+ years 1.2 g) or ceftriaxone IM/IV only if it will not delay transfer.
  • Proptosis, chemosis, arterialised vessels, pulsatile tinnitus (carotid-cavernous fistula) → Same-day emergency ophthalmology, via ED
  • Unexplained bruising, bleeding or petechiae: adult → consider very urgent FBC ≤48 hours, act on the result the same day. Child with unexplained petechiae or hepatosplenomegaly → Immediate specialist assessment

1Confirm — uncomplicated only if all present

Sharply demarcated bright-red patch, painless, normal acuity and pupils, posterior limit visible; no photophobia, discharge, significant trauma or proptosis.

Traumatic: gentle acuity, pupils, extent; any abnormal → block 0. Painful red eye = uveitis, angle closure or keratitis, not SCH.

2Check — and document

☐ Acuity each eye  ☐ Swinging torch (RAPD)  ☐ Posterior limit  ☐ BP  ☐ Anticoagulants, antiplatelets, NSAIDs  ☐ Lens wearer with symptoms: lens out, cornea, evert lid if safe

Don't miss: mechanism that does not fit, child or older adult → safeguarding.

3Decide — who needs tests

  • First, uncomplicated: usually no tests; check BP.
  • Recurrent, bilateral, extensive or unexplained: reassess medicines, triggers, BP → consider FBC with platelets; INR if on warfarin; renal/liver if indicated.
  • Travel + fever: malaria film, leptospirosis serology; possible VHF → isolate, UKHSA pathway before sampling.

4Anticoagulation — do not stop for an uncomplicated SCH

WarfarinINR if not recent, recurrent or over-anticoagulation possible
DOACINR does not measure effect; renal function, adherence as indicated
Already stoppedContact the prescribing team promptly; assess restarting. Major trauma or bleed → decide with them

5Treat — reassure, then treat the contributors

  • No specific treatment. Cold compress in the first 24 hours; lubricant drops if gritty (e.g. hypromellose 0.3%, carbomer gel) — doses per BNF and local formulary. No rubbing; no added aspirin or NSAIDs; no driving restriction.
  • Raised BP → manage per NICE NG136; confirm with ABPM or HBPM (hypertension protocol).
  • Straining → fibre and fluid, then a macrogol or lactulose. Chronic cough → investigate the cause. Lens fit → optician. Dry eye → long-term lubricants.

6Monitor

  • Course: usually clears in 1–2 weeks, larger bleeds longer; may look bigger or darker over the first few days. Review if not cleared, spreading or vision affected.
  • Anticoagulated: record a minor bleed; repeated → review risk–benefit.
  • Post-intravitreal injection, at the site → expected. Elsewhere, pain or reduced vision → eye team.

7Refer — urgent routes are in block 0

  • Contact lens wearer, traumatic SCH, normal vision (foreign-body exclusion) → Ophthalmology 24–48 h. Suspected abrasion, retained foreign body or keratitis → Same day
  • Haematology: low platelets, clotting abnormality, suspected bleeding diathesis.
  • Ophthalmology Routine: recurrent and atypical, persistent, repeatedly in one spot, with a mass, or unexplained.
  • Safeguarding: child → CG89, paediatrics if NAI suspected; adult → local procedures (Care Act 2014).

8Never

  • Reassure without a measured acuity in each eye.
  • Pad, press, IOP, lid eversion or drops if open globe suspected.
  • Stop an anticoagulant or antiplatelet for uncomplicated SCH without advice.
  • Delay 999 transfer to give antibiotics for suspected meningococcal disease.
  • Diagnose hypertension from one clinic reading.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Very unwell with a fever, or a rash that does not fade under a glass — call 999.”   Same day “Vision changes, pain or light sensitivity, double vision, the eye swells or bulges, or a new injury — contact us, an urgent eye service or A&E today. Bleeding or bruising elsewhere — contact us the same day.”   Expect: “It may look bigger or darker for a few days, then fades over a week or two.”   Anticoagulant: “Keep taking it. Vomiting blood, a nosebleed that won't stop, severe headache, weakness or slurred speech — 999 or A&E; black stools or heavy periods — us or NHS 111 the same day.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Block 0 red flags excluded Acuity, pupils and posterior limit documented BP checked; medicines, lenses and mechanism reviewed Anticoagulant plan agreed; tests or review booked if recurrent Safety-net and colour-change warning given and documented
