GORD and acid reflux in adults

For: adults with heartburn or acid regurgitation in primary care, and long-term PPI review. Not for: children; chest pain with cardiac features (assess as possible ACS); unexplained iron-deficiency anaemia (use the iron-deficiency anaemia protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG184NG12 (updated April 2026)NG231MHRAGM formulary
Core rule: screen the NG12 alarm features first and never let a PPI trial delay the referral; then a full-dose PPI taken 30–60 minutes before breakfast, with the step-down agreed at the first prescription.

0Act now — screen at every contact

  • Haematemesis, melaena, coffee-ground vomiting, syncope or haemodynamic instability → 999 if unstable otherwise Same-day emergency assessment — not a referral form.
  • Food bolus obstruction → Same-day emergency endoscopic assessment
  • Dysphagia at any age, or aged 55+ with weight loss plus upper abdominal pain, reflux or dyspepsia → Suspected cancer pathway referral — never a stronger PPI.
  • Upper abdominal mass consistent with stomach cancer → consider a Suspected cancer pathway referral; left supraclavicular node → the same, upper GI. Hepatomegaly → consider Urgent direct-access ultrasound ≤2 weeks
  • Aged 45+ with persistent unexplained hoarseness or an unexplained neck lump → consider Suspected cancer pathway referral (laryngeal).

1Confirm — clinical diagnosis; no OGD if uncomplicated

  • Heartburn (worse lying, bending, after meals) + acid regurgitation, no alarm features → GORD; no OGD, pH study or manometry.
  • Exertional pain, jaw/arm radiation, breathlessness, sweating → assess as possible ACS first.
  • Throat symptoms alone → not LPR yet: look for ENT, respiratory, allergic and drug causes.

2Check — only what changes management

☐ Weight/BMI  ☐ Epigastric mass, hepatomegaly, left supraclavicular node  ☐ Drugs: NSAIDs, aspirin, bisphosphonates, CCBs, nitrates, doxycycline — stop or switch only where safe  ☐ Dyspepsia → H. pylori breath/stool test off PPI 2 weeks  ☐ FBC if anaemia suspected

Don't miss: unexplained iron-deficiency anaemia → upper- and lower-GI pathways.

3Decide — who gets what

  • Mild or intermittent → alginate/antacid PRN + lifestyle; a PPI is not obligatory.
  • Typical GORD → full-dose PPI 4 or 8 weeks. Uninvestigated dyspepsia → 4 weeks + H. pylori test-and-treat.
  • Severe oesophagitis (LA C/D) → 8 weeks full dose, then offer full-dose long-term maintenance.
  • Pregnancy → lifestyle and alginates first; omeprazole if needed; review after delivery.
  • Lifestyle: weight loss, nothing within 3 h of lying down, bed-head blocks, stop smoking, triggers.

5Treat — timing matters as much as dose

Step 1–2Lifestyle, drug review; alginate after meals and at bedtime (also the bridge later)
Step 3 · first lineOmeprazole 20 mg or lansoprazole 30 mg OD, 30–60 min before breakfast
Step 4 · H. pylori +Current GM regimen (7 days); see below
Step 5 · partialTiming and adherence first → BD or switch PPI; famotidine for nocturnal breakthrough
  • H. pylori: PPI BD + amoxicillin + clarithromycin or metronidazole. Penicillin allergy → PPI BD + clarithromycin 500 mg BD + metronidazole 400 mg BD. Not for GORD without dyspepsia.
  • Not responding: cardiac, functional or eosinophilic oesophagitis? Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — and step down

  • Controlled 4–8 weeks → lowest dose or on-demand; stop gradually or abruptly by shared decision; warn about rebound.
  • Keep the PPI: severe oesophagitis; Barrett's (gastroenterology first); indicated NSAID/antiplatelet gastroprotection.
  • Barrett's (NG231): long-segment OGD 2–3 yearly; short with intestinal metaplasia 3–5 yearly — check enrolment.
  • Consider magnesium if at risk (digoxin, diuretics); B12/FBC only when indicated.

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent in block 0)

  • 55+: treatment-resistant dyspepsia; upper abdominal pain + low Hb; raised platelets or nausea/vomiting + weight loss, reflux, dyspepsia or upper abdominal pain → consider non-urgent direct-access OGD.
  • Gastroenterology: refractory on optimised PPI; normal OGD but persistent; Barrett's; LA C/D; suspected eosinophilic oesophagitis; younger, relapsing off PPI.

8Never

  • Attribute new dysphagia or weight loss to reflux, or answer it with a stronger PPI.
  • Delay a suspected cancer referral to trial or withdraw a PPI — tell the endoscopy service about any PPI; no routine washout.
  • Anchor on “reflux” in chest pain in someone with cardiac risk.
  • Raise the dose before checking timing and adherence.
  • Routinely deprescribe in confirmed Barrett's without gastroenterology, or stop an indicated PPI only for an observational association.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Vomiting blood or black tarry stools — that is 999 or A&E, not an appointment — the same if you faint or feel very unwell.”   Swallowing: “Trouble swallowing, food sticking or losing weight without trying — tell me straight away. If food sticks and will not go down, or you cannot swallow your saliva, go to A&E the same day.”   New PPI: “Take it 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast and give it the full four- or eight-week course.”   Stopping: “Heartburn may flare for a while — use an alginate antacid, and come back if it does not settle.”
