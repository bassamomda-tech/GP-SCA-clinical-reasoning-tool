Cellulitis and erysipelas

For: adults and children with acute cellulitis or erysipelas. Not for: infected bites (NG184), diabetic-foot infection (NG19) or leg-ulcer infection (NG152) — separate pathways. Children: never adult doses — NG141 children’s table or BNFC; under 1 month → specialist antibiotic advice.

Core rule: pain out of proportion is necrotising fasciitis until proven otherwise — 999, never a trial of oral antibiotics; and bilateral, chronic, itchy red legs are rarely cellulitis.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Pain out of proportion, spread over hours, dusky or purple skin, haemorrhagic bullae, necrosis, crepitus, numbness, very unwell → necrotising fasciitis → 999 emergency surgical referral.
  • High-risk sepsis (NG253 if 16+; age-specific NG254 under 16, not Eron; NG255 if pregnant or recently pregnant) or Eron IV → 999 + pre-alert No fever does not exclude sepsis.
  • Orbital signs: proptosis, painful or restricted eye movements, double vision, reduced acuity or colour vision, RAPD, too swollen to examine → Same-day emergency ophthalmology/ENT or paediatrics Periorbital without orbital signs → Same-day specialist or paediatric advice
  • Hot joint that will not move, or deep bone pain under an ulcer or wound → septic arthritis or osteomyelitis → Same-day hospital
  • Eron III, severe or rapidly spreading infection, or child under 1 who is unwell → Same-day hospital assessment
  • Diabetic foot: deep tissue or bone infection, ischaemia with ulceration, gangrene, fever or sepsis → Immediate acute referral + tell the foot team. Any other active foot problem → MDT foot service ≤1 working day

1Confirm — exclude non-infective redness first

Cellulitis: acute, hot, red, swollen, tender, spreading, usually one leg. Erysipelas: sharp raised edge — managed the same. Darker skin: rely on warmth, swelling, tenderness.

MimicClue → action
Venous eczemaBilateral, itchy, scaly, chronic, well → emollient, topical steroid, no antibiotics
DVT · CharcotWells + ultrasound (can coexist) · diabetic foot → MDT foot service

2Check — every case

☐ Full observations  ☐ Portal: toe webs, ulcer, wound  ☐ Lymphoedema, diabetes, frailty  ☐ Genuine allergy?  ☐ Pregnancy  ☐ Clarithromycin interactions (statins, DOACs, QT)

Don't miss: abscess → drain. Do not routinely swab (only broken skin with a specific uncommon-pathogen risk).

3Decide — consider referral or specialist advice

Children under 1, frail, immunocompromised; near eyes or nose; lymphangitis; uncommon pathogen; failing or unable to take oral antibiotics (explore OPAT or virtual ward).

5Treat — oral first line, 5–7 days (NG141 adult doses)

First lineFlucloxacillin 500 mg–1 g QDS, 5–7 days (1 g off-label — document why). Empty stomach.
Penicillin allergyClarithromycin 500 mg BD; pregnancy: erythromycin 500 mg QDS; or doxycycline 200 mg day 1, then 100 mg OD; 5–7 days
Near eyes or noseCo-amoxiclav 500/125 mg TDS, 7 days; allergy: clarithromycin 500 mg BD + metronidazole 400 mg TDS, 7 days. Seek specialist advice.
Severe / IVHospital or OPAT; review IV by 48 hours, switch to oral when possible.
  • Doxycycline: not in pregnancy or breastfeeding, not routinely under 12. Children: flucloxacillin; allergy → clarithromycin (BNFC doses).
  • Elevate, analgesia, treat the portal. Up to 14 days only after reassessment. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Review

  • Consider marking the edge. Worsening rapidly at any time, or not improving in 2–3 days → reassess: diagnosis, abscess, DVT, complication, organism, adherence, IV.
  • Day 5–7: residual redness alone is not failure. Known MRSA and failing → microbiology.

7Recurrence and refer

  • Prevent: treat tinea, ulcers, eczema, oedema; compression individualised after arterial assessment.
  • Do not routinely offer prophylaxis. Specialists may consider it after ≥2 episodes treated in hospital or under specialist advice in 12 months; review at least 6-monthly.
  • Routine recurrence, lymphoedema, diagnostic doubt, non-healing ulcer → lymphoedema, dermatology or vascular service.

8Never

  • Trial oral antibiotics or phone reassurance when pain is out of proportion.
  • Repeated antibiotics for bilateral venous eczema or lipodermatosclerosis.
  • Use adult doses in children, or doxycycline in pregnancy.
  • Change or extend antibiotics for residual redness alone.
  • Skip eye movements, acuity and colour vision in periorbital swelling.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Severe pain out of keeping with the skin, skin dark, purple or blistered, or very unwell — clammy, confused, very breathless, little urine — call 999.”   Eye: “Eye bulging, pain moving it, double or blurred vision — A&E straight away.”   Same day “Redness spreads past the line I've drawn, it gets worse quickly, fever or shivering, or not starting to get better within 2 to 3 days — contact us the same day.”   Expected course: “Redness fades slowly — normal if pain, heat and swelling are going.”
