Giant cell arteritis

For: people aged 50 or over with suspected GCA, and their follow-up on steroids. Not for: an isolated raised ESR/CRP with no clinical features (look for another cause), or polymyalgic symptoms without cranial or visual features (use the polymyalgia rheumatica protocol).

BSR GCA guideline 2020NICE TA518NOGG 2024NICE NG243DVLA
Core rule: strongly suspected GCA → start prednisolone in this consultation and refer to the fast-track pathway the same day; never wait for bloods, ultrasound, biopsy or the specialist — sight is the clock.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Any new visual disturbance — transient loss, curtain or field defect, diplopia, sudden reduction — even if fully recovered → oral prednisolone now if GCA strongly suspected → Same-day ophthalmology / acute GCA pathway; specialist decides on IV methylprednisolone.
  • Sight already lost in one eye → prednisolone 60 mg daily to protect the other eye → Same-day eye assessment
  • Stroke or TIA symptoms, or too unwell to travel → 999 / ED; state the GCA suspicion and the steroid given.
  • Strongly suspected GCA, no visual or ischaemic complication → prednisolone 40–60 mg today → GCA fast-track, same working day — in all cases seen within 3 working days.
  • Jaw or tongue claudication → ischaemic risk; refer the same working day. Ischaemic relapse on the taper → treat as new GCA, Same-day specialist

1Confirm — clinical first, specialist confirms later

Aged 50+, something new: headache (often temporal), scalp tenderness, jaw or tongue claudication, visual disturbance, or PMR stiffness with systemic upset. Normal artery ≠ excluded.

Test (via fast-track)Key points
Ultrasound (temporal + axillary)First-line, trained operator; yield falls on steroid — scan before or as soon as possible after
Temporal artery biopsy≥1 cm; still useful after treatment starts
ESR / CRPSupport, never confirm; normal does not exclude

Negative or equivocal test ≠ excluded. Never use the ACR/EULAR 2022 score to decide treatment or referral.

2Check — same day, never delaying the first dose

☐ ESR, CRP, FBC, U&E, LFT, glucose  ☐ Temporal arteries  ☐ Acuity, fields, pupils, fundi  ☐ BP, weight, HbA1c  ☐ Fracture risk

Don't miss: large-vessel GCA (fever, weight loss, no headache; arm claudication, unequal arm BPs, bruit); cranial symptoms in PMR.

3Decide — who treats, and where

  • Strongly suspected → GP starts prednisolone and refers now; IV methylprednisolone only via the specialist.
  • Isolated raised ESR/CRP, no clinical features → not GCA: think infection, malignancy, other inflammation, drugs.
  • Poor or atypical steroid response → reconsider the diagnosis, don't just raise the dose.

5Treat — prednisolone, single morning dose

Start40–60 mg once daily; 60 mg if sight lost. Enough to reach the specialist.
Taper 1Settled → reduce by 10 mg every 2 weeks to 20 mg
Taper 2Then 2.5 mg every 2–4 weeks to 10 mg
Taper 3 · template onlyThen 1 mg every 1–2 months; follow the specialist/local protocol
  • Bone from day 1: ≥7.5 mg/day for ≥3 months (postmenopausal women, men 50+) → assess, likely bone protection; DXA need not come first. Calcium and vitamin D.
  • Steroid Emergency Card (≥5 mg/day for ≥4 weeks), sick-day rules. PPI only by GI risk. Aspirin not routine. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — written schedule agreed with the specialist

  • Template: weeks 1, 3, 6; months 3, 6, 9, 12. Confirm scan or biopsy done.
  • Every review: cranial, visual, polymyalgic, systemic, vascular relapse; BP, glucose, weight, mood, cataract/glaucoma, bone, infection.
  • ESR/CRP rise alone → review, look for another cause; don't raise prednisolone on markers alone.
  • Tocilizumab: markers unreliable — serious infection with normal CRP. No live vaccines on these doses (Green Book ch. 6).

7Refer — back to the specialist

  • Repeated relapse, can't taper or steroid harm → methotrexate, or tocilizumab (TA518: relapsing/refractory; usually stopped at 1 year).
  • Non-ischaemic relapse → reassess; last effective dose only per agreed protocol; inform the team.
  • Limb claudication, unequal arm BPs, bruits → large-vessel imaging.
  • Shared care from ~month 3: agree in writing who adjusts dose and checks bloods.

8Never

  • Wait for ESR/CRP, ultrasound, biopsy or the specialist before the first dose.
  • Treat a visual symptom that has recovered as a routine referral.
  • Withhold or stop steroid to improve test yield, or cancel the diagnosis on one negative test.
  • Stop the steroid suddenly, or let the patient reduce it themselves.
  • Tell the patient they are cleared to drive — the DVLA process decides.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden weakness, numbness or trouble speaking — call 999.”   Same day “Any change in your eyesight, even if it recovers — go straight to the eye emergency service or A and E. Headache or jaw ache back as the dose comes down — ring us that day.”   Steroid: “Never stop it suddenly; vomiting or can't take it — get urgent medical advice.”   Driving: “No driving while you have eye symptoms or until your sight and fields are checked; a field defect must be reported to the DVLA.”
