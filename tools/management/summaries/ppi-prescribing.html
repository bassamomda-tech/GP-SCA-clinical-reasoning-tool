PPI prescribing and deprescribing

For: adults starting, reviewing or stopping a proton pump inhibitor (PPI). Not for: acute GI bleeding (same-day specialist, block 0) or diagnosing reflux (use the GORD protocol); antithrombotic choice is in antiplatelets and anticoagulants.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG184NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)MHRA clopidogrel–PPIGMMMG formularySPC (emc)
Core rule: document the indication, give a defined course on an acute script, never let a PPI trial delay endoscopy or cancer referral, and on clopidogrel avoid omeprazole and esomeprazole.

0Act now — check first, every time, including in people already on a PPI

  • Haematemesis or melaena (significant acute GI bleeding) → Same-day specialist; heavy bleeding or shocked → 999
  • Significant or symptomatic anaemia, persistent vomiting with dehydration, severe acute abdominal pain → Same-day assessment
  • Dysphagia at any age, or aged 55+ with weight loss plus upper-abdominal pain, reflux or dyspepsia → Suspected cancer pathway referral — do not delay it to trial a PPI. Upper-abdominal mass consistent with stomach cancer → consider one.
  • New alarm features on a PPI → act as above, even if symptoms seem controlled: control on acid suppression is not reassurance.

1Confirm — a valid indication, written on the prescription

Dyspepsia, GORD, peptic ulcer, H. pylori eradication, gastroprotection, Barrett's, severe oesophagitis, stricture, Zollinger–Ellison, specialist or palliative use. Check previous endoscopy.

Uninvestigated dyspepsia, no alarm features → H. pylori test-and-treat early; negative or still symptomatic → defined PPI course.

2Check — before you prescribe

☐ Culprit drugs (NSAIDs, aspirin, CCBs, nitrates, bisphosphonates, steroids, SSRIs)  ☐ Clopidogrel?  ☐ Lifestyle  ☐ No referral needed → consider an alginate first

Don't miss: H. pylori breath or stool test → off the PPI 2 weeks. Endoscopy → follow the local service; don't routinely stop it.

3Decide — gastroprotect when GI-bleed risk outweighs PPI harms

5Treat — optimise before escalating

StartOmeprazole 20 mg or lansoprazole 30 mg daily before breakfast, acute script + review date
Not working? FirstDiagnosis, adherence, timing, triggers, NSAIDs, H. pylori, endoscopy needed?
ThenConsider omeprazole 40 mg daily or a PPI switch; alginate or H2-receptor antagonist for breakthrough
Still not workingTwice daily generally only on specialist advice, documented indication or local pathway. Don't escalate indefinitely → refer
  • Gastroprotection: lansoprazole 15–30 mg or omeprazole 20 mg. On clopidogrel: pantoprazole (or lansoprazole, rabeprazole).
  • H. pylori positive → current Greater Manchester regimen; check prior macrolide, metronidazole, quinolone use and penicillin allergy type.
  • Not interchangeable mg-for-mg. Doses: NICE/SPC.

6Monitor and deprescribe

  • Long-term PPI: review at least annually (indication, dose, alarm features) → lowest effective dose, or deprescribe.
  • Keep (specialist-documented): Barrett's, LA grade C/D oesophagitis, stricture, Zollinger–Ellison, recurrent ulcer bleeding, high-risk gastroprotection, palliative.
  • Deprescribe: short or low-dose → stop; prolonged or high dose → reduce over weeks; alginate rescue plan. Culprit drug stopped → stop the PPI.
  • No routine Mg, B12 or iron tests; consider Mg if symptomatic or on diuretic or digoxin. C. difficile → stop an unnecessary PPI; suspected interstitial nephritis → stop.

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent routes are in block 0)

  • 55+: treatment-resistant dyspepsia, upper-abdominal pain with low Hb, or raised platelets or nausea/vomiting with NG12 symptoms → consider non-urgent direct-access endoscopy.
  • Refractory despite optimising, or failed eradication → gastroenterology. Barrett's, LA C/D or stricture → specialist follow-up. Suspected Zollinger–Ellison → gastroenterology (fasting gastrin).

8Never

  • Omeprazole or esomeprazole with clopidogrel — and never stop the clopidogrel without specialist advice.
  • A PPI trial that delays indicated endoscopy or a suspected cancer referral.
  • An H. pylori breath or stool test within 2 weeks of a PPI.
  • A reflex PPI with every NSAID, or a PPI left on after the culprit drug stops.
  • Assume persistent, severe, progressive or alarm symptoms after stopping are just rebound — reassess.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you vomit blood or pass black, tarry stools, call 999 or go to A&E straight away.”   Same-day “Difficulty swallowing, weight loss, or severe symptoms — contact us the same day.”   Starting: “This is a short, full-strength course with a review, not a permanent tablet.”   Stopping: “Some heartburn for days or weeks is common — an alginate helps; book to see us if it keeps going.”
