Glaucoma — the GP's part

For: adults with chronic open-angle glaucoma, ocular hypertension, suspected glaucoma or angle-closure disease, and anyone with an acutely painful red eye. Not for: diagnosing or titrating glaucoma — that sits with the hospital eye service or an accredited community glaucoma service. No NG12 suspected-cancer criterion applies.

Core rule: a painful red eye with haloes is acute angle closure until proven otherwise — phone the eye team for same-day assessment; and never stop glaucoma drops without speaking to the eye team.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Acute angle closure: sudden eye pain or brow ache, blurred vision, haloes, nausea or vomiting, red eye, hazy cornea, mid-dilated poorly reacting pupil → Same-day ophthalmology — phone the on-call eye team now (no eye service → ED, letter ‘suspected acute angle closure’). While waiting: supervise, analgesia, antiemetic; no dilating drops; do not delay transfer.
  • Topiramate with blurred vision or eye pain (acute myopia, often bilateral, usually first month) → Same-day ophthalmology; prescriber urgently to stop or withdraw it, balancing seizure risk.
  • Any painful red eye with reduced vision or an abnormal pupil; sudden visual or field loss; suspected GCA with visual symptoms → Same-day, even if not glaucoma.
  • Warning attacks (evening haloes and aching that settle), new gradual field loss, suspected steroid pressure rise → Urgent eye assessment ≤1 week; an attack that does not settle → same-day.
  • On timolol drops with wheeze, bronchospasm, syncope, bradycardia or heart block → withhold if necessary → Urgent eye-team call for another plan; never leave glaucoma untreated.

1Confirm — found at the sight test, confirmed by the eye service

NG81 thresholds (optometry, not a GP rule): optic nerve damage, glaucomatous field defect, or IOP ≥24 mmHg on Goldmann-type applanation after considering a repeat; never non-contact tonometry alone. Optometrist already referred → no duplicate GP referral.

2Check — the medicines, both ways

☐ Repeats vs clinic letter  ☐ Timolol, incl. combination bottles  ☐ Asthma, COPD, bradycardia, syncope  ☐ Brimonidine + MAOI or relevant tricyclic  ☐ Topiramate, anticholinergics  ☐ Prolonged/high-dose steroids

Don't miss: haloes and evening aching matter more than ‘glaucoma’ on the problem list.

3Decide — narrow angle or treated open angle?

  • No blanket ‘avoid in glaucoma’ (e.g. antihistamines, SSRIs): treated open angle ≠ narrow angle.
  • Nebulised ipratropium: mouthpiece or well-fitting mask in narrow angles; don't withhold essential bronchodilators.
  • Steroid eye drops only under ophthalmic supervision.
  • Narrow-angle risk: long-sighted, older, female, South or East Asian ancestry.

5Treat — all specialist-initiated (NG81)

First360° SLT — new COAG (not advanced) or OHT ≥24 mmHg at lifetime risk; not pigment dispersion
AlternativeGeneric PGA if SLT unsuitable, declined or not enough — e.g. latanoprost 1 drop daily, evening
IntoleranceSwitch, don't stop: another generic PGA → beta-blocker → other classes; preservative-free if sore
AdvancedSurgery with augmentation; consider SLT or surgery if at risk despite 2 classes
  • Timolol: 0.25% twice daily → 0.5% if inadequate. CI: asthma ever, severe COPD, sinus bradycardia, sick sinus, 2nd/3rd-degree block without pacemaker.
  • PGA: warn of permanent iris browning, longer lashes. Doses per SPC, BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — adherence is the treatment

  • Technique: demonstrate, then watch; recheck with adherence if IOP not controlled (NG81). Ask how many doses were missed last week.
  • Close the eye or press the inner corner 2 minutes; gap between drops. Arthritis, tremor → aids or a carer.
  • Not lost to follow-up — a route to avoidable sight loss.

7Refer — routine; urgent ones are in block 0

  • Glaucoma clinic: intolerance, new contraindication (asthma on timolol), poor adherence, lost to follow-up, wants laser.
  • DVLA (patient's duty): car or motorcycle — condition in both eyes (or the only seeing eye); bus or lorry — one or both eyes. Advise and document.
  • Family: parents, siblings, children aged 40+ → free NHS sight test (England).

8Never

  • Dilating drops in suspected angle closure, or optometry instead of same-day hospital care.
  • Pilocarpine or acetazolamide in the surgery unless the eye team advises.
  • Stopping glaucoma drops for a side effect without the eye team.
  • Timolol (or a combination bottle) in asthma, severe COPD or heart block.
  • Topiramate stopped in epilepsy without an urgent alternative plan.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “Sudden eye pain, blurred vision or haloes round lights — especially after a new medicine or a nebuliser — go to eye casualty or the emergency department the same day.”   Acute closure: “It needs the eye specialists today, not tomorrow — I'm phoning them now.”   Drops: “They don't make you feel different, but they protect side vision that doesn't come back. Don't stop them without talking to us.”   Driving and family: “Check whether you need to tell the DVLA; relatives aged 40 or over can get a free sight test.”
