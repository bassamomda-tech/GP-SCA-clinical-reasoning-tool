Deep vein thrombosis — confirmed proximal DVT

For: non-pregnant adults with objectively confirmed proximal DVT. Not for: suspected DVT before imaging or PE (use the pulmonary embolism page), pregnancy (RCOG — refer directly), under 18s (paediatric pathway) or isolated distal DVT (local VTE/haematology pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG158 (updated Aug 2023)NG12 (updated April 2026)MHRA (June 2019)GMMMG DOAC (Feb 2026)RCOG GTG 37b
Core rule: start a DOAC dosed by Cockcroft–Gault creatinine clearance without waiting for bloods, decide provoked or unprovoked (it sets the duration and the cancer review), and every patient leaves knowing sudden breathlessness means 999.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Sudden breathlessness, pleuritic chest pain, haemoptysis or collapse → 999 pulmonary embolism. Warn explicitly.
  • Massive, tense, painful, dusky leg (phlegmasia, limb-threatening iliofemoral DVT) → Same-day vascular assessment
  • Melaena, haematemesis, heavy vaginal bleeding, persistent epistaxis, haematuria, unexplained marked bruising or a bleed that will not stop → Same-day emergency assessment; 999 if heavy bleeding or collapse.
  • Head injury while anticoagulated, however minor → Same-day emergency department, even if initially well (bleeding can be delayed and silent).
  • Worsening leg pain and swelling despite treatment (clot extension or wrong diagnosis) → Same-day reassessment: adherence and dose, then specialist advice.

1Confirm — site, then provoked or unprovoked

Wells score and NICE pathway; a D-dimer never confirms DVT. Ultrasound proximal → this page; isolated distal → local pathway. Exclude PE clinically.

CategoryMeans
ProvokedSurgery, immobility, long-haul travel, combined pill, HRT, pregnancy, trauma
UnprovokedNo transient factor → cancer review (block 2)
OtherActive cancer, recurrent, unusual site, APS → specialist

2Check — take first, but start treatment without waiting

☐ FBC  ☐ Renal function with Cockcroft–Gault creatinine clearance (current weight, not eGFR)  ☐ LFTs  ☐ PT and APTT  ☐ Pregnancy test where relevant  ☐ Bleeding risk, interactions

Unprovoked: history, examination, baseline bloods; investigate further only if symptoms or signs suggest cancer (NG12). No whole-body screen; no routine thrombophilia tests.

3Decide — which anticoagulant

  • Most → DOAC first-line: apixaban or rivaroxaban (rivaroxaban 15/20 mg with food).
  • DOAC unsuitable → LMWH ≥5 days then dabigatran or edoxaban, or LMWH with warfarin ≥5 days and until INR ≥2.0 on 2 consecutive readings.
  • Established triple-positive APS → LMWH with warfarin until INR therapeutic, then warfarin; haematology advice. Suspected only → don't automatically withhold a DOAC; ask haematology.
  • Active cancer → consider a DOAC (tumour site, bleeding risk, interactions); else LMWH ± VKA.
  • Contraindicated → urgent specialist advice; no routine IVC filter.

4Duration — the decision at 3 months

Provoked, factor resolved≥3 months, then consider stopping if uncomplicated
Unprovoked≥3 months, then consider continuing, particularly if bleeding risk is low
Cancer-associated3–6 months, then review
Recurrent VTEUsually long-term; haematology

5Treat — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Mobilise as tolerated; no routine bed rest. Stockings only for ongoing leg symptoms, not routine prevention.
  • Combined pill or HRT → review; stop the combined pill; discuss progestogen-only or LNG-IUS.
  • Bleeding risk: no NSAIDs unless agreed; review antiplatelets, SSRIs, OTC/herbal; plan procedures. Flights: no self-given LMWH or aspirin.

6Monitor

  • First follow-up: dose, adherence, bleeding, leg; alert card given. Long-term: review at least annually — still needed?
  • Creatinine clearance when renal function changes and at least annually (more often if older or frail) → re-dose. FBC per protocol; anaemia → seek a bleed.

7Refer — specialist input

  • Haematology: recurrence on anticoagulation (confirm it; ask why), unusual site, APS, severe renal impairment, high bleeding risk, hard duration calls.
  • Pregnancy → LMWH; refer directly to obstetrics and haematology.
  • Cancer symptoms in unprovoked DVT → NG12 route for that symptom.

8Never

  • A DOAC in established triple-positive APS, or to treat VTE in pregnancy.
  • Delay treatment waiting for baseline bloods, or dose a DOAC on eGFR instead of Cockcroft–Gault.
  • Skip the cancer review in unprovoked DVT — or order an untargeted whole-body screen.
  • Stockings for routine prevention, or advice to rest in bed.
  • Switch agent after recurrence without asking why.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden breathlessness, sharp chest pain worse when you breathe in, coughing up blood, or collapse — call 999.”   Bleeding: “Black or bloody stools, vomiting blood or a bleed that will not stop — 999 or the emergency department straight away; say you are on a blood thinner. Any head injury — emergency department straight away, even if you feel well. Heavy vaginal bleeding, a nosebleed that won't stop, blood in urine or large bruises — 111 or emergency department the same day.”   The leg: “Worse pain or swelling — contact us the same day; very swollen, tense, painful or discoloured — emergency department straight away.”   Doses: “Take it every day; missed doses leave you unprotected. Don't stop it without advice. Carry your alert card.”
