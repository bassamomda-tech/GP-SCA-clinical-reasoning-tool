Tuberculosis (TB)

For: suspected active TB, latent TB testing, BCG and support during treatment in primary care. Not for: starting or changing TB treatment (TB service), contact tracing decisions (TB service and health protection team), children with suspected TB (paediatric TB service).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG33Green Book ch.32Notification Regs 2010NICE NG12CoSRH 2022
Core rule: chest X-ray and three sputums, urgent TB service referral and notification on suspicion — never start TB treatment in primary care.

0Act now

  • Headache, fever, confusion, drowsiness, neck stiffness (TB meningitis) → 999 or same-day admission.
  • Massive haemoptysis or respiratory distress → 999
  • Back pain with new limb weakness or bladder or bowel change → Same-day as cord compression.
  • Disseminated TB in an immunosuppressed person or young child → Same-day assessment.
  • Aged 40+ with unexplained haemoptysis → Suspected cancer pathway (NG12).

1Suspect

Cough over 3 weeks with weight loss, night sweats, fever or haemoptysis — especially in risk groups: high-incidence country, contact, HIV or immunosuppression, homelessness, prison, drug or alcohol misuse.

SiteFirst step
PulmonaryChest X-ray + 3 sputums (one early-morning)
Lymph node, spine, abdomenUrgent referral with TB in mind

2Check

☐ HIV test ☐ FBC, CRP, U&E, LFTs ☐ HbA1c ☐ household contacts, children under 5

Don't miss: a normal X-ray or negative IGRA does not exclude active TB.

3Decide — in parallel

  • X-ray suggests TB or high suspicion → Urgent TB service; don't wait for culture.
  • Notify the health protection team on suspicion: in writing within 3 days, phone if urgent.
  • Cover coughs, avoid new contacts, stay off work until the TB service advises.

5Treat — TB service only

2 monthsRifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol
Then 4 monthsRifampicin and isoniazid
RifampicinCheck every interaction: pills, implants, DOACs, warfarin, methadone
ContraceptionCopper IUD, LNG-IUD or DMPA
  • Doses per the TB service, BNF and local formulary. No 'trial' of TB drugs.

6Monitor — every contact

  • Jaundice, vomiting, abdominal pain → stop and contact the TB service the same day.
  • Vision change on ethambutol → stop and contact the TB service the same day.
  • Adherence; orange urine and tears are expected.

7Prevent

  • New entrants 16–35, arrived in last 5 years from incidence ≥150 per 100,000 → IGRA (programme areas).
  • Neonatal BCG for at-risk infants (40 per 100,000), after SCID screening.

8Never

  • Start or 'trial' TB treatment in primary care.
  • Wait for sputum culture before referring.
  • Wait for confirmation before notifying.
  • Prescribe on rifampicin without checking interactions.
  • Give an empirical fluoroquinolone when TB is suspected.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you cough up a lot of blood, can't breathe, or become confused or very drowsy with a bad headache.”   Same-day “On TB tablets, if your skin or eyes turn yellow, you keep vomiting or your eyesight changes, stop them and contact the TB team or us the same day.”   Reassure: “TB is curable and treatment is free for everyone.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
