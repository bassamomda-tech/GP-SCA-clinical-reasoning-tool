Type 2 diabetes in Ramadan

For: adults with type 2 diabetes who intend to fast, ideally seen 6–8 weeks before Ramadan. Not for: type 1 or complex insulin (diabetes team), pregnancy (obstetric and diabetes teams), established DKA (DKA protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
IDF-DAR 2021BIMA 2020NICE NG28MHRAGMMMG
Core rule: assess 6–8 weeks before Ramadan, stratify the risk, move the bigger dose to iftar and trim the suhoor dose, and agree the break-the-fast rules out loud — testing glucose does not invalidate the fast.

0Act now — break the fast; do not wait for sunset

  • Vomiting, abdominal pain, dehydration, drowsiness, confusion, deep/rapid breathing, significant hyperglycaemia or ketonaemia (suspected DKA or HHS) → Same-day: immediate hospital assessment; confused, collapsed, severely dehydrated, unable to travel safely or unstable → 999. On an SGLT2 inhibitor glucose may be near normal — act on symptoms and blood ketones.
  • Glucose below 3.9 mmol/L, or any hypo symptoms (sweating, tremor, palpitations, confusion) → Break the fast now, treat, do not resume that day — even with no meter to hand.
  • Glucose above 16.7 mmol/L → break the fast, recheck; hydration and ketones if unwell or on an SGLT2 inhibitor. Persistent or with symptoms → Same-day advice.
  • Any acute illness, vomiting or unable to keep hydrated → break the fast, sick day rules, resume when well.

2Check — 6–8 weeks before Ramadan

☐ HbA1c, glucose profile  ☐ Hypo frequency and awareness  ☐ Previous fasting (“dizzy afternoons” = probable hypos)  ☐ Every drug, incl. hidden sulfonylurea in combination tablets  ☐ eGFR, CVD, frailty  ☐ Pregnancy / breastfeeding  ☐ Work, ability to monitor, fluid restrictions

3Decide — a shared decision

  • Tier 1 → explain the medical reasons, the exemption and alternatives (non-consecutive or winter fasts, feeding others, fidyah); offer a trusted scholar.
  • Fasting against advice → do not abandon care: document; education, monitoring, specialist support.
  • Trial fast: consider only for selected lower-risk people. No non-urgent new agents or major changes during Ramadan — make them now.
  • Lifestyle: late suhoor, slow-release carbohydrate; water and sugar-free fluids iftar to suhoor (individual advice in HF, advanced CKD, dialysis); no strenuous exercise before iftar.

5Treat — bigger dose to iftar; suhoor dose is the dangerous one

DrugPlan
MetforminTotal usually unchanged; OD → iftar, BD → iftar + suhoor; no excessive single dose

  • Written drug plan for all; complex insulin with specialist input; meter and SMBG training. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • SMBG: suhoor, morning, midday, mid-afternoon (highest risk), pre-iftar, 2 h after iftar, any time unwell.
  • During: review fasting tier 1–2 (SGLT2i and insulin earlier): readings, hypos, dehydration; same-day advice access.
  • After: revert doses and timings (not left on reduced insulin); review episodes; HbA1c in time.

7Refer — 6–8 weeks ahead, not mid-month

  • Diabetes team: type 1; complex insulin, or insulin without safe-fasting experience; SGLT2i decisions; recurrent hypos or poor awareness; brittle control; advanced complications; CKD 4–5.
  • Obstetric and diabetes teams: pre-existing diabetes in pregnancy, or insulin/SU-treated GDM, intending to fast.

8Never

  • Wait for a meter reading before breaking the fast for hypo symptoms.
  • Rely on glucose to exclude DKA on an SGLT2i, or just move it to iftar.
  • Glibenclamide where avoidable, or a daytime (lunch) insulin bolus.
  • Abandon care when a high-risk patient fasts, or leave fasting doses running afterwards.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Vomiting, tummy pain, drowsiness, confusion, deep fast breathing, high ketones or very high sugar — break the fast and get seen in hospital straight away; call 999 if you are confused, collapsed or very unwell. Do not wait for sunset.”   Break and check: “Below 3.9 or above 16.7, or any shakiness, sweating or confusion — break the fast. If you cannot check, break it anyway.”   Testing: “The guidance says testing your sugar does not break your fast — test as often as you need.”   After: “Come back after Ramadan to put your doses back to normal.”
