Benign prostate enlargement and male LUTS

For: men (and trans women and non-binary people with male reproductive organs) with lower urinary tract symptoms in primary care. Not for: PSA testing without symptoms (follow the Prostate Cancer Risk Management Programme); surgery and minimally invasive procedures (urology).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG97NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)NICE TA290 · TA999MHRA DSU (May 2026)SPC (emc)
Core rule: exclude retention, haematuria, a malignant-feeling prostate and neurological causes first; then sort voiding, storage or night-time urine and match the drug to the dominant problem.

0Act now — deal with these before treating symptoms

  • Acute retention → Same-day catheterisation; ED if no prompt catheter, sepsis, severe pain, renal impairment, haematuria, trauma or neuro concern. Offer an alpha-blocker before catheter removal.
  • Chronic retention (palpable bladder, residual >1 litre, overflow or nocturnal incontinence) → Urgent creatinine, upper-tract imaging, urology; with renal impairment or hydronephrosis → Same-day
  • New retention, hesitancy or incontinence + back pain, saddle loss, bilateral sciatica, reduced anal tone or leg weakness → Emergency cauda equina pathway.
  • Fever, rigors, perineal pain, tender prostate → acute prostatitis: urine culture, local antibiotics, no vigorous prostatic massage; septic, can't void or worsening → Same-day admit
  • Malignant-feeling prostate → refer, whatever the PSA · PSA above age threshold → consider referral → Suspected cancer pathway
  • ≥45 unexplained visible haematuria without UTI, or recurring after treated UTI · ≥60 non-visible + dysuria or raised WCC → Suspected cancer pathway

1Confirm — LUTS and prostate enlargement are not synonymous

Voiding: hesitancy, weak or intermittent stream, straining, incomplete emptying, terminal dribble. Storage: frequency, urgency, urge incontinence, nocturia. Post-micturition: dribble after leaving the toilet. Nocturnal polyuria: excess share of 24-hour urine made at night.

3-day frequency-volume chart + IPSS at baseline: 0–7 mild, 8–19 moderate, 20–35 severe.

2Check — first visit, per CG97

☐ Medicines, diabetes, heart failure, sleep apnoea  ☐ Abdomen, genitals, DRE  ☐ Dipstick  ☐ Informed PSA offer  ☐ eGFR and residual if retention suspected

NG12 PSA thresholds (micrograms/litre): 40–49 >2.5 · 50–59 >3.5 · 60–69 >4.5 · 70–79 >6.5; judgement <40 and ≥80. Treat UTI before PSA.

3Decide — not everything needs a tablet

  • Mild or not bothersome → explain, reassure, conservative measures alone.
  • Everyone: fluid timing; less caffeine, fizzy drinks, alcohol; medicine review (diuretic timing, opioids, antihistamines, decongestants).
  • Storage → offer supervised bladder training. Dribble → urethral milking.
  • Moderate to severe → drug matched to the dominant symptom.

5Treat — by symptom type and prostate size

VoidingOffer an alpha-blocker, e.g. tamsulosin 400 micrograms once daily
Large gland>30 g or PSA >1.4 ng/ml + high progression risk → offer a 5-ARI; consider + alpha-blocker
StorageOffer an antimuscarinic (consider adding to an alpha-blocker); unsuitable → mirabegron, vibegron
Nocturnal polyuriaTreat cause, fluid timing → consider late-afternoon loop diuretic; desmopressin only after causes excluded, others failed
  • Alpha-blocker: postural hypotension, falls; caution with antihypertensives, PDE5 inhibitors; floppy iris.
  • 5-ARI (MHRA): finasteride — depression, suicidal ideation, possibly persistent sexual dysfunction; dutasteride — precaution. Pregnant partner: no handling broken tablets; condom.
  • Antimuscarinic: anticholinergic burden; no oxybutynin if frail. Doses: BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • PSA on a 5-ARI ≥6 months: roughly double it; new baseline; confirmed rise from nadir → assess.
  • Desmopressin: sodium at baseline and 3 days (aged ≥65: also 4–8 days, 1 month); <135 mmol/L → stop.
  • Mirabegron: BP before and during; not if ≥180/110 uncontrolled. Not improving? Drug matched, long enough, taken?

7Refer — specialist assessment (urgent ones in block 0)

  • Recurrent or persistent UTI, retention, renal impairment from lower tract dysfunction, stones; stress incontinence; bothersome despite (or not wanting) conservative and drug treatment.

8Never

  • Reassure on a normal PSA when the prostate feels malignant.
  • Read PSA in acute prostatitis, or on a 5-ARI without doubling it.
  • An antimuscarinic in poor emptying without specialist advice (retention).
  • Desmopressin with CrCl <50 mL/min, fluid overload, polydipsia, SIADH or past hyponatraemia.
  • Homeopathy, phytotherapy or acupuncture for LUTS; a PDE5 inhibitor with nitrates.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “Can't pass urine — contact us or NHS 111 straight away; A&E if not seen quickly, or fever, severe pain or blood.”   Same day: “Blood in your urine, even if it stops; fever, shivers, pain between your legs or burning — contact us the same day (or NHS 111).”   Finasteride / dutasteride: “Low mood or thoughts of self-harm — contact us the same day; tell us about sexual problems.”   Alpha-blocker: “Get up slowly; tell us if faint or you fall; tell the eye surgeon.”
