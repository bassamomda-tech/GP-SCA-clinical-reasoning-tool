Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) and PMDD

For: cyclical premenstrual symptoms in primary care, including after hysterectomy with ovaries conserved. Not for: a depressive or anxiety disorder present all month (use the depression protocol, and treat the premenstrual worsening too) or perimenopause (use the menopause protocol).

RCOG Green-top 48 (2016)NICE NG225NICE NG222 (updated Dec 2025)UKMEC 2025BNF
Core rule: the timing is the diagnosis — prove the luteal pattern with a two-cycle prospective diary, and ask directly about suicidal thoughts, because a cyclical illness is still dangerous on the worst days.

0Act now — ask directly about suicidal thoughts, intent, plans and means; no risk scores

  • Immediate danger, or self-harm or overdose needing medical treatment → 999 / A&E
  • Current intent, a plan, recent serious self-harm, inability to remain safe, psychosis or risk to a child → Same-day mental-health assessment via the local crisis pathway. Child at risk → also local safeguarding procedures.
  • Luteal-phase suicidal thoughts without current intent → matter even if they lift after the period: documented luteal-phase safety plan (warning signs, coping, contacts) and close follow-up.
  • Past elevated mood, reduced need for sleep, grandiosity → possible bipolar: mental-health advice before antidepressant monotherapy.
  • Severe PMDD, marked impairment or significant risk → may justify starting an SSRI or hormonal treatment promptly while the diary continues. Unrelated red flags (persistent intermenstrual or postcoital bleeding, pelvic mass, unexplained weight loss) → investigate on their own pathway.

1Confirm — no blood test; the pattern over time

Prospective daily diary for ≥2 cycles (DRSP) before a definitive diagnosis, unless risk or impairment needs urgent treatment.

2Check — before choosing treatment

☐ Diary started  ☐ Suicide risk and safety plan  ☐ Bipolar features  ☐ Pregnancy plans and contraception  ☐ UKMEC: BP, BMI, smoking, VTE, migraine with aura  ☐ Current medicines

Do not request reproductive hormone levels; FBC, ferritin or TSH only if the history suggests anaemia or thyroid disease. Don't miss: progestogen side effects from contraception or HRT; perimenopause.

3Decide — by symptoms, contraception needs and preference

  • Everyone: explain and validate; exercise, sleep, regular meals, less caffeine and alcohol (supportive only). Lifestyle first is not required.
  • First line: an SSRI, a drospirenone-containing COC (may be considered) if UKMEC allows, or CBT — alone or with medication.
  • Mild: vitamin B6 may be considered (limited evidence); avoid high or prolonged doses (neuropathy).
  • All-month symptoms: treat the underlying disorder and the premenstrual worsening.

5Treat — RCOG steps

First lineSSRI, low dose, continuous or luteal (from after ovulation / ~14 days before expected period until bleeding). Drospirenone COC, continuous or shortened breaks. CBT.
Second lineOptimise or change the SSRI; SSRI + eligible COC; consider spironolactone for selected physical symptoms (renal function, K⁺, contraception).
Specialist-supportedTransdermal estradiol (off-label) for severe, treatment-resistant symptoms — needs contraception and endometrial protection.
SpecialistGnRH analogue + add-back HRT beyond 6 months, bone density. Rarely hysterectomy + BSO after a successful GnRH trial.
  • SSRI: off-label; taper after continuous use; check bleeding risk with anticoagulants or NSAIDs. Doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • SSRIs, estradiol and GnRH analogues are not contraception. An LNG-IUS alone does not treat PMS.

6Monitor — the diary judges the treatment

  • SSRI, aged 18–25 or at increased suicide risk: review 1 week after starting or a dose rise, and no later than 4 weeks after starting.
  • After 1–2 cycles: response vs diary baseline, adverse effects, adherence. After 2–3 cycles: continue, alter or refer.
  • At least annually; sooner if treatment changes, pregnancy is planned, symptoms or risk worsen, postpartum or perimenopause.

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent in block 0)

  • Gynaecology / specialist premenstrual service: severe despite SSRI, COC and CBT; estradiol, GnRH or surgery being considered; progestogen intolerance or complex contraception.
  • Mental-health services: suspected bipolar disorder, persistent all-month mood disorder, risk significant despite a safety plan, recurrent self-harm, diagnostic uncertainty.

8Never

  • A risk score, or a low/medium/high label, to predict suicide or self-harm (NG225).
  • A combined pill with migraine with aura (UKMEC 4).
  • Antidepressant monotherapy when bipolar disorder is suspected, without mental-health advice.
  • The pill alone for an all-month depression labelled as PMS.
  • A GnRH analogue to “prove” PMDD without a specialist; hysterectomy with ovaries conserved or endometrial ablation for PMS.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you ever feel you can’t keep yourself safe, call 999 or go to A&E straight away; for urgent help that isn’t an emergency, contact us the same day or call 111 and choose the mental health option.”   New SSRI: “If you feel agitated or more anxious, or your mood or suicidal thoughts get worse, contact us the same day.”   Diary: “Keep the diary going — it tells us whether treatment is working.”
