Eating disorders

For: suspected or known anorexia nervosa, atypical anorexia, bulimia, binge eating disorder, ARFID or insulin omission in type 1 diabetes. Under 18: age- and sex-adjusted criteria and the local CAMHS eating-disorder pathway. Not for: loss of appetite as a symptom (use the appetite loss and nutritional assessment pathway, which holds the NG12 cancer routes).

NICE NG69 (2017, updated 2020)MEED 2022 (RCPsych CR233)NICE CG32BAPEN 2018
Core rule: refer the moment you suspect it, whatever the BMI, SCOFF or readiness — physical risk (MEED), not the weight, decides urgency, and refeeding is never started in primary care.

0Act now — assess physical risk first, every time; do not wait for the eating-disorder service

  • Collapse, severe chest symptoms, altered consciousness, severe breathlessness or suspected life-threatening arrhythmia → 999
  • MEED: one red or ≥2 amber parameters → urgent senior discussion with the eating-disorder and acute medical teams; Same-day medical assessment. Adult red: awake HR <40, systolic BP <90 / significant postural change / recurrent syncope, prolonged QTc, K⁺ <2.5 mmol/L, BMI <13. BMI alone never decides.
  • Syncope, chest pain or palpitations, severe dehydration, severe electrolyte abnormality or hypoglycaemia, ECG abnormality, severe weakness, acute food/fluid refusal → Same-day medical assessment
  • Refeeding syndrome developing (low or falling phosphate, K⁺ or Mg²⁺ after feeding, with deterioration) → Emergency admission
  • Significant suicide risk or self-harm → Same-day or emergency assessment. Insulin omission or DKA in type 1 diabetes → Same-day eating-disorder, diabetes and acute teams (DKA protocol).

1Confirm — the diagnosis is clinical

SCOFF ≥2 = positive screen → clinical assessment. A screening aid only: a negative SCOFF never overrides history, examination or weight trajectory.

DisorderKey features
AnorexiaRestriction, low weight, fear of weight gain, body-image disturbance
Atypical anorexiaAll criteria except low weight — can be severe at any BMI
BulimiaBinges + compensation, normal or raised BMI; dental erosion, Russell's sign, low K⁺
Binge eatingBinges without compensation; often presents as obesity
ARFIDRestriction without body-image disturbance (outside NG69)

2Check — quantify physical risk, not the diagnosis

☐ Weight trajectory, BMI, pulse, lying/standing BP, temperature  ☐ SUSS  ☐ ECG  ☐ U&E, phosphate, magnesium, glucose, FBC  ☐ Suicide, self-harm, safeguarding  ☐ Refeeding risk

Don't miss: abnormal LFTs are not automatically starvation; appetite loss without body-image disturbance → organic work-up.

3Decide — refer on suspicion

  • Suspected → refer immediately to the age-appropriate eating-disorder service (CEDS), whatever the BMI, SCOFF or readiness (Bolton / Greater Manchester pathway).
  • Under 18 → CAMHS eating-disorder urgent pathway (standard: urgent 1 week, routine 4 weeks).
  • Urgent + same-day medical assessment if physical compromise, rapid weight loss, severe restriction or purging, suicidality, electrolyte or ECG abnormality, refeeding concern.

5Treat — delivered by the specialist service

AnorexiaAdults: CBT-ED, MANTRA or SSCM. Under 18: family therapy
Bulimia (adults)Guided self-help → individual CBT-ED if not effective at 4 weeks
Binge eatingGuided self-help → CBT-ED. No weight-loss messaging
Medicines · adjunct onlyNever the sole treatment; specialist-led
  • Refeeding risk (CG32: BMI <16, loss >15% in 3–6 months, little intake >10 days, or low K⁺, phosphate or Mg²⁺) → urgent specialist assessment; no calorie prescription in primary care.
  • Any psychotropic coordinated with the team (QTc, electrolytes, overdose) — doses per BNF and local formulary. Fit note; involve family with consent.

6Monitor

  • Each review: intake, weight trajectory, purging/exercise, pulse, BP, mental-health risk; full MEED if deteriorating.
  • Bloods and ECG by current risk and the specialist plan; DEXA risk-directed.
  • Anorexia not in specialist care → review at least annually.

7Refer — other routes

  • Depression → NHS Talking Therapies (mild–moderate) or psychiatry (severe). ARFID → local ARFID pathway.
  • Not improving? Re-score MEED, check engagement, rethink the diagnosis, treat comorbidity → back to CEDS.

8Never

  • Let a normal BMI or a negative SCOFF reassure you.
  • Delay referral for waiting lists or until the patient feels "ready".
  • Prescribe a calorie or refeeding plan in primary care — cardiac risk peaks during refeeding.
  • Start medication for anorexia without specialist advice, or levothyroxine for a low T3 of starvation.
  • Act as the therapist or police their eating; or discharge from GP care.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Collapse, severe chest symptoms, altered consciousness, severe breathlessness — call 999.”   Same-day “Fainting, chest pain, palpitations, severe weakness, can't keep fluids down, severe dehydration or a very slow pulse — same-day medical assessment.”   Weight loss ≥1 kg a week despite treatment → same-day; suicidal thoughts with intent or plan, or self-harm → same-day or emergency.   Relapse: “Setbacks are part of this. Coming back early keeps you safe.” Beat for patients and families.
