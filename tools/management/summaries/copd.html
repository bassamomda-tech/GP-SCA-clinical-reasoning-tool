COPD in adults

For: adults with suspected or confirmed COPD — diagnosis, stable care and the exacerbation. Not for: children, or asthma without COPD (use the asthma protocol); new unexplained symptoms that may be lung cancer (NICE NG12 pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG115 (reviewed July 2026)NG114NG12BTS/ICS · BTS home oxygenGOLD 2024 (context only)
Core rule: confirm on post-bronchodilator spirometry, judge every flare against the patient's own baseline, and give controlled oxygen to 88–92% — never uncontrolled high-flow, never withheld in critical hypoxaemia.

0Act now — know the baseline SpO₂ first, then check every time

  • Severe flare: breathless at rest, RR ≥25, SpO₂ acutely below baseline, confusion, drowsiness, cyanosis, exhaustion → 999 Controlled O₂ (e.g. Venturi 24–28%) to 88–92%; air-driven nebulised salbutamol + ipratropium where available; oral prednisolone. Tell the crew the target.
  • Drowsy, asterixis, headache on oxygen (CO₂ narcosis) → titrate to 88–92%, do not abruptly remove oxygen → 999
  • Sudden dyspnoea, unilateral absent breath sounds, hyperresonance (pneumothorax); suspected PE (disproportionate hypoxia) → 999 Pneumonia (fever >38.5 °C, hypotension, consolidation) → 999 or same-day hospital.
  • Moderate flare not better in 1–2 h, SpO₂ 88–92% and falling, new cor pulmonale, first flare, poor home support → Same-day hospital
  • Unexplained haemoptysis aged 40+, or CXR suggesting lung cancer → Urgent suspected cancer pathway Aged 40+ with ≥2 unexplained symptoms (≥1 if ever smoked) → offer CXR ≤2 weeks

1Confirm — never on pre-bronchodilator spirometry

Post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC <0.70 in a symptomatic person with a risk factor (aged ≥35, smoker or ex-smoker, occupational dust or fumes). No routine reversibility testing. An old label with no post-bronchodilator ratio → get one.

Asthmatic features = previous asthma or atopy, higher blood eosinophils, FEV₁ variation ≥400 ml, or diurnal peak-flow variation ≥20%.

2Check — at diagnosis

☐ CXR  ☐ FBC (incl. eosinophils)  ☐ BMI  ☐ MRC + CAT  ☐ Smoking

Selected: α₁-antitrypsin if <40, minimal smoking or family history; ECG + BNP if cor pulmonale; CT if bronchiectasis, fibrosis or cancer suspected. New or disproportionate symptoms → reassess via NG12, not COPD.

3Decide — non-drug work first

  • Smoking cessation at every contact — the only thing that slows FEV₁ decline. Varenicline or cytisinicline, NRT, behavioural support.
  • Pulmonary rehab → offer at MRC ≥3; refer early after an admission for a flare.
  • Vaccines: influenza, COVID-19, pneumococcal, RSV per the Green Book.
  • Written self-management plan; rescue pack only for selected, educated patients. BMI <20 → dietitian.

Step CSpecialist: azithromycin, roflumilast, biologics
  • Before every step: smoking, watched technique, rehab, second diagnosis (heart failure, anaemia, PE, cancer).
  • LAMA alone is fine if stable or LABA unsuitable. Triple: offer from LABA + ICS; consider from LABA + LAMA (symptoms only → 3-month trial).
  • ICS: document why, review yearly, warn of pneumonia. No asthmatic features → LABA + LAMA instead.
  • Flare: more salbutamol + prednisolone 30 mg OD × 5 days + antibiotic on NG114 judgement, not purulence alone: amoxicillin 500 mg TDS, doxycycline 200 mg then 100 mg OD, or clarithromycin 500 mg BD, 5 days; non-macrolide if on azithromycin. Check allergy, pregnancy, renal function, QT. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Yearly (6-monthly if severe): MRC, CAT, flares, technique, BMI, mood.
  • Stable SpO₂ ≤92% → LTOT assessment (never just after a flare).
  • After a flare: prompt review — SpO₂ at baseline? rehab, rescue pack.
  • ≥2 steroid courses/year → review ladder, bone protection, adrenals.

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Aged <40, little smoking or family history; fast FEV₁ fall; doubt.
  • Flaring on optimised triple → respiratory, plus bronchiectasis work-up.
  • Severe emphysema (FEV₁ <50%) → lung-volume reduction.
  • Advanced disease: advance care planning, ReSPECT; low-dose oral morphine and fan for refractory breathlessness.

8Never

  • Uncontrolled high-flow oxygen or a 94–98% target if hypercapnia is possible.
  • A COPD label from pre-bronchodilator spirometry, or no spirometry at all.
  • A 14-day prednisolone course, or a taper after a single 5-day course.
  • ICS stopped despite asthmatic features, or kept with no documented reason.
  • LTOT for breathlessness without hypoxaemia, or assessed after a flare.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you become drowsy or confused, your lips go blue, or you are exhausted and your breathing is not improving with the usual treatment.”   Same-day “If you start the rescue pack and are not improving within a day or two — or your oxygen levels drop — contact us the same day.”   Rescue pack: “Let us know each time you use it.”   Home oxygen: “Smoking near it risks serious facial burns and fire.”
