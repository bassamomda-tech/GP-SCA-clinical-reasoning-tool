Infective conjunctivitis

For: adults and children with a red, sticky or watery eye from suspected infection. Not for: painful red eye, reduced vision or neonates (same day), or allergic conjunctivitis (separate protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CKSUKHSA exclusion tableMHRA 2021College of Optometrists
Core rule: check visual acuity and ask about pain, photophobia and contact lenses in every red eye; most conjunctivitis needs self-care, not antibiotics.

0Act now

  • Pain, photophobia, reduced acuity, ciliary flush, corneal staining, abnormal pupil → Same-day ophthalmology
  • Contact lens wearer with a painful red eye → stop lenses, Same-day eye assessment
  • Neonate under 28 days with conjunctivitis → Same-day paediatrics / ophthalmology
  • Hyperacute profuse pus (gonococcal) → Same-day ophthalmology; chemical splash → irrigate, then Same-day eye casualty

1Confirm

TypeClues
BacterialPurulent, lids stuck; 5–7 days
ViralWatery, follicles, preauricular node; 1–2 weeks
ChlamydialChronic, follicular, genital symptoms

2Check

☐ Visual acuity each eye ☐ Fluorescein ☐ Pupils ☐ Contact lenses ☐ Sexual history if chronic ☐ Pregnancy

Don't miss: herpes simplex (dendritic ulcer) — same-day; never steroids.

3Decide

  • Self-care for most.
  • Consider a topical antibiotic if severe, or a delayed prescription if not improving after about 3 days.
  • No antibiotic for viral conjunctivitis.

5Treat

Self-careClean lids, cool compress, lubricants, hygiene
First lineChloramphenicol 0.5% drops 2-hourly for 2 days, then 4-hourly; ointment at night
AlternativeFusidic acid 1% twice daily for 7 days
STIChlamydia or gonorrhoea: systemic treatment via sexual health

Under 2: chloramphenicol can be prescribed (MHRA 2021). Doses per BNF/BNFC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Bacterial: return if not improving after 7 days of treatment.
  • Viral: return if vision blurs, pain develops, or beyond 3 weeks.

7Refer

  • Chlamydial or gonococcal → sexual health (partner notification).
  • Persistent despite treatment or recurrent → ophthalmology or community urgent eye care.

8Never

  • Treat a painful red eye with reduced vision as conjunctivitis.
  • Prescribe topical steroids for a red eye in primary care.
  • Let a contact lens wearer keep wearing lenses.
  • Manage a neonate's conjunctivitis without same-day assessment.
  • Exclude a child from school for conjunctivitis.

9Safety-net

Same-day "Contact us or NHS 111 the same day if your eye becomes painful, bright light hurts, your vision blurs, or you wear contact lenses and it is getting worse. A baby under four weeks with a red or sticky eye needs to be seen the same day."   "Your child doesn't need to stay off school."
