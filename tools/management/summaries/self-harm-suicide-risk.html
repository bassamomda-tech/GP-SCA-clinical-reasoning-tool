Self-harm and suicide risk

For: anyone who has self-harmed or has suicidal thoughts, seen in primary care or after an ED attendance. Not for: managing the medical effects of overdose (emergency department). Under 18 → same-day CAMHS or paediatric discussion and safeguarding.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG225 (2022)NICE QS34NICE NG222NCISHDHSC strategy 2023
Core rule: treat physical harm first, never score risk as low, medium or high to decide care, agree a safety plan, and make contact again within 48 hours.

0Act now — physical harm and high concern for safety

  • Any intentional overdose, however small, even if well → Same-day ED; drowsy, confused, unknown amount or time → 999
  • Paracetamol overdose: symptom-free early; timed level from 4 hours, acetylcysteine best within 8 hours → Same-day ED
  • Serious wound, burn, ligature or attempted hanging → Same-day hospital; airway, breathing, reduced consciousness or heavy bleeding → 999
  • Plan or intent, preparations, psychosis, severe agitation or hopelessness, cannot agree a safety plan → Same-day crisis or liaison assessment (England: NHS 111 option 2)
  • In immediate danger and will not wait → 999; do not leave them alone. Under 18 → Same-day CAMHS / paediatrics + safeguarding

1Confirm — ask directly

What happened, when, method, planning, intent; suicidal thoughts, plans, preparations, access to means, previous attempts, what has held them back. Asking does not put the idea in someone's head.

DoDo not
Psychosocial assessment of needs and what drives the self-harmUse risk tools or scales to predict suicide or repetition, or to decide care

2Check

☐ Mental illness ☐ Alcohol and drugs ☐ Debt, housing, abuse, loss ☐ Physical illness and pain ☐ Medicines at home ☐ Protective factors ☐ Pregnancy or postnatal

Don't miss: the patient who looks well after paracetamol, and older adults, in whom self-harm is more often lethal in intent.

3Decide — where today

  • Physical harm → emergency department; high concern for safety → crisis or liaison team today.
  • Otherwise → community plan: safety plan, means, support, follow-up.
  • Consider a structured psychological intervention tailored to self-harm; DBT-A for young people who frequently self-harm.

5Treat — the safety plan

PlanWarning signs, coping, people and places, crisis numbers, written and shared
MeansReturn surplus medicines; trusted person holds them; remove ligatures
SupportFamily or friends with consent; debt, abuse, bereavement services
Prescribe safelyLimited quantities; lower-toxicity choices; no drug to reduce self-harm
  • Under 25 starting an antidepressant → review within 1 week. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — keep in contact

  • GP assessed, concerns remain → initial aftercare within 48 hours, same clinician if possible.
  • After ED → read the letter, make contact soon, ideally within 48 hours.
  • Missed appointment → phone; contact the mental health team if unreachable.

7Refer

  • Ongoing self-harm or significant mental illness → community mental health team, NHS Talking Therapies or CAMHS.
  • Practical help: debt advice, domestic abuse, drug and alcohol services, social prescribing.

8Never

  • Label someone low, medium or high risk and allocate care by that label.
  • Keep an overdose patient in primary care because they look well.
  • Prescribe large quantities of medicines that are toxic in overdose.
  • Treat a repeat episode as 'the same as usual' without fresh assessment.
  • Leave a person you believe is in immediate danger on their own.

9Safety-net — say it, write it down

999 "If you feel you can't keep yourself safe, or you've taken something or hurt yourself badly, call 999 or go to A&E now."   Same-day "To talk to someone today, ring NHS 111 and choose option 2, or call Samaritans on 116 123 at any time."   "Keep your safety plan where you can find it, and we'll speak again within two days."

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
