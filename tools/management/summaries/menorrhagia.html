Heavy menstrual bleeding

For: women with heavy periods in primary care. Not for: bleeding after the menopause (cancer route, block 0; bleeding on HRT → the bleeding-on-HRT pathway) or possible pregnancy (hCG first). Pain-led periods → dysmenorrhoea.

NICE NG88 (updated July 2026)NG12 (updated April 2026)FSRH UKMECBSH/BSG ironGMMMG formulary
Core rule: she defines “heavy”; ask about pregnancy plans before any treatment, take an FBC in every woman and act on it, treat in parallel with investigation, and never assume iron-deficiency anaemia is just the periods.

0Act now — the emergency and the cancer routes

  • Acute torrential bleeding with tachycardia, hypotension, dizziness or collapse → Emergency admission · 999 if collapsed or shocked
  • Unexplained post-menopausal bleeding aged 55+, not attributable to HRT → Suspected cancer pathway (2 weeks) Under 55 → consider one.
  • Ascites, or a pelvic or abdominal mass not obviously fibroids → Suspected cancer pathway Ovarian symptoms 40+ → CA125 per NG12 age thresholds; 39 or under → consider ultrasound.
  • Abnormal-looking or abnormal-feeling cervix → consider a Suspected cervical cancer (colposcopy) pathway referral — a normal screening result must not delay it.
  • Iron-deficiency anaemia at any adult age → offer quantitative FIT; ≥10 µg Hb/g → Suspected colorectal cancer pathway High concern despite low FIT → still refer. Coeliac serology.
  • Rapidly enlarging pelvic mass, or new pain and bulk with a known fibroid → Urgent gynaecology assessment and imaging

1Confirm — the diagnosis is the impact

NG88: loss that interferes with quality of life — don't measure or estimate it. Ask: flooding, clots, hourly or double protection, night changes, days off (the review baseline).

2Check — FBC in all; the rest targeted

☐ FBC in every woman, in parallel with treatment  ☐ Consider ferritin  ☐ hCG if pregnancy possible  ☐ Chlamydia screen if at risk

Examine if related symptoms, abnormal pattern, suspected structure or before LNG-IUS. Ultrasound if palpable uterus or mass, persistent intermenstrual bleeding, treatment failure, fibroids or adenomyosis suspected. No routine TFTs or hormone panels.

3Decide — one question reorganises the ladder

  • “Are you hoping to get pregnant?” — ask first; it picks the ladder (block 5).
  • Start treatment now, alongside any investigation — not after it.

5Treat — split by contraception and pregnancy plans

Step 1 · contraception OKLNG-IUS 52 mg — consider first (no pathology, fibroids <3 cm not distorting cavity, or adenomyosis). Warn of irregular bleeding; allow ≥6 cycles.
Step 2 · LNG-IUS declinedTranexamic acid, NSAID, CHC (check UKMEC) or norethisterone 5 mg TDS day 5–26 (not contraceptive).
Trying to conceiveTranexamic acid from onset of bleeding ± mefenamic acid; stop if no better after 3 cycles.
Fibroids ≥3 cm / distorted cavityRefer; start tranexamic acid, NSAID or CHC meanwhile.
  • Cautions: tranexamic — VTE history or risk, CHC, renal; mefenamic — GI bleed, renal, CVD, NSAID asthma, anticoagulants.
  • Acute heavy bleeding, stable: exclude pregnancy, local temporary regimen; severe or persistent → Same-day gynaecology
  • Iron: ferrous sulfate 200 mg daily or alternate days until Hb and stores restored. Doses per BNF and GM formulary.

6Monitor

  • ~3 months (sooner if worse): impact vs baseline, FBC, iron adherence. No better → change, investigate or refer.
  • Iron failing → adherence, coeliac, ongoing loss; IV iron per local pathway.
  • Annually: FBC · BP, BMI, UKMEC on CHC · LNG-IUS expiry.

7Refer — gynaecology; keep treating while she waits

  • Fibroids ≥3 cm or cavity distorted · adenomyosis · persistent intermenstrual bleeding · treatment failed or not tolerated.
  • Hysteroscopy ± sampling (not blind biopsy): persistent or irregular bleeding, failure, obesity/PCOS, tamoxifen, Lynch.

8Never

  • Attribute iron-deficiency anaemia to periods without thought — offer FIT.
  • Take the FBC and not act on it, or stop iron as soon as haemoglobin corrects.
  • Luteal-phase-only progestogen (days 15–26), danazol or etamsylate for HMB.
  • Delay treatment for investigation or referral.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

A&E now · 999 “If you feel faint, dizzy, breathless or your heart is racing, go to A&E now — call 999 if you collapse or feel very unwell.”   Same day “Soaking a pad every hour for more than a couple of hours, or bleeding suddenly much heavier or more painful — contact us the same day.”   Within a week “Bleeding between periods or after sex, any bleeding after the menopause, or new pelvic pressure or a lump — book to be seen within a week.”   Coil: “Irregular bleeding at first is expected — come back if troublesome.”   Iron: “Stools go dark; keep taking it after your blood count is normal, until we say.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
