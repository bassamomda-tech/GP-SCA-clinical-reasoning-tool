Asthma in adults and young people aged 12+

For: people aged 12 and over — diagnosis, chronic care and the acute attack in primary care. Not for: children aged 5–11 (different ladder — use the children's asthma protocol) or hospital/ICU care of the attack.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG245 (Nov 2024)BTS/SIGN 158 (acute)NRAD 2014NICE QS25MHRA
Core rule: grade every attack on PEF, SpO₂, RR, HR and speech, never on how settled they look; never leave confirmed asthma on SABA alone; review promptly after every attack.

0Act now — measure PEF (% best), SpO₂, RR, HR, speech first; the worst feature decides

  • Life-threatening — any one: PEF <33%, SpO₂ <92%, silent chest, cyanosis, poor effort, arrhythmia, exhaustion, altered consciousness, hypotension → 999 stay with them; O₂ 15 L/min non-rebreather, then titrate; O₂-driven nebulised salbutamol + ipratropium 500 micrograms; prednisolone 40–50 mg PO or IV hydrocortisone 100 mg.
  • Acute severe — any one: PEF 33–50%, RR ≥25, HR ≥110, cannot complete sentences (SpO₂ may be ≥92%) → 999 while treating: back-to-back nebulised salbutamol 2.5–5 mg + O₂ + prednisolone 40–50 mg.
  • Stridor or angio-oedema after a drug or food = anaphylaxis → 999 IM adrenaline 500 micrograms (0.5 mL of 1:1000) first. Acute severe in pregnancy → 999 O₂ to SpO₂ 94–98%, salbutamol + prednisolone as usual, alert obstetrics.
  • Moderate (PEF 50–75%, SpO₂ ≥92%) not improving after initial treatment, nocturnal presentation, previous near-fatal attack, recent admission, repeated steroid courses or poor support → Same-day hospital assessment. Responding (PEF >75% after bronchodilator) → GP review next day

1Confirm — objectively, in this order

TestAsthma confirmed if
1 · 17+: blood eosinophils (Eos) or FeNOEos > lab range, or FeNO ≥50 ppb
1 · 12–16: FeNO≥35 ppb
2 · Spirometry + reversibilityFEV₁ up ≥12% and ≥200 ml
3 · PEF diary, if no timely spirometryVariability ≥20% over 2 weeks
4 · Still suspectedRefer for bronchial challenge

Normal FeNO does not exclude it. Code suspected until confirmed; no long-term treatment on a trial alone — but never delay acute treatment.

2Check — diagnosis and every review

☐ Occupation ☐ Smoking/vaping ☐ Rhinitis, BMI ☐ Past attacks ☐ RCP 3 or ACT ☐ SABA count ☐ Technique

High risk: previous ICU/ventilation (flag it), >1 SABA a month, ≥3 drug classes, ED/admission <12 months, poor ICS adherence, psychosocial factors.

3Decide — who gets what

  • Confirmed asthma → no SABA-only regimen: ICS/formoterol AIR where suitable, or another ICS regimen.
  • Legacy ICS + SABA, well controlled → not wrong; plan any switch.
  • Everyone: written action plan (MART-specific if on MART), stop smoking, annual flu vaccine, treat rhinitis; BMI >30 → 5–10% weight loss.

4Target — control

MeasureGoal → if not met
ACT20–25 · 16–19 → check technique, adherence, triggers · ≤15 → prompt review
Reliever<3 days a week, no night waking, no attacks → else step up
SABA issues≤2 inhalers a year → else structured review (≥1 a month = high risk)

5Treat — review each step at 8–12 weeks

Step 1 · AIRLow-dose ICS/formoterol as needed only (e.g. Symbicort 200/6, 1 puff PRN)
Step 2 · low-dose MARTSymbicort 100/6 or 200/6, 1 BD + PRN
Step 3 · moderate MARTSymbicort 200/6, 2 BD + PRN (Fostair 100/6, 18+; not 200/6)
Step 4 · biomarker gateFeNO or eosinophils raised → refer. Both normal → 8–12-week trial of montelukast or a LAMA
  • Before every step: technique, adherence, triggers (smoking, work, NSAIDs, β-blockers incl. timolol drops), diagnosis.
  • Moderate attack: extra MART puffs to the product maximum, or salbutamol 4–10 puffs via spacer every 10–20 min; prednisolone 40–50 mg OD ≥5 days where indicated.
  • Step down after ≥3 months' control, with plan and early review. Limits and licences per SPC; montelukast — MHRA mood warning. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • After any attack: prompt review, within 48 hours a reasonable standard: steroids, PEF, trigger, technique, step up if indicated, update the plan.
  • At least annually: control score, technique, action plan, reliever count, smoking, flu jab, BMI; spirometry commonly every 1–2 years.
  • ≥2 steroid courses a year → escalate, biomarker gate, refer; consider bone and adrenal protection.

7Refer — respiratory specialist

  • Raised FeNO or Eos at step 4; uncontrolled after LTRA/LAMA trial; ≥2 steroid courses or ≥1 admission in 12 months.
  • Better away from work → promptly, to an occupational-asthma service. Diagnostic doubt → challenge test. Dysfunctional breathing → physiotherapy. Pregnancy, severe or deteriorating → early specialist.

8Never

  • Be reassured by a silent chest or normal PaCO₂ in a distressed patient — neither means improvement.
  • Start, continue or reissue a SABA-only regimen in confirmed asthma.
  • Stop ICS or MART in pregnancy — poor control harms the baby more.
  • Step up before checking technique, adherence, triggers and diagnosis.
  • Leave a life-threatening attack, or wait for the ambulance to treat.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you cannot speak in full sentences, your lips go blue, your reliever isn't touching it, or your peak flow drops below a third of your best.”   Same-day “If you need your reliever again within four hours, or wake at night with it, contact us the same day.”   Steroids: “Take the whole 5-day course.”   Review: “I want to see you within a couple of days of any attack, even if you feel fine.”
