Ulcerative colitis in adults

For: known UC in adults; the IBD flare plan comes first. Not for: inpatient ASUC (trust protocol); starting thiopurines or biologics, or pouch symptoms (IBD team).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG130NG199CG118NG12 (Apr 2026)BSG IBD 2025
Core rule: grade every flare with Truelove and Witts and send stool for culture and C. difficile before steroids — but never delay admission for acute severe colitis; steroids induce remission, they never maintain it.

0Act now — not all bloody diarrhoea in UC is a flare

  • Suspected toxic megacolon (distension with systemic toxicity, reduced bowel sounds), peritonism, heavy rectal bleeding with instability → 999 No antimotility drugs or opioids; alert gastro and surgeons.
  • Acute severe UC: ≥6 bloody stools a day plus any one of temperature >37.8°C, HR >90, Hb <105 g/L, ESR >30 mm/h → Same-day admission — phone the on-call gastroenterologist for direct admission and IV steroids. Do not wait for stool results.
  • Moderate flare not clearly better at 48–72 h of prednisolone, any worsening, can't keep fluids or tablets down, more bleeding, anaemia, low albumin, doubt about severity, severe flare on immunosuppression, suspected C. difficile → Same-day specialist, not a bigger dose.
  • Fever on azathioprine, mercaptopurine or a biologic → urgent FBC, Same-day review (neutropenic sepsis, CMV colitis).
  • Painful red eye with photophobia → Same-day ophthalmology. Pyoderma gangrenosum → Same-day dermatology; never debride.

1Confirm — grade severity, then know the extent

GradeBloody stools/daySystemicSetting
Mild<4None (Hb >115, ESR <20)Community
Moderate4–6Minimal (Hb >105, ESR <30)Community via clear local pathway; gastro ≤48–72 h
Severe≥6Any one (block 0)Same-day admission

Extent (last colonoscopy): proctitis · left-sided (to splenic flexure) · extensive — it decides the route.

2Check — every flare

☐ Obs, abdomen, PR, hydration, weight  ☐ Stool culture + C. difficile before steroids; parasites after travel  ☐ FBC, CRP, U&E, LFTs, albumin, ferritin  ☐ NSAIDs, antibiotics, hospital contact, 5-ASA adherence, pregnancy

Don't miss: infection; CMV on immunosuppression. Calprotectin or FIT never delays admission.

3Decide

  • Most flares: IBD nurse helpline first — the rescue plan may cover it.
  • Steroid dependence (≥2 courses in 12 months, or can't wean below 10 mg) → refer, not another course.
  • Confirmed C. difficile: vancomycin 125 mg QDS orally, 10 days.
  • Avoid starting NSAIDs; paracetamol for pain.

4Target — review on the clock

48–72 h phone → 5–7 days review (stools falling? C. difficile back?) → taper as planned. 5-ASA steps at ~4 weeks. Goal: mucosal healing, not just symptoms.

5Treat — by extent, then by severity

ProctitisMesalazine 1 g suppository nocte; inadequate → add oral 5-ASA or topical steroid.
Left-sided · extensiveLeft-sided: topical 5-ASA; no remission by 4 weeks → consider adding high-dose oral. Extensive: both.
Moderate · oral steroidPrednisolone 40 mg daily, taper planned at prescribing (4–8 weeks); 5-ASA continued.
SpecialistThiopurine (TPMT first); advanced therapies per NICE/GM pathway; colectomy.
  • Steroids: never stop abruptly after >3 weeks; >3 months → alendronate 70 mg weekly + calcium/vitamin D.
  • Maintain: 5-ASA for most; side effects → switch formulation. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — per GM shared care

  • 5-ASA: renal function before, ~3 months, then yearly; unexplained creatinine rise → discuss with gastro.
  • Thiopurine: FBC, LFTs, U&E 2-weekly until stable, monthly ×3, then ≥12-weekly. Cytopenia or rising LFTs → withhold, IBD team urgently. Interactions: allopurinol, febuxostat, trimethoprim.
  • Annually: steroids, FBC, iron, vaccines (no live on immunosuppression), bone, mood.

7Refer — urgent ones are in block 0

  • Urgent gastro 5–7 days non-severe flare on immunosuppression; positive stool culture; steroid dependence.
  • NG12: rectal mass → consider referral (no FIT); other colorectal features → FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g → suspected cancer pathway. Negative FIT never overrides concern.
  • Surveillance: from 8 years (BSG) or 10 (CG118); PSC yearly.

8Never

  • Loperamide, codeine, antispasmodics or opioids in an active flare (toxic megacolon).
  • Oral prednisolone at home for acute severe colitis, or delaying admission for stool results.
  • Steroids to maintain remission, or a bigger dose instead of specialist review.
  • Azathioprine or mercaptopurine without a TPMT result.
  • Methotrexate or mycophenolate in pregnancy; stopping effective UC treatment without specialist advice.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If your tummy becomes swollen and very painful or goes rigid, or you pass a lot of blood and feel faint or confused — call 999.”   Same day “Six or more bloody stools a day, a fever, not clearly better within two to three days of starting steroids, getting worse, or you can't keep the tablets down — contact us the same day.”   Mesalazine: “Keep taking it when well; don't stop without asking us.”   Immunosuppression: “Fever, sore throat, infection, jaundice, bruising or bleeding — checked the same day. Ask before any vaccine.”
