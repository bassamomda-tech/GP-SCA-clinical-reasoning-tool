Syphilis

For: sexually active adults with suspected or confirmed syphilis in primary care. Not for: treatment itself (sexual health clinic), congenital syphilis in infants (paediatrics) or children beyond the neonatal period (safeguarding; BNFC).

Core rule: think syphilis with any painless ulcer or unexplained rash, test serology and HIV, and refer to sexual health — eye, ear or nerve symptoms are same-day at any stage.

0Act now

  • Blurred vision, red or painful eye, floaters (ocular syphilis) → eye casualty and sexual health Same-day
  • Sudden hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo (otosyphilis) → ENT and sexual health Same-day
  • Headache, cranial nerve palsy, confusion, stroke-like symptoms (neurosyphilis) → acute medicine Same-day
  • Reactive antenatal screen or suspected syphilis in pregnancy → specialist Urgent; same-day with symptoms.

1Confirm — stages

StageClue
Primary (9–90 days)Painless indurated ulcer, local nodes
SecondaryNon-itchy rash incl. palms and soles, mucous patches, condylomata lata, alopecia
Early / late latentSerology only (within / beyond 2 years)
TertiaryGummas, aortitis, neurosyphilis

2Check

☐ Sexual history ☐ Syphilis serology (treponemal screen ± RPR) ☐ HIV test ☐ Chlamydia and gonorrhoea NAAT ☐ Hepatitis B and C as appropriate ☐ Pregnancy ☐ Eyes, ears, neurology. Don't miss: negative serology in early primary syphilis — repeat at 6 and 12 weeks.

3Decide

  • Suspected or confirmed → sexual health clinic; lesion or symptoms → same or next working day.
  • Don't give antibiotics for an ulcer before swabs unless advised.

5Treat — by sexual health

EarlyBenzathine penicillin 2.4 million units IM, single dose.
Late latentBenzathine penicillin weekly for 3 doses.
AllergyCeftriaxone mainly; no macrolides.
Eye, ear, CNSIV or daily IM under specialist care.
  • Warn about the Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction. Doses: NICE, SPC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • RPR titre at 3, 6 and 12 months; fourfold fall = response; fourfold rise → re-refer.
  • Partner look-back: primary 3 months, secondary and early latent 2 years.
  • Testing: at least annually if at risk; every 3 months for higher-risk MSM.

7Refer — and notify

  • Positive serology without symptoms → sexual health Next working day
  • Congenital syphilis is notifiable; acquired syphilis is reported by sexual health services.

8Never

  • Dismiss a painless genital, anal or oral ulcer.
  • Treat a rash on palms and soles without thinking of syphilis.
  • Forget the HIV test.
  • Use azithromycin to treat syphilis.
  • Leave visual or hearing symptoms in a patient with syphilis for routine review.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “Blurred vision, a painful red eye, sudden hearing loss, ringing in the ears, dizziness, a bad headache or weakness — get seen today and say you may have syphilis.”   After treatment: “A fever and aches in the first day is an expected reaction.”   Partners: “The clinic will help you tell partners, anonymously if you prefer.”
