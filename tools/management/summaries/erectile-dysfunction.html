Erectile dysfunction

For: men with erectile dysfunction (ED) assessed and treated in primary care. Not for: priapism or acute cardiac symptoms (emergency routes, block 0); second-line treatment (alprostadil, vacuum device fitting, prosthesis — urology/andrology); starting testosterone (specialist-guided, shared care).

EAU 2026BSSMPrinceton IIINICE NG12 (updated April 2026)Drug Tariff · GMMMG
Core rule: check the repeat list and poppers for nitrates, nicorandil or riociguat and ask about exertional cardiac symptoms before any PDE5 inhibitor — ED is a cardiovascular risk marker, not proof of coronary disease.

0Act now — screen before treating

  • Erection lasting 4 hours or more, painful or not → 999 / ED emergency urology. Approaching 4 hours, recurrent, or after intracavernosal treatment → Same-day urology advice.
  • New ED + exertional chest pain or dyspnoea, syncope, palpitations → Same-day assessment, defer PDE5 inhibitor; ACS or unstable → 999
  • Nitrates, nicorandil, riociguat or poppers → PDE5 inhibitor contraindicated. Chest pain within 24 hours of sildenafil or vardenafil, or 48 hours of tadalafil → 999, say when the tablet was taken.
  • Syncope or severe hypotension after a PDE5 inhibitor → 999 · Sudden vision or hearing loss → stop the drug, Same-day assessment.
  • Sudden severe testicular pain (possible torsion) → 999 / A&E · Severe penile pain, haematospermia + perineal pain, systemically unwell prostatitis, recent pelvic or perineal trauma → Same-day
  • Depression with hopelessness or suicidal thoughts → assess suicide risk now and arrange immediate mental-health assessment Same-day

1Confirm — classify: organic, psychogenic or mixed

PatternFeatures
OrganicGradual · absent morning erections · consistent · vascular risk factors
PsychogenicSudden · situational · morning erections preserved · identifiable precipitant
Mixed (most common)Organic cause + psychological overlay → treat both

IIEF-5 (5–25): 5–7 severe · 8–11 moderate · 12–16 mild–moderate · 17–21 mild · ≥22 no ED; score at baseline, re-score to monitor.

2Check — investigate proportionately

☐ BP, BMI, CV history, exercise tolerance  ☐ HbA1c or glucose, lipids, QRISK3 where appropriate  ☐ Medicines, recreational drugs  ☐ Mood  ☐ Genitals if indicated  ☐ Hypogonadal features → morning testosterone, repeat if low

NG12: consider PSA + DRE after discussion; UTI/prostatitis first. PSA (micrograms/L): 40–49 >2.5 · 50–59 >3.5 · 60–69 >4.5 · 70–79 >6.5.

No routine ECG or exercise test for ED alone if stable. Don't miss: absent femorals + claudication → vascular assessment.

3Decide

  • Organic or mixed → PDE5 inhibitor first line, correcting the cause in parallel.
  • Psychogenic or relationship factor → psychosexual therapy alongside.
  • Possible culprit medicine → review with the responsible clinician; never stop abruptly.
  • Testosterone only for confirmed hypogonadism, specialist-guided.
  • Lifestyle: stop smoking, activity, weight plan, alcohol no more than 14 units/week, sleep/OSA, cycling.

5Treat — PDE5 inhibitor, taught properly

First lineSildenafil 50 mg about 1 hour before; adjust 25–100 mg; max once daily
For spontaneityTadalafil 10 mg → 20 mg on demand (36-hour window), or 5 mg daily in selected men
AlternativeVardenafil 10 mg → 20 mg; avoid congenital long QT, class IA/III antiarrhythmics
True failureUrology: alprostadil, vacuum device, prosthesis — not GP-started
  • Before calling it failure: timing, dose, stimulation, food, adherence, several attempts at maximum tolerated dose.
  • Alpha-blocker: stable first, lowest PDE5 dose. Recent MI or stroke, unstable angina, significant aortic stenosis → avoid or seek advice.
  • Generics need no SLS endorsement; branded, alprostadil, vacuum devices SLS-restricted. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • After any change: IIEF-5, technique, side effects, CV symptoms. Not responding → technique, testosterone, drugs, psychology, then specialist.
  • Annually: CV review — BP, lipids, HbA1c, weight, smoking.
  • On testosterone: shared-care plan; haematocrit >0.54 → specialist advice.

7Refer — urgent ones in block 0

  • NG12: malignant-feeling prostate → refer; PSA above threshold, or non-painful testicular enlargement or change in shape or texture → consider Suspected cancer pathway
  • Cardiology first: active cardiac symptoms, unstable CVD, uncertain exercise capacity. Urology: PDE5 failure, Peyronie's, post-prostatectomy, recurrent priapism. Endocrine: secondary hypogonadism, hyperprolactinaemia, pituitary.

8Never

  • A PDE5 inhibitor with nitrates, nicorandil, riociguat or poppers.
  • A PDE5 inhibitor before assessing new exertional cardiac symptoms or unstable CVD.
  • Stop beta-blockers, antidepressants or antihypertensives abruptly for ED.
  • Testosterone on one low result, or as a routine add-on when testosterone is normal.
  • Advise waiting for a prolonged erection to settle, or treat priapism in primary care.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “An erection that lasts more than three to four hours, painful or not, is an emergency — go to A&E straight away. Chest pain or breathlessness during sex — stop, call 999, say when you took the tablet.”   The tablet: “Never with nitrate heart medicines or poppers. It works with arousal — give it several tries.”   Same day: “Sudden loss of sight or hearing — stop the tablet and get seen the same day.”
