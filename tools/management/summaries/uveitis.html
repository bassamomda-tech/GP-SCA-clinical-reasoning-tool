Uveitis — recognise and refer

For: adults and children with a painful, photophobic red eye or new posterior eye symptoms, and people with recurrent uveitis or JIA. Not for: diagnosis or treatment — slit lamp and treatment are ophthalmology-led. NICE NG65 covers over 16s; children follow the local paediatric ophthalmology pathway. Back pain → axial spondyloarthritis.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG65 (over 16s)College of Optometrists CMGsRCOphth JIA uveitis screening (2006)
Core rule: a painful, photophobic red eye is suspected acute anterior uveitis until a slit lamp says otherwise — same-day ophthalmology, with no steroid drops and no blood tests first.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected acute anterior uveitis: eye pain, red eye (ciliary flush), photophobia, blurred vision, watering, small or irregular pupil → Same-day ophthalmology — eye casualty, ED or urgent eye service; no bloods or routine optometry first.
  • Acute angle closure: severe pain, vomiting, haloes, marked visual loss, cloudy cornea, fixed mid-dilated pupil → Immediate, same day emergency eye assessment (eye casualty or ED).
  • Curtain or shadow, sudden or new field loss, marked visual reduction (retinal tear or detachment) → Immediate, same day eye casualty or ED.
  • New floaters, flashes, distortion or reduced vision → Urgent ≤24 h ophthalmic assessment — consider retinal tear as well as posterior uveitis.
  • Contact-lens painful red eye, corneal opacity, herpetic keratitis, scleritis (boring pain, tender globe) → Same-day ophthalmology.
  • Any child (with or without JIA) with red eye, photophobia, visual change or abnormal pupil → Same-day via the local paediatric ophthalmology pathway.

1Confirm — by the eye team, never in primary care

Slit lamp, IOP and dilated fundus are specialist only: uveitis cannot be excluded in primary care, and the pupil alone cannot separate it from angle closure.

Don't miss: posterior, chronic and JIA uveitis may be painless.

3Decide — a bounded GP role

  • Recurrent flares: written ophthalmology flare plan → follow it, confirm eye follow-up. No plan → Same-day ophthalmic advice or assessment, no steroid drops.

4Systemic questions — guided, not a panel

  • Ask: inflammatory back pain, arthritis, enthesitis, family history, psoriasis, IBD, oral or genital ulcers, sarcoid features, infection, travel, immunosuppression.
  • HLA-B27 (NG65, over 16s): guided by ophthalmology or rheumatology after confirmation, never before referral. A negative result or normal CRP/ESR does not exclude spondyloarthritis.

5Treat — ophthalmology starts it; GP supports

Anterior (eye team)Steroid + cycloplegic drops after slit lamp, IOP, infection excluded
Posterior (eye team)Cause-led: antimicrobial, steroid, steroid-sparing
  • While waiting (GP): dark glasses, simple analgesia, no patching, contact lenses out.
  • Underlying disease co-managed with its specialty. Doses set by ophthalmology; check the BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — the eye is ophthalmology's

  • Eye: acuity, IOP, cataract, synechiae, macular oedema, response — ophthalmology only.
  • Shared care: systemic steroids, DMARDs or biologics only under a current written agreement; abnormal results, infection, toxicity or ocular relapse → specialist advice.
  • Seek specialist advice for recurrent or persistent flares, or on biological or targeted synthetic DMARDs (NG65).
  • JIA: check slit-lamp screening is arranged; actively follow up missed appointments. High-street optometry does not replace it.

7Refer — non-urgent; urgent ones are in block 0

  • Chronic or painless anterior uveitis → prompt ophthalmology; urgency per vision and local pathway.
  • Rheumatology (NG65): confirmed anterior uveitis, joint or back pain from before 45, no psoriasis, HLA-B27 positive.
  • Asymptomatic JIA → paediatric ophthalmology slit-lamp screening.
  • Sarcoidosis, IBD, Behçet's, infection → relevant specialty.

8Never

  • Start or restart steroid eye drops without a written flare plan — they mask or worsen infective keratitis; IOP must be checked first.
  • Empirical steroid/antibiotic combination drops for a red eye, or someone else's eye drops.
  • Delay referral for HLA-B27 or other bloods, or send an indiscriminate uveitis screen (ACE, ANA, syphilis, TB, toxoplasma).
  • Put flashes or floaters down to uveitis without a dilated fundus examination.
  • Patch the eye, or let someone with impaired vision drive.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Immediate “If you see a shadow or curtain, lose part of your vision, your vision suddenly gets worse or the pain becomes severe, go to eye casualty or A&E straight away.”   Same day “If the pain, light sensitivity or blurred vision comes back, or you notice new floaters or flashes, contact the eye team or us the same day.”   Driving: “Don't drive while your vision is blurred, and don't drive yourself to the eye unit with severe pain or marked loss of vision.”
