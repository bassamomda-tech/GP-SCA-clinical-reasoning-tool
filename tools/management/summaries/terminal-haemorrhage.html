Catastrophic haemorrhage (palliative)

For: adults with advanced cancer, liver or haematological disease at risk of a terminal bleed, at home, in a care home or hospice. Not for: bleeding in a patient not on a palliative pathway (usual emergency care), or the wider last-days plan (care of the dying; agitation: palliative delirium and agitation).

Core rule: the benefit is banked before the bleed — flag the risk, agree the ceiling of care, put crisis midazolam and dark towels in the house. In the moment: do not leave the patient, and never rely on subcutaneous midazolam in a major bleed.

0Act now — follow the agreed plan

  • Catastrophic bleed, plan is active treatment → 999 or the agreed emergency pathway immediately (second person calls); stay with the patient.
  • Catastrophic bleed, plan is comfort-focused → do not leave the patient; position, dark towels, calm reassurance; Same-day call the agreed hospice, district nursing or out-of-hours service straight away — not 999 unless the plan says otherwise.
  • Sentinel (herald) bleed — streak of haemoptysis, tumour oozing, small variceal bleed → Same-day review: diagnosis, ceiling of care, anticoagulants, crisis drugs, family and out-of-hours briefing. Timing of a major bleed is unpredictable.

1Confirm — who is at risk; flag it early

RiskExamples
TumourHead and neck or central lung tumours near great vessels; fungating or eroding tumours; carotid blow-out
Varices / liverOesophageal varices; hepatic failure with coagulopathy
BloodSevere thrombocytopenia; advanced haematological malignancy
DrugsDOACs, warfarin, antiplatelets

Not only cancer: advanced liver and haematological disease carry the same risk. Major bleed: visible massive bleeding, hypotension, cool clammy peripheries, air hunger, sense of impending doom.

2Check — what the shared flag records

☐ Source and likely mechanism  ☐ Sentinel bleed yet?  ☐ Ceiling of care; is hospital transfer / 999 wanted?  ☐ DNACPR / ReSPECT  ☐ Preferred place of care  ☐ Last anticoagulant dose  ☐ Crisis midazolam dose and route  ☐ Who to call first  ☐ Where towels and drugs are  ☐ Family understanding

Don't miss: ask early if it is still treatable — radiotherapy, endoscopy and banding, embolisation, topical measures.

3Decide — weeks before, not at 3 am

  • Ceiling of care: record active (admission, transfusion, intervention) or comfort-focused at home/hospice; most catastrophic terminal bleeds are not survivable.
  • DNACPR individualised, within ReSPECT; it concerns CPR only, not “no treatment”.
  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelets → review urgently against goals; stopping may be appropriate if comfort-focused. Do not stop antiplatelets automatically (recent coronary stent). Document why.
  • Honest conversation, where appropriate, with patient and family, detail tailored to the individual.

5Treat — prepare, then in the moment

In the houseCrisis midazolam prescribed in mg and route (not mL), just-in-case box, written crisis instruction
At the bedsideDark green or blue towels, gloves, waste bags
Stay and positionBleeding lung down if known; haematemesis upright if conscious, lateral if not; protect the airway
Midazolam 5–10 mg statOnly if distressed and conscious enough to benefit; lower end if frail or benzodiazepine-naïve
  • Route: fastest safe — IV, IM, buccal or sublingual per local GMEC regimen; not SC; not buccal/sublingual if the mouth is filling with blood. Doses per BNF/PCF8 and local formulary.
  • Syringe driver running → continue it; give the crisis dose as a separate stat dose and record it.
  • Tranexamic acid → consider for recurrent or low-volume bleeding after assessing source, renal function and thrombotic risk; must not delay transfer, airway care or symptom relief.

6Monitor — afterwards

  • Document drug, dose, route, time and who was present; verify death.
  • Room cleared with dignity before the family return.
  • Debrief family and team; consider proactive bereavement and staff support.
  • Review other at-risk patients: flag, ceiling, crisis drugs, towels.

7Refer — in advance, not at crisis

  • Specialist palliative care / hospice early for high-risk patients.
  • Active options where they fit goals: embolisation, banding, palliative radiotherapy.
  • District nurses, out-of-hours, ambulance → brief them; plan on EPaCCS or special patient note.

8Never

  • Give crisis midazolam subcutaneously in a major bleed, or rely on the syringe driver.
  • Leave the patient to fetch a cannula or equipment.
  • Treat a sentinel bleed as reassurance because it stopped.
  • Leave the ceiling of care unrecorded or unshared with out-of-hours.
  • Set up carer buccal midazolam where heavy oral bleeding is likely.

9Safety-net — what to tell the family; record it

999 “If a big bleed happens, stay close, use the dark towels and keep them calm. If the plan is hospital treatment, call 999 straight away.”   Same-day “If the plan is care at home, ring the district nurse, hospice or out-of-hours straight away, not 999. Being there with them is enough.”
