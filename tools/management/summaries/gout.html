Gout in adults

For: adults with suspected or confirmed gout. Not for: a possibly septic joint (block 0), or CPPD and other inflammatory arthritis (refer if unclear).
Reviewed September 2026 · next review March 2027
NICE NG219BSR 2017MHRA (febuxostat)SPCLocal formulary
Core rule: exclude the septic joint first; then discuss urate-lowering therapy (ULT) with everyone, treat to urate <360 µmol/L, not to a dose, and never stop ULT during a flare.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Hot swollen joint with suspected infection, systemic upset, immunosuppression, diabetes, significant comorbidity, a prosthetic joint or diagnostic doubt → Refer immediately via the local care pathway (NG219 1.1.3); no steroid injection until infection is excluded. CRP and white count do not separate gout from sepsis, and they co-exist.
  • Systemically unwell with a polyarticular flare → Same-day
  • Any new rash on allopurinol (DRESS/SJS risk) → stop the drug → Same-day assessment
  • Colchicine toxicity (diarrhoea, vomiting, muscle pain or weakness, neuropathy, bruising, sore throat) → stop → Same-day advice
  • Azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine on the list → no routine allopurinol; specialist before prescribing (fatal marrow suppression).

1Confirm — clinical, plus the urate rule

Suspect: rapid-onset severe pain, redness, swelling of a 1st MTP joint, or tophi. Consider: other joints, or chronic inflammatory joint pain.

FindingMeaning / action
Serum urate ≥360 µmol/LSupports gout; not diagnostic alone
<360 in a flare, strong suspicionRepeat ≥2 weeks after it settles
Atypical or uncertainAspirate: negatively birefringent needles; mimics are CPPD, cellulitis, other arthritis

2Check — gout is a metabolic disease

☐ U&E/eGFR before colchicine and ULT  ☐ LFTs (febuxostat)  ☐ BP, HbA1c, lipids, QRISK  ☐ Drivers: diuretic, ciclosporin, alcohol  ☐ Flares, tophi, stones, CKD

Unexplained bone or soft-tissue lump, or bone pain with weight loss → NG12 route, not gout.

3Decide — flare, then urate

  • Flare: NSAID, colchicine or short oral steroid, by kidneys, gut, heart, interactions and preference; monotherapy first. NSAID and colchicine unsuitable → consider IA or IM steroid.
  • ULT: discuss with everyone. Offer for multiple or troublesome flares, CKD 3–5, diuretics, tophi or chronic gouty arthritis. Start ≥2–4 weeks after a flare settles (or during frequent flares with cover).
  • Lifestyle: less alcohol and sugary drinks; gradual weight loss; no restrictive “gout diet”. Diuretic for hypertension → discuss a CCB or losartan if suitable.

4Target — treat to target, not to dose

PersonSerum urate
Everyone on ULT<360 µmol/L
Tophi, chronic gouty arthritis, or flares despite <360Consider <300

5Treat — ULT ladder

1st · allopurinol100 mg daily (lower in significant renal impairment or frailty) → raise ~4-weekly to target; smaller steps in CKD. First-line in major CV disease.
CoverIf chosen and safe, offer low-dose colchicine, often up to 6 months. Unsuitable → low-dose NSAID or steroid.
Or · febuxostat80 mg daily → 120 mg if not <360 after 2–4 weeks. Caution in major CV disease (MHRA).
Both fail→ refer: uricosurics, IL-1 blockade.
  • Flare: NSAID + gastroprotection when indicated (avoid in CKD, heart failure, anticoagulation, GI bleed). Colchicine — usual adult dose 500 micrograms 2–4 times daily, max 6 mg per course (SPC; NICE CKS) — after renal, hepatic and interaction checks (clarithromycin, azoles, verapamil, diltiazem, ciclosporin). Prednisolone e.g. 30–35 mg daily for 3–5 days.
  • Before every step: adherence, urate, cover, drivers. Doses per SPC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Urate 4-weekly while titrating (above target → next step), then annually, lifelong.
  • U&E/eGFR with each titration in CKD. LFTs on febuxostat; significant rise → stop or switch. Warfarin → recheck INR.
  • Still flaring? Adherence, dose stuck at 100 or 300 mg, cover, drivers, diagnosis (CPPD, sepsis) → then rheumatology.

7Refer — consider rheumatology (routine)

  • Diagnosis uncertain; young-onset gout.
  • ULT contraindicated, not tolerated or ineffective; urate >360 on maximum tolerated ULT; severe tophi.
  • CKD 3b–5 (eGFR <45), transplant, azathioprine or ciclosporin, urate stones. Keep treating while waiting.

8Never

  • Inject steroid into a joint until septic arthritis has been adequately excluded.
  • Stop allopurinol or febuxostat during a flare.
  • Allopurinol with azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine without a specialist protocol.
  • Stop titrating at 100 or 300 mg while the urate is above target.
  • Full-dose colchicine on top of prophylactic colchicine — use an NSAID or steroid.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “A hot swollen joint with fever, shivers or feeling properly unwell — contact us, or NHS 111 if we are closed, the same day.”   Allopurinol: “Any rash, especially in the first few months — stop the tablet and contact us the same day.”   Colchicine: “Diarrhoea, vomiting, muscle pain or weakness, numbness or tingling, unusual tiredness, bruising or a sore throat — stop it and contact us the same day.”   Flare: “Not improving after a few days, spreading with fever, or passing much less urine — contact us the same day.”   ULT: “The daily tablet continues through flares, for life.”
