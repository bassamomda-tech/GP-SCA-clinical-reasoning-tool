Irritable bowel syndrome in adults

For: adults with IBS-type symptoms, new or established. Not for: children; positive coeliac serology or suspected IBD (coeliac, Crohn's or UC pages); chronic diarrhoea without IBS criteria (chronic diarrhoea pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG61 (updated 2017)NG12 (updated April 2026)HTG690 (formerly DG56)HTG320 (formerly DG11)BSG 2021
Core rule: IBS is a positive diagnosis only with no red flags and FBC, CRP or ESR and coeliac serology done; subtype it (the drug follows the dominant symptom) and keep the NG12 safety net live for life.

0Act now — an IBS label never retires the red flags

  • New change in bowel habit or iron-deficiency anaemia (any age), abdominal mass, rectal bleeding with unexplained abdominal pain or weight loss under 50, unexplained weight loss with abdominal pain at 40+, unexplained rectal bleeding, abdominal pain or weight loss at 50+, anaemia without iron deficiency at 60+ → FIT; ≥10 µg Hb/g → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Rectal mass, a strongly suspicious presentation, or FIT cannot be obtained → Suspected cancer pathway — do not wait for FIT.
  • Woman aged 50+ with new IBS-type symptoms in the last 12 months → assess for ovarian cancer rather than assuming IBS; apply the NG12 ovarian pathway.
  • Woman aged 40+, persistent or frequent (particularly >12 times a month) bloating, early satiety or loss of appetite, pelvic or abdominal pain, urinary urgency or frequency → CA-125; at or above the age threshold (≥35 IU/mL at 40–49, ≥31 at 50–59, ≥24 at 60–69, ≥25 at 70–79, ≥31 at 80+) → Urgent direct-access ultrasound. Aged 39 or under: do not rely on CA-125 alone; consider urgent direct-access ultrasound.

1Confirm — positive diagnosis, then subtype

Abdominal pain or discomfort ≥6 months, related to defecation or altered stool frequency or form, plus ≥2 of: straining, urgency or incomplete evacuation; bloating; worse with eating; mucus. No red flags.

Subtype: IBS-C (constipation), IBS-D (diarrhoea) or IBS-M (mixed) — it directs the drug and the fibre advice.

2Check — everyone meeting IBS criteria

☐ FBC  ☐ CRP or ESR/plasma viscosity  ☐ Coeliac serology (on gluten)  ☐ Faecal calprotectin if IBD is possible and cancer is not suspected  ☐ CA-125 only by block 0 criteria

Don't miss: a positive coeliac screen or raised calprotectin means IBS is not the sole diagnosis until assessed (block 7).

3Decide — diet and lifestyle first, for everyone

  • Regular meals, fluids; less caffeine, alcohol, fizzy drinks, fatty or processed food, sorbitol. Activity; address stress.
  • Fibre: discourage bran (insoluble) if it worsens symptoms; if supplementing, ispaghula (soluble), started low.
  • Probiotic if wished: one product for 4–12 weeks; continue only if clear benefit.
  • Persistent symptoms → structured low-FODMAP, ideally dietitian-led.

5Treat — the dominant symptom

Step 1 · by symptomPain: mebeverine 135 mg TDS (GM first choice), hyoscine butylbromide or peppermint oil. C: macrogol. D: loperamide 2 mg, titrated
Step 2 · persistent painAmitriptyline 5–10 mg at night (off-label), titrated, usually ≤30 mg.
Step 3 · TCA ineffectiveConsider an SSRI.
RefractoryIBS-C ≥12 months, laxatives of different classes failed → consider linaclotide (review 3 months). IBS-D → review for bile acid diarrhoea, microscopic colitis, coeliac, IBD.
  • Before a TCA: QT/cardiac disease, glaucoma, urinary retention, constipation, seizures, mood/suicidality, serotonergic or sedating drugs; caution if older. Hyoscine: anticholinergic. Peppermint can worsen reflux. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — a long-term relapsing condition

  • Each review: match treatment to the current subtype; stop ineffective drugs; re-screen block 0 and confirm the diagnosis still fits.
  • TCA or SSRI: at 4 weeks, then every 6–12 months: benefit, adverse effects, safety; stop if no benefit.

7Refer — Routine

  • Diagnostic doubt (atypical features, raised calprotectin, positive coeliac serology — keep eating gluten) → gastroenterology.
  • Refractory (no drug response after 12 months) → consider referral for psychological interventions (CBT, hypnotherapy); earlier if preferred or with psychological comorbidity.
  • Severe impact: major psychological or quality-of-life impact.

8Never

  • Accept an IBS label without FBC, CRP or ESR and coeliac serology.
  • Let an established IBS label explain away new alarm features.
  • Start lactulose in IBS (NICE: discourage it), or give generic "eat more fibre" or bran advice.
  • Use loperamide in suspected inflammatory or infectious diarrhoea, significant distension or possible obstruction.
  • Use CA-125 as an IBS test, or leave a patient on unsupervised long-term exclusion diets.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Within a week “Bleeding from the back passage, unintentional weight loss, feeling increasingly tired or pale, a tummy or back-passage lump, symptoms that wake you at night or steadily worsen, or a new change in your usual pattern — contact us to be seen within a week, even years from now.”   Women: “Bloating, feeling full quickly, pelvic pain or passing urine more often on most days — contact us within a week for a blood test (CA-125) and possibly a scan.”   Diet: “Fibre advice depends on your type — please don't just add bran.”   It's real: “IBS is a genuine condition, not ‘in your head’ — but stress can flare it, so we treat both.”
