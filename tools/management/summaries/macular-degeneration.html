Age-related macular degeneration — the GP's part

For: adults with new central distortion or blur, or known AMD under a macula service. Not for: grading AMD or choosing anti-VEGF — the macula service decides. Sudden profound loss, curtain or floaters → same-day eye pathways; suspected GCA → GCA protocol. AMD is not a suspected-cancer pathway condition.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027

Core rule: new distortion or central blur in an older adult is suspected wet active AMD until the macula service says otherwise — refer within 1 working day, even with a ‘dry’ label, and never delay referral to test.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • New distortion, central blur or grey patch, or a fall in reading vision → Urgent ≤1 working day macula service via the local direct route (optometrist with OCT or rapid-access clinic), even if vision seems fine; not A&E.
  • Known AMD (even ‘dry’ or ‘inactive’) with new distortion or a drop in vision → Urgent ≤1 working day macula service directly via the clinic-letter route.
  • Sudden profound painless loss, a curtain or field defect, or new flashes with a shower of floaters → Same-day eye assessment. Retinal artery occlusion or transient monocular loss → also stroke/TIA pathway immediately (specialist within 24 hours).
  • Over 50, visual symptoms plus headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, constitutional or polymyalgia symptoms (GCA) → Same-day assessment, inflammatory markers, start corticosteroids immediately per local GCA pathway.
  • After an intravitreal injection: severe or increasing pain, marked redness, photophobia, worse vision or more floaters → Same-day treating eye unit's emergency line.
  • Suspected retinal vein occlusion → Urgent ≤1 week ophthalmology; same day if severe loss, neovascularisation, severe pain or raised pressure.

1Confirm — the eye service confirms, not the GP

OCT confirms; angiography only if OCT does not exclude neovascular disease. A distorted Amsler grid supports referral; a normal grid does not rule out wet AMD.

2Check — without delaying referral

☐ Onset: days–weeks (wet) vs months–years  ☐ Each eye separately (cover one)  ☐ Acuity each eye with usual glasses or pinhole, if available  ☐ Other causes: diabetic macular oedema, vascular occlusion, detachment, optic neuropathy, recent cataract surgery, hydroxychloroquine  ☐ Function, falls, driving, mood  ☐ Hallucinations

Don't miss: a ‘dry AMD’ letter does not mean the eye cannot convert to wet.

3Decide — the conversations primary care owns

  • Smoking is the most clearly modifiable risk → offer stop-smoking support at every contact, especially with one eye affected.
  • Supplements: NICE does not recommend routine antioxidant or zinc; don't initiate or prescribe AREDS-type preparations routinely. Self-purchase → discuss with the eye service; no beta-carotene in current or previous smokers.
  • Hallucinations with insight → name Charles Bonnet syndrome; not dementia or psychosis on its own. Insight lost → look for other causes.

6Monitor — keep them in treatment

  • Intervals set by the macula service; missed visits risk reactivation and permanent loss. Ask about falls, mood, loneliness.
  • Help with transport, carers, injection anxiety, clashing appointments.
  • Fellow eye is at risk: teach checking each eye separately; act on new distortion the same way.

7Refer — routine; urgent ones are in block 0

  • Gradual central loss without distortion → optometrist review, referral per local pathway.
  • Low-vision clinic (registration not required), ECLO, council rehabilitation, sight-loss charities.
  • CVI as soon as eligible, even during treatment (QS180).
  • DVLA (patient's duty): car — AMD in both eyes or only seeing eye; bus or lorry — one or both eyes. Below 6/12 both eyes or 20 m plate → must not drive. Advise and document.

8Never

  • A routine letter, A&E or the next routine review for suspected wet AMD.
  • Delay referral for acuity or Amsler, or be reassured by a normal Amsler grid.
  • Accept ‘dry AMD, nothing to be done’ as a reason to ignore new distortion.
  • Prescribe AREDS-type supplements routinely, or beta-carotene to smokers or ex-smokers.
  • Let an AMD label distract from GCA, retinal artery occlusion or detachment.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “If straight lines ever start to bend or a new smudge appears, ring the eye clinic the same day, not at your next appointment. If it gets suddenly much worse, or you see a curtain or lots of new floaters, contact us or the eye unit straight away.”   After injections: “Severe pain, redness, light sensitivity or worse vision — call the eye unit's emergency number the same day.”   Driving: “With AMD in both eyes you must tell the DVLA.”
