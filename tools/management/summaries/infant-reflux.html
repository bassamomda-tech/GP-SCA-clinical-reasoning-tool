Reflux in infants (GOR and GORD)

For: infants with regurgitation seen in primary care. Not for: a baby with any block 0 red flag (same-day pathway); suspected cow's milk protein allergy (use the CMPA protocol) or faltering growth work-up (use the faltering growth page).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG1 (updated 2019)NICE QS112NICE CG116BNF for ChildrenNHS safer sleep
Core rule: a thriving baby who possets needs reassurance and a feeding review, not a drug — screen the red flags, follow the feeding steps (never thickener and alginate together), and use acid suppression only as a 4-week trial, stopped if there is no clear benefit.

0Act now — these are not reflux until proven otherwise

  • Unexplained green or yellow-green vomit → Same-day emergency assessment; discuss urgently with paediatrics/surgery (same-day upper-GI contrast study). Do not manage as reflux.
  • Progressively worsening or forceful vomiting under 2 months → Same-day paediatric assessment for possible pyloric stenosis.
  • Haematemesis → Same-day assessment; distinguish swallowed blood from GI bleeding.
  • Abdominal distension with vomiting, systemic illness or fever, dehydration, bulging fontanelle or abnormal neurology, recurrent apnoea, cyanosis or suspected aspiration → Same-day assessment
  • Happening now: pauses in breathing, turning blue or grey, a fit, floppy or very hard to wake, or no wet nappy for 12 hours → 999

1Confirm — GOR versus GORD is the whole decision

PictureFeaturesNext
GOR (physiological)Regurgitation in a well infant, normal feeding and growth; ~90% resolve by 1 yearReassure; feeding review
GORDRegurgitation plus marked, persistent distress, feeding difficulty or refusal, faltering growth, or a complicationFeeding steps; then consider a drug trial
One symptom, no visible regurgitationCrying, feeding difficulty or faltering growth aloneNot automatically reflux — look for another cause

Late onset after 6 months is atypical → reconsider the diagnosis (not an emergency in itself).

2Check — every infant

☐ Effortless regurgitation or forceful vomit; colour, blood  ☐ Feed volume, frequency, teat flow, technique; intake in mL/kg/day against the weight  ☐ Wet nappies, hydration  ☐ Weight and growth trajectory  ☐ Abdomen, neurology, general condition  ☐ Eczema, urticaria, blood or mucus in stool, diarrhoea, constipation

Don't miss: new regurgitation after 8 weeks with faltering growth, frequent regurgitation or marked distress → consider UTI.

3Decide

  • Well, thriving → reassurance is the treatment, plus a feeding review.
  • Allergic or GI features (eczema, urticaria, blood or mucus in stools, diarrhoea, constipation, food refusal, strong atopy) → consider CMPA: allergy history, elimination normally 2–6 weeks, then reintroduction. Not from isolated posseting.
  • Overt regurgitation plus unexplained feeding difficulty, marked distress or faltering growth, after feeding measures → consider a 4-week PPI or H2RA trial.
  • Isolated posseting, or a baby who just cries → no acid suppression.

5Treat — in order, one at a time

Step 1 · formula-fedReview feeding; reduce feed volume only if excessive for weight; smaller, more frequent feeds, same total daily intake
Step 2 · formula-fedThickened formula. Unsuccessful → stop it, then alginate trial 1–2 weeks
BreastfedBreastfeeding assessment by someone trained; then, if frequent regurgitation with marked distress, consider alginate 1–2 weeks
Step 3 · criteria metPPI (e.g. omeprazole) or H2RA, 4-week trial, dosed by weight per BNFc
  • Alginate: continue only with clear benefit, try stopping periodically; check product sodium content.
  • Positioning: upright after feeds while awake and supervised; every sleep flat on the back in a clear cot.
  • Doses per BNF for Children and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • After feeding advice: reassess — most physiological GOR settles.
  • Every review: weight and feeding. Faltering growth or feed refusal → escalate or refer.
  • Drug trial at 4 weeks: no clear benefit → stop; no indefinite repeats. Document indication, duration and review date.
  • Failed trial → revisit CMPA and feeding, not a higher dose.

7Refer — paediatrics; emergencies are in block 0

  • Reflux with faltering growth, or persistent feed refusal or aversion.
  • Refractory despite optimised feeding and a drug trial, or symptoms recur after stopping (possible endoscopy).
  • Regurgitation beyond 1 year, late onset, dysphagia, atypical features or diagnostic doubt.

8Never

  • A thickener and an alginate together.
  • A PPI or H2RA for simple posseting in a thriving baby, or because the baby cries.
  • Escalating a failed acid-suppression trial — stop it and rethink.
  • Sleep-positioning devices, wedges, cot elevation, or prone or side sleeping.
  • Metoclopramide, domperidone or erythromycin without specialist paediatric agreement, other options tried and benefit outweighing risk.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Pauses in breathing, turning blue or grey, a fit, or a baby who is floppy, very hard to wake or seems seriously unwell — call 999. No wet nappy for 12 hours — call 999 or go to A&E.”   Same-day “Green or yellow-green vomit, blood in the vomit, or forceful vomiting that is getting worse; fever, drowsiness, a bulging soft spot or a swollen tummy; refusing feeds, keeps vomiting, fewer wet nappies or not gaining weight — get seen the same day (call us, or 111 out of hours).”   Reassurance: “In a happy, growing baby, posseting is normal and almost always settles by their first birthday.”
