Contraception

For: anyone seeking contraception or emergency contraception (EC), including under-16s (Fraser) and the over-40s. Not for: HRT (use the HRT protocol — HRT is not contraception); under 13 (safeguarding referral); complex medical disease (specialist advice).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
UKMEC 2025FSRH guidelinesNICE CG30NICE PH51NICE NG12
Core rule: reasonably exclude pregnancy and start a method today, run the UKMEC screen before any combined method (aura, BP, VTE, smoking), and offer the copper IUD to everyone requesting EC.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • After IUD fitting: severe abdominal pain, fever, shock → 999. On CHC: leg swelling, chest pain, breathlessness → 999 or same-day
  • New aura or new severe headache on CHC → stop the oestrogen; switch to a POP or LARC Same-day. Positive pregnancy test, missing IUD threads, PID after an IUD → Same-day review
  • EC request → Same-day, never delayed for a pregnancy test. Offer Cu-IUD to all: within 5 days of first UPSI or earliest estimated ovulation (whichever is later). Else UPA 30 mg ≤120 h (then delay progestogen methods 5 days) or LNG 1.5 mg ≤72 h (≤96 h off-label; 3 mg if >70 kg, BMI >26 or enzyme inducer). Oral window passed → Urgent Cu-IUD referral
  • Undiagnosed abnormal vaginal bleeding → investigate before any hormonal method. Unexplained breast lump aged 30+ → refer; cervix looks cancerous → consider Suspected cancer pathway (NG12)
  • Under 13, or concern about coercion, exploitation or trafficking → Urgent safeguarding referral before prescribing. Under 16 → document Fraser.

1Confirm — not pregnant, then UKMEC

FSRH pregnancy-exclusion criteria ± urine βhCG; a negative test cannot exclude pregnancy after recent UPSI. >21 days since LMP, no UPSI → no test; document why.

UKMECCombined hormonal contraception (CHC) — key examples
4 neverMigraine with aura (any age) · current/past VTE or thrombogenic mutation · BP ≥160/100 · ≥35 y smoking ≥15/day · breastfeeding <6 weeks postpartum · current breast cancer · IHD or stroke · major surgery with long immobility · decompensated cirrhosis, liver adenoma or cancer
3 avoidBMI ≥35 · ≥35 y smoking <15/day or stopped <1 year · controlled hypertension or BP 140–159/90–99 · first-degree relative with VTE <45 · immobility · breast cancer >5 years ago · gallbladder disease

Postpartum: POP/implant any time; no CHC <3 weeks; IUD ≤48 h or from 4 weeks. Progestogen-only and IUD methods suit most CHC exclusions — check UKMEC (ukmec.org).

2Check — before prescribing

☐ BP, BMI documented before CHC  ☐ Aura, smoking, own/family VTE, breast cancer  ☐ Enzyme inducers, lamotrigine  ☐ STI risk  ☐ Smear status  ☐ Fertility plans. Not needed: thrombophilia screen (unless VTE history), ultrasound, FSH/LH; an overdue smear never delays contraception.

3Decide — effectiveness first, then her choice

  • Everyone → offer a choice of all methods including LARC — not a default; among UKMEC 1–2 she decides. Year-1 typical-use failure: LARC <1% · DMPA ~4% · pill/patch/ring ~7–9% · condoms/barrier ~13–24%. Condoms with any method — the only STI protection.
  • Lighter periods → LNG-IUD or implant. No hormones → Cu-IUD. Pregnancy soon → avoid DMPA.
  • Enzyme inducers (carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin, St John’s Wort, some antiretrovirals) → DMPA, Cu-IUD or LNG-IUD. Lamotrigine → prefer non-oestrogen, or seek advice.

5Treat — quick-start today

  • First choices: LNG-IUD 52 mg · Cu-IUD · implant · desogestrel 75 mcg POP · levonorgestrel 30 mcg COC (lowest VTE).
  • Cover: immediate if started days 1–5 (LNG-IUD 1–7; Cu-IUD any day); else condoms 7 days (desogestrel or traditional POP 2 days).
  • Pregnancy not excluded, ongoing risk → quick-start pill or implant, test in 3 weeks. No LNG-IUD.
  • Missed COC (21/7): 1 pill → take now, no extra cover. ≥2 → latest pill + condoms 7 days; week 1 with UPSI → consider EC; week 3 → skip the break.
  • Late POP (traditional >3 h, desogestrel >12 h, drospirenone >24 h) → take now; condoms 48 h (drospirenone 7 days). Vomit ≤3 h → another pill.
  • Doses per BNF and local formulary/PGD.

6Monitor

  • CHC: BP, BMI, aura, smoking at 3 months, then yearly. DMPA: review ≥2-yearly. Implant: 3 years.
  • Bleeding >3 months: exclude pregnancy, STI, adherence, interactions, cervix (endometrium if at risk) → 3-month LNG COC (if CHC suitable) or 5-day mefenamic acid (off-label) → switch.
  • Switching: overlap by the new method’s time-to-effective — no gap.
  • Age 50: stop CHC; advise switching from DMPA. POP/implant/LNG-IUD to 55. Non-hormonal: stop after 2 years’ amenorrhoea under 50, 1 year at 50+.

7Refer — routine unless stated

  • Sexual health: LARC fitting if untrained, complex UKMEC 3–4, failed insertion. Specialist: enzyme inducers, multiple UKMEC 3, HIV, transplant, immunosuppressed, post-bariatric.
  • Gynaecology: lost threads, device not seen on ultrasound ± X-ray (give EC + interim method); sterilisation. Deep implant → removal service.
  • PID with IUD, no better at 48–72 h → Urgent gynaecology. Breast cancer → oncology.

8Never

  • CHC with migraine with aura (any age), or after a new aura.
  • Send her away to wait for a period, or leave a gap when switching.
  • LNG-IUD if pregnancy not excluded; any IUD with PID, purulent cervicitis, gonorrhoea or symptomatic chlamydia.
  • Rely on CHC, POP, implant or patch with an enzyme inducer.
  • Blame the method for bleeding >3 months before examining and testing.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Severe tummy pain with a fever after a coil fitting, or chest pain and breathlessness on the pill — call 999.”   CHC: “New headache with visual changes, or calf pain or swelling — stop the pill, contact us now.”   Coil/implant: “Pain, fever, missing threads or pregnancy symptoms — contact us.”   Pills: “Missed-pill and sickness rules are on this leaflet.”
