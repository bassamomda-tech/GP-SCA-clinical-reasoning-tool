Nail disorders

For: adults with new or changed nails in primary care. Not for: fungal skin infection elsewhere (use the fungal skin infection protocol), an active diabetic foot problem (NG19 foot pathway) or children with fungal nails (specialist advice).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)BAD onychomycosis 2014NG19CG153NG141
Core rule: a new or changing solitary pigmented nail in an adult is possible nail-unit melanoma until assessed; confirm fungus by mycology before any oral antifungal, and never incise a herpetic whitlow.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • New or changing solitary pigmented band in an adult, irregular or variegated pigment, Hutchinson's sign, nail-plate destruction, bleeding, subungual nodule, or unexplained persistent dystrophy of one nail → Urgent dermatology (suspected cancer pathway); do not biopsy.
  • Kanavel signs, a felon, lymphangitis or rapidly spreading infection, pain out of proportion, suspected deep abscess, septic arthritis or osteomyelitis → Same-day hand surgery; septic → 999
  • Finger clubbing, aged 40+ → consider Urgent direct-access CXR (within 2 weeks); younger by clinical context. Fever with a murmur → Same-day medical assessment (endocarditis).
  • Periungual inflammation on an EGFR inhibitor or anti-VEGF drug → Same-day oncology contact
  • On terbinafine: signs of liver injury (jaundice, dark urine, pale stools, pruritus, nausea, abdominal pain, fever, rash) → stop it, Same-day review + LFTs.
  • Diabetes with an active foot problem → MDT foot service ≤1 working day; sepsis, ischaemia, gangrene, deep infection → Same-day acute

1Confirm — pattern, then proof

Mostly fungal, psoriatic or traumatic; distribution not diagnostic.

ConditionClues
OnychomycosisToenails, asymmetric, yellow-brown, debris; tinea pedis
PsoriasisIrregular pits, oil drop sign; skin or joint disease
Trauma · lichen planusHaematoma · thinning, pterygium
Chronic paronychiaSwollen fold, lost cuticle, wet work
Herpetic whitlowClustered vesicles, severe pain, no fluctuance

2Check — only what the examination directs

☐ All 20 nails, skin, joints, feet  ☐ Clippings for microscopy and culture: varnish off, subungual debris, dry pack not swab  ☐ If indicated: FBC, ferritin, TFTs, LFTs, albumin  ☐ Diabetes: NG19 foot check

Don't miss: negative culture doesn't exclude fungus — repeat if suspicion high.

3Decide — who gets treatment

  • Oral antifungal only if mycology-positive and benefit outweighs harm; diabetes raises the stakes.
  • Topical alone only if <80% of nail, lunula spared; lacquers not routinely supported (GMMMG).
  • Psoriatic nails → treat the psoriasis. Care: nails dry and short, treat tinea pedis, gloves for wet work.

4Target — judge in months, not weeks

ProblemGoal
OnychomycosisMycological + clinical cure; toenails often ≥1 year
KoilonychiaIron for about 3 months after Hb normalises

5Treat — the confirmed diagnosis

Dermatophyte (first line)Terbinafine 250 mg daily: fingers 6 weeks, toes 12–16 weeks. Not in liver disease; CYP2D6.
Candida / mould / failureItraconazole 200 mg BD 7 days a month: 2 pulses fingers, 3 toes. Not in heart failure.
Acute paronychiaFluctuant → drain, culture pus. Cellulitis → flucloxacillin 500 mg QDS 5–7 days (penicillin allergy: clarithromycin 500 mg BD).
Herpetic whitlowDo not incise. Consider early aciclovir, especially if severe, recurrent or immunocompromised.
  • Chronic paronychia: stop wet work, topical corticosteroid; imidazole only if yeast suspected.
  • Nail psoriasis: potent topical steroid or calcipotriol + betamethasone to the fold for months. Children → specialist advice. Doses per BNF and GMMMG formulary.

6Monitor

  • Terbinafine: LFTs at baseline and about 4–6 weeks.
  • No healthy proximal regrowth → reassess diagnosis; recurrence → repeat mycology first.
  • Psoriasis: annual PEST, plus ask about back pain.
  • Clubbing: re-examine; repeat CXR if chest symptoms or progression.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Dermatology: nail lichen planus early; resistant chronic paronychia; doubt after negative mycology; biopsy needed.
  • Respiratory: yellow nail syndrome. Rheumatology: psoriatic nails + joint symptoms; nail-fold capillary changes.
  • Diabetes, moderate/high foot risk → foot protection service.

8Never

  • Incise or drain a herpetic whitlow.
  • Start an oral antifungal without positive mycology.
  • Blame progressive nail pigmentation in an adult on trauma or ethnic melanonychia without dermatology review.
  • Use the 7-point checklist on a nail lesion, or biopsy one in primary care.
  • Dismiss clubbing, or treat deep hand-space infection with oral antibiotics alone.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A hot, swollen, throbbing finger, red streaks up the arm, fever or feeling unwell — get seen the same day; call 999 if very unwell.”   Dark streak: “If it widens or spreads onto the skin, contact us within a week, not at a routine review.”   Terbinafine: “Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, pale stools, severe itching, persistent sickness, tummy pain, fever or rash — stop them, contact us the same day.”   Clubbing: “See a GP within 2 weeks — you may need a chest X-ray.”
