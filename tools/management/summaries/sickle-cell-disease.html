Sickle cell disease

For: children and adults with sickle cell disease (HbSS, HbSC, HbSβ-thal), shared care with haematology. Not for: trait alone (counselling, block 7); disease-modifying treatment, transfusion or pregnancy care (specialist-led; pregnancy is joint haematology–obstetric care).

Core rule: never treat it as “just another crisis” — screen every contact for chest, neuro, fever, pallor/spleen and priapism, never delay or ration analgesia, and keep penicillin and vaccines watertight.

0Act now — none is safe to observe at home; tell them it is sickle cell

  • Shock, severe hypoxia, major respiratory distress, seizure, new focal neurology, sudden severe headache, new confusion, reduced consciousness, suspected severe sepsis or a rapidly deteriorating child → 999 and pre-alert.
  • Possible acute chest syndrome: chest pain, cough, breathlessness, fever or SpO₂ below their baseline, often during a painful episode → Same-day hospital; 999 if unstable; oxygen if hypoxic. Do not wait for a chest X-ray.
  • Any fever, even well-looking and on penicillin (hyposplenism) → Same-day hospital; 999 if systemically unwell.
  • Sudden pallor, lethargy, enlarging spleen or liver, Hb well below baseline (sequestration; aplastic crisis, usually parvovirus B19) → Same-day hospital; 999 if shocked, very pale and lethargic, or deteriorating.
  • Painful erection lasting more than 1 hour → Same-day ED/urology (999 or A&E). No ice packs.
  • Pain not controlled by the home plan, or atypical pain → Same-day day unit or ED per care plan. Do not delay transfer to optimise opioids.

Handover: phone the team or ED; send genotype, baseline Hb/SpO₂, medicines and last hydroxycarbamide dose, analgesia taken and when, transfusions and antibodies, care plan. Document the decision time.

1Confirm — know the genotype and the baseline

Newborn screening or lab haemoglobin analysis (HPLC), not the FBC. HbSβ⁰ behaves like HbSS. Record genotype, baseline Hb and SpO₂, spleen status, transfusions and antibodies, care plan and named centre — compare with their own baseline.

Trait (HbAS) is not SCD: no penicillin or asplenia vaccine schedule; it matters for partner testing and contraception (UKMEC 2025: prefer alternatives to combined hormonal methods).

2Check — every contact

☐ Temperature, SpO₂ against baseline, RR, HR, conscious level  ☐ Chest, neurology, abdomen; spleen in children  ☐ Pain score, what was taken and when  ☐ Penicillin repeats and vaccines  ☐ Hydroxycarbamide bloods in date  ☐ Contraception (UKMEC 2025: combined methods not automatically ruled out in SCD)

Don't miss: acute chest syndrome often develops during a painful crisis — “just pain” on day 1 can be hypoxia on day 2.

3Decide — pain at home, no red flags

  • Believe the pain score; asking for a specific opioid is not drug-seeking.
  • Follow the home plan: regular paracetamol, NSAID if appropriate (renal, pregnancy, asthma), opioid only if the plan includes it.
  • Opioids → add laxative and antiemetic; no new strong-opioid regimen without specialist or local protocol support. Normal oral hydration (not excessive), warmth, treat infection.
  • Agree a specific time limit and route back. Hospital (not GP) standard: analgesia within 30 min of arrival.

5Treat — prevention is the GP’s core job

  • Penicillin V twice daily per the haemoglobinopathy plan (erythromycin if allergic); never stop without specialist discussion.
  • Vaccines per the live Green Book: pneumococcal, MenACWY, MenB, yearly flu, COVID-19.
  • Hydroxycarbamide: specialist-started and titrated (children HbSS/HbSβ⁰ from 9 months); contraception for men and women.
  • Folic acid if haematology advises. No routine iron — ferritin first. Crizanlizumab, voxelotor withdrawn: do not start; still taking → urgent specialist discussion before any change.
  • Code SCD and hyposplenism for recalls; audit penicillin collection — adolescents often stop. Doses per BNF/BNFC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Hydroxycarbamide: FBC, reticulocytes, renal, LFTs at the shared-care interval. Absent or overdue → do not renew. Unexpected cytopenia → haematology the same day.
  • Annual specialist review; TCD yearly, age 2 to ≥16 (HbSS/HbSβ⁰) — a missed TCD is a safety issue.
  • Sudden visual loss → Same-day ophthalmology. Review mood, chronic pain, opioid harms, leg ulcers, sleep.

7Refer — non-emergency

  • Pregnancy on hydroxycarbamide → Urgent haematology + obstetrics. Stuttering priapism → urgent urology + haematology.
  • Carrier couples (1 in 4 risk) → partner testing, counsellor.
  • Routine: no specialist team; pre-conception (haematology + obstetrics, higher-dose folic acid); transition from school year 9; chronic pain; psychology.

8Never

  • Pethidine.
  • Hold a patient in primary care for an opioid review, a callback or tomorrow’s appointment when the home plan has failed.
  • Treat fever as viral at home — penicillin does not make fever safe.
  • Iron without a ferritin, a generic transfusion target, or ice packs for priapism.
  • Renew hydroxycarbamide when monitoring is absent or overdue.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Weakness, trouble speaking, a fit or a sudden severe headache — call 999 straight away. Very unwell, confused, very breathless or getting worse fast — 999.”   Same day “A temperature, chest pain or breathlessness, a painful erection that won’t go down after an hour, or for your child sudden paleness, floppiness or a swollen tummy — go straight to hospital today as your plan says, and tell them you have sickle cell.”   Pain: “If your usual painkillers don’t control it, go to hospital — don’t wait for an appointment with us.”   Carry: the care plan and a card with genotype, baseline Hb and centre contact.
