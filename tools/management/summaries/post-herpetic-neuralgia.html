Post-herpetic neuralgia

For: adults with dermatomal pain persisting or recurring 3 months or more after shingles, in primary care. Not for: acute shingles (use the shingles page), trigeminal neuralgia or other neuropathic pain (use those pages), or pain that does not fit PHN (reconsider the diagnosis).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG173 (updated 2020)NG193Green Book ch. 28aNHS EnglandGMMMG
Core rule: one CG173 medicine at a time — switch, don't stack; in older people weigh falls, sedation and cognition, and consider a topical option for localised pain. The goal is to reduce the pain, not abolish it.

0Act now — this may not be PHN, or the zoster is active

  • New bladder or bowel (sphincter) symptoms → immediate emergency assessment 999 / A&E
  • New motor weakness, progressive or spreading sensory loss, or another atypical neurological feature → Same-day review; reconsider the diagnosis.
  • Eye symptoms or suspected herpes zoster ophthalmicus → Same-day ophthalmology
  • Ear involvement, facial weakness or suspected Ramsay Hunt; disseminated or extensive zoster; immunocompromised with active or complicated zoster → Same-day review
  • New shingles rash → Same-day advice; antivirals per local acute-zoster criteria (e.g. over 50 within 72 hours), renal-dose adjusted.

1Confirm — a clinical diagnosis

Pain that persists or recurs in the affected dermatome ≥3 months after shingles onset; burning, aching or stabbing. Allodynia (pain from clothing or touch) is characteristic, not diagnostic alone.

FindingThink
Dermatomal, after the rash, allodynia; risk higher if older, severe acute pain or rash, ophthalmicPHN
Bilateral, non-dermatomal or progressive; systemic features; pain unexplained by the rashMay not be PHN — reconsider (block 0, 7)

2Check — before prescribing

☐ Pain, distress, sleep, mood, function  ☐ Falls and cognition  ☐ Medicines, incl. opioids and CNS depressants  ☐ Renal function before gabapentinoids  ☐ Skin and neurological distribution

Don't miss: UKHSA Shingrix eligibility — previous shingles is no reason to withhold it.

3Decide — choose for the person

  • Offer one of amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin or pregabalin, chosen for frailty, falls, cognition, renal, hepatic and cardiac function, mood, interactions and misuse risk.
  • Localised pain: avoiding or intolerant of oral treatment → consider capsaicin 0.075% cream; oral ineffective or unsuitable → consider a lidocaine 5% plaster (NHS England/GMMMG restricted; not CG173).
  • Falls, sedation or cognitive effects → local treatment may beat escalating. Everyone: pacing, soft clothing; treat low mood and insomnia.

4Target

GoalMeans
PainReduce, not abolish — often eases over months
Continue a medicineOnly with meaningful benefit; harms evident → switch, even below maximum dose

5Treat — NICE CG173, one at a time

First lineOne CG173 agent; start low, titrate more slowly in older, frail or multimorbid people.
Fails or not toleratedSwitch to another; taper the first, with a short planned overlap if needed.
TopicalCapsaicin cream 3–4 times daily, ≥4 h apart, intact skin; reassess at 8 weeks. Lidocaine plaster: ≤3 at once, 12 h on / 12 h off; reassess at 2–4 weeks.
Specialist onlyCombination or high-dose therapy, tramadol, capsaicin 8% patch.
  • Gabapentin, pregabalin: Class C, Schedule 3; dose by renal function; sedation, falls, misuse; respiratory depression with opioids or CNS depressants, care over 65 (MHRA).
  • Amitriptyline: anticholinergic load, postural hypotension, confusion, glaucoma, cardiac disease. Duloxetine: serotonergic interactions, hyponatraemia, hepatic injury.
  • Doses per BNF and local formulary; GMMMG policy takes precedence.

6Monitor

  • Early review during titration: tolerability and adverse effects; titrate to an effective, tolerated dose.
  • Efficacy after an adequate trial at a tolerated dose (commonly 4–8 weeks) → continue only if it helps; otherwise switch.
  • Regular reviews: pain, function, sleep, mood, adverse effects, continued need.

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent routes are in block 0)

  • Pain clinic: pain severe or limiting sleep or function; no benefit or intolerance after sequential trials; specialist-only treatment needed; major medicine harms; diagnosis uncertain.
  • Mental health: significant depression or psychosocial component; consider CBT/ACT for significant distress or disability.

8Never

  • Add a second systemic agent instead of switching — combinations only with specialist advice.
  • Start strong opioids, long-term tramadol, a capsaicin 8% patch or non-CG173 medicines in primary care.
  • Stop a gabapentinoid abruptly — reduce with a plan.
  • Present antivirals as treating or preventing PHN.
  • Treat falls in an older person on these medicines as incidental.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “New problems with your bladder or bowels — call 999 or go to A&E straight away.”   Same-day “New weakness or numbness that spreads, the pain changes, or you feel generally unwell — contact us the same day. A new shingles rash, especially near your eye or ear — get advice the same day.”   New tablets: “They take a few weeks to reach the right dose; they can cause drowsiness and dizziness — take care driving.”   Expectations: “The aim is less pain and better sleep — it often eases over time.”
