Osteoarthritis

For: adults with joint pain in primary care. Not for: a hot swollen joint (same-day acute pathway), suspected inflammatory arthritis (NG100 rheumatology referral) or suspected bone cancer or myeloma (NG12) — see block 0.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG226QS87NG12NG100MHRA DSU
Core rule: diagnose on the history, make exercise and weight loss the treatment, add a topical NSAID before anything oral, and refer for joint replacement on quality of life — not on an X-ray, a score or a BMI.

0Act now — not every sore joint is osteoarthritis

  • Hot, swollen, painful joint (fever may be absent; prosthetic joint = high risk) → Same-day acute assessment (local septic arthritis pathway). Don't inject; don't delay antibiotics if systemically unwell.
  • Sudden severe pain after minor trauma in an older person (hip or tibial plateau fracture) → Same-day assessment and imaging
  • Inflammatory pattern: small joints of hands or feet, more than one joint, or 3 months or more since onset → Urgent rheumatology, even with normal CRP or negative RF/anti-CCP.
  • Progressive bone pain, rest pain away from the joint, mass or previous cancer → prompt imaging; adult X-ray suggests bone sarcoma → consider Suspected cancer pathway
  • Aged 60+, persistent bone pain (especially back) or unexplained fracture → offer FBC, calcium, ESR/PV, protein electrophoresis and free light chains together; suggest myeloma → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Rapidly worsening hip pain on long-term steroids or with heavy alcohol use (avascular necrosis) → Image promptly rather than labelling as OA.

2Check — examination and impact

☐ Effusion and warmth  ☐ Range, deformity, instability, gait  ☐ Hip and spine if knee pain  ☐ Function, sleep, mood, work  ☐ Weight/BMI  ☐ NSAID risks: GI, renal, CV, anticoagulants, pregnancy

Don't miss: soft, boggy MCP or wrist swelling is not OA → NG100 pathway.

3Decide — core treatment for everyone

  • Information: common, does not inevitably get worse, movement does not wear the joint out.
  • Offer tailored therapeutic exercise (strengthening plus aerobic); consider supervised sessions. Warn pain may rise at first.
  • Overweight or obesity → offer weight-loss support: any loss helps, 10% beats 5%.
  • Consider walking aids (lower limb). Manual therapy only with exercise. Insoles, braces, splints not routinely.

5Treat — every drug is an adjunct to exercise

Step 1 · topical NSAIDOffer for knee; consider for other joints (less useful for the hip)
Step 2 · oral NSAID + PPITopical ineffective or unsuitable → consider; lowest dose, shortest time; offer a PPI
Step 3 · joint injectionConsider a steroid if other drugs fail or to support exercise; relief 2–10 weeks
Only if · not routineParacetamol or weak opioid: infrequent, short-term, when all other drugs are unsuitable or ineffective
  • Before an oral NSAID: GI, renal, liver, CV risk; ACEi/ARB + diuretic ("triple whammy"), CKD, age, pregnancy.
  • Injection: exclude infection; warn of flare and diabetic glucose rise. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — agree follow-up (QS87)

  • Each review: goal, exercise, weight, mood, sleep. No structured exercise → fix before escalating. New heat or swelling → block 0.
  • Oral NSAID: BP, renal function, GI symptoms, PPI, still needed? → step down or stop if no functional gain.
  • Regular opioid, no functional benefit → slow taper (MHRA).

7Refer — routine

  • Hip, knee or shoulder: quality of life substantially affected, non-surgical care ineffective or unsuitable → consider joint-replacement referral.
  • ≥3 months core treatment first where appropriate; don't delay if unsuitable, severe impairment, rapid deformity or diagnostic doubt.
  • No scoring tools; never exclude for age, sex, smoking, comorbidity or weight.
  • Physio, hand therapy, podiatry, weight management: local GM pathways.

8Never

  • Strong opioids, glucosamine, intra-articular hyaluronan, acupuncture or dry needling, or electrotherapy (including TENS); arthroscopic lavage or debridement.
  • Routine paracetamol or weak opioids.
  • Inject a joint before infection has been reasonably excluded.
  • An oral NSAID without a PPI; systemic diclofenac in established IHD, PAD, cerebrovascular disease or heart failure.
  • Systemic NSAIDs from 28 weeks of pregnancy (avoid from 20 weeks unless clinically necessary).

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same-day “If a joint becomes hot and swollen, or you feel feverish or unwell with it, contact us the same day.”   Red flags: “Pain at night with weight loss, a lump, or pain away from the joint — come back promptly.”   Exercise: “It may ache more for the first couple of weeks; that's normal and isn't damage.”   Steroid injection and diabetes: “Your sugars may rise for a few days — check them as we agreed.”
