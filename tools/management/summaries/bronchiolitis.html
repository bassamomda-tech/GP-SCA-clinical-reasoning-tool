Bronchiolitis

For: children under 2 (mostly under 1) with suspected bronchiolitis in primary care. Not for: recurrent wheeze over 1 year (viral-induced wheeze or asthma protocols) or the febrile child without chest signs (NICE NG143).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG9 (2021 update)NICE NG143Green Book ch. 27aNirsevimab SPC
Core rule: look for apnoea, cyanosis, severe distress and low saturations first; then judge feeding and risk factors; prescribe nothing that does not work.

0Act now

  • Apnoea (observed or reported) or looks seriously unwell → 999
  • Severe respiratory distress: grunting, marked recession, rate over 70/minute → 999
  • Central cyanosis → 999
  • Rate over 60, intake 50–75% of usual, clinical dehydration, or SpO₂ persistently under 92% on air → consider Same-day paediatrics; lower threshold if high risk

1Confirm — clinical diagnosis

Coryza for 1–3 days, then persistent cough + tachypnoea or recession + wheeze or crackles, under 2 years. Worst on days 3–5.

FeatureThink again
Fever over 39°C or focal signsPneumonia
Recurrent wheeze over 1 yearViral wheeze or asthma
Paroxysms, apnoeaWhooping cough

2Check

☐ Respiratory rate (full minute) ☐ Saturations ☐ Heart rate, temperature ☐ Feeds as % of usual, wet nappies ☐ Day of illness

Don't miss: risk factors — under 3 months, born before 32 weeks, chronic lung or significant heart disease, neuromuscular disorder, immunodeficiency. No routine tests.

3Decide

  • No 999 signs, feeding well → home with supportive care.
  • Borderline + risk factors, early in illness or carers unable to cope → same-day referral.

5Treat — supportive only

FeedsSmall, frequent; saline drops before feeds may help
ComfortParacetamol or ibuprofen for distress from fever
HomeSmoke-free; safe sleep on the back
Do not useSalbutamol, steroids, antibiotics, montelukast, hypertonic saline

Doses per BNFC and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Reassess the same day if feeding falls to about half, breathing worsens or carers are worried.
  • Getting worse after day 5 or a new fever → reassess for pneumonia.

7Prevent

  • Pregnancy: RSV vaccine from 28 weeks (since 1 September 2024).
  • Nirsevimab: eligible high-risk infants and those born before 32 weeks (replaced palivizumab). Check Green Book ch. 27a.

8Never

  • Prescribe salbutamol, steroids or antibiotics for bronchiolitis.
  • Rely on a normal saturation reading over a baby who looks unwell.
  • Send home a baby under 6 weeks with apnoea.
  • Forget that day 1–2 is not the worst day.
  • Give over-the-counter cough medicines under 6 years.

9Safety-net

999 "Call 999 if your baby has pauses in breathing, goes blue around the lips, grunts or sucks in under the ribs, or is very hard to wake."   Same-day "Contact us or NHS 111 the same day if feeds drop below half of usual, no wet nappy for 12 hours, breathing is much faster, or you are worried."   "It usually peaks on days 3 to 5; the cough can last up to 3 weeks."
