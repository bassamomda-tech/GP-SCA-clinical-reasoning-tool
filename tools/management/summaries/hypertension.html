Hypertension in adults

For: adults aged 18 and over. Not for: pregnancy (use the hypertension in pregnancy protocol, NG133), children, or heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (use the heart-failure pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG136 (updated Feb 2026)NG203NG133NG238NG12
Core rule: confirm out of office before labelling, treat by absolute risk, climb A → C → D only after checking adherence, and check U&E 1–2 weeks after every ACE inhibitor or ARB change.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Clinic BP ≥180 systolic or ≥120 diastolic plus retinal haemorrhage or papilloedema, new confusion, chest pain, signs of heart failure or AKI → Same-day specialist
  • Suspected phaeochromocytoma (episodic headache, palpitations, sweating, pallor, labile or postural BP) → Urgent specialist — do not wait for tests.
  • Tearing chest or interscapular pain, with or without an arm-to-arm BP difference → 999 (possible aortic dissection).
  • New hypertension in pregnancy, especially after 20 weeks → Urgent maternity discussion; ≥160/110 needs immediate hospital treatment.
  • ≥180/120 with none of the above → bloods, ACR, ECG and fundi now. Target-organ damage → consider treating today without waiting for ABPM. No damage → Repeat BP or ABPM ≤7 days

Clinic 140/90–179/119 → ABPM (HBPM if unsuitable). Hypertension = clinic ≥140/90 and ABPM/HBPM average ≥135/85.

3Decide — who gets drugs

  • Stage 2, any age → offer drugs.
  • Stage 1, under 80 with target-organ damage, CVD, renal disease, diabetes or QRISK ≥10% → discuss drugs.
  • Stage 1, under 60, QRISK <10% → consider drugs alongside lifestyle.
  • Aged 80+, stage 1 → consider if clinic BP >150/90.
  • Lifestyle for everyone: salt ≤6 g a day, 150 min a week of activity, weight, alcohol ≤14 units a week, stop smoking. Agree one change.

  • Before every step: adherence, home readings, maximum tolerated doses, BP-raising drugs (e.g. NSAIDs, steroids, COCP).
  • ACEi cough → ARB. Black African / African–Caribbean origin → consider an ARB rather than an ACEi.
  • Usual: ramipril, amlodipine, indapamide — doses per BNF and GM medication pathway.

6Monitor

  • ACEi/ARB: U&E before, and 1–2 weeks after each start or dose rise. K⁺ ≥6.0 → stop, seek urgent advice. Creatinine up ≥30% or eGFR down ≥25% → find a cause; reduce or stop if none.
  • Indapamide: Na⁺/K⁺ at ~2 weeks. Spironolactone: U&E within 1 month.
  • Review about 4-weekly until at target, then annually: BP, adherence, U&E, ACR, HbA1c, lipids.
  • QRISK ≥10% → offer atorvastatin 20 mg (NG238).

7Refer — routine specialist

  • Under 40 with confirmed hypertension, or above target on 4 drugs.
  • Secondary-cause clues: low K⁺ (Conn's — send aldosterone:renin ratio); abrupt onset, flash pulmonary oedema or big eGFR fall on ACEi/ARB (renal artery stenosis); radio-femoral delay (coarctation).
  • Bus or lorry driver, BP consistently ≥180 systolic or ≥100 diastolic → stop driving, tell the DVLA.

8Never

  • An ACE inhibitor or ARB in pregnancy — switch before conception (labetalol → nifedipine → methyldopa, per NG133).
  • An ACE inhibitor and an ARB together.
  • A diagnosis from one clinic reading (except severe BP with target-organ damage).
  • A step-up before adherence, technique and white-coat effect are checked.
  • This ladder alone in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden severe headache, weakness on one side, sudden vision change, chest pain or confusion — call 999.”   New tablets: “Stand up slowly; don't stop a tablet without talking to us.”   ACE inhibitor: “A dry cough — ring us. Lip, tongue or throat swelling — 999.”   Sick days: “Vomiting or diarrhoea — contact us; we may pause your ACE inhibitor or ARB and water tablet.”
