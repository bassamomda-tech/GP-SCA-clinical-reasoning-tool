Dysmenorrhoea

For: period pain in women and young people in primary care, including suspected endometriosis or adenomyosis. Not for: acute pelvic pain with possible pregnancy (pregnancy test first, block 0). Bleeding-led → heavy menstrual bleeding; PID → PID; established chronic pain → chronic pain.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG73 (updated 2024)NICE NG88NG12 (updated April 2026)FSRH CHC · UKMEC
Core rule: a normal examination, ultrasound or MRI does not exclude endometriosis — treat on symptoms while investigating, and refer on the NG73 clinical thresholds, not on the scan.

0Act now — the acute causes and the cancer routes

  • Sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain with nausea and vomiting (torsion) → Same-day emergency gynaecology — no outpatient scan first.
  • Pelvic pain, pregnancy possible → test, whatever the contraception. Positive + pain or bleeding → Immediate early pregnancy assessment Syncope, instability, severe pain → 999 / A&E
  • Fever, offensive discharge, cervical excitation (PID) → antibiotics per local pathway + STI screen now; severe, abscess or pregnant → Same-day hospital
  • Ovarian symptoms 40+, persistent or frequent → CA-125 (NG12 age thresholds) → Urgent direct-access ultrasound if at or above; 39 or under → consider it. Ascites or a mass not obviously fibroids → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Cervix consistent with cancer → consider Suspected cancer pathway, despite a normal smear. Unexplained post-menopausal bleeding 55+, not HRT → suspected cancer pathway; under 55 → consider.
  • Severe pain from the first period with obstructed flow, a mass or primary amenorrhoea → Urgent adolescent gynaecology (acute retention of flow → Same day)

1Confirm — primary or secondary is the whole question

PrimarySecondary
OnsetWithin a few years of menarcheYears later, often 20s–40s
TimingWith or just before the bleed; 8–72 hoursDays before, after, or acyclical
CourseStable or improvingProgressive; “treatment stopped working”
CluesNausea, backache; normal examDeep dyspareunia, cyclical bowel or urinary symptoms, heavy bleeding, subfertility

NG73: suspect endometriosis on symptoms alone — imaging is for planning, not permission.

2Check — targeted, not routine

☐ Impact: days of school or work lost  ☐ Pregnancy test if possible  ☐ STI NAAT if PID possible  ☐ FBC + ferritin if heavy  ☐ Urinalysis if urinary symptoms

Examine: offer abdominal ± pelvic, with consent and a chaperone; declined → abdominal only, don't delay treatment. Transvaginal ultrasound: offer to all with suspected endometriosis, even if examination is normal. New pain with an IUD → threads, ultrasound, infection screen.

3Decide

  • Treat empirically now, in parallel with imaging and referral.
  • Fertility plans first: trying to conceive → hormones don't improve spontaneous pregnancy; early gynaecology/fertility referral.
  • Adolescents: CHC for pain regardless of sexual activity.
  • Adjuncts: heat, TENS, exercise, sleep, stop smoking.

5Treat — the ladder

Step 1 · analgesiaParacetamol or NSAID: mefenamic acid 500 mg TDS · naproxen 500 mg, then 250 mg 6–8-hourly · ibuprofen 400 mg TDS. Early, regular, 2–3 days.
Step 2 · CHCAfter UKMEC review; continuous or extended regimens are options.
Step 3 · progestogenLNG-IUS 52 mg (consider if heavy bleeding, adenomyosis) · desogestrel 75 µg · depot 150 mg IM 13-weekly (bone, fertility delay) · implant.
No contraception wantedNorethisterone 5 mg TDS day 5–24, 3–4 cycles — not contraceptive; not first line.
  • NSAID: renal, GI bleed, anticoagulant, NSAID asthma, pregnancy; PPI if GI risk. CHC: aura = UKMEC 4. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Analgesia ~3 months (NG73); hormones earlier if side effects or failure. Pain days, days lost, NSAID timing.
  • LNG-IUS ≥6 cycles: irregular bleeding may last 3–6 months or more; keep if pain improves.
  • Annually: UKMEC, BP, BMI · bone on depot · fertility plans.

7Refer — on the clinical picture, regardless of imaging

  • Gynaecology (NG73): affects daily life · persistent/recurrent · treatment failed, not tolerated or contraindicated · pelvic signs · fertility priority · normal scan, suspicion remains.
  • Endometriosis centre: endometrioma · deep (bowel, bladder, ureter) · extra-pelvic.
  • Suspected endometriosis, 17 or under → adolescent gynaecology or endometriosis service. Subfertility → fertility referral in parallel.

8Never

  • Stop treatment or referral because the scan or examination is normal.
  • Dismiss a new or escalating pattern as “just periods”, or cyclical dyschezia as IBS.
  • Routine opioids, or GnRH analogues in primary care.
  • CA-125 or primary-care MRI to diagnose endometriosis.
  • Make pelvic examination a condition of treatment.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

A&E now · 999 “Sudden severe one-sided pain with vomiting, or pain with a positive pregnancy test that is severe or comes with dizziness, fainting or shoulder-tip pain — go to A&E straight away, or call 999 if you collapse.”   Same day “Any other pain with a positive pregnancy test, or pain with a fever and offensive discharge — contact us the same day.”   Within a week “Pain that changes, becomes constant or stops responding, bleeding between periods or after sex, or new bowel or bladder symptoms — book to be seen within a week.”
