Angioedema

For: adults and children with acute or recurrent angioedema in primary care. Not for: full anaphylaxis treatment and observation (use the anaphylaxis protocol); starting specialist HAE or urticaria drugs (immunology / specialist service).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
Resuscitation Council UK 2021NICE NG258 (May 2026)BSACI 2015UK C1-INH consensus 2014NICE TA339
Core rule: airway beats aetiology — tongue, mouth or throat swelling or suspected anaphylaxis means 999 and IM adrenaline now; then ask “hives or no hives?” and check for an ACE inhibitor, however long it has been taken.

0Act now — the first decision is airway, not mechanism

  • Tongue, floor of mouth, pharynx or larynx swelling; voice change, stridor, drooling, difficulty swallowing or breathing → 999
  • Anaphylaxis suspected or not excludable, with A/B/C compromise → IM adrenaline 1 mg/mL, anterolateral thigh, repeat after 5 minutes if no better; don't wait to work out the mechanism → 999
  • Position: flat, legs raised if circulatory compromise; semi-recumbent if breathing is difficult; left lateral in pregnancy. No better after 2 doses = refractory → repeat IM every 5 minutes until the ambulance arrives.
  • Rapidly progressive oral/facial swelling, no airway symptoms yet → emergency admission now 999. Mild, stable isolated lip swelling → Urgent review, not automatic adrenaline.
  • Known HAE: throat tightness, voice change or severe abdominal attack → on-demand treatment per plan, then hospital 999

1Confirm — hives or no hives?

FeatureHistaminergicBradykinin (ACEi, HAE)
Wheals / itchUsually presentAbsent; tight or painful
CourseSettles in 24–48 hLasts 2–5 days
Abdominal attacksRareCommon in HAE; mimic surgical abdomen
Adrenaline, antihistamine, steroidGoodPoor or none

No wheals is not diagnostic alone. Chronic urticaria: most days >6 weeks; rarely allergic.

2Check — history does most of the work

☐ Medicines: ACE inhibitor (any duration), sacubitril/valsartan, ARB, gliptin (esp. vildagliptin) with an ACEi, NSAID/aspirin, oestrogen  ☐ Triggers, family history, abdominal attacks, photos  ☐ Serum C4 if recurrent without wheals, not on an ACEi or persisting after stopping it

Don't miss: C4 can be normal between attacks — if suspicion remains, ask immunology. No broad allergy panels.

3Decide — treat by mechanism

  • Histaminergic: remove trigger, antihistamine; chronic → block 5.
  • ACEi: stop permanently, record as a serious adverse reaction; switch class (e.g. calcium-channel blocker). ARB: small cross-risk — individualise; after severe airway angioedema, specialist advice first.
  • Sacubitril/valsartan: never with an ACEi (36-hour washout); previous angioedema → SPC and specialist advice.
  • Pregnancy: no ACE inhibitors — review urgently; don't assume 4× antihistamine is acceptable.

4Target — the goal for each type

UrticariaControlled, then step the dose down gradually
HAECoded; ACEi/oestrogen alerts; plan and on-demand drug in date

5Treat — histaminergic / chronic urticaria ladder

1 · First lineNon-sedating H1 (cetirizine, loratadine), standard dose, daily
2 · Uncontrolled 2–4 wkConsider off-label up-dosing to max 4×; document
3 · Still uncontrolledRefer via local pathway; montelukast only per local guidance (MHRA)
SpecialistOmalizumab (NICE TA339) or ciclosporin
  • Auto-injectors: two after suspected anaphylaxis (unless an easily avoidable drug cause and specialist/local guidance agree); not for ACEi/HAE alone or chronic urticaria. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor — every row has an action

  • Post-ED anaphylaxis → within days: referral made, two pens, trained.
  • ACEi stopped → 2–4 weeks + BP; may recur for some weeks; recurs after that → reassess, C4.
  • Urticaria every 2–4 weeks while titrating; HAE annually with immunology.

7Refer — mechanism-driven (urgent ones in block 0)

  • After suspected anaphylaxis → specialist allergy service (NG258).
  • Low C4 or HAE family history → immunology Urgent routine (± haematology if later-life onset).
  • Urticaria failing high-dose antihistamine → local pathway Routine
  • RAAS blockade needed after severe ACEi angioedema → cardiology/allergy advice.

8Never

  • Delay IM adrenaline to work out the mechanism when anaphylaxis can't be excluded.
  • Swap one ACE inhibitor for another, rechallenge, or clear it because it was taken for years.
  • Rely on antihistamine or steroid for swelling without hives — think ACEi and C4.
  • Label recurrent abdominal pain with swellings IBS without a C4.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If your lips, mouth, tongue or throat suddenly swell, your voice changes, or you struggle to breathe or swallow, call 999 — and use your adrenaline pen first if you have one.”   Pen: “Call 999 whenever you use it; no better after 5 minutes — use the second pen.”   Same-day “New swelling elsewhere, such as hands, feet or genitals — contact us or 111 the same day; severe tummy pain with swelling — go to A&E now.”   Chronic: “Take the antihistamine every day, and photograph any swelling.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed

Airway and anaphylaxis checked; 999 and adrenaline if needed Hives or no hives decided and recorded Full medicine review (ACEi any duration); C4 if indicated; pregnancy considered Culprit stopped and coded; ladder, referral and review date set 999 and same-day safety-net given and documented
