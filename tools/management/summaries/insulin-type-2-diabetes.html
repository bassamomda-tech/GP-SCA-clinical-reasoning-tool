Insulin in type 2 diabetes

For: adults with type 2 diabetes starting or intensifying insulin (Greater Manchester). Not for: type 1, DKA/HHS (DKA), pregnancy, pre-conception or breastfeeding (diabetes–antenatal team), children, specialist regimens. Oral ladder → type 2 diabetes; unwell → sick day rules — unwell on insulin.

NICE NG28GMMMG formulary & insulin aid (local)DVLAMHRABNF
Core rule: prescribe by brand with “units” written in full, titrate basal against the fasting glucose pattern, and if fasting is at target but HbA1c is not, do not add more basal — the problem is after meals.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Suspected DKA or HHS — vomiting, dehydration, abdominal pain, drowsiness, deep breathing, ketones, very high glucose (on an SGLT2 inhibitor even if glucose normal) → 999 / A&E immediate hospital assessment; stop the SGLT2 inhibitor.
  • Possible type 1 or LADA — lean build, ketosis-prone, rapid weight loss or catabolic symptoms, young age at onset → Same-day specialist advice; basal insulin alone is dangerous.
  • Unable to manage insulin safely when ill → Same-day advice (out of hours, NHS 111). Never omit or stop insulin without clinical advice.
  • Hypo <4 mmol/L, conscious and able to swallow → 15–20 g fast-acting carbohydrate, recheck in 10–15 min, repeat if <4, then longer-acting carbohydrate; nothing by mouth if unconscious or unsafe to swallow.

1Confirm — insulin is the right next step

  • Consider insulin for symptomatic or marked hyperglycaemia, suspected insulin deficiency or catabolism, or targets not met despite appropriate non-insulin treatment.
  • Symptomatic → consider insulin or a sulfonylurea; HbA1c ≥75 mmol/mol → consider basal + short/rapid-acting (separate or premixed).

2Check — baseline before the first dose

3Decide — which basal, what stays

  • Offer basal insulin once or twice daily by shared decision (carer use, nocturnal hypos, once-daily wish; cost if equally suitable) and GMMMG formulary.
  • Isophane (Insuman Basal®, Insulatard®) suits many; analogue (Semglee®) for nocturnal hypos or carer use. Humulin I®, Abasaglar® only if clear need (supply). No new Levemir®; switch all by 30 November 2026.
  • Keep metformin; usually the SGLT2 inhibitor (pause if unwell). Stop or substantially reduce the sulfonylurea unless documented reason. GLP-1 RA only within the specialist pathway; stop a DPP-4 inhibitor with a GLP-1 RA or tirzepatide.
  • Structured education: technique, sites, titration, hypos, sick days, DVLA; expect weight gain.

5Treat — GMMMG local doses

Step 1 · start10 units once daily, same time each day. Frail, older, advanced renal impairment, high hypo risk → 6–10 units.
Step 2 · self-titrate+2 units every 3 days on the 4–7-day fasting pattern to target without hypos. Fasting <4 → treat, don't increase, reduce 10%.
Step 3 · HbA1c still highFasting not at target → keep titrating. Fasting at target → check post-meal glucose; basal-plus (4 units rapid-acting with the largest-rise meal, +2 units twice weekly), twice-daily premix, or GLP-1/insulin via specialist.
Step 4 · specialistUncontrolled on twice-daily premix, or little response to genuine titration → diabetes team.
  • Before every increase: diet, alcohol, activity, illness, technique, adherence; examine sites for lipohypertrophy. Any hypo: reduce dose, stop any sulfonylurea, find the cause.
  • Prescribe by brand, strength and device; “units” in full. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • HbA1c every 3–6 months until stable, then 6-monthly.
  • Every review: hypos, awareness, sites, technique, weight, adherence; record sick-day and DVLA advice.
  • Annually: feet, eyes, U&E/eGFR, urine ACR, lipids, BP; no SGLT2 inhibitor → offer per NG28 unless contraindicated.

7Refer — diabetes team · and DVLA

  • Recurrent severe or unaware hypos (relax the target meanwhile); very high insulin needs; Toujeo® or degludec (GMMMG criteria); pregnancy or pre-conception (diabetes–antenatal team).
  • DVLA: notify if insulin continues beyond 3 months; Group 2 stop on starting insulin, pending a DVLA decision. Test before the first journey, every 2 hours; not below 4.0 mmol/L; impaired awareness → stop driving.

8Never

  • Write “insulin glargine”, “U” or “iu”, or switch product, device or concentration automatically.
  • Add more basal when fasting is at target but HbA1c is high.
  • Change a dose on one reading, or escalate against lipohypertrophy or missed doses.
  • Omit or stop insulin during illness without clinical advice.
  • Start basal insulin alone when type 1 or LADA is possible.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Vomiting and unable to keep fluids down, drowsy, tummy pain, fast or deep breathing, or ketones high — call 999 or go to A&E now, even if your sugar is normal on an SGLT2 tablet.”   Same day “Contact us the same day (out of hours, 111): a hypo that needed help, happened at night or driving, or you can't explain; repeated lows; illness lasting more than 24 hours.”   Titration: “Two units up every three days if morning readings are above target. Below four: treat it, don't increase, come down ten per cent and tell us.”   Sick days: “Don't stop or skip your insulin without speaking to us first.”
