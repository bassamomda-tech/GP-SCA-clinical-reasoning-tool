Temporomandibular disorders (TMD)

For: adults with jaw-joint or chewing-muscle pain, clicking, locking or restricted opening that fits TMD. Not for: undifferentiated jaw pain (use the jaw pain pathway); suspected giant cell arteritis (use the GCA protocol); trigeminal neuralgia (use the trigeminal neuralgia protocol).

DC/TMD (2014)GIRFT OMFS (2023)NICE NG12 (red flags)SPC (emc)
Core rule: name the type and examine fully — teeth, opening, joint, muscles, bite, nodes, cranial nerves and, aged 50 or over, GCA — then treat uncomplicated TMD conservatively; no routine muscle relaxants, benzodiazepines or irreversible bite treatment.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Spreading facial or dental infection with airway compromise → 999 emergency, not a routine OMFS referral.
  • Aged ≥50 with new jaw or tongue claudication (chewing or talking, relieved by rest), new headache, scalp tenderness, polymyalgia → Same-day GCA pathway; FBC, CRP, ESR, U&E/LFT, but do not delay steroids or assessment if suspicion is high.
  • Visual symptoms with GCA features → Same-day emergency ophthalmology or acute medicine.
  • Acute dislocation, significant trauma or suspected fracture, profound trismus, unable to eat or drink, dysphagia, floor-of-mouth swelling, dental swelling with fever → Same-day emergency, dental or OMFS. Do not manipulate or reduce the jaw untrained.
  • Unexplained oral ulcer >3 weeks or persistent unexplained neck lump → consider Suspected cancer pathway (NG12). Lip or oral lump, red or red-and-white patch → consider Urgent dental referral.
  • Trigeminal-type pain with sensory loss, bilateral, onset <40, deafness, visual or other neurological signs → Urgent specialist advice

1Confirm — name the type

Click without pain, locking or limitation → reassurance only.

2Check — before the TMD label

☐ Teeth, gums  ☐ Opening, deviation, locking  ☐ Joint, masseter, temporalis; resisted movement  ☐ Bite  ☐ Cranial nerves, sensation  ☐ Neck nodes, parotid, face  ☐ Otoscopy if ear symptoms  ☐ ≥50: temporal arteries, scalp  ☐ Bruxism, stress, sleep

Don't miss: unilateral or progressive pain, swelling, numbness, new bite change, weight loss → reconsider TMD.

3Decide — who gets what

  • Uncomplicated TMD → conservative care, short-term analgesia; most need no OMFS.
  • Painless click → reassure; no splint just to remove a click.
  • Troublesome bruxism or selected persistent TMD → a dentist may consider a reversible, monitored splint.
  • Persistent pain, reassessed, chronic primary or neuropathic phenotype → consider low-dose TCA (off-label); specialist advice may help.

5Treat

Self-careSoft diet for a while; no gum or wide opening; heat, massage; move gently; ease clenching
AnalgesiaParacetamol or an oral NSAID, short term only, local formulary
DentalReversible splint after dental assessment; must not alter the bite
Not settlingRe-check self-care, bruxism, mood, diagnosis → refer
  • NSAID: check renal, GI, cardiovascular risk, anticoagulant, antiplatelet, steroid, NSAID-sensitive asthma; never two; gastroprotect by risk. Paracetamol: lower maximum if low weight, frail, liver disease, high alcohol. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Not improving with conservative care → review; sooner if function deteriorating.
  • Analgesia: no prolonged regular use without reassessing the diagnosis and medication overuse.
  • Splint: dentist reviews; worse pain, bite or function → review.

7Refer — routine

  • Dentist: caries, gum disease, odontogenic pain, occlusal assessment, bruxism and splints.
  • Physio, MSK or facial-pain service: persistent muscular pain or movement dysfunction.
  • Oral medicine or OMFS (local pathway): closed lock, marked restriction, recurrent dislocation, progressive, disabling, failed conservative trial, joint disease, diagnostic doubt — not chronicity alone.

8Never

  • Routine benzodiazepines or muscle relaxants; any course exceptional, time-limited, reviewed.
  • Irreversible bite adjustment, extensive restorative work or orthodontics for TMD pain.
  • Forceful jaw manipulation in primary care.
  • Prolonged jaw immobilisation, or a routine TCA for uncomplicated mechanical TMD.
  • Persistent, progressive or unilateral facial pain called TMD without reassessment.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Difficulty breathing or swallowing, or swelling spreading under your tongue or into your neck: call 999.”   Same-day “If you're 50 or over and your jaw aches when you chew, with a new headache or scalp tenderness, get help the same day — if your vision changes, go to A&E or eye casualty straight away. Jaw locked, can't open or can't eat or drink, facial swelling with fever, a lump, a red or red-and-white patch or an ulcer that won't heal: get help the same day.”   Review: “If it is not improving, come back for a review.”
