Otitis media and otitis externa

For: the painful or discharging ear in primary care, children and adults (NICE NG91 covers under 18s only; adult AOM → local antimicrobial guideline). Not for: glue ear in detail (use the otitis media with effusion page) or an adult with a new hearing problem (use the hearing loss chapter).

NICE NG91 (updated 2022)NG233NG98NG143UKHSA antimicrobial summary
Core rule: look behind the ear and at the whole patient before the drum — AOM is pain relief first with antibiotics by NG91 criteria, otitis externa is treated in the canal, and severe or persistent ear pain in diabetes or immunosuppression is necrotising otitis externa until ENT says otherwise.

0Act now — screen first; any of these changes the destination before any drug is chosen

  • Mastoiditis (swelling, redness or tenderness behind the ear, pinna pushed forwards) or severe systemic infection → hospital Same-day
  • Severe headache, neck stiffness, drowsiness, vomiting, seizures, focal neurology or new facial palsy with an ear infection → 999 / emergency
  • Diabetes, immunosuppression or frailty + severe, persistent or night-time pain, granulation tissue or failed topical treatment → possible necrotising otitis externa: Same-day ENT; sepsis, nerve palsy, neurological signs → A&E.
  • Sudden hearing loss over ≤3 days, not wax, canal oedema or effusion → ENT or ED Immediate · seen ≤24 h. Vesicles with facial weakness (Ramsay Hunt) → same-day ENT.
  • Febrile under-5, any NG143 red feature → Same-day urgent paediatric; immediately life-threatening → 999. Cochlear implant with severe infection → same-day ENT.

1Confirm — middle ear, canal or effusion?

  • AOM: bulging or opaque drum, lost landmarks or reduced mobility — a red drum alone is not enough.
  • Otitis externa: pain on moving tragus or pinna; red, swollen canal with debris.
  • Glue ear: dull or retracted drum, muffled hearing, little pain.
  • Normal ear, persistent one-sided otalgia with dysphagia, hoarseness, weight loss or smoking → Urgent head and neck

2Check — examine more than the drum

☐ General condition (under 5 febrile: NG143 traffic light) ☐ Canals, drums, pinna ☐ Behind the ear ☐ Cranial nerve VII ☐ Teeth, jaw, throat, neck ☐ Hearing ☐ Diabetes

Don't miss: chronic foul discharge with an attic pocket or crust = cholesteatoma → ENT; do not irrigate.

3Decide — AOM antibiotics, under 18s (NG91)

  • Everyone: offer paracetamol or ibuprofen; usually better in about 3 days.
  • Systemically very unwell, signs of a more serious illness, or high risk of complications → offer an immediate antibiotic.
  • Otorrhoea, or under 2 with both ears infected → consider no, back-up or immediate antibiotic.
  • Others → consider no antibiotic or a back-up (use if not improving by 3 days or much worse).
  • Adults: NG91 does not apply — consider dental, jaw, malignancy, cholesteatoma; amoxicillin 500 mg–1 g three times daily for 5–7 days (CKS).

5Treat — doses per BNF / BNFC and local formulary

AOM · dropsConsider phenazone + lidocaine, 4 drops 2–3 times a day ≤7 days, if no immediate antibiotic and no perforation or otorrhoea
AOM · antibioticAmoxicillin 5–7 days. Allergy: clarithromycin (erythromycin in pregnancy). Worse after ≥2–3 days → co-amoxiclav
OE · everyoneAnalgesia, local heat, keep dry; remove the cause (water, cotton buds, hearing aid)
OE · by severityMild: acetic acid 2% (over 12s). Moderate/severe: antibiotic ± steroid drops 7 days (neomycin only if drum intact)
  • Can't see the drum → aural toilet, microsuction or wick; no aminoglycoside drops if perforation or grommet possible.
  • Cellulitis beyond the canal or systemic illness → start oral flucloxacillin (clarithromycin if allergic) and refer to ENT to exclude necrotising otitis externa.

6Monitor

  • OE not easing at 48–72 h → check drop delivery and canal; think fungal, foreign body, dermatitis, necrotising disease.
  • Glue ear (under 12s, NG233): observe 3 months with hearing test and tympanometry; no antibiotics, decongestants or steroids. Persistent hearing loss → discuss aids or grommets.

7Refer

  • Urgent ENT canal too swollen for drops; cholesteatoma with facial weakness, vertigo, severe pain or fast-worsening hearing.
  • Adult of Chinese or SE Asian origin, hearing loss + effusion without a cold → consider suspected cancer pathway (NG98).
  • Routine ENT / audiology recurrent AOM or OE, chronic discharge, persistent perforation, glue ear >3 months with hearing loss.

8Never

  • A third course of drops for severe, night-time ear pain in a diabetic — same-day ENT.
  • Rely on a back-up prescription with mastoid, systemic, facial palsy or intracranial signs.
  • Aminoglycoside drops with a perforation or patent grommet unless ENT advises; irrigate a suspected perforation or cholesteatoma.
  • Oral antibiotics for uncomplicated otitis externa.
  • Delay sudden-hearing-loss referral for audiology or a trial of antibiotics.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “A fit, severe headache with stiff neck, very drowsy or hard to wake, or a floppy child — call 999.”   Same day “Swelling behind the ear, ear sticking out, weak face, sudden hearing loss, new dizziness or blisters on the ear — come back today.”   AOM: “Regular pain relief. Not better in 3 days or much worse — contact us; the back-up prescription may then be used.”   Otitis externa: “Keep it dry, no cotton buds, finish the drops. Much worse or night pain with diabetes — same day; not easing in 2–3 days — come back.”
