Postnatal (perinatal) depression

For: women with low mood in pregnancy and the first year after childbirth. Not for: depression outside the perinatal period (depression protocol); anxiety or OCD-led presentations (anxiety, OCD).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG192NG222 (updated Dec 2025)MBRRACE-UK 2025SPS / UKDILASSPC (emc)
Core rule: separate baby blues, depression and postpartum psychosis; ask directly about suicide and harm to the baby at every contact; assess for bipolar disorder by history before any antidepressant.

0Act now — maternal suicide is the leading cause of maternal death from 6 weeks to 1 year after pregnancy

  • Suspected postpartum psychosis (sudden confusion, mania, hallucinations, delusions, marked behavioural change, rapidly shifting mood; usually first 2 weeks, possible up to 1 year) → Immediate specialist perinatal assessment ≤4 h of referral Do not leave her alone or book a routine appointment.
  • Immediate danger, can't maintain safety, or serious risk to the woman or infant → 999 Immediate risk to the infant or lack of capacity → safeguarding and mental-health legislation pathways take priority.
  • Suicidal thoughts, intent or plan, recent self-harm, or thoughts of harming the infant → Same-day structured risk assessment + crisis or perinatal mental-health input.
  • Severe depression (can't care for self or infant, not eating or drinking, severe agitation or retardation, rapid deterioration, severe insomnia with activation or mania) → Same-day mental-health assessment, not simply therapy plus an SSRI.

1Confirm — severity is clinical, not a score

Consider the Whooley questions at antenatal and postnatal contacts. Positive, concern or higher risk → fuller assessment; consider EPDS or PHQ-9 to support it (not diagnostic).

2Check — document the risk assessment

☐ Thoughts of death or self-harm, intent, plan, means  ☐ Thoughts of harming the baby  ☐ Psychosis or mania  ☐ Bipolar history: elevated mood, reduced need for sleep, past admission or postpartum psychosis, family history  ☐ Domestic abuse, substance use  ☐ Bonding, infant care, safeguarding  ☐ Supervision, crisis contact, follow-up

Don't miss: anaemia or thyroid disease (incl. postpartum thyroiditis) → consider FBC/ferritin, TFTs if features suggest; OCD intrusive thoughts.

3Decide — stepped care (CG192)

  • Mild or subthreshold → facilitated self-help or a psychological intervention.
  • Mild to moderate → guided self-help, CBT or IPT (prioritised perinatal Talking Therapies); medication considered by history and preference.
  • Moderate to severe → therapy and/or an antidepressant after a benefit–risk discussion, including untreated illness for mother and baby. Do not delay specialist referral.
  • Everyone: family help with feeds and sleep (a safety intervention), health visitor, peer support (e.g. PANDAS), bonding.

5Treat — antidepressant, if chosen

  • Before starting: bipolar assessed by history. Suspected bipolar → no antidepressant monotherapy without specialist advice.
  • Breastfeeding: sertraline is often a preferred SSRI (low milk transfer). Weigh previous response, history, preference, infant gestation and health. Do not advise stopping breastfeeding automatically.
  • Already on an effective antidepressant → do not stop it without discussion, and never abruptly.
  • Titrate per product information — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.
  • Watch the infant: sedation, agitation, sleep disturbance, diarrhoea, poor feeding, poor weight gain — especially if premature, unwell or exposed to several psychotropics.

6Monitor

  • 1–2 weeks after starting: mood, risk, side effects and the infant.
  • Ongoing clinical review, EPDS or PHQ-9 to support it; coordinate with the health visitor.
  • Continue ≥6 months after remission; consider longer if relapse risk is high (recurrent or severe, bipolar-spectrum, previous postpartum psychosis). Stop gradually.
  • Bipolar or previous postpartum psychosis → explicit night-time support and escalation plan.

7Refer — specialist perinatal mental health

  • Moderate to severe, bipolar, complex or treatment-resistant; any history of severe mental illness, even if well.
  • Clear first-degree family history of severe perinatal mental illness → consider referral; extra vigilance in the first 2 weeks.
  • Inpatient care within 12 months of birth → Mother and Baby Unit normally offered.
  • Infant welfare concern → safeguarding per local pathways. Future pregnancy → pre-conception planning.

8Never

  • Let a low EPDS or negative Whooley exclude risk or set urgency.
  • An antidepressant before a bipolar history (mania, admission, family).
  • Leave suspected psychosis alone or wait for a routine appointment.
  • Advise stopping breastfeeding by default; stop an effective antidepressant abruptly.
  • "Baby blues" beyond 2 weeks, or if worsening or impairing.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you or the baby are in immediate danger, call 999.”   Crisis: “If you feel unsafe or have frightening thoughts about yourself or the baby, contact us straight away — we can get help the same day.”   Rapid change: “Confused, can't sleep at all, or thinking or behaving very differently — that needs urgent help straight away.”   New antidepressant: “More agitated or low in the first weeks — contact us the same day.”   “It's common and treatable — not your fault, and it doesn't make you a bad parent.”
