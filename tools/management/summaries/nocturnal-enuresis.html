Nocturnal enuresis (bedwetting)

For: children and young people under 19 with bedwetting, primary or secondary. Not for: adults. Constipation → child constipation page; suspected UTI → UTI in children; suspected diabetes → same-day paediatric diabetes team.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG111BNF for ChildrenDesmopressin product informationNICE NG18
Core rule: treat constipation first and never blame — reward agreed behaviours, not dry nights; desmopressin only with the fluid rule: no drinks (sips only) from 1 hour before to 8 hours after.

0Act now — desmopressin hyponatraemia and suspected diabetes

  • On desmopressin: seizure, confusion or unusual drowsiness (possible hyponatraemia) → stop desmopressin → 999
  • On desmopressin: headache, nausea or vomiting → stop desmopressin → Same-day medical assessment.
  • Thirst, polyuria, weight loss, or abrupt secondary onset → finger-prick blood glucose (and ketones); suspected type 1 diabetes → Same-day paediatric diabetes team. Do not wait for HbA1c or laboratory tests.
  • Previously dry child wetting again with pain passing urine → urinalysis, assess for UTI Same-day (UTI in children).

1Confirm — pattern, not just "wet nights"

PrimaryNever dry
SecondaryRelapse after ≥6 months dry → UTI, diabetes, constipation, stressors, safeguarding; exclude physical causes before assuming stress
Non-monosymptomaticWith daytime symptoms: >7 or <4 voids/day, urgency, daytime wetting, dysuria, straining, poor stream, recurrent UTIs, toilet avoidance. None = monosymptomatic

2Check

☐ Constipation (stools, pain, withholding, soiling)  ☐ Daytime symptoms  ☐ Psychosocial, safeguarding  ☐ Diary: wet/dry nights, fluids, bowels  ☐ Urinalysis only if recent or secondary onset, daytime symptoms or dysuria, UTI history, unwell, or diabetes suspected — not routine in typical primary monosymptomatic enuresis. Don't miss: occult constipation despite daily stools.

3Decide

  • Constipation → treat fully first. Significant or predominant daytime symptoms → address or refer; mild ones need not delay alarm or desmopressin.
  • Choose by goal: long-term cure → alarm; rapid or short-term cover (sleepovers, camps), or alarm unsuitable → desmopressin. Do not exclude under-7s on age alone.
  • Lifestyle: adequate daytime fluids, no excessive restriction; no caffeine or large drinks before bed; void before bed.

4Target

AlarmAt least 2 uninterrupted dry weeks
DesmopressinSustained dryness, tested by withdrawal every 3 months

5Treat — by goal, after the basics

Step 1 · enuresis alarmFirst-line for long-term cure in a motivated child and family; needs weeks; positive reinforcement. Restart promptly if relapse
Step 2 · desmopressinOffer over 7; consider at 5–7 only after individual assessment. 200 micrograms at night; incomplete response after 1–2 weeks → consider dose increase
Step 3 · refractoryConsider alarm plus desmopressin, or desmopressin alone, by acceptability and response. Partial response → may take 1–2 h before bed
Step 4 · specialist onlyAnticholinergic only with desmopressin, with daytime symptoms, after specialist assessment
  • Every desmopressin night: no drinks (sips only) 1 h before to 8 h after; omit if vomiting, diarrhoea, fever or the child cannot comply.
  • Doses per BNFC, product information and local formulary.

6Monitor — diary throughout

  • Alarm, 4 weeks: response → continue; none → reassess. 3 months not dry → continue only if improving and motivated.
  • Desmopressin, 4 weeks: response → continue 3 months; none → consider stopping. Longer term: 1-week withdrawal every 3 months; partial responders may improve for up to 6 months.
  • Not dry? Recheck constipation, daytime symptoms, adherence, secondary causes.

7Refer — local paediatric continence/enuresis service

  • Severe or persistent daytime wetting or urgency, recurrent UTIs, abnormal stream, neurological or structural concerns, refractory constipation.
  • Not responding to appropriate courses of alarm and/or desmopressin, or significant comorbidity.
  • Safeguarding or psychosocial concerns → follow local safeguarding pathways.

8Never

  • Punish, or turn changing the bedding into a punishment.
  • Reward dry nights — a child cannot choose to be dry.
  • Escalate before constipation is treated fully.
  • Give desmopressin without the fluid rule, or during vomiting, diarrhoea or fever.
  • Use an anticholinergic alone for monosymptomatic bedwetting, or impose excessive daytime fluid restriction.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “On desmopressin, if your child has a fit, is confused or is unusually drowsy — call 999.”   Same-day “If they get a headache, feel sick or vomit, stop the tablets and get them seen the same day. If a previously dry child starts wetting again with thirst, weight loss or pain passing urine — see us the same day.”
Fluid rule: “No drinks, or only small sips, from 1 hour before the dose until 8 hours after; skip it if your child is vomiting, has diarrhoea, has a fever or can't stick to the limits.”   Reassure: “It's common and not your child's fault — many children improve with age, and effective treatments are available.”
