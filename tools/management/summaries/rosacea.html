Rosacea

For: adults with rosacea managed in primary care. Not for: acne vulgaris (use the acne protocol), seborrhoeic dermatitis or blepharitis alone (use those pages); diagnostic doubt or atypical distribution → dermatology advice.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BAD rosacea guideline (2021)PCDSMHRA brimonidineLocal formularySPCs
Core rule: treat the dominant feature, keep steroids off the face, time-limit oral antibiotics, and ask about the eyes at every review — eye pain, photophobia or reduced vision means same-day ophthalmology.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Eye pain, photophobia, reduced or blurred vision, pain on eye movement, or suspected keratitis → Same-day ophthalmology — do not wait for treatment failure.
  • Contact-lens wearer with a painful red eye, photophobia or reduced vision → may be microbial keratitis, not rosacea → Same-day ophthalmology

2Check — every new presentation

☐ Eye symptoms and contact-lens use  ☐ Facial steroid use  ☐ Triggers  ☐ Distress, low mood, anxiety  ☐ Pregnancy plans

Don't miss: acne, seborrhoeic, periorificial or contact dermatitis, lupus, dermatomyositis, photodermatosis, steroid-induced rosacea.

3Decide — everyone, then by feature

  • Everyone: non-soap cleanser, fragrance-free moisturiser, daily broad-spectrum sunscreen; a trigger diary (heat, sun, alcohol, spicy food, hot drinks, stress).
  • Persistent redness: green-tinted camouflage can help.
  • Not merely cosmetic: record distress in the severity; consider psychological support or camouflage.

TreatmentReview
Topicals8–12 weeks; then intermittent or ongoing maintenance may be considered — not automatic

5Treat — by dominant feature (local formulary)

Papulopustular 1stAzelaic acid 15% gel or metronidazole 0.75%, twice daily, by patient preference
Then (restricted)Ivermectin 10 mg/g cream once daily, about 3 months, if those fail or are unsuitable
More severeConsider time-limited oral tetracycline, about 3 months: doxycycline 40 mg MR (licensed); doxycycline 100 mg or lymecycline 408 mg off-label
Severe persistent erythemaBrimonidine gel only after other options, before laser referral (local guidance)
  • Brimonidine: warn about rebound (up to 16%) first; once daily, thin layer on intact skin, SPC amount; caution in bradycardia, hypotension, CVD, antihypertensives.
  • Flushing: triggers first; propranolol off-label, not routine — selected patients only, after asthma/COPD, bradycardia, hypotension, heart block screen.
  • Doxycycline: plenty of water, upright; separate from iron, calcium, antacids. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — eyes at every review

  • Ask: gritty or dry eyes, blepharitis, styes, photophobia, pain, blurred vision, sticky discharge.
  • Mild ocular: lid hygiene, warm compresses, preservative-free lubricants. Facial therapy alone is not enough.
  • Pregnancy: azelaic acid generally preferred; ivermectin lacks safety data; avoid tetracyclines unless specialist advice.

7Refer — routine

  • Dermatology: resistant papulopustular disease; laser/IPL for erythema or telangiectasia; severe flushing; severe disease when pregnant or trying to conceive; doubt; substantial distress.
  • Rhinophyma → dermatology or plastics. Ophthalmology: eye symptoms troublesome despite lid care.

8Never

  • Topical steroids on the face without a clear separate indication and plan — or an abrupt unsupervised stop after prolonged potent use (seek dermatology advice).
  • Brimonidine on irritated, broken or recently lasered skin, or for papules or telangiectasia.
  • Prolonged or open-ended oral antibiotics, or topical antibiotic monotherapy as a strategy.
  • A tetracycline when pregnancy is planned or possible without discussing safer options; doxycycline under 12 (generally avoided).
  • Minocycline unless there is no suitable alternative.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “If your eyes become painful, sensitive to light or your vision blurs, contact us straight away so an eye specialist can check them the same day.”   Treatment: “Sunscreen, gentle skincare and your triggers matter as much as the creams; we'll review it after a couple of months. Please don't use steroid creams on your face.”   Brimonidine: “If the redness or burning gets worse, stop it and tell us.”
