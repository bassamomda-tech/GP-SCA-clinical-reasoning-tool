Vasovagal syncope and orthostatic hypotension

For: over 16s with a blackout thought to be a faint or postural. Not for: the undifferentiated blackout (use the transient loss of consciousness pathway) or a suspected seizure (use the epilepsy protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CG109ESC Syncope 2018DVLA fitness to driveSPC (emc)
Core rule: vasovagal is a diagnosis of exclusion — exertional or supine collapse is cardiac until assessed; do the ECG and lying/standing BP, but never let them delay an urgent referral.

0Act now — exclude a cardiac cause first, every time

  • Unstable, ongoing chest pain, severe breathlessness, sustained arrhythmia, complete heart block or Mobitz II, major injury or incomplete recovery → 999 / same-day emergency
  • Thunderclap headache with LOC (subarachnoid haemorrhage) → Same-day emergency CT head.
  • Syncope during exertion or while lying flat (HOCM, aortic stenosis, arrhythmia) → Urgent cardiovascular assessment ≤24 h; no exertion or driving until assessed.
  • Any ECG abnormality — QTc >450 or <350 ms, Brugada, bundle branch or any heart block, ventricular arrhythmia, pre-excitation, ischaemic change → ≤24 h; same-day if unstable or QT marked. Review QT-prolonging drugs; no driving.
  • Heart failure, murmur, new unexplained breathlessness, family history of sudden cardiac death under 40 or inherited cardiac disease, palpitations or chest pain before the blackout → ≤24 h
  • Over 65 with no prodrome → consider ≤24 h; no prodrome at any age needs cardiac exclusion.

1Confirm — the witness account first

Uncomplicated faint = 3 Ps (posture, provoking factor such as pain or a procedure, progressive prodrome) + full recovery + satisfactory ECG + no cardiac, neurological or metabolic alternative.

Favours seizure: sustained tonic-clonic activity, head turning, clearly bitten tongue, prolonged confusion. Favours syncope: rapid reorientation; brief jerks and incontinence can occur in a faint.

No single feature is diagnostic — uncertainty remains → refer.

2Check — promptly, without delaying urgent referral

☐ 12-lead ECG with QTc  ☐ BP lying/seated, then standing ≥1 min (up to 3): fall ≥20 systolic or ≥10 diastolic = postural hypotension  ☐ Drug changes in last 2 weeks  ☐ Bloods only if indicated (glucose if hypo possible)

Don't miss: a murmur → aortic stenosis or HOCM (≤24 h, echo).

3Decide — route by risk and mechanism

  • Typical or situational faint, normal ECG, no red flags → primary care.
  • Orthostatic → drug review first, treat dehydration; refer if persists or unclear.

7Refer — non-urgent (urgent in block 0)

  • First unexplained syncope over 45 → cardiology ≤2 weeks
  • Suspected seizure → neurology ≤2 weeks; stop driving.
  • Recurrent unexplained, normal ECG and monitoring → cardiology ≤4 weeks; ask about tilt test.
  • Carotid sinus hypersensitivity → Routine cardiology; stop alpha-blockers.

5Treat — lifestyle first; drugs are specialist-led

Step 1 · allCounter-manoeuvres at the first warning: sit or crouch, cross legs, tense leg muscles. Drug review; stockings if orthostatic.
Step 2 · orthostaticFludrocortisone — consider only with specialist advice or local protocol; avoid in heart failure.
Step 3 · refractory orthostaticMidodrine — specialist-led only. Last dose ≥4 h before bed; supine BP lying flat at every visit.
Step 4 · refractory vasovagalBeta-blockers, SSRIs and pacing are specialist decisions.
  • Fluids; salt only where appropriate — check first for heart failure, CKD, cirrhosis, uncontrolled or supine hypertension. No fixed prescriptions.
  • Review: antihypertensives, alpha-blockers, diuretics, nitrates, antidepressants, antipsychotics, opioids, benzodiazepines. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor — review, driving and work

  • Review: episode diary, repeat lying/standing BP, results, driving. Recurs despite lifestyle → specialist advice before any drug.
  • Ambulatory ECG by frequency: Holter (very frequent), event recorder (every 1–2 weeks), loop recorder (infrequent).
  • Fludrocortisone or midodrine: supine and standing BP, electrolytes, weight.
  • Driving: current DVLA advice for event, licence group and cause, documented with start date. Seizure suspected → stop now. No hazardous work pending investigation.

8Never

  • Diagnose vasovagal without excluding a cardiac cause — say the cardiac plan out loud.
  • Delay an urgent or emergency referral to complete the ECG or standing BP.
  • Reassure a first unexplained blackout over 45 as vasovagal without investigation.
  • Start a beta-blocker or antiepileptic before specialist review, or document “epilepsy” before confirmation.
  • Carotid sinus massage in primary care, or “come back if it happens again” as the only plan.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Chest pain that does not settle, severe breathlessness, a blackout with a major injury or without full recovery, or a sudden severe headache — call 999.”   Same day “Another blackout — especially with no warning, during exercise, or sitting or lying down — new palpitations, or any blackout causing injury: contact us the same day.”   Driving: state the DVLA rule, its start date and whether they must notify. Involve family or carers; consider safeguarding if a vulnerable adult has repeated unexplained collapse.

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
