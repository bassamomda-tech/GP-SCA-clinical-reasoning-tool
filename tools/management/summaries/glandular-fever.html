Glandular fever (infectious mononucleosis)

For: teenagers and adults (and children) with suspected EBV glandular fever in primary care. Not for: threatened airway, splenic rupture or deep-neck infection (block 0); fatigue meeting ME/CFS criteria (NICE NG206 pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG84 (2018)NICE NG12 (Apr 2026)NICE NG206 (2021)BHIVA/BASHH/BIA 2020UKHSA exclusion table
Core rule: screen the airway and spleen before you reassure, confirm with EBV serology rather than a Monospot, never give amoxicillin, and apply NG12 to any node, spleen or blood result that EBV does not explain.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Stridor, respiratory distress, cannot swallow saliva or maintain the airway, reduced consciousness → 999. Severe tonsillar enlargement, impaired swallowing, stable airway → Same-day hospital/ENT.
  • Sudden or severe left-upper-quadrant or left shoulder-tip pain, dizziness, pallor, tachycardia, hypotension or collapse (splenic rupture, even without trauma) → 999.
  • Severe systemic illness or sepsis → 999. Unable to take fluids → Same-day assessment for IV fluids. Quinsy (uvular deviation, trismus) or neck swelling → Same-day ENT.
  • Petechiae, bruising, bleeding, pallor, dark urine or jaundice → Same-day FBC, film, LFTs; same-day haematology/medicine discussion if significant thrombocytopenia, haemolysis, jaundice or abnormal film.
  • Child or young person: unexplained petechiae or hepatosplenomegaly → Immediate specialist assessment; pallor, persistent fatigue, unexplained fever, persistent infection, generalised nodes, bone pain, bruising or bleeding → offer a very urgent FBC ≤48 h; unexplained nodes or splenomegaly → consider Very urgent referral ≤48 h.
  • Adult, unexplained lymphadenopathy (or splenomegaly, non-Hodgkin) → consider Urgent suspected cancer pathway. Pallor, persistent fatigue, generalised nodes, petechiae or hepatosplenomegaly → consider Very urgent FBC ≤48 h.

1Confirm — a diagnosis earned by a test

Raises it: posterior cervical, axillary or inguinal nodes; palatal petechiae; splenomegaly. Lowers it: no cervical nodes, no fatigue.

EBV serology (local pathway) is preferred. A negative Monospot, especially in week 1 or young children, does not exclude EBV.

2Check — where confirmation alters advice

☐ FBC, differential, film if indicated  ☐ EBV serology  ☐ Offer HIV test (opt-out)  ☐ LFTs if jaundice or marked illness  ☐ Pregnancy test if relevant

Don’t miss: EBV not supported → CMV, toxoplasma, HIV (promptly if pregnant). Blasts or falling counts are not glandular fever.

3Decide — treatment is supportive

  • No routine steroids or antivirals; steroids only for airway or blood complications (specialist).
  • Antibiotic by NG84: FeverPAIN 0–1/Centor 0–2 → none; 2–3 → consider none or back-up; 4–5/Centor 3–4 → consider immediate or back-up.
  • School or work: no exclusion once well enough; no kissing or shared drinks while unwell.

4Target — spleen and sport

5Treat

EveryoneFluids, paracetamol if suitable, lozenges, rest; no alcohol while unwell or LFTs abnormal
IbuprofenOnly if hydrated and no contraindication; avoid with significant thrombocytopenia
If antibioticPhenoxymethylpenicillin 5–10 days; allergy: clarithromycin (erythromycin if pregnant). Never amoxicillin, ampicillin, co-amoxiclav
Amoxicillin rashStop it. Record as delayed rash during EBV, not “penicillin allergy”, unless urticaria, angio-oedema, wheeze, mucosal or blistering features

Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Most recover in 2–4 weeks; tiredness lasts longer. Review sooner if worse, or if not improving, nodes or spleen not reducing, abnormal LFTs, fatigue limiting, or diagnosis unconfirmed → NG12 if unexplained or atypical.
  • All four ME/CFS symptoms ≥6 weeks (adults) / ≥4 weeks (children), significant functional loss, no better explanation → suspect ME/CFS (NG206); no fixed graded exercise.

7Refer — non-urgent routes

  • Paediatrics or ME/CFS service where NG206 criteria are met (children promptly to a paediatrician).
  • Allergy clinic if penicillin label uncertain; ENT for recurrent tonsillitis after recovery; sexual health if HIV positive (local Bolton / Greater Manchester routes).

8Never

  • Give amoxicillin, ampicillin or co-amoxiclav while EBV is suspected.
  • Let a negative early Monospot, or an impalpable spleen, reassure you.
  • Code an EBV-amoxicillin rash automatically as penicillin allergy.
  • Give a vague “a while” for contact sport — write the date.
  • Let a confirmed EBV result dismiss persistent or atypical nodes, spleen or bloods.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you get sudden pain high on the left of your tummy or in the tip of your left shoulder, or feel faint — or you can’t swallow your spit, are struggling to breathe or have noisy breathing — call 999.”   Same-day “If you can’t keep drinking, or notice bruising, bleeding, tiny spots on the skin, dark urine or yellow eyes, you need to be seen the same day.”
