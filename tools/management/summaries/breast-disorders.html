Benign breast disorders

For: women and men with breast pain, a lump, nipple discharge or change, galactorrhoea or gynaecomastia. Not for: confirmed or managed breast cancer (use the breast cancer protocol) or lactational infection (use mastitis and breast abscess).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NICE QS12NICE CG164RCOG Green-top 12Local breast-unit pathways
Core rule: apply NG12 first — an unexplained breast lump at 30 and over is a suspected cancer pathway referral however benign it feels, and referral is never delayed for primary-care imaging or blood tests.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Aged 30+ with an unexplained breast lump (with or without pain), or aged 50+ with discharge, retraction or other concerning change in one nipple → Suspected cancer pathway — no imaging or bloods first.
  • Skin changes suggesting cancer at any age (peau d'orange, tethering, dimpling, ulceration), or unexplained axillary lump aged 30+ → consider Suspected cancer pathway
  • Man with a hard, eccentric or fixed mass, nipple distortion, skin change, discharge or axillary nodes → Suspected cancer pathway — do not assume gynaecomastia.
  • Unilateral, persistent, ulcerated or non-responsive nipple or areolar change (possible Paget's), or breast inflammation not settling after treatment, recurring or in a non-lactating patient → Urgent breast assessment — no repeated empirical courses.
  • Lump in pregnancy or lactation that persists or is suspicious → Urgent breast assessment, not after delivery. Prolactin markedly raised, visual-field or cranial nerve signs, significant headache → Urgent endocrine

2Check — every breast consultation

☐ Both breasts, axillae, supraclavicular nodes  ☐ Document size, mobility, fixation, skin, nipple, discharge  ☐ Pregnancy or lactation, cycle, trauma, drugs, family history

3Decide — refer or manage

  • Lump under 30 → consider non-urgent referral; urgent if hard, fixed, enlarging, skin, nipple or axillary change, or strong family history.
  • Rapidly enlarging lump or fat necrosis (mimics cancer, even after trauma), aged 30+ → suspected cancer pathway.
  • Unilateral spontaneous discharge under 50 → breast assessment by local pathway.

4Target — the goal for each condition

ConditionGoal
MastalgiaDiary over ≥2 cycles; most settle. Pain alone is not a referral criterion
Reassured, not referredExamination, reassurance and safety-net documented

5Treat — mastalgia ladder; cause-first elsewhere

Step 1Supportive bra (at night if severe) + simple analgesia
Step 2Topical NSAID (e.g. diclofenac gel) if suitable
Step 3Diary ≥2 cycles; review CHC, HRT, antidepressants
Step 4 · specialistRefractory after 3 months → breast clinic; tamoxifen or danazol specialist only
  • Evening primrose oil not routinely recommended. Doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • Galactorrhoea: pregnancy test where relevant, prolactin, TSH, drug review.
  • Gynaecomastia: drug review first. Atypical only → testosterone, LH/FSH, hCG, LFTs, renal, TFTs; examine testes.

6Monitor

  • Benign lump on triple assessment: no routine follow-up. Cyst aspirated: follow clinic advice; never repeat-aspirate in primary care.
  • Drug changed for galactorrhoea or gynaecomastia → reassess; recheck prolactin in galactorrhoea.

7Refer — urgent routes are in block 0

  • Routine breast clinic: lump under 30, no suspicious features; refractory mastalgia; gynaecomastia >12 months or distressing.
  • Urgent breast clinic: bloody aspirate, residual mass or rapidly refilling cyst; breast abscess.
  • Endocrinology: unexplained prolactin rise, or causative drug cannot change.
  • Family history: NICE CG164 criteria, local pathway.

8Never

  • Let a benign-feeling lump or wish for reassurance displace an NG12 referral.
  • Delay a suspected cancer referral for imaging or bloods; start danazol or tamoxifen in primary care.
  • Repeat antibiotics for breast inflammation, or eczema treatment for a unilateral nipple change, without reassessment.
  • Assume unilateral male enlargement is gynaecomastia; stop an essential drug without the prescriber.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Urgent “If the lump grows or becomes hard or fixed, the skin dimples, the nipple turns in or changes, discharge comes from one side on its own or is blood-stained, redness persists, or a lump in pregnancy or breastfeeding does not settle — come back straight away, even if you were reassured recently.”   Referral: “The rule is your age and finding, not my judgement. The clinic usually examines, scans and, if needed, does a needle test in one visit.”   Breast awareness: “Know what is normal for you and report change.”
