Restless legs syndrome

For: adults with restless legs in primary care, including pregnancy and renal disease. Not for: nocturnal leg cramps (use the leg cramps protocol), drug-induced akathisia, or insomnia alone (use the insomnia protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
AASM 2025IRLSSG criteriaNICE NG12BSG 2021MHRA · BNF
Core rule: check ferritin and TSAT — unexplained iron-deficiency anaemia means NG12 FIT and GI investigation, not iron and discharge; correct iron and drug triggers first, generally prefer an alpha-2-delta ligand, and never raise the dose for augmentation.

0Act now — these mean it is not simple RLS

  • Leg weakness, a sensory level, saddle numbness, or new bladder or bowel change → Same-day emergency A&E or on-call spinal team to exclude cord or cauda equina compression.
  • Unexplained iron-deficiency anaemia on work-up → offer quantitative FIT; ≥10 µg Hb/g → Suspected cancer pathway (colorectal). Negative or unreturned FIT does not exclude cancer — refer if concern persists.
  • Men and post-menopausal women with new unexplained IDA → upper and lower GI investigation; check coeliac serology. Premenopausal: menstrual loss first. Don't delay oral iron.
  • Painful, unilateral or fixed-position symptoms → not typical RLS: think radiculopathy, neuropathy or claudication; examine pulses and neurology.
  • New restlessness on a dopamine antagonist (antipsychotic, metoclopramide, prochlorperazine) → akathisia: review and stop the drug.

1Confirm — all four URGE criteria, plus one exclusion

CriterionMeans
Urge to moveCrawling, creeping or fizzing feeling
Rest worsensStarts or worsens sitting or lying
Gets better movingRelief for as long as the movement lasts
Evening / nightCircadian — the most discriminating feature
Not better explainedNot cramps, akathisia, neuropathy, venous disease or positional

Partner-reported kicks, family history, poor sleep: supportive, not required.

2Check — ferritin is the test that changes management

☐ Neurology and pulses (ABPI if claudication)  ☐ Ferritin + TSAT + FBC (morning, no iron for 24 h)  ☐ By context: CRP, U&E/eGFR, HbA1c, B12/folate, TFTs, coeliac serology, pregnancy test  ☐ Drug, caffeine, alcohol, nicotine triggers

Ferritin rises with inflammation — read with CRP; never use serum iron alone. No routine imaging or sleep study.

3Decide — iron and triggers before drugs

  • Ferritin ≤75 µg/L or TSAT <20% → consider iron (no universal ferritin target). 75–100 µg/L with significant symptoms → specialist or local-pathway advice on IV iron.
  • Aggravating drugs (sedating antihistamines, mirtazapine, SSRIs/SNRIs, dopamine antagonists) → switch or stop where possible.
  • Intermittent symptoms → non-drug measures; reserve regular medication for frequent, troublesome, sleep-disrupting symptoms.
  • Pregnancy → non-drug measures, iron per obstetric guidance, review folate; drugs generally avoided. Dialysis / renal failure → liaise with renal team.
  • Self-care: trial less evening caffeine, alcohol, nicotine; stretching, warm bath, sleep hygiene.

5Treat — persistent, troublesome symptoms

Iron firstFerrous sulfate or fumarate + vitamin C, alternate days; IV iron (local pathway) if intolerant, malabsorption or no response
Generally preferredAlpha-2-delta ligand: gabapentin or pregabalin in the evening, titrated; check renal function first
Selected patientsDopamine agonist only if alpha-2-delta unsuitable or ineffective; lowest effective dose, regular review
AvoidRegular levodopa. Opioids only via a specialist, for refractory disease
  • Gabapentinoids: renal dose adjustment; start lower if older, frail, respiratory disease, OSA, falls risk or other sedatives (MHRA: respiratory depression).
  • Dopamine agonist: discuss augmentation, impulse-control disorders and sudden sleep first. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Ferritin + TSAT ~3 months after starting iron. Still low → malabsorption, ongoing loss (revisit NG12), IV iron.
  • Every dopamine-agonist review: earlier, more intense or spreading = augmentation → don't raise the dose; recheck iron, specialist advice for gradual switch. Ask directly about gambling, shopping, hypersexuality, binge eating → reduce or withdraw.

7Refer — most RLS stays in primary care

  • Neurology: diagnostic doubt, refractory disease, augmentation needing a switch, complex dopamine-agonist use, early-onset or rapidly progressive disease.
  • Sleep medicine: suspected OSA, parasomnia, severe sleep disruption. Pregnancy, severe or refractory → obstetric, sleep or neurology.
  • New unilateral, painful or claudication features → re-examine; neurology or vascular.

8Never

  • Replace iron and discharge when iron-deficiency anaemia is unexplained.
  • Increase the dopamine-agonist dose for worsening symptoms — think augmentation.
  • A dopamine agonist as routine first-line, or regular levodopa.
  • Stop gabapentin or pregabalin abruptly; avoid abrupt dopamine-agonist withdrawal.
  • Mistake cramps, akathisia or claudication for RLS.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

A&E / 999 “New weakness in your legs, numbness around your bottom or genitals, or new problems controlling your bladder or bowels — go to A&E or call 999 now.”   Dopamine agonist: “Urges to gamble, shop or other compulsive behaviour — tell us. Sleepiness or falling asleep suddenly — do not drive or use machinery, and tell us.”   Gabapentin / pregabalin: “Drowsiness, dizziness, falls or breathing problems — tell us; don't stop suddenly.”   Augmentation: “Symptoms earlier in the day or worse on treatment — contact us; don't just take more.”
