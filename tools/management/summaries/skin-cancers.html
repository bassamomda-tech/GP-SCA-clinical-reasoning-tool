Skin cancers — melanoma, SCC and BCC

For: adults with a suspicious skin lesion in primary care. Not for: children (paediatric dermatology via the local pathway), treatment of confirmed cancer (specialist), or actinic keratosis treatment (AK protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)NICE NG14NICE NG34BAD BCC · cSCC
Core rule: score every changing pigmented lesion — 3 or more on the weighted 7-point checklist is a suspected cancer pathway referral — and never excise a possible melanoma in the surgery.

0Act now — suspected cancer pathway (appointment within 2 weeks)

  • Pigmented lesion scoring 3 or more, or dermoscopy suggests melanoma → refer Suspected cancer pathway
  • Lesion suggesting nodular melanoma (elevated, firm, growing; may be amelanotic) → consider Suspected cancer pathway
  • Lesion raising suspicion of SCC — growing keratotic or ulcerated nodule, tender, bleeding; lip, ear; transplant recipient → consider Suspected cancer pathway
  • Changing nail pigment or Hutchinson's sign, changing acral lesion, palpable regional nodes → Suspected cancer pathway

1Confirm — weighted 7-point checklist

FeaturePoints
Change in size · irregular shape · irregular colour2 each
Diameter 7 mm or more · inflammation · oozing · change in sensation1 each

Score 3 or more → refer. Strong concern despite a low score → refer.

2Check

☐ New or changing? Over how long? ☐ Bleeding, itch, non-healing ☐ Previous skin cancer, family history, immunosuppression, sunburn, sunbeds ☐ Whole skin, nails, palms, soles ☐ Regional nodes ☐ Dermoscopy if trained ☐ Images per local pathway. Don't miss: amelanotic nodular melanoma.

3Decide — which pathway

  • Suspected BCC: consider routine referral; suspected cancer pathway only if site or size makes delay unacceptable.
  • Bowen's, actinic keratosis: local pathway or treat if trained.
  • Low-suspicion pigmented lesion: dated photo and review in 8 weeks.

4Target — prevention goals

AdviceGoal
SunscreenSPF 30 or higher, 4- or 5-star UVA
Shade11am to 3pm in the UK summer; never burn; no sunbeds
Self-examinationMonthly, photograph lesions

5Treat — primary care role

1 ReferRight pathway; history, score, size, site, immune status in the letter.
2 ImagesDermoscopic and close-up per local teledermatology — never delay for them.
3 Low-risk BCCExcision or topical treatment by accredited GPs only.
4 After diagnosisNG14 follow-up, self-examination, vitamin D if advised.
  • All excised skin to histology. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Monitor

  • Reviewed lesion: 8 weeks with a dated photograph; any change → suspected cancer pathway.
  • Referral made: confirm the appointment and the result (practice safety-netting).
  • High risk (transplant, previous skin cancer, many atypical moles): whole-skin checks, low threshold.

7Refer — non-urgent

  • Most suspected BCCs Routine
  • Many atypical moles or strong family history for assessment Routine

8Never

  • Shave, curette or biopsy a lesion that could be melanoma.
  • Reassure without scoring, or without a dated review and safety-net.
  • Dismiss a pink, firm, growing nodule because it is not pigmented.
  • Delay a suspected cancer referral waiting for photographs.
  • Discard an excised specimen without histology.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

Suspected cancer pathway “I'm referring you to be seen within two weeks. Most of these are not cancer. If you haven't heard within a week, call us.”   Reassured: “If it changes in size, shape or colour, bleeds, itches or doesn't heal within a few weeks, contact us — don't wait for the review.”   Prevention: “Shade, clothing, SPF 30 with high UVA stars, no sunbeds.”

✓Safe consultation check — all five done, case safely managed
