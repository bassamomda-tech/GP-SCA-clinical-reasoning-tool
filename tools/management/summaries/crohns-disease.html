Crohn's disease — triage, monitoring and shared care

For: adults with known Crohn's disease in primary care. Not for: replacing the patient's IBD flare plan or the local IBD pathway; children (exclusive enteral nutrition, not prednisolone, is first-line induction); ulcerative colitis (use the UC protocol). Biologics and immunomodulators are specialist-led.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG129CG118NG12 (Apr 2026)BSG IBD 2025ECCO 2024
Core rule: consider abscess, perforation, obstruction and infection before any steroid — never let “it's a flare” be the whole diagnosis; steroids induce, never maintain; examine the perianal area every time.

0Act now — check first, every time

  • Peritonism or perforation, complete obstruction (colicky pain, bilious vomiting, distension, no flatus or stool), PR bleeding with tachycardia or hypotension, shock, severe sepsis, perianal abscess with sepsis → 999
  • Suspected abscess or inflammatory mass (RIF mass, fever, markedly raised CRP, non-response) → Same-day gastro / surgery — no prednisolone. Tender fluctuant perianal mass → Same-day colorectal drainage
  • Fever on a biologic or immunomodulator → urgent FBC, Same-day review. Cough, weight loss, night sweats on anti-TNF → Same-day TB team
  • Can't keep fluids down, persistent vomiting, severe pain, new weight loss >10% in 3 months, new fistula, jaundice, or flare worse or not clearly better within a few days of steroids → Same-day gastro. Painful red eye → Same-day ophthalmology

1Confirm — flare, complication, infection or functional?

The Montreal phenotype, not the symptom score, decides the treatment:

BehaviourAnswer is
B1 inflammatoryMedical therapy
B2 stricturing (obstruction risk)Dilatation or surgery — not steroids
B3 penetrating (fistula, abscess)Joint gastro, radiology, surgery
Location L1–L3 · L4 · +pL4 upper GI adds a PPI · p = perianal

Harvey-Bradshaw Index is an adjunct only — never alone to decide admission or steroids.

2Check — before calling it a flare

☐ Obs, hydration, abdomen, perianal look, weight ☐ Stool incl. C. difficile if new or worse diarrhoea ☐ FBC, CRP, ESR, albumin, U&E, LFTs, iron studies, B12/folate where relevant, vitamin D ☐ Calprotectin if stable ☐ NSAIDs, antibiotics, travel, pregnancy

Don't miss: a low calprotectin does not exclude small-bowel disease or complications.

3Decide — who gets a steroid

  • Complication suspected or infection not considered → no steroid (don't delay emergency care).
  • Uncomplicated flare → ring the IBD nurse (rescue protocol); if a steroid is used, per the patient's flare plan.
  • Lifestyle: offer smoking cessation support at every contact; dietitian if active disease with weight loss.

4Target — review rhythm after a flare

48–72 h phoneImproving? Fever, worse pain? C. difficile result?
5–7 daysNot improving → same-day specialist reassessment
1–2 weeksFace to face: FBC, CRP, weight, perianal; start taper
4–8 weeksCalprotectin, nutrition, surveillance confirmed

5Treat — induce, then spare the steroid

InducePrednisolone per flare plan; document duration and taper. Budesonide (specialist) may be considered for mild–moderate ileocaecal disease
Protect boneProlonged or repeated steroids → fracture-risk assessment; calcium/vitamin D when indicated
MaintainThiopurine (TPMT first) or methotrexate — specialist-initiated, shared care. Not oral 5-ASA
Biologic · surgeryIBD team only. Stricture, abscess, fistula → surgical opinion
  • Before any steroid: diabetes, BP, mood, bone risk; steroid card where appropriate; never stop a prolonged course abruptly. Repeated courses → urgent steroid-sparing review.
  • Avoid regular NSAIDs and opioids where possible; no loperamide or codeine if obstruction suspected. Doses: NICE/SPC.

6Monitor — per local shared care

  • Thiopurine / methotrexate: FBC, LFTs, renal function. Cytopenia or hepatotoxicity → withhold, ask specialist.
  • Anti-TNF: IGRA and hepatitis B surface antigen before; fever → same-day review.
  • Annually: B12 (terminal ileal disease), vitamin D, bone, mood, smoking. Vaccinate before immunosuppression; no live vaccine without IBD-team advice. Colonic disease → surveillance colonoscopy booked.

7Refer

  • Urgent gastro (5–7 days) flare with raised CRP or weight loss; steroid dependence; lost biologic response; perianal fistula.
  • NG12: FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g → suspected cancer pathway; unexplained anal mass or ulcer, or lymphadenopathy → consider referral. Negative FIT never overrides concern.
  • Pregnancy: plan with the IBD team; don't stop maintenance without advice.

8Never

  • Prednisolone while abscess, perforation, obstruction or sepsis is suspected.
  • Steroids to maintain remission, or for a fibrotic stricture or perianal fistula.
  • Let a “flare” explain new weight loss, anaemia or a changed symptom pattern.
  • Methotrexate in pregnancy — negative test first, counselling recorded.
  • Start, switch or stop a biologic in primary care; azathioprine without TPMT.

9Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Severe tummy pain with vomiting, a swollen tummy and no wind or stool — that could be a blockage. Call 999.”   Same day “Pain worse despite steroids, a fever, can't keep anything down, a new lump, painful swelling around your bottom, a painful red eye or blurred vision, yellow skin, or not clearly better within a few days — contact us the same day, or NHS 111 if we are closed.”   Immunosuppressed: “Any fever or infection — contact us the same day; check with us before any live vaccine.”   Smoking: “Stopping smoking is one of the most powerful treatments for Crohn's.”
