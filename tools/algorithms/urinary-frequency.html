1
Safety

Red Flags — Retention, Cord Compression, Sepsis and the Cancer Routes

Frequency is a symptom with two quite different mechanisms: passing large volumes (polyuria — diabetes, hypercalcaemia, drugs, fluid intake, nocturnal polyuria) or passing small volumes often (bladder or outlet problem). Screen for the dangerous causes first.

Saddle anaesthesia, new bladder or bowel dysfunction, bilateral leg weakness Cauda equina or spinal cord compression → 999 or same-day emergency assessment per local pathway
Acute urinary retention Painful inability to pass urine, palpable tender bladder → Same-day catheterisation / urology
Chronic retention with renal impairment Painless palpable bladder, overflow dribbling, nocturnal enuresis, rising creatinine → Same-day urology (high-pressure retention)
Fever, rigors, loin pain Pyelonephritis or urosepsis → Same-day; 999 if high-risk sepsis criteria (NICE NG253). Men with febrile UTI → think acute prostatitis
Polyuria, thirst, weight loss, ketones New diabetes with possible DKA or hyperosmolar state → Same-day (999 if drowsy, vomiting, Kussmaul breathing)
Visible haematuria aged 45 or over Unexplained without UTI, or persisting or recurring after successful UTI treatment → Suspected cancer bladder and renal suspected cancer pathway referral (NICE NG12)
Non-visible haematuria aged 60 or over With dysuria or a raised white cell count → Suspected cancer bladder suspected cancer pathway referral (NICE NG12) — see the haematuria algorithm
Hard, irregular or craggy prostate → Suspected cancer prostate suspected cancer pathway referral regardless of PSA
Persistent urgency or frequency with bloating, early satiety or pelvic pain Woman aged 40 or over → Suspected cancer CA125 and ultrasound per NICE NG12 ovarian criteria; pelvic mass → suspected cancer pathway referral
Polyuria with confusion, constipation, abdominal pain Possible hypercalcaemia → Same-day calcium; adjusted calcium above 3.5 mmol/L, or 3.0–3.5 mmol/L with symptoms → same-day admission (see the hypercalcaemia algorithm)

Chronic retention is often painless and presents as frequency, nocturia or "incontinence"; a palpable bladder with renal impairment is high-pressure retention that threatens the kidneys. New bladder symptoms with back pain or neurological symptoms must prompt questions about saddle sensation and leg weakness.

NICE NG12 (updated April 2026) uses visible haematuria at 45 or over (unexplained, or persisting after UTI treatment) and non-visible haematuria at 60 or over with dysuria or a raised white cell count as suspected cancer pathway criteria; a hard, irregular prostate needs referral regardless of the PSA; and persistent urinary urgency or frequency is one of the ovarian cancer symptoms that should prompt a CA125 in women aged 40 or over.

2
Diagnose

History — Volume, Pattern and Bother

Big or small volumes?
Large volumes each time with thirst → polyuria (diabetes mellitus, hypercalcaemia, diuretics, lithium, high fluid or caffeine intake, rarely diabetes insipidus). Small volumes with urgency → bladder or outlet cause
Storage symptoms
Urgency, frequency (more than 8 voids a day), nocturia, urgency incontinence → overactive bladder (OAB), UTI, bladder pain syndrome, bladder stone or tumour
Voiding symptoms
Hesitancy, weak or intermittent stream, straining, terminal dribble, incomplete emptying → bladder outlet obstruction (prostate in men; prolapse or urethral stricture), underactive bladder
Acute with dysuria
Dysuria, new urgency, cloudy or offensive urine, suprapubic pain → UTI (see the dysuria algorithm); in younger adults consider STIs (urethritis)
Nocturia
How many times; volumes at night; ankle swelling, breathlessness lying flat (heart failure); snoring and apnoeas (OSA); evening fluids, alcohol and caffeine; timing of diuretics
Pain
Bladder pain relieved by voiding, worse with filling, with frequency and no infection → bladder pain syndrome / interstitial cystitis; pelvic pain in women → endometriosis
Women
Vaginal bulge or dragging (prolapse), stress leakage, menopausal symptoms (genitourinary syndrome of menopause), pregnancy, previous pelvic surgery or radiotherapy
Men
Prostate symptoms; use the IPSS to quantify; erectile dysfunction; family history of prostate cancer; Black ethnicity (higher prostate cancer risk)
Drugs & intake
Diuretics, SGLT2 inhibitors, lithium, calcium channel blockers (nocturia via oedema), anticholinergics or opioids (retention), ketamine use (ulcerative cystitis); daily fluid, caffeine and alcohol intake
Neurological
MS, Parkinson's, stroke, spinal disease, diabetes (neuropathic bladder), cognitive impairment

The first question — "large or small amounts?" — divides the differential in half. A 3-day frequency-volume chart answers it objectively and also shows total intake, functional bladder capacity and the proportion of urine passed at night. In men, splitting voiding from storage symptoms decides whether an alpha-blocker or an OAB treatment is likely to help (NICE CG97).

3
Diagnose

Examination

General
BP, pulse, temperature; weight and BMI; oedema, JVP and chest if nocturia (heart failure); signs of dehydration in polyuria
Abdomen
Palpable bladder (retention), renal angle tenderness, pelvic or abdominal mass
Men
External genitalia (phimosis, meatal stenosis) and digital rectal examination — prostate size, consistency, nodules, tenderness (prostatitis)
Women
Vaginal examination with consent if prolapse, atrophy, pelvic mass or pain: prolapse (bulge on straining), vaginal atrophy, pelvic floor contraction, pelvic mass
Neurological
When suggested by history: lower-limb power, reflexes, sensation including saddle area; anal tone if cauda equina is a concern (in the emergency setting)

NICE CG97 asks for an examination of the abdomen, external genitalia and a digital rectal examination in men with LUTS; NG123 asks for a pelvic examination in women with urinary symptoms to look for prolapse, atrophy and pelvic floor function. A palpable bladder after voiding changes the whole plan.

4
Diagnose

Investigations

Everyone
Urine dipstick: nitrites and leucocytes (UTI), blood (haematuria criteria), glucose, protein. Send urine culture if UTI suspected in men, pregnancy, over 65, recurrent or treatment failure (NICE NG109)
Frequency-volume chart
3-day bladder diary: voids, volumes, intake, leakage. Nocturnal polyuria if more than about 33% of 24-hour output is passed at night in older adults; global polyuria if more than about 40 mL/kg in 24 h
Polyuria
HbA1c or glucose, U&E, adjusted calcium; lithium level if on lithium. Consider NT-proBNP if heart failure is suspected (NICE NG106)
Men
U&E if renal impairment or retention suspected. PSA: offer counselling on the benefits and limits (Prostate Cancer Risk Management Programme) and test if LUTS suggest bladder outlet obstruction from an enlarged prostate, the prostate feels abnormal, or the patient wants it after counselling; in men with LUTS, NG12 asks you to consider PSA and DRE — use NG12 age-specific thresholds (see the high PSA algorithm)
Post-void residual
Bladder scan if incomplete emptying, palpable bladder, neurological disease or before an antimuscarinic in at-risk patients
Not routinely
Cystoscopy, urodynamics or imaging before initial conservative treatment (unless red flags); urine cytology

The bladder diary is the most informative free test in this presentation (NICE NG123 and CG97): it separates high intake, global polyuria and nocturnal polyuria from OAB, which need completely different treatments. PSA testing in men with LUTS is offered after a balanced discussion of false positives and negatives; a hard or irregular prostate needs referral whatever the PSA.

5
Diagnose

Differential Diagnosis — Polyuria or Bladder?

Infection
UTI · prostatitis · urethritis
Acute dysuria, urgency, positive nitrites; fever or perineal pain in men → prostatitis
Overactive bladder
Urgency ± urge incontinence
Small volumes, normal urine, often idiopathic; worse with caffeine and alcohol
Polyuria
Diabetes · hypercalcaemia · drugs
Large volumes and thirst; diuretics, SGLT2 inhibitors, lithium (nephrogenic DI), high fluid or caffeine intake
Nocturnal polyuria
Heart failure · OSA · evening fluids
Diary: much of the output at night; oedema redistribution, CKD, older age
Men — outlet
Benign prostatic enlargement
Voiding symptoms, enlarged smooth prostate; often mixed with OAB — see male LUTS
Women — pelvic
Prolapse · atrophy · pregnancy
Bulge, incomplete emptying; vaginal dryness and urgency after menopause; early pregnancy
Bladder pain syndrome
Interstitial cystitis
Pain with filling relieved by voiding, sterile urine, often for months; consider ketamine cystitis
Must-not-miss
Retention · tumour · stone · neuro
Chronic retention, bladder carcinoma (haematuria, smoker), carcinoma in situ (irritative symptoms, sterile pyuria), bladder stone, MS or cord lesion

Persistent irritative symptoms with sterile pyuria or non-visible haematuria in a smoker should make you think of bladder carcinoma in situ, which mimics OAB. In older adults nocturia is commonly multifactorial: nocturnal polyuria, reduced bladder capacity, prostate disease and sleep disturbance all contribute, so the diary guides which factor to treat first.

6
Refer

Referral Criteria

999
Suspected cauda equina or cord compression (or same-day emergency pathway locally); high-risk sepsis
Same-day
Acute retention; chronic retention with renal impairment; pyelonephritis not manageable at home; suspected DKA or hyperosmolar state; symptomatic hypercalcaemia
Suspected cancer · NICE NG12
Visible haematuria aged 45 or over (unexplained, or after UTI treated); non-visible haematuria aged 60 or over with dysuria or raised white cell count; abnormal prostate on DRE or PSA above the age-specific threshold; pelvic mass; raised CA125 with abnormal ultrasound
Urology
Recurrent or persistent UTI in men, bladder pain syndrome not settling, suspected stone, palpable bladder after voiding, LUTS not responding to conservative and drug treatment, men wanting to discuss surgery (NICE CG97)
Urogynaecology
Symptomatic prolapse at or beyond the introitus, OAB failing conservative and two drug options, previous pelvic surgery, voiding dysfunction in women (NICE NG123)
Other
Diabetes insipidus suspected (normal glucose and calcium with dilute polyuria) → endocrinology; heart failure → NT-proBNP pathway; OSA → sleep service

Most frequency and nocturia is managed in primary care after excluding infection, polyuria and red flags. Specialist referral is for retention, suspected cancer, persistent or recurrent infection in men, refractory OAB and women with significant prolapse or voiding problems.

7
Treat

Management at This Consultation

UTI
Treat per NICE NG109
Non-pregnant women: nitrofurantoin (if eGFR 45 or more) or trimethoprim for 3 days per local resistance; men: 7 days and send culture. Doses per local antimicrobial guidance
Overactive bladder
Bladder training First-line
At least 6 weeks of scheduled voiding with gradually longer intervals; women — supervised pelvic floor training if mixed incontinence; reduce caffeine
OAB not helped by training
Antimuscarinic — mind the anticholinergic burden
Lowest-burden choice from the local formulary, for example solifenacin usual adult dose 5 mg once daily (SPC). Avoid oxybutynin immediate-release in frail older women (NICE NG123). Check post-void residual and cognitive status; review at 4 weeks
Antimuscarinic unsuitable, ineffective or not tolerated
Mirabegron (or vibegron)
Mirabegron usual adult dose 50 mg once daily; 25 mg in severe renal impairment (eGFR 15–29) or moderate hepatic impairment (SPC, NICE TA290). Check BP before and during — contraindicated in severe uncontrolled hypertension, 180/110 mmHg or more (MHRA)
PolyuriaTreat the cause — new diabetes (start the diabetes pathway same day if symptomatic hyperglycaemia), hypercalcaemia work-up, review diuretic timing (take in the morning), lithium review with the mental health team
NocturiaDiary-led — reduce evening fluid, caffeine and alcohol; raise legs in the afternoon if oedema; treat heart failure or OSA. Desmopressin for nocturnal polyuria only after excluding other causes and with sodium checks (hyponatraemia risk — extra caution and sodium monitoring at 65 or over, per MHRA and the SPC; often on specialist advice)
Men — voidingModerate to severe voiding symptoms — offer an alpha-blocker, for example tamsulosin modified-release usual adult dose 400 micrograms once daily (SPC); warn about postural hypotension and floppy iris syndrome before cataract surgery. 5-alpha reductase inhibitor if prostate is large or PSA raised and high progression risk — see male LUTS
Women — atrophyVaginal oestrogen for OAB with vaginal atrophy in postmenopausal women (NICE NG123); prolapse — pelvic floor training, pessary or referral
Bladder painBladder pain syndrome — exclude infection and carcinoma in situ; diet trial, analgesia, refer if persistent

Next steps: see Female urinary incontinence · Male LUTS · Benign prostate enlargement · UTI in women · UTI in men · Recurrent UTI · Pelvic organ prolapse · Prostatitis

Bladder training is first-line for OAB in women and men and works for many without medication (NICE NG123, CG97). Antimuscarinics add a cumulative anticholinergic load that is linked with cognitive impairment, falls and constipation in older people; NICE NG123 asks clinicians to consider the anticholinergic burden and not to offer immediate-release oxybutynin to frail older women. Mirabegron avoids the anticholinergic effect but can raise BP, hence the MHRA advice to measure it before and during treatment.

Nocturia is often nocturnal polyuria, which an antimuscarinic will not fix; desmopressin causes hyponatraemia, particularly in older people.

8
Lifestyle

Conservative Measures

Fluids Aim for about 1.5–2 L a day unless advised otherwise; not too little (concentrated urine irritates) and not too much.
Caffeine & alcohol Reduce or stop tea, coffee, cola, energy drinks and alcohol, especially in the evening.
Bladder diary Repeat the 3-day chart after changes — it shows progress and keeps motivation.
Weight Weight loss if BMI over 30 improves urgency and leakage.
Constipation Treat it — a loaded rectum worsens urgency and emptying.
Men Double voiding, urethral milking for post-micturition dribble, evening fluid timing.
Smoking Stop — reduces bladder cancer risk and cough-related leakage.
Night-time safety Night light, clear route to the toilet, commode if needed — nocturia is a falls risk.

Conservative measures are first-line in both NICE NG123 and CG97 and should be tried properly, with a review, before drug treatment unless symptoms are severe. Many people restrict fluids to control frequency, which concentrates the urine and can worsen urgency.

9
Safety

Follow-Up and Safety-Netting

Safety-net 999
Numbness between the legs or around the back passage, new weakness of the legs, or loss of bladder or bowel control
Safety-net same-day
Unable to pass urine, fever with loin pain or shivering, blood in the urine, extreme thirst with weight loss or vomiting, confusion
4 weeks
Review an antimuscarinic or mirabegron for benefit and side effects (dry mouth, constipation, confusion; BP on mirabegron); alpha-blocker response (NICE CG97 suggests 4–6 weeks)
6 weeks to 3 months
Bladder training review with a repeat diary; switch drug or refer if inadequate
Results
Act on glucose, calcium, U&E, PSA and culture; repeat dipstick after UTI treatment if haematuria was present

Antimuscarinics and alpha-blockers both show their effect within a month, so an early review finds non-responders and side effects. Haematuria that persists after a treated UTI is one of the NG12 triggers and is easily lost if the dipstick is not repeated.
