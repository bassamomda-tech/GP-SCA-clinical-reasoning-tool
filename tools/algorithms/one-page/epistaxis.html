Epistaxis (nosebleeds)

For: adults and children with a nosebleed in primary care. Not for: packing, pledgets, posterior bleeds, tranexamic acid or anticoagulant reversal (ENT/emergency protocols). Child under 2 → block 2.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BRS consensus 2017ENT UK 2023NICE NG12NICE NG24MHRA DOACs
Core rule: most nosebleeds are anterior and stop with correct pressure. But one-sided bleeding with blockage needs urgent ENT to exclude a tumour, and the rare deaths are mostly in elderly anticoagulated patients with heavy or posterior bleeds.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • Massive bleeding or haemodynamic compromise (hypotension, tachycardia, pallor, dizziness); airway compromise or aspiration risk → 999
  • Major trauma or suspected skull-base/facial fracture → 999
  • Uncontrolled despite correct pressure and local measures, suspected posterior bleed still bleeding, or severe bleeding on significant anticoagulation → 999 / A&E
  • While waiting: sit forward, pinch the soft part, spit not swallow. Record the anticoagulant and last DOAC dose time. GPs do not give vitamin K or PCC.

1Triage — after 10–15 min of correct, uninterrupted pressure

SituationAction
StoppedAssess (block 3); find the source
Still bleedingReassess urgently. Active after further pressure and local measures → Same-day ENT / A&E
Transfer earlierCompromise, anticoagulated with significant bleeding, blood in the airway/pharynx, frailty or major comorbidity, or unable to assess safely
Posterior signsBlood entering the pharynx, both nostrils, no visible anterior point (none diagnostic alone) → A&E

2Screen — one row is enough

  • On warfarin, DOAC or antiplatelet, not stopping → Same-day. INR if on warfarin; INR does not assess DOACs.
  • Persistent one-sided obstruction, blood-stained discharge or recurrent one-sided bleeds; facial swelling, anosmia, cranial-nerve signs, nasal mass → Urgent ENT (local head and neck pathway). No biopsy or repeated cautery.
  • Adolescent male, one-sided obstruction + profuse bleeding (angiofibroma) → Urgent imaging / ENT; no biopsy.
  • Bruising, petechiae, bleeding gums, heavy periods → FBC + clotting ≤48 h (consider in adults, offer in children). Child, unexplained petechiae → Immediate specialist
  • Child under 2 years → Same-day paediatrics: bleeding disorder or non-accidental injury; follow local safeguarding procedures.
  • Recurrent bleeds from both nostrils → Urgent ENT (HHT, systemic cause).

3Assess — once blocks 0–2 are answered

☐ Pulse, BP, postural BP if volume depleted  ☐ Anterior rhinoscopy (speculum + headlight): Little's area, perforation, crusting  ☐ Oropharynx for blood  ☐ Telangiectasia (lips, tongue, fingers), bruising, petechiae, pallor

Bloods only if recurrent or severe, bleeding elsewhere, anticoagulated or coagulopathy suspected; FBC + iron if recurrent. Raised BP → repeat after haemostasis; do not lower it rapidly.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Anterior (Little's area) — commonest; visible one-sided bleed that stops with pressure → primary care.
  • Posterior — persists despite anterior measures, blood in the pharynx → A&E (packing or embolisation).
  • Secondary — anticoagulant, bleeding disorder, liver disease → treat the cause plus local measures.
  • HHT — recurrent bleeds from childhood + telangiectasia and/or family history → HHT specialist centre.
  • Uncertain / no source seen — stopped or posterior; say so → ENT nasendoscopy (still bleeding → same-day).

5Treat today — only these

  • First aid: sit forward, pinch the soft part (not the bridge) 10–15 min, mouth-breathe, spit out blood; do not release to check.
  • Silver nitrate cautery only for a discrete visible anterior point, by a trained, equipped clinician, after co-phenylcaine. Never both sides of the septum at once (4–6 weeks apart); do not force in young children.
  • Naseptin four times daily for 10 days — check peanut/soya allergy — or petroleum jelly/saline. Doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • Anticoagulant/antiplatelet: do not routinely stop for a minor, controlled bleed; discuss with the prescriber if clinically significant.

6Refer — bleeding settled (still bleeding → same-day)

  • Recurrent despite cautery or Naseptin, or no anterior source seen → Urgent ENT (nasendoscopy).
  • Suspected HHT → HHT specialist centre; check FBC and iron status.
  • Prolonged APTT, or bleeding history suggestive even with normal PT/APTT, or family history → haematology.
  • Septal perforation enlarging or symptomatic → ENT.

7Never reassure by

  • A bleed that has stopped, if an adult has one-sided blockage or discharge.
  • A normal FBC or PT/APTT when the history suggests a bleeding disorder.
  • A normal INR in a patient taking a DOAC.
  • "Nosebleeds are common in children" — not under 2 years.
  • High BP as the explanation for the bleed.
  • A "refractory" bleed before checking the first-aid technique — but never delay A&E for ongoing bleeding.

8Safety-net — teach first aid, say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for very heavy bleeding (soaking towels, clots the size of a fist), feeling faint or collapsing, chest pain, or choking on blood.” A&E “Lasting over 10–15 minutes despite pinching correctly — go to A&E straight away.” Same day: “Contact us today if bleeds get more frequent or heavier, bleed after cautery, new one-sided blockage or facial pain, new bruising or spots, or you become pale and tired.”

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
