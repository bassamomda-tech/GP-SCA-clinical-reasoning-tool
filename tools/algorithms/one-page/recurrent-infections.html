Recurrent infections in adults

For: adults in primary care with repeated or unusual infections. Not for: children (paediatric pathways); the acute management of a single infection (see the infection protocols).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review October 2027
NICE NG253NG12BHIVA 2020Green Book ch.7PVL-SA
Core rule: treat today's infection safely, then look for the secondary cause — diabetes, HIV, blood cancer, drugs, absent spleen — before blaming bad luck; FBC, film, immunoglobulins, electrophoresis, HbA1c and HIV test.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Sepsis high-risk features — new confusion, systolic ≤90, HR >130, RR ≥25, SpO₂ <92%, mottled skin, non-blanching rash → 999
  • Necrotising infection (pain out of proportion), meningism, spinal abscess (back pain + fever + neurology) → 999

2Screen — serious causes

  • Neutropenic sepsis — unwell within 30 days of anticancer treatment or on neutropenia-causing drugs → Same-day acute oncology; do not wait for FBC.
  • No spleen + fever → standby antibiotic now, Same-day hospital.
  • Bruising, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, night sweats → very urgent FBC (48 hours); film with blasts → Same-day haematology.
  • Paraprotein / abnormal free light chains → Suspected cancer haematology.
  • Pneumocystis, oral candida + weight loss → Same-day HIV test and advice.

3Assess

☐ Infection count, sites, organisms, admissions  ☐ Drugs: steroids, DMARDs, biologics, chemo  ☐ FBC + film, HbA1c, U&E, LFTs/albumin, CRP  ☐ IgG/IgA/IgM + electrophoresis  ☐ HIV test  ☐ Cultures before antibiotics

Boils: ask the lab for PVL testing on pus. Chest: X-ray; HRCT via respiratory.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Chest — COPD, bronchiectasis, aspiration, same-lobe pneumonia (cancer), antibody deficiency.
  • UTI — menopause, emptying, stones, diabetes; men → structural.
  • Boils — S. aureus carriage, PVL (clusters, young, necrotic), diabetes, hidradenitis.
  • Thrush — diabetes, SGLT2i, antibiotics, steroids, HIV.
  • Systemic — diabetes, HIV, CLL/myeloma/lymphoma, drugs, asplenia, CVID.
  • Uncertain — infection diary with microbiology, review in 3 months, immunology advice.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Current infection: culture first; local antimicrobial guide.
  • Asplenia: Green Book vaccines; standby amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily (clarithromycin if allergic); alert card.
  • Boils: drain; PVL or recurrent S. aureus → decolonise per health protection advice (chlorhexidine 4% wash + nasal mupirocin 2%, 5 days), household together.
  • Suspected immunodeficiency: inactivated vaccines; avoid live vaccines until immune status known.

6Refer routinely and review

  • Immunology: warning signs + low immunoglobulins, or no secondary cause.
  • HIV clinic: reactive test — within 2 weeks (BHIVA).
  • Respiratory: suspected bronchiectasis. Urology: men with recurrent UTI.

7Never reassure by

  • "Just unlucky" without counting serious infections.
  • A normal white count — check the film and immunoglobulins.
  • Not offering an HIV test.
  • A normal CRP in an immunosuppressed patient.
  • Repeated antibiotics for boils without drainage or PVL testing.
  • Treating recurrent chest infections without a chest X-ray.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “High temperature with shivering, feeling very unwell, confused or breathless, or a rash that doesn't fade when pressed — call 999.” Same-day “No spleen and a fever — start your emergency antibiotics and get seen the same day.”

Not clearly better in 48 hours → contact us. Remote consultation with any 999 feature → convert to a 999 call.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented

No sepsis or block 2 feature Infections counted with microbiology FBC/film, Ig, electrophoresis, HbA1c done HIV test offered; drugs reviewed Vaccines, referral and safety-net recorded
