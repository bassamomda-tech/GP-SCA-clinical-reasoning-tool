Incidental renal mass

For: adults with a renal mass or cyst found on imaging done for another reason, and GP aftercare after kidney cancer treatment. Not for: children with an abdominal mass (block 2; abdominal mass algorithm); choosing, stopping or changing anticancer treatment (oncology team).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG256NG12Bosniak v2019NICE NG234GM urology pathway
Core rule: a solid enhancing or indeterminate mass, or a Bosniak III–IV cyst, needs prompt urology/uro-oncology referral per the local pathway — an imaging finding is not itself an NG12 criterion, so never wait for haematuria. Close the loop on every incidental finding: a named team and booked imaging.

0Stop — call 999 now — emergency admission

  • Known or suspected angiomyolipoma + sudden severe flank or abdominal pain, pallor, faintness or shock — acute haemorrhage (Wunderlich) → 999 emergency admission.
  • Adjusted calcium >3.5 mmol/L, or hypercalcaemia at any level with confusion, AKI or persistent vomiting → 999
  • While waiting: no imaging or blood tests in primary care first.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Unexplained visible haematuria aged ≥45, or persisting or recurring after a treated UTI → Suspected cancer pathway (NG12).
  • New bilateral leg oedema (IVC tumour thrombus) or severe symptoms → Same-day hospital. Renal vein/IVC involvement on imaging or suspected metastases → Urgent specialist.
  • Bone pain + hypercalcaemia (metastatic RCC?) → Urgent urology/uro-oncology; calcium 3.0–3.5 → Same-day assessment. Myeloma suspected → local haematology pathway.
  • New hypertension + hypokalaemia, or adrenal-looking lesion → endocrinology via the adrenal incidentaloma pathway (metanephrines, aldosterone–renin ratio, 1 mg dexamethasone test).
  • Child with a palpable abdominal mass, enlarged organ or unexplained visible haematuria → consider very urgent referral ≤48 h (NG12); Same-day if unwell. Do not palpate repeatedly.
  • After treatment: new neurological symptoms or spinal pain suggesting metastases → Same-day acute oncology/MSCC pathway (NG234). On immunotherapy, new diarrhoea, breathlessness, jaundice, confusion, hypotension or polyuria → Same-day oncology.

3Assess

☐ Report: solid/cystic, enhancement, fat, Bosniak  ☐ Haematuria, flank pain, weight loss, smoking, family history  ☐ Flank mass, BP, left varicocoele, leg oedema  ☐ Urinalysis (non-visible = 1+ or more); FBC, U&E/eGFR, calcium; LFTs if indicated; ACR if eGFR <60 or proteinuria

Not in primary care: urine cytology, renal biopsy, routine bone scan. Ultrasound cannot assign Bosniak → specialist CT or MRI.

4Classify — specialist-led; GP acts on the category

  • Solid enhancing mass (single, bilateral or multifocal) = RCC vs oncocytoma vs lipid-poor AML. NG256: <2 cm consider active surveillance; ≥2 cm surgery, or surveillance/ablation if unsuitable or declined.
  • Fat on CT = angiomyolipoma, benign; with tuberous sclerosis → specialist TSC centre.
  • Uncertain or indeterminate → treat as solid enhancing; never leave unfollowed.

5Treat today — reduce risk, protect the kidneys

  • Smoking cessation → NHS Stop Smoking Service; weight management (NG246); BP control.
  • Kidneys: avoid unnecessary or prolonged NSAIDs; an eGFR check must not delay cancer imaging.
  • After nephrectomy: creatinine/eGFR; eGFR <60 → offer ACR and manage per CKD guidance; ACE inhibitor/ARB only where indicated (albuminuria).
  • On TKI or immunotherapy: follow the oncology plan; treat BP by standard principles; never change anticancer therapy or start steroids without oncology. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer routinely — after blocks 0–2

  • Age ≤46, multiple tumours, 1st/2nd-degree relative with renal cancer, syndromic features → clinical genetics + urology (NG256). Suspected HLRCC: no biopsy.
  • AML painful, previous bleed (now stable), high-risk vascular features or growth → urology ≤2 weeks. No repeat ultrasound without a specialist plan.

7Never reassure by — close the loop on every incidental finding

  • “Probably a cyst” on ultrasound — it cannot assign a Bosniak category.
  • No haematuria and a well patient — most RCC are now found incidentally.
  • Not meeting NG12 — a solid enhancing mass still needs prompt referral.
  • “Benign AML” with sudden flank pain and faintness — a bleed is 999.
  • A surveillance plan nobody booked — confirm scan dates and the named team.
  • A growing lesion or new enhancement on surveillance — prompt referral.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for sudden severe pain in your side or tummy with feeling faint or collapse.” Same-day “On immunotherapy, new diarrhoea, breathlessness or yellow skin — or new weakness, numbness or bladder or bowel problems — contact the oncology team today.” Same week “Contact us or your urology team this week for blood in your urine, new pain or a lump in your side, or weight loss you cannot explain.”
