Itchy eye

For: adults and children with itchy eyes, including neonates with discharge, in hours or OOH. Not for: a painful red eye without itch (use the red eye algorithm) or an eye injury (foreign body algorithm); ongoing care is in the allergic conjunctivitis, blepharitis and dry eye protocols.

Core rule: itch usually means allergy — but pain, photophobia, reduced vision or a painful red eye in a contact-lens wearer means it is not simple allergy: same-day ophthalmology, and never start steroid drops.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Acute angle-closure glaucoma — severe pain, haloes, cloudy cornea, nausea/vomiting, headache, visual disturbance (also in a patient on steroid drops) → 999 / emergency ophthalmology.
  • Severe orbital or facial infection with systemic illness, neurological symptoms or rapidly progressive swelling → 999
  • Anaphylaxis with airway or systemic compromise → 999
  • Do not palpate the globe for pressure, give IV acetazolamide from primary care, or delay transfer.

2Screen — same-day ophthalmology

  • Reduced or changed acuity, moderate/severe pain, photophobia, ciliary flush (uveitis) → Same-day
  • Contact-lens wearer with painful red eye → lens out now, no patch, no steroid → Same-day emergency. Corneal haze, opacity, ulcer or staining → Same-day
  • Dendritic staining (herpes simplex), V1 rash or Hutchinson's sign (zoster), new diplopia or restricted movements → Same-day
  • Neonate with purulent discharge → Same-day paediatrics/ophthalmology; gonococcal and chlamydial swabs.
  • VKC or AKC with photophobia, reduced vision or corneal staining → Same-day; otherwise VKC → Within 1 week

3Assess

☐ Acuity each eye  ☐ Redness pattern, discharge  ☐ Lid margins  ☐ Cornea + fluorescein  ☐ Pupils, eye movements  ☐ Evert upper lid: child with severe chronic itch, lens wearer with lens intolerance  ☐ Lenses, drops, cosmetics, atopy

No tests for typical seasonal allergy; specific IgE only if it changes management; swab if purulent, neonatal or chronic follicular (chlamydia PCR, sexual history). No slit lamp and any doubt → refer.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Allergic conjunctivitis — bilateral itch, watery, seasonal or perennial, atopy, rhinitis → block 5.
  • Blepharitis / meibomian gland dysfunction — lid-margin itch, morning crusting → lid hygiene.
  • Dry eye — burning, grit, blur clearing on blinking → lubricants.
  • Contact dermatoconjunctivitis — periorbital eczema from drop preservative (BAK) or cosmetics → stop agent, preservative-free drops.
  • Giant papillary conjunctivitis — lens wearer, itch on removal, papillae → lenses out, optometry.
  • Uncertain — unilateral, chronic follicular or not responding → reconsider and refer.

5Treat today — only these

  • Allergic: topical antihistamine or mast-cell stabiliser per local formulary (sodium cromoglicate, ketotifen, azelastine, olopatadine); mast-cell stabilisers before the season. Cold compresses, no rubbing, no lenses while active. Loratadine 10 mg or cetirizine 10 mg OD if rhinitis.
  • Blepharitis: warm compress → lid massage → lid scrub, long term. Antibiotic ointment may be considered for selected anterior disease; doxycycline usually after specialist advice (not in pregnancy, breastfeeding or under 12).
  • Dry eye: lubricants, preservative-free if frequent use or ocular-surface disease; ointment at night if severe.
  • No topical steroid from primary care; do not renew ophthalmology steroids without review. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer non-urgently

  • Within 1 week ophthalmology: persistent AKC; GPC not settling; follicular conjunctivitis not settling after 4 weeks; recurrent severe allergy needing steroid.
  • Routine optometry/ophthalmology: dry eye or blepharitis despite an adequate trial. Minor eye conditions service (same or next day) for mild–moderate cases without red flags.
  • Allergy clinic: rhinoconjunctivitis uncontrolled on antihistamine + nasal steroid, occupational allergy, immunotherapy. Sexual health: suspected chlamydial conjunctivitis.

7Never reassure by

  • "It itches" when there is pain, photophobia or reduced vision.
  • A contact-lens wearer's painful red eye labelled allergy or conjunctivitis.
  • No giant papillae when the upper lid was not everted.
  • A cornea that looks clear by direct light alone.
  • An absent Hutchinson's sign — it does not exclude eye involvement.
  • Failed antihistamines met with more antihistamine — think dry eye, preservative allergy, chlamydia, VKC.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Severe eye pain with haloes, headache or vomiting — call 999.” Same day “If the eye becomes painful, light hurts, your vision changes, or a lens-wearing eye turns red and painful (take the lens out), get same-day eye assessment.” Same week review if not controlled after 2–4 weeks of treatment, and reconsider the diagnosis.
