Medically unexplained (functional) symptoms

For: persistent physical symptoms not adequately explained by structural disease: FND, chronic primary pain, fibromyalgia, IBS, ME/CFS, health anxiety. Not for: a new symptom not yet assessed (use that symptom's algorithm first). Children: think of fabricated or induced illness (block 2).

NICE NG193NG206CG61NG252RCP fibromyalgia 2022
Core rule: a functional diagnosis is positive, not "nothing wrong" — validate, explain, one targeted screen. But a functional label never explains a new objective sign, weight loss or night sweats: assess these as a new presentation.

0Stop — act now — the functional label does not change these

  • Sudden-onset focal neurological deficit → 999 stroke pathway, even with known FND.
  • Suicidal thoughts with immediate danger → 999. Active ideation without immediate danger → Same-day Greater Manchester urgent mental-health / crisis pathway.
  • Child in immediate danger from fabricated or induced illness → children's social care or police 999 now.

1Triage — what has changed since the last assessment?

ChangeAction
Danger nowBlock 0 → 999
New objective signNew neurological sign, unable to eat, drink or mobilise, acute psychiatric episode → Same-day
New systemic featureUnexplained weight loss, night sweats, new pattern → investigate as a new presentation
Stable, nothing newNo new tests; document why. Planned review as agreed.

2Screen — do not miss organic disease or risk

  • New or progressive UMN, cerebellar or cranial nerve signs → Same-day acute medicine or neurology. FND and organic disease coexist.
  • Weight loss and anorexia, night sweats (cancer, IBD, adrenal insufficiency) → investigate, whatever the label.
  • First multi-system presentation in an older adult → lower threshold (paraneoplastic, autoimmune encephalitis, neurodegeneration).
  • Rapid loss of mobility, eating, drinking or self-care → Same-day assessment, then MDT review.
  • Depression, self-harm, hopelessness → ask directly: thoughts, intent, plans, means, protective factors. PHQ-9 item 9 is only a prompt.
  • Child, repeated unexplained symptoms, parent driving investigation → Same-day safeguarding lead and paediatric safeguarding referral.

3Assess — once, properly

☐ Full relevant examination at first presentation; document it  ☐ FND positive signs: Hoover's sign, give-way weakness, non-dermatomal sensory loss  ☐ PHQ-9 / GAD-7 if mood or anxiety suspected  ☐ One symptom-directed screen: FBC, ferritin, CRP/ESR, U&E, LFTs, calcium, TFTs, HbA1c, B12/folate, coeliac serology as the pattern fits

Not: tests "to reassure", repeats of normal tests, parallel specialty referrals, one MRI for every neurological symptom.

4Classify — the most specific positive label

  • FND — positive, internally inconsistent signs → explain the signs; FND rehabilitation (physio, OT, SLT, psychology) (NG252).
  • Chronic primary pain / fibromyalgia → supervised group exercise, staying active; consider ACT or CBT (NG193).
  • IBS → diet, soluble fibre, symptom-targeted drugs (CG61). Rectal bleeding, iron deficiency, weight loss, nocturnal symptoms, mass → reassess.
  • ME/CFS → flexible energy management; ME/CFS specialist team (NG206).
  • Health anxiety → CBT via NHS Talking Therapies; agree no repeat tests and planned, not crisis-driven, appointments.
  • Uncertain — the work-up may be incomplete. Say so; agree what would trigger re-investigation.

5Treat today — the consultation is the treatment

  • Validate and explain: "Your symptoms are real"; a functional mechanism, never "all in your head", "stress" or "nothing wrong".
  • Written co-produced plan: functional goals, one screen and no repeats, review schedule, re-investigation criteria.
  • Chronic primary pain, aged 18+: consider an antidepressant (amitriptyline, citalopram, duloxetine, fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline); start low; review at 4–6 weeks, stop if ineffective.
  • Do not start gabapentinoids, opioids, NSAIDs, paracetamol or benzodiazepines for chronic primary pain. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • Anxiety, depression, health anxiety, chronic pain → NHS Talking Therapies (self-referral possible).
  • FND → neurology and FND rehabilitation. ME/CFS → specialist team. Fibromyalgia → rheumatology or pain only if uncertain or complex; IBS → gastroenterology if atypical.
  • Severe (housebound, job loss, frequent attendance) or psychiatric comorbidity → functional symptoms MDT / liaison psychiatry.

7Never reassure by

  • An existing FND or "MUS" label when there is a new objective sign.
  • Normal tests alone ("all normal") with no functional explanation.
  • Repeating normal tests to settle anxiety.
  • An old screen when weight loss or night sweats are new.
  • Assuming a functional diagnosis lowers suicide risk.
  • Graded exercise or fixed activity increases in ME/CFS.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for sudden weakness, numbness, a drooping face or loss of speech, or if you feel you may act on thoughts of ending your life.” Same-day “Contact us today for new weakness or unsteadiness, or if you cannot eat, drink or get about.” Earlier review: “Book an earlier appointment, not the next planned one, for weight loss you can't explain, night sweats or a clear change in your symptoms.”

Follow-up: planned reviews at agreed intervals by severity, risk and preference, not crisis-driven; 4–6 weeks after starting an antidepressant or therapy referral.
