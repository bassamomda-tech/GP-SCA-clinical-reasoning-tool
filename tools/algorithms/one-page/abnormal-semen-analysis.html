Abnormal semen analysis in primary care

For: men with an abnormal semen analysis, as part of a couple's fertility assessment. Not for: acute testicular pain or a lump alone (testicular pain / lump algorithms); partner's work-up (infertility protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG257 (2026)WHO manual 2021NICE NG12 (April 2026)EAU 2026British Andrology Society
Core rule: one abnormal result is not a diagnosis — repeat it, examine the testes, investigate both partners in parallel; but azoospermia or a testicular lump never waits for the repeat, and never start testosterone in a man trying to conceive.

0Act now — before interpreting any count

  • Sudden severe testicular pain, pain with vomiting or abdominal pain, or suspected torsion → 999 or A&E immediately; do not drive.
  • Unexplained intratesticular mass, or non-painful enlargement or change in shape or texture (any age) → consider Suspected cancer urology referral (NG12) and urgent direct-access ultrasound; do not wait for tumour markers or the repeat sample.

5th centiles, not fertility cut-offs. Abnormal → repeat ideally at 3 months (as soon as possible if azoospermia or severe oligozoospermia).

2Screen — prompt andrology / reproductive medicine

  • Azoospermia, <1 million/mL, or prior chemo- / radiotherapy needing urgent fertility preservation → Prompt; do not wait for the repeat.
  • Absent vas (CBAVD) → andrology + CFTR testing; partner testing, counselling via genetics / fertility.
  • Small testes + raised FSH, or Klinefelter features (gynaecomastia, tall) → andrology / genetics; karyotype; no testosterone.
  • Low testosterone with low or normal LH/FSH, galactorrhoea, headache or visual symptoms → prolactin; pituitary failure → Prompt endocrine / andrology.
  • Varicocele: sudden, non-reducible, isolated right-sided or not decompressing supine → scrotal + renal / retroperitoneal ultrasound; discuss with urology.

3Assess

☐ Cryptorchidism, torsion, mumps, epididymo-orchitis, surgery, chemotherapy, CF family history, sexual symptoms  ☐ Ask directly: anabolic steroids, testosterone, sulfasalazine, cannabis, alcohol, heat  ☐ After two abnormal analyses (sooner if indicated): examine testes, epididymides, vasa, varicocele (standing, Valsalva), sexual characteristics  ☐ Consider morning testosterone + LH/FSH (repeat testosterone if low)  ☐ Idiopathic azoospermia: karyotype + Y microdeletion (Y also if <1 million/mL; consider karyotype if persistent <5 million/mL); CFTR if suspected obstruction or vasal abnormality

Not in primary care: sperm DNA fragmentation, routine scrotal ultrasound or cortisol, karyotype for mild isolated abnormality, MRI for mildly raised prolactin.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Hypogonadotrophic — low FSH, LH and testosterone: Kallmann (anosmia), prolactinoma, anabolic steroids, obesity → specialist gonadotrophin therapy, not testosterone.
  • Testicular — raised FSH, small testes: Klinefelter, cryptorchidism, chemotherapy → andrology.
  • Obstructive — normal FSH and testes (suggestive, not proof): CBAVD, post-infective, ejaculatory duct (low volume / pH) → andrology.
  • Retrograde ejaculation — diabetes, pelvic surgery, tamsulosin → post-ejaculatory urine.
  • Mild, borderline abnormality — lifestyle, medicine review, repeat in 3 months; normal → reassure; abnormal → refer.
  • Uncertain — say so; refer the couple.

5Treat today — only these

  • Stop anabolic steroids and exogenous testosterone if fertility is desired; review sulfasalazine and other culprit medicines.
  • Lifestyle: stop smoking (NHS Stop Smoking Service), cannabis, recreational drugs; alcohol within 14 units/week; weight if BMI ≥30; avoid excessive scrotal heat (evidence limited).
  • No supplements as standard treatment; folic acid for the person who may become pregnant, not routinely for men. Clomifene or gonadotrophins for idiopathic impaired semen: only in a clinical trial; no androgens.
  • Support both partners (Relate, Fertility Network UK).

6Refer routinely

  • Abnormal result confirmed on repeat → refer the couple to reproductive medicine; no further 6–12 months' wait.
  • At presentation if the person trying to conceive is 36 or over, or either partner has a known or predisposing cause; otherwise after 12 months of regular unprotected intercourse.
  • Palpable varicocele + abnormal semen analysis, no better explanation → specialist to consider treatment; not subclinical ones; promise nothing.
  • Karyotype abnormality, Y microdeletion, CFTR variant or CBAVD → clinical genetics; counselling before assisted conception. NHS IVF access follows the Greater Manchester ICB policy.

7Never reassure by

  • A value above the reference limit — it does not guarantee fertility.
  • Normal tumour markers when there is a testicular lump.
  • Waiting for the repeat, or a further year, before referring azoospermia.
  • Treating low testosterone with testosterone in a man trying to conceive.
  • A history without a direct question about anabolic steroids.
  • Investigating one partner first — both run in parallel.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden severe pain in a testicle, or pain with vomiting or tummy pain: call 999 or go to A&E now — don't drive yourself.” Urgent “A new lump, swelling, or change in the shape or feel of a testicle: book an urgent GP appointment or call 111 — don't wait for the fertility review.” Review: book the repeat sample; agree who reviews the result and when.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
