Haematuria in adults — visible and non-visible

For: adults with visible haematuria (VH) or dipstick non-visible haematuria (NVH) in primary care. Not for: children (use the haematuria in children algorithm).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NICE NG203NICE NG109NICE NG118BAUS/Renal Association
Core rule: never let a UTI, an anticoagulant or vaginal atrophy explain haematuria away — recheck the dipstick after treating infection, and refer visible haematuria aged ≥45 (unexplained, or persisting/recurring after a treated UTI) on the suspected cancer pathway.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Haemodynamic instability — tachycardia, hypotension, pallor with heavy VH, or urosepsis with shock → 999 ED. Do not transfer an unstable patient independently.
  • Clot retention — VH with inability to pass urine or bladder distension → Same-day emergency/urology assessment; 999 if unstable, collapsing or major ongoing haemorrhage.
  • Trauma + haematuria (renal laceration, urethral injury) → Same-day / 999 for imaging.

1Triage — NICE NG12 thresholds, applied precisely

FindingAction
VH, aged ≥45Unexplained (no UTI), or persisting/recurring after a treated UTI → Urgent suspected cancer pathway (urology)
NVH, aged ≥60Unexplained, with dysuria or raised blood WCC → Urgent suspected cancer pathway
Aged ≥60, UTIsRecurrent or persistent unexplained UTI → consider Non-urgent urology
VH, under 45Urological assessment, Routine or Urgent by clinical picture
Persistent NVH≥1+ on 2 of 3 dipsticks, no criteria above → eGFR, ACR, BP; urology or renal by findings

2Screen — serious causes

  • Flank pain + fever/rigors (urosepsis, obstructed stone, pyelonephritis) → Same-day ED.
  • Suspected nephritic or nephrotic syndrome → Same-day nephrology discussion.
  • Haematuria + ACR ≥30 mg/mmol, ACR ≥70, AKI, rapidly falling eGFR, RBC casts → Urgent nephrology.
  • Weight loss + loin mass, mass on imaging, PSA above age threshold or malignant-feeling prostate → Urgent suspected cancer pathway.
  • Women ≥55, VH + low Hb, thrombocytosis or high glucose → also consider direct access ultrasound (endometrial cancer).
  • Child with unexplained VH (Wilms') → consider ≤48 h very urgent referral.

3Assess

☐ VH or NVH: fresh strip, investigate ≥1+  ☐ Exclude menstruation, exercise, beetroot/rifampicin, catheter trauma  ☐ Smoking, dyes/aromatic amines, drugs  ☐ BP, abdomen, loins, bladder  ☐ MSU if infection suspected  ☐ FBC, U&E/eGFR, ACR; clotting if anticoagulated  ☐ Men with VH: consider DRE + PSA after discussion (age-specific thresholds)

Not routine in primary care: microscopy to confirm a dipstick, cytology, CT urography (urology selects imaging), awaiting serology before nephrology referral.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • UTI — LUTS, fever; treat, then recheck the dipstick.
  • Stone — loin-to-groin colic → renal colic pathway.
  • Glomerular — proteinuria, hypertension, oedema, casts; VH after URTI (IgA) → nephrology, not urology.
  • Prostate — LUTS; do not attribute to BPH until referral criteria are assessed.
  • Bladder/renal cancer — painless VH, smoker, occupational exposure → apply NG12.
  • Uncertain — repeat dipstick, eGFR, ACR, BP; refer by findings.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Lower UTI, non-pregnant woman: nitrofurantoin 100 mg MR BD 3 days (avoid if eGFR <45); trimethoprim 200 mg BD 3 days if contraindicated. Recheck dipstick after.
  • Small distal ureteric stone, stable: NSAID + paracetamol; consider off-label tamsulosin 400 micrograms OD; normal hydration.
  • Anticoagulated: do not stop routinely; check INR; specialist advice before withholding.
  • Smoking cessation → NHS Stop Smoking Service. Vaginal oestrogen for menopausal symptoms never delays investigation.

6Refer routinely and review

  • Ureteric stone not passed after 4 weeks → routine urology.
  • Persistent NVH in primary care or after negative urology → dipstick, BP, eGFR, ACR annually; nephrology if ACR rises, eGFR falls or hypertension develops.
  • After a suspected cancer referral → confirm it was received and booked; chase if not. Document any declined investigation.

7Never reassure by

  • A treated UTI — persistent haematuria at NG12 thresholds still needs urgent referral.
  • Anticoagulants or antiplatelets — investigate on the same pathway.
  • Vaginal atrophy, or a trial of vaginal oestrogen.
  • BPH or a raised PSA — both may need assessment.
  • A normal ultrasound — it does not exclude bladder cancer.
  • Antibiotics prescribed — do not delay investigation when concern is high.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you have heavy bleeding or clots, cannot pass urine, collapse or have severe pain.”

Same day: “Contact us the same day if you see new blood in your urine, or get a high fever with urinary symptoms or rapidly worsening loin pain.”

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
