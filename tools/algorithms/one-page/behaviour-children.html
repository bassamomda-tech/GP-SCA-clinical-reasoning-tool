Behaviour concerns — children and young people

For: children and young people under 18 whose parents, carers or school raise behaviour concerns. Not for: adults. Sleep as the main problem: sleep problems in children algorithm. Children's doses: BNFC.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG87CG128CG158NG225
Core rule: behaviour is communication — safeguarding first, then ask whether it is pervasive across settings; gather school information without delaying a referral, and start support before any diagnosis.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • Overdose or serious self-injury → 999
  • Reduced consciousness or a fit with new behaviour change → 999

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Safeguarding concern: disclosure, injuries, neglect, domestic abuse, exploitation → child-protection procedures Same-day
  • Self-harm or suicidal thoughts with intent or a plan → CAMHS crisis Same-day
  • Neurological features: headache, vomiting, ataxia, seizures, personality change → paediatrics Same-day
  • Regression: language after age 3, motor at any age → paediatrician or neurologist Urgent
  • Possible seizures → epilepsy-experienced paediatrician within 2 weeks
  • Psychosis, intoxication, eating-disorder features → Same-day if unstable

3Assess — home, school and the child

☐ Behaviour, settings, onset, triggers  ☐ Development, speech, play, friendships  ☐ Family, adversity, parental wellbeing  ☐ Adolescents seen alone (HEADSS)  ☐ Growth plotted; dysmorphic and neurocutaneous signs; injuries  ☐ Hearing and vision checked  ☐ School information with consent (SDQ)

Bloods, genetics and EEG are not routine in primary care.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • ADHD — two or more settings, from early childhood → specialist ADHD service if impairment persists.
  • Autism — social communication + restricted patterns → autism team on reasonable concern.
  • Anxiety or low mood → CAMHS or school support.
  • Conduct problems → group parent training (ages 3–11).
  • Learning, hearing, vision, sleep, family stress, trauma → address the cause.
  • Uncertain — gather school information; review in 4–10 weeks.

5Treat today — doses per SPC, BNF and local formulary

  • Listen and plan: explain that diagnosis needs information from more than one setting.
  • Parenting: praise, routines, calm boundaries; refer to a group parent-training programme. ADHD features: consider watchful waiting of up to 10 weeks with ADHD-focused parent support.
  • School: liaison letter with consent; SEN support needs no diagnosis.
  • Do not start ADHD medication, antipsychotics or melatonin in primary care.

6Refer routinely

  • Reasonable concern about autism → autism team (check hearing in parallel).
  • Persistent ADHD symptoms with impairment → specialist ADHD service.
  • Developmental concerns → community paediatrics; emotional difficulties → CAMHS.
  • Audiology, optometry, speech and language therapy, Early Help.

7Never reassure by

  • A calm child in clinic.
  • A verbal, academically able child (autism).
  • Behaviour at home only, without asking about the home.
  • Waiting for school agreement before referring.
  • Labelling hypervigilance after trauma as ADHD without asking.
  • A normal newborn hearing screen years ago.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for an overdose or serious injury, a fit lasting more than 5 minutes, or if your child is very drowsy or hard to wake.” Same-day “Contact us today if your child talks about suicide or self-harm, gets new severe headaches or vomiting, loses skills, changes suddenly, or you worry they are being harmed.”

Follow-up: 4–10 weeks — school information, parenting programme, hearing and vision; keep reviewing while waiting.
