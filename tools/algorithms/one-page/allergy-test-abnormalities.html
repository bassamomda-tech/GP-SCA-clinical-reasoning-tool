Allergy test abnormalities

For: children and adults in primary care with a positive specific IgE (sIgE) or skin prick test (SPT), a raised total IgE or tryptase, or a drug-allergy label. Not for: full anaphylaxis, urticaria or rhinitis management (use the anaphylaxis, urticaria and allergic rhinitis protocols).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG258 (2026)Resus Council UK 2021NICE CG116NICE CG183BSACI
Core rule: a positive sIgE or SPT shows sensitisation, not allergy. Interpret it only with a compatible history: never prescribe an adrenaline auto-injector (AAI) or an elimination diet for a positive test alone, and never defer AAIs after a reaction compatible with anaphylaxis.

0Stop — call 999 now — anaphylaxis: adrenaline first, never delayed

  • Sudden airway, breathing or circulation problems, usually with skin or mucosal changes (skin may be absent) · tongue or laryngeal angioedema · recurrent or biphasic symptoms after a reaction → 999 say “anaphylaxis”.
  • IM adrenaline 1:1000, anterolateral thigh, without delay: adult and child >12 years 500 micrograms · 6–12 years 300 micrograms · 6 months–6 years 150 micrograms · <6 months 100–150 micrograms. Repeat after 5 minutes if airway, breathing or circulation problems persist.
  • While waiting: remove the trigger; lie flat, legs raised (sit up cautiously if breathing is difficult); no standing or walking. Never delay adrenaline for antihistamines, steroids, IV access or tryptase. Hospital transfer after treatment.

2Screen — history, not the test, triggers these

  • Past reaction compatible with anaphylaxis → two AAIs by brand + written plan (not for an easily avoidable drug); Urgent allergy clinic.
  • Systemic reaction to a bee or wasp sting → two AAIs; Urgent venom immunotherapy assessment.
  • Suspected mast-cell disease or persistently raised baseline tryptase → Urgent allergy / haematology.
  • Recurrent angioedema without urticaria → C4, C1-inhibitor; Urgent allergy clinic.
  • Severe immediate or delayed drug reaction (DRESS, SJS/TEN, AGEP) → avoid; Urgent allergy clinic.
  • Child: severe eczema + multiple food sensitisations + poor growth → Urgent paediatric allergy + dietitian.

3Assess

☐ History: reaction, onset (minutes = IgE), cofactors (exercise, alcohol, NSAIDs, infection), tolerated since?  ☐ sIgE only to allergens the history suggests  ☐ After adult anaphylaxis: tryptase as soon as possible, second ideally within 1–2 h (≤4 h from onset)

Not in primary care: broad panels, components without specialist advice, food challenge, patch tests. No role: IgG, Vega, hair, kinesiology.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 negative

  • Inhalant allergy — positive pollen, mite or pet sIgE + matching symptoms → block 5.
  • Sensitisation only — positive test, allergen tolerated → explain; no avoidance.
  • Pollen-food syndrome — oral itch within minutes of raw fruit or nuts, cooked tolerated → explain; no elimination of tolerated foods.
  • Low-risk penicillin label (delayed maculopapular rash, no systemic features) → approved local de-labelling pathway.
  • Contact dermatitis — type IV, not IgE → patch testing in dermatology / allergy clinic if not responding.
  • Uncertain — specialist allergy advice before any avoidance or AAI.

5Treat today — only these

  • AAI: two in-date devices by brand, dose checked against weight, age and product; brand-specific training and written plan; check both are physically carried.
  • Hay fever: intranasal steroid (beclometasone 2 sprays each nostril BD, or fluticasone propionate 2 sprays each nostril OD) + cetirizine 10 mg OD. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.
  • Montelukast only for rhinitis with asthma; warn about neuropsychiatric effects (MHRA).
  • Optimise asthma control; dietitian if multiple or nutritionally important foods are excluded.

6Refer routinely

  • Moderate–severe rhinitis despite pharmacotherapy → Routine specialist immunotherapy (Acarizax per local guidance commissioning).
  • Recurrent severe infections + low immunoglobulins → immunology.
  • Occupational allergen identified → occupational physician.

7Never reassure by

  • A normal tryptase: it does not exclude anaphylaxis, especially food or respiratory reactions.
  • “Pollen-food syndrome is always mild”: systemic reactions occur (lipid-transfer protein, nuts, cofactors).
  • Antihistamines or steroids: they do not treat airway, breathing or shock problems.
  • AAIs on the repeat list: check two in-date devices are carried.
  • A beta-blocker in someone at anaphylaxis risk: adrenaline works less well; record it, tell the allergy team.
  • A positive test alone: never label, exclude foods or prescribe an AAI on it.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If your throat or tongue swells, you struggle to breathe or wheeze, or feel faint: use your auto-injector into the outer thigh straight away, then call 999 and say ‘anaphylaxis’. Lie down with your legs raised, or sit up if breathing is hard; do not stand or walk. Use the second device after 5 minutes if you are not better or symptoms come back.”

Yearly review (food or venom allergy): weight and device dose, expiry, two carried, technique, plan, asthma control, beta-blockers. Montelukast: “stop and contact us promptly if your mood, sleep or behaviour changes.”
