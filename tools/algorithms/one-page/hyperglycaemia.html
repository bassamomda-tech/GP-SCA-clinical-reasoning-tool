Reasoning GP · Algorithm · 1-page summary

Raised HbA1c or high blood glucose

For: adults with a raised HbA1c or glucose in primary care. Not for: children and young people (same-day paediatric pathway) or pregnancy (same-day antenatal diabetes team); known diabetes (see the protocols).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review October 2027
NICE NG28 (2026)NG17NG12PH38JBDS
Core rule: before labelling type 2, check ketones if symptomatic, ask about weight loss and speed of onset, and in anyone 60+ with weight loss arrange urgent CT for pancreatic cancer.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • DKA: vomiting, abdominal pain, Kussmaul breathing, drowsiness, or blood ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L (urine ≥2+) → 999. Glucose may be near-normal on an SGLT2 inhibitor.
  • HHS: very high glucose, profound dehydration, confusion or drowsiness (mixed DKA/HHS occurs) → 999
  • Sepsis or acute foot infection with systemic illness → 999

1Triage — confirm the diagnosis (NICE NG28)

ResultMeaning → action
HbA1c ≥48Diabetes; repeat if asymptomatic; one result if symptomatic
HbA1c 42–47Non-diabetic hyperglycaemia → NDPP, annual HbA1c
Fasting ≥7.0Diabetes (confirm if asymptomatic)
Random ≥11.1Diabetes only with symptoms

HbA1c unreliable → use glucose: rapid onset, suspected type 1, pregnancy, anaemia, haemolysis, haemoglobinopathy, transfusion, advanced CKD, steroids.

2Screen — serious causes

  • Possible type 1 / LADA — weight loss, ketones, rapid onset, young or low BMI, any age → Same-day diabetes team. Ketones 1.5–2.9 → Same-day
  • Pancreatic cancer (NG12) — 60+ with new diabetes and weight loss → Urgent CT abdomen (direct access).
  • Pregnant with raised glucose → Same-day antenatal diabetes team.
  • Active foot disease → Same-day foot protection/MDT service.

3Assess

☐ Glucose + blood ketones if symptomatic  ☐ Weight, BMI, BP, feet  ☐ U&E/eGFR, ACR, lipids, LFTs, FBC  ☐ QRISK3  ☐ Drugs: steroids, antipsychotics  ☐ Autoimmunity, family history, pancreatitis, alcohol

Targeted: GAD antibodies (do not wait for them), ferritin, Cushing's tests. Eye screening referral at diagnosis.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Type 2 (commonest) — gradual, overweight, family history, no ketones → block 5.
  • Drug-induced — steroids, antipsychotics → evening glucose, review drug.
  • MODY — young, non-obese, multigenerational family history → diabetes team.
  • Pancreatic / endocrine — pancreatitis, haemochromatosis, Cushing's → targeted tests.
  • Stress hyperglycaemia — acute illness, normal HbA1c → recheck at 3 months.
  • Uncertain — unwell or ketotic: treat as type 1; otherwise discuss before labelling.

5Treat today — type 2; doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • MR metformin 500 mg daily with food, titrate weekly to 1–2 g (eGFR ≥30), then add an SGLT2 inhibitor within weeks (NICE NG28 2026).
  • Before the SGLT2i: previous DKA, dehydration, ketogenic diet; not while unwell; ketone and sick-day advice. Caution in frailty.
  • Established CVD: add semaglutide up to 1 mg weekly. Atorvastatin 20 mg if QRISK3 ≥10%.
  • 42–47: no routine medication — Diabetes Prevention Programme, lifestyle.

6Refer routinely and review

  • Everyone: structured education within 3 months; eye screening at diagnosis.
  • Diabetes team (routine): possible MODY, pancreatic or difficult steroid-induced diabetes. Endocrinology: suspected Cushing's.
  • Review: metformin tolerance within 2–4 weeks; HbA1c at 3 months.

7Never reassure by

  • Age or BMI — type 1 and LADA occur in overweight adults.
  • A normal glucose on an SGLT2 inhibitor — check ketones.
  • A normal HbA1c with rapid symptoms, anaemia or pregnancy.
  • One borderline HbA1c in an asymptomatic person (repeat it).
  • "Start metformin and see in 3 months" in a 60+ person losing weight.
  • A capillary meter reading as a diagnosis.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you start vomiting, get tummy pain, very fast or deep breathing, or become drowsy or confused, call 999.” Same-day “Very thirsty, passing lots of urine and losing weight, or can't keep fluids down — contact us the same day; stop the flozin and metformin until eating and drinking again.”

Remote consultation with any 999 feature → convert to a 999 call. Ketones or tablet failure at any point → rethink the type.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
