Thigh pain

For: adults, children and young people with thigh pain, face to face, in hours or OOH. Not for: major trauma (emergency trauma pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG158CG147NG12CG124NG59
Core rule: most thigh pain is muscular, but clear the vascular, infective, compartment and bone-cancer emergencies — and a hip fracture in the older faller — first. A normal X-ray does not exclude bone malignancy or a stress fracture.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Acute limb ischaemia — sudden pain, pallor, pulselessness, paraesthesia, paralysis or marked coldness → 999 and notify the vascular service. Keep the limb flat, no compression; document time of onset; don't delay for bloods or imaging.
  • Necrotising fasciitis / sepsis — pain out of proportion to skin signs, rapidly spreading erythema, toxicity, crepitus or skin necrosis → 999
  • Suspected PE with haemodynamic compromise, syncope, severe hypoxia or marked deterioration → 999.
  • Cauda equina — acute bladder/bowel dysfunction, saddle anaesthesia, or a progressive neurological deficit → 999 / emergency. Document neurological findings before referral.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Compartment syndrome: pain out of proportion, on passive stretch, tense → Immediate ED / orthopaedics. Pulses do not exclude it.
  • Hip fracture: older faller, can't weight-bear, shortened rotated leg → Urgent ED with analgesia; 999 if unstable or unsafe to transfer.
  • DVT (swelling, VTE risk, no PE features): two-level Wells → Same-day DVT pathway
  • Bone or joint infection, pathological fracture → Same-day. Rest pain or tissue loss (CLTI) → Urgent vascular, often same-day phone.
  • Bone sarcoma (NG12): X-ray suggests it → consider a Suspected cancer pathway. Child or young person with unexplained bone pain or swelling: consider X-ray ≤48 h; suggests sarcoma → consider referral ≤48 h
  • Soft-tissue lump increasing in size (NG12): consider urgent USS ≤2 weeks (child or young person ≤48 h); suggestive or uncertain → consider referral. Never inject, biopsy or excise.

3Assess — both limbs, joint above and below

☐ Swelling, haematoma, wasting, mass  ☐ Compartment tension, temperature, capillary refill  ☐ Femoral, popliteal, pedal pulses both sides (Doppler if hard to feel)  ☐ Hip, knee, lumbar spine  ☐ L2–L4: knee extension power, knee jerk, femoral stretch  ☐ Inguinal nodes, hernia

Targeted tests only: CK for diffuse myalgia or statin; HbA1c for amyotrophy; age ≥60 with persistent bone pain or unexplained fracture → offer myeloma bloods (NG12). Not: routine panels or PSA, imaging an improving strain, D-dimer when DVT is likely.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Muscle strain / tear — sudden in sport; pain on resisted movement → graded loading.
  • Meralgia paraesthetica — burning anterolateral patch, normal power and reflexes; weakness or reflex loss → rethink.
  • L2–L4 radiculopathy — radicular pain down the front of the thigh → NICE NG59 pathway.
  • Referred — pain on hip movement (hip OA, fracture), knee, hernia.
  • Claudication — buttock/thigh cramp at a fixed distance, weak pulses or bruit → ABPI. Diabetic amyotrophy — painful quadriceps wasting.
  • Uncertain or not improving — reassess for fracture, haematoma, tendon rupture, infection, DVT, malignancy or referred pain; do not escalate analgesia.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Strain: relative rest, early movement, progressive loading. No compression or elevation if fracture, compartment or vascular compromise is possible.
  • Analgesia: paracetamol ± topical or short oral NSAID (caution: PAD, renal, elderly); opioids short term only. No automatic amitriptyline or gabapentin.
  • Claudication: supervised exercise, stop smoking, BP and diabetes control; antiplatelet and statin once PAD is established.
  • Meralgia: reassure, loosen tight belts, address weight. Amyotrophy: glycaemic control and physiotherapy; recovery takes months.

6Refer routinely

  • Lifestyle-limiting claudication → vascular.
  • Refractory strain, significant tear, tendon rupture, large haematoma, hip/knee pathology → orthopaedics / MSK. Femoral stress or atypical femoral fracture → Urgent
  • Radiculopathy still limiting despite conservative care (no fixed week threshold) → spinal.
  • Use the local (Bolton) DVT, vascular, MSK and sarcoma pathways.

7Never reassure by

  • Present pulses — they do not exclude compartment syndrome.
  • A normal X-ray — not sarcoma, stress fracture or hip fracture (then MRI; CT if MRI not within 24 h).
  • A negative D-dimer when DVT is likely — go straight to ultrasound.
  • Thigh circumference — it documents asymmetry, it does not diagnose DVT.
  • Mild skin signs when pain is out of proportion.
  • A "strain" label that is not improving.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Leg swelling with breathlessness, chest pain, fainting or coughing blood; a leg that is suddenly cold, pale, numb or weak; new leg weakness or numbness, bladder or bowel trouble, or numbness between the legs.” Immediate ED “Severe pain with a tense, swollen thigh; spreading redness, fever or rapidly worsening pain (999 if unwell); unable to weight-bear after a fall.”

Same day “New or increasing swelling of the thigh or calf.” ≤1 week “Worsening bone pain, a new or growing lump, or weight loss” (≤48 h for a child or young person). Record diagnosis, red flags checked, plan and review interval.
