Sore mouth and tongue

For: adults and children with a sore mouth, tongue, ulcers, white or red patches, or burning. Not for: dental treatment (own dentist, or NHS 111 for urgent dental care) or ulcers during anticancer treatment (acute oncology pathway, UKONS).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12BSOMBNF / BNFCUKONSSDCEP MRONJ
Core rule: examine the whole mouth (lift the tongue, palpate the floor of the mouth) and the neck. An unexplained oral ulcer over 3 weeks or a persistent neck lump → NICE NG12 says consider a suspected cancer pathway referral; local safety policy is to refer by default. Never delay it with a topical steroid.

0Stop — call 999 now — before the full history

  • Airway: stridor, respiratory distress, drooling, “hot potato” voice, rapidly progressive dysphagia (epiglottitis) → 999. Keep upright and calm, no tongue depressor, never leave alone.
  • Ludwig's angina: spreading floor-of-mouth and neck swelling, trismus, “woody” submandibular space, with airway or breathing symptoms → 999
  • SJS/TEN: painful erosions with blistering or skin peeling, target lesions, fever, eye or genital involvement → emergency transfer, 999 if extensive or unwell. Stop the suspected drug now; no topical oral steroid.
  • Suspected quinsy → Same-day ENT; 999 if stridor or airway compromise.

FindingAction
Ulcer >3 wkUnexplained, or persistent neck lump: NG12 “consider”; local default → Suspected cancer pathway

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Deep infection, can't drink, dehydrated (often primary herpes), or immunocompromised with fast-spreading disease → Same-day hospital / OMFS
  • Ulcers on chemotherapy or radiotherapy: think neutropenia → Acute oncology pathway
  • MRONJ: exposed bone or non-healing socket on bisphosphonate or denosumab → Urgent dental / OMFS. Do not stop the drug.
  • Behçet's: recurrent oral and genital ulcers, eye, skin or vascular features → Urgent specialist
  • Fixed white plaques with immunosuppression or weight loss → offer an HIV test; oral medicine or sexual health.
  • Awaiting cancer clinic and it bleeds, grows or nodes appear → Same-day call to the team

3Assess — good light, gloves, whole mouth

☐ Lips, buccal, gums, palate, oropharynx  ☐ Lateral and ventral tongue, floor of mouth (bimanual)  ☐ Neck nodes  ☐ Drugs: NSAIDs, nicorandil, methotrexate, antiresorptives, inhaled steroid, antibiotics

Targeted tests only: FBC, ferritin, B12, folate; coeliac serology; HbA1c; HIV test. Not for a typical single minor aphthous ulcer. No broad autoimmune panels or swabs for a first thrush episode.

4Classify — only once blocks 0–2 are clear

  • Aphthous — shallow, grey base, red halo; minor 1–5 mm, heals in 1–2 weeks.
  • Candida — plaques wipe off to a red base; inhaled steroid, antibiotics, dentures, diabetes.
  • Lichen planus — white lacy striae, both cheeks → dental / oral medicine surveillance.
  • Geographic tongue — migrating red patches, pale borders; benign.
  • Burning mouth — normal mucosa, peri- or postmenopausal; diagnosis of exclusion.
  • Uncertain — persistent ulcer with no benign diagnosis → Urgent dental / oral surgery

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Confirmed aphthous: hydrocortisone 2.5 mg buccal tablets, or betamethasone mouthwash (off-label, spit out); benzydamine; SLS-free toothpaste.
  • Candida: miconazole oral gel, or nystatin if on warfarin (MHRA). Fluconazole only if extensive, severe or refractory (check pregnancy, interactions). Inhaled steroid: rinse and spit, spacer.
  • Herpes: topical aciclovir early; primary gingivostomatitis — consider oral aciclovir if early and severe (BNFC); fluids.
  • Burning mouth: exclude causes; psychological therapy. Clonazepam only after oral medicine advice.

6Refer routinely

  • Lichen planus; refractory major aphthae; burning mouth needing drugs → oral medicine.
  • Deficiency found → treat and recheck at 3 months.
  • Dentures: out at night, cleaned daily, dental review. Smoking → NHS Stop Smoking.

7Never reassure by

  • A topical steroid trial for an ulcer over 3 weeks.
  • A painful, soft or benign-looking lesion: cancer can be all three.
  • A non-smoker or non-drinker (HPV-related cancer).
  • Looking only at the front of the mouth.
  • Treating a white patch that will not wipe off as thrush.
  • A red patch that is not painful, swollen or bleeding.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for difficulty or noisy breathing, drooling or being unable to swallow saliva, or fast-spreading swelling under the tongue or in the neck.” Same day: “Blistering or peeling skin or sore eyes with mouth sores, being unable to eat or drink enough, or a fast-spreading mouth infection — contact us today.” Give a date: “Come back if any ulcer, lump or patch has not healed within 3 weeks.” Track every cancer referral to an outcome.
