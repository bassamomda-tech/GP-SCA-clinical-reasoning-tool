Nausea and vomiting in adults

For: adults with nausea or vomiting, in hours or OOH, face to face or remote. Not for: children (use the vomiting in children algorithm); ongoing pregnancy care (use the nausea and vomiting in pregnancy protocol) or palliative care (use the palliative nausea and vomiting protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NG127NG243RCOG GTG 69MHRA
Core rule: the company the vomiting keeps tells you the cause — headache, abdominal signs, vertigo, thirst or weight loss. Pregnancy test whenever pregnancy is possible. An antiemetic that fails means reassess the diagnosis first, not add another drug.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • Peritonism (rigid, guarded abdomen, rebound); drowsy, confused or shocked; acute stroke; DKA with Kussmaul breathing, shock, altered consciousness or severe illness → 999
  • Thunderclap headache (SAH); neck stiffness, photophobia, fever or non-blanching rash (meningitis, meningococcal disease) → 999; meningococcal disease strongly suspected → IM/IV benzylpenicillin 1.2 g unless it delays transfer.
  • Chest pain, sweating or breathlessness with vomiting (silent MI) → 999, without waiting for an ECG. Haematemesis with fresh red blood, shock, syncope or known cirrhosis → 999
  • Suspected adrenal crisis (hypotension, pigmentation, low Na, high K; steroids stopped or vomited) → hydrocortisone 100 mg IM/IV + 999; no wait for cortisol.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Bilious or faeculent vomit + distension + absolute constipation → Same-day surgical; nil by mouth, no prokinetic
  • Vomiting worse on waking or lying flat + headache, papilloedema, focal signs, new seizure → Same-day neuro / acute
  • Diabetes: ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L, or lower with hyperglycaemia or dehydration → Same-day hospital
  • No fluid kept down >24 h, AKI, low sodium or potassium; stable haematemesis → Same-day admission
  • Pregnant: severe NVP (fluids not kept down, weight loss ≥5%, AKI); >20 weeks with hypertension, headache, epigastric pain or jaundice → Same-day obstetric; BP ≥160/110 → 999
  • Dysphagia, or ≥55 with weight loss + upper abdominal pain, reflux or dyspepsia → Suspected cancer pathway. ≥60 with vomiting + weight loss → consider urgent CT.

3Assess — once blocks 0–2 are answered

☐ Pulse, lying and standing BP, RR, SpO₂, temperature, conscious level  ☐ Abdomen: distension, hernias, scars, succussion splash  ☐ Fundoscopy, neurology, nystagmus, gait  ☐ Pregnancy test (repeat if LMP uncertain)  ☐ ECG if older or diabetic  ☐ Persistent or unwell: FBC, U&E, LFT, glucose, calcium. No abdominal X-ray.

4Classify — the cause picks the antiemetic

  • Gastroenteritis — diarrhoea, normal physiology → fluids.
  • Vestibular — vertigo, nystagmus → cyclizine 50 mg TDS or prochlorperazine; not ondansetron.
  • Drug — opioid, GLP-1 agonist dose rise, digoxin → reduce or stop.
  • Gastric stasis, obstruction excluded → metoclopramide 10 mg TDS, max 30 mg/24 h and 5 days; not in Parkinson's.
  • Migraine → prochlorperazine or metoclopramide + triptan. Cannabis hyperemesis → stop cannabis.
  • Uncertain — say so; recheck diagnosis, hydration and route before a second antiemetic.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Cause first; small frequent sips of ORS.
  • One antiemetic by mechanism, regular, with a stop date; not retained → buccal prochlorperazine. Domperidone ≤1 week, max 30 mg/day; not with QT or cardiac disease.
  • Pregnancy: doxylamine/pyridoxine, cyclizine, promethazine or prochlorperazine; metoclopramide or ondansetron second line after discussing risks.
  • Sick-day rules in writing: never stop insulin in type 1; steroid plan; pause metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, ACEi/ARB, diuretics, NSAIDs.

6Refer routinely

  • Persistent unexplained nausea despite treatment, gastroparesis, cyclical vomiting → gastroenterology.
  • ≥55, vomiting + weight loss, reflux, dyspepsia or upper abdominal pain → consider non-urgent OGD.
  • Self-induced vomiting → eating disorder service; urgently if bradycardia, hypotension or low potassium.

7Never reassure by

  • Diarrhoea — it does not exclude obstruction with overflow, ischaemia or sepsis.
  • Normal bowel sounds.
  • Pain eased by vomiting.
  • A normal glucose in someone on an SGLT2 inhibitor.
  • "Just vomiting" in an older or diabetic patient — it can be an MI.
  • "Probably a virus" still vomiting at 48 hours.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for severe tummy pain with a hard tummy, vomiting blood, a sudden severe headache, drowsiness or confusion, chest pain, a fit, a rash that does not fade under a glass, or sudden weakness or slurred speech.” Same day: “Contact us today if you cannot keep any fluid down for 24 hours, pass very little urine, feel dizzy standing, have a headache worse on waking or lying flat, new weakness or visual change, or raised ketones.”

Remote or OOH: see face to face if observations, examination, fundoscopy, pregnancy test or ketones cannot be done, or if diabetic, on steroids, pregnant, frail or alone.
