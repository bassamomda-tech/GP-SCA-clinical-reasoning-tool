Hypocalcaemia in adults

For: adults with a low albumin-adjusted calcium (below the laboratory range), in hours or OOH. Not for: children (paediatric advice); hospital IV treatment (full algorithm, step 6); long-term hypoparathyroidism care (specialist plan, step 7).

Core rule: act on the albumin-adjusted calcium (ionised if unwell or discordant), never a raw total. Below 1.90 mmol/L, tetany, stridor, a seizure or marked QT prolongation is an emergency however well the patient seems. Check magnesium and PTH before replacing — calcium will not correct while magnesium is low.

0Stop — call 999 now — IV calcium gluconate is hospital-only

  • Adjusted Ca²⁺ <1.90 mmol/L, even asymptomatic — oral calcium is too slow and unreliable → 999
  • Tetany or carpopedal spasm, laryngospasm or stridor, hypocalcaemic seizure at any level → 999. Stridor with neck swelling after neck surgery may be a haematoma → 999 and urgent surgical/anaesthetic help.
  • Marked QT prolongation, ventricular arrhythmia or syncope → 999 / same-day hospital
  • While waiting: ECG — do not delay it for other results; no outpatient IV calcium. Other acute symptoms (perioral tingling, cramps) → Same-day hospital assessment with an ECG.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • After thyroid or parathyroid surgery: new perioral tingling, paraesthesia, cramps or dysphonia → Same-day assessment and contact the operating team; tetany, stridor or seizure → 999
  • Suspected acute pancreatitis → Same-day hospital admission whatever the calcium (<2.0 marks severe disease).
  • Hypoparathyroidism, cannot keep calcium or calcitriol down (vomiting, diarrhoea) → Same-day: endocrine team or hospital.
  • Cancer feature (bone pain, fracture, weight loss, raised ALP); ≥60 with persistent bone pain → offer FBC, calcium, ESR/PV, electrophoresis, free light chains → Suspected cancer pathway if myeloma (NG12).

3Assess — face to face; not by telephone

☐ Chvostek and Trousseau signs (more often positive when acute)  ☐ ECG if symptomatic, acute or severe: prolonged QT/ST, bradycardia  ☐ Repeat adjusted calcium with albumin (ionised if unwell or discordant), PTH, 25-OH vitamin D, phosphate, magnesium, U&E and eGFR  ☐ If indicated: ALP, coeliac serology, cortisol

Not in primary care: IV calcium; calcitriol, alfacalcidol or PTH analogues unless under formal shared care.

4Classify by PTH — only once urgency is settled

  • PTH low or inappropriately normal = hypoparathyroidism: post-surgical (commonest), autoimmune, magnesium depletion, infiltration, radiation → endocrinology.
  • PTH high = secondary: vitamin D deficiency (commonest), CKD (phosphate high), malabsorption (coeliac, IBD, bariatric), pseudohypoparathyroidism.
  • PTH suppressed: hungry bone, osteoblastic metastases, pancreatitis, tumour lysis, rhabdomyolysis.
  • Low magnesium (alcohol, PPI, diuretic, diarrhoea) blocks PTH. Drugs: denosumab, bisphosphonates, cinacalcet.
  • Uncertain or not responding to replacement → endocrinology; recheck magnesium first.

5Treat today — mild, asymptomatic, cause known

  • Low magnesium: correct first (local formulary; hospital if severe, symptomatic or renal impairment); review the PPI.
  • Vitamin D deficiency: local guidance (consider renal impairment, granulomatous disease, PHPT, stones); loading about 300,000 IU over 6–10 weeks, then maintenance. Calcium if intake poor.
  • CKD: colecalciferol or ergocalciferol; alfacalcidol specialist-led (NG203).
  • Before denosumab: calcium, renal function, vitamin D; correct first; calcium before each dose, plus within 2 weeks of the first dose if predisposed (CrCl <30). Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Refer — by letter, after block 0–2

  • Low PTH, pseudohypoparathyroidism, no response at 6 weeks → Urgent endocrinology.
  • CKD with secondary hyperparathyroidism → renal. Malabsorption or positive coeliac serology → gastroenterology; keep eating gluten (NG20).

7Never reassure by — never leave <1.90 in an inbox overnight

  • The patient looking well — below 1.90 is an emergency regardless.
  • A low total calcium with a low albumin — adjust before prescribing.
  • Calcium and vitamin D started with no magnesium or PTH checked.
  • Perioral tingling within 72 hours of thyroidectomy — calcium until proven otherwise.
  • Colecalciferol alone for low-PTH hypocalcaemia — needs specialist calcitriol.
  • A normal-looking patient with symptoms but no ECG.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for spasm of the hands or feet that will not relax, a fit, difficult or noisy breathing, or palpitations with fainting or collapse.” Same-day “Contact us today for new tingling round the mouth or fingertips, or cramps — especially after neck surgery. With hypoparathyroidism, if you cannot keep your calcium or calcitriol down, contact the endocrine team or go to hospital.”

Remote or OOH: Chvostek, Trousseau and the QT cannot be assessed by phone — adjusted calcium below 2.10, or any symptom, needs face to face with an ECG.
