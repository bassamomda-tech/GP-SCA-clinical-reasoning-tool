Bruising — easy or unexplained

For: adults with new, recurrent or unexplained bruising, including those on antiplatelets or anticoagulants. Not for: children, who need a separate paediatric and safeguarding pathway (block 1 shows the immediate route only). Follow local haematology, anticoagulation and safeguarding pathways.

NICE NG12NICE CG89BSH ITPUKHCDO/BSH VWD 2024NICE TA697
Core rule: two things you cannot miss — leukaemia and maltreatment. Get an FBC and a full medicine history; never stop an antithrombotic reflexively, and never start steroids for low platelets.

0Stop — call 999 now — do not delay transfer for GP tests

  • Major or critical-site bleeding (intracranial, GI, retroperitoneal), haematemesis, melaena, significant haemoptysis, syncope or haemodynamic compromise → 999
  • Severe headache, neurological symptoms, collapse or reduced consciousness with low platelets or an anticoagulant; non-blanching rash with fever or seriously unwell (meningococcal) → 999
  • Suspected DIC or thrombotic microangiopathy with systemic illness; rapidly expanding deep haematoma, airway-threatening oral bleeding or suspected compartment syndrome → 999
  • While waiting: no reversal in primary care; record anticoagulant, dose, time of last dose, renal function.

2Screen — local platelet thresholds, not NICE

  • Platelets <20 × 10⁹/L; oral blood blisters, mucosal bleeding, widespread petechiae → Same-day haematology.
  • Blasts, pancytopenia, film suggesting leukaemia → Immediate specialist.
  • Bruising + pallor, fatigue, fever, infections, nodes or hepatosplenomegaly → consider very urgent FBC ≤48 h (NG12); weight loss, sweats + nodes → consider lymphoma referral.
  • Spontaneous deep haematoma or haemarthrosis; suspected HIT; significant bleeding on an antithrombotic → Same-day
  • Platelets <50 or falling → same-day or Urgent; 50–100 stable or unexplained abnormal PT/APTT → Urgent advice.

4Classify — mechanism, then next step

  • Actinic / steroid purpura — fragile sun-damaged forearms, no bleeding history → reassure; reconsider if atypical.
  • Drug-induced — new drug (e.g. quinine, heparin, valproate, trimethoprim) → review it; FBC.
  • ITP — isolated platelets <100, normal clotting, other causes excluded → discuss with haematology.
  • Inherited (VWD, haemophilia) — lifelong; normal tests do not exclude VWD → haematology.
  • Acquired coagulopathy (liver, vitamin K, DIC); vascular — IgA vasculitis (palpable purpura), scurvy, Ehlers-Danlos.
  • Uncertain / "easy bruising" — benign only after normal FBC and clotting, no bleeding history.

5Treat today — only these

  • Antithrombotics: weigh indication and thrombotic risk before stopping. Aspirin not indicated → stop; NSAID → paracetamol.
  • Warfarin: INR 5–8, no bleeding → omit 1–2 doses, reduce, recheck promptly. INR >8, no bleeding → stop, oral vitamin K, recheck ≤24 h. Minor bleeding, INR >5 → Same-day vitamin K. Local protocol; doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • DOAC, minor bruising: adherence, dose, CrCl, interactions; avoid NSAIDs; no PT/APTT.
  • Never here: prednisolone for low platelets; desmopressin for suspected VWD. Scurvy → ascorbic acid per local guidance.

6Refer routinely — and follow up

  • Convincing bleeding history → haematology, even if tests are normal. Stable VWD; stable ITP (platelets >50).
  • Atypical or ulcerated purpura → dermatology.
  • Follow-up: drug stopped → FBC at 1 week; confirm cancer appointment received; DOAC → CrCl at least yearly.

7Never reassure by

  • Normal FBC and clotting, or no family history, when the bleeding history is convincing.
  • "Easy bruising" in a baby who is not mobile, or on a child's ears, neck, cheeks or buttocks.
  • Labelling bruising in a frail adult as age or falls without asking about safety at home.
  • Treating low platelets as ITP before haematology has excluded leukaemia.

8Safety-net — verbal and written, then document it

999 “Call 999 for a severe headache, confusion, collapse or new weakness; vomiting blood, black stools or coughing up blood; heavy bleeding or feeling faint; a rash that does not fade with fever or feeling very unwell; or a very swollen joint with numbness.” Any head injury on an anticoagulant → A&E straight away, even if well. Same day “Contact us or NHS 111 today for new widespread pin-point spots, blood blisters in the mouth, bleeding that won't stop with pressure, a nosebleed over 10–15 minutes (A&E), bruises spreading fast, blood in urine or from the back passage, a new swollen muscle or joint, or bruising with fever, paleness, marked tiredness, swollen glands or weight loss.”

Anticoagulants: know the medicine, dose and indication; carry an alert card.
