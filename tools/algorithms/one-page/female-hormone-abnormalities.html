Female hormone abnormalities

For: adolescents and adults with an abnormal or requested FSH/LH, oestradiol, prolactin, testosterone or progesterone. Not for: pregnancy (test first); absent puberty → paediatric endocrinology (block 2).

Core rule: pregnancy test first, then choose hormones by phenotype — no universal panel. POI needs HRT, not reassurance, and rapid virilisation or a markedly raised testosterone needs urgent specialist assessment, not a PCOS label.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Pituitary apoplexy: sudden severe headache + visual loss, diplopia, altered consciousness or vomiting → 999 No outpatient MRI.
  • Adrenal crisis: any of shock, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, hypoglycaemia or deterioration, especially with known or possible adrenal insufficiency → 999
  • Haemodynamic instability, severe hypoglycaemia, acute neurological/visual symptoms → 999
  • While waiting: adrenal crisis → hydrocortisone 100 mg IV/IM now, IV 0.9% saline per protocol; no waiting for bloods.

1Triage — read the pattern with the clinical picture

PatternMeaning → next step
FSH ↑, under 40Possible POI → repeat FSH in 4–6 weeks
Aged 45 or overMenopause is clinical; no FSH (40–45: consider)
FSH/LH low/normal, E2 ↓Hypothalamic or pituitary → prolactin, TSH
Prolactin ↑Mild → repeat rested ± macroprolactin; drugs, TSH
Testosterone ↑Mild → PCOS (exclusion); >4 nmol/L → endocrinology; about ≥5 → confirm and investigate promptly (SfE 2025); about ≥6–7 → block 2

2Screen — serious causes

  • Rapid virilisation (deep voice, clitoromegaly, muscle change) or testosterone about 6–7 nmol/L or higher → Urgent endocrinology/gynaecology; no repeat-test delay if virilised.
  • Ascites or unexplained pelvic/abdominal mass → Suspected cancer referral. Persistent bloating/satiety/pelvic pain → NG12 CA-125 (40+)/ultrasound.
  • Unexplained postmenopausal bleeding ≥55, not HRT → Suspected cancer; <55 consider.
  • Haematocolpos or suspected torsion → Same-day gynaecology.
  • Very high/rising prolactin, field loss, suspected Cushing's → Urgent endocrinology.
  • Primary amenorrhoea, no secondary sexual characteristics → Urgent karyotype, paediatric endocrinology.

3Assess

☐ Pregnancy test ☐ Cycle, contraception, medicines ☐ BMI, exercise, eating disorder ☐ Hirsutism, clitoromegaly, Cushing features, galactorrhoea ☐ Visual fields if high prolactin ☐ Progesterone only for fertility, ~7 days before the period

Not routinely: LH:FSH ratio, AMH for POI, CA-125 screening, IGF-1, fixed day-21 progesterone.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • PCOS — adults 2 of: ovulatory dysfunction, hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovaries/AMH (adolescents: first two). Exclusion diagnosis.
  • POI — under 40, symptoms + raised FSH on two samples 4–6 weeks apart.
  • Functional hypothalamic — low FSH/LH and oestradiol; energy deficit, exercise, stress.
  • Hyperprolactinaemia — drugs, hypothyroidism, stress; pituitary if very high or rising.
  • Menopause — 45+: clinical; 12 months with no periods, off hormonal contraception.
  • Uncertain — pelvic ultrasound; refer.

5Treat today — only these

  • POI: offer HRT or combined hormonal contraceptive (CHC) unless contraindicated, until at least average menopause age; progestogen if uterus. HRT is not contraception.
  • PCOS: lifestyle, no weight stigma; CHC first line if not seeking pregnancy (VTE-risk check). Prolonged amenorrhoea → endometrial protection (CHC, LNG-IUS or intermittent progestogen).
  • Menopause HRT: consider transdermal if VTE risk (incl. BMI >30). Review 3 months, then yearly — not by FSH.
  • Hypothalamic: restore energy intake, reduce excess exercise; sex steroids = specialist. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Refer routinely — and monitor

  • POI → reproductive medicine (fertility, DXA); Urgent if complex.
  • PCOS with heavy or persistent bleeding, ovarian pathology → gynaecology.
  • Anovulatory infertility → fertility service, not GP letrozole.
  • PCOS: glycaemia every 1–3 years by risk. POI: repeat DXA by result, not yearly.

7Never reassure by

  • A PCOS label when virilisation is rapid or testosterone markedly raised.
  • "Polycystic ovaries" on a scan alone, or the LH:FSH ratio.
  • A single FSH — or leaving confirmed POI without HRT.
  • A low day-21 progesterone in a long or irregular cycle.
  • A normal testosterone when hyperandrogenism is clinically obvious.
  • FSH/LH as ovarian tumour markers, or CA-125 in isolation.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for a sudden severe headache with loss of or double vision, or if very unwell with vomiting, faintness or confusion.” Same-day “Sudden severe pelvic pain — get seen today.” Urgent “Your voice deepening or body hair changing fast, or any bleeding after the menopause — contact us promptly.”

Remote or OOH: any block 0 feature → 999, not a booked appointment. POI: HRT is not contraception. Follow-up: review booked.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented

No block 0–2 trigger applies Pregnancy excluded; tests chosen by phenotype and correctly timed Abnormal result confirmed (FSH twice; prolactin rested) POI/PCOS plan: HRT/CHC or endometrial protection; contraception discussed Safety-net given; review date booked
