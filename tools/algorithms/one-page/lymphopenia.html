Lymphopenia (low lymphocyte count)

For: adults with a low absolute lymphocyte count on FBC in primary care. Not for: children (age-specific ranges; discuss with paediatrics) or inpatients with severe sepsis or COVID-19 (hospital team). Known HIV, CVID or SLE: follow the specialist plan, plus blocks 0 and 8.

Core rule: most lymphopenia is transient and reactive, so confirm and trend it; offer an HIV test with a low threshold if it is unexplained or persistent; never start PCP prophylaxis or stop a DMARD on the total lymphocyte count alone.

0Act now — hospital today

  • Severely unwell + fever + cytopenias with organomegaly, hepatitis, coagulopathy, neurological features or very high ferritin (HLH/MAS) → Immediate hospital; discuss with haematology. Do not start dexamethasone or etoposide in primary care.
  • Fever or sepsis with severe lymphopenia (<0.5 × 10⁹/L), or suspected PCP, CMV, toxoplasmosis, disseminated fungal infection or TB → Same-day hospital. Offer an HIV test.
  • Severe respiratory, neurological or systemic symptoms; pancytopenia, blasts or a major blood-film abnormality → Same-day hospital
  • New HIV with CD4 <200: same day if opportunistic infection or unwell; if stable → urgent HIV-service review, not admission for the CD4 alone.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Fever, night sweats, weight loss + lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly or mediastinal mass → Urgent suspected-cancer pathway (NG12). Do not wait for LDH or CT.
  • Progressive lymphopenia on serial FBCs → Urgent haematology
  • Positive HIV test, suspected acute HIV, low CD4 → HIV service ≤2 weeks; same day if unwell, pregnant or seroconverting.
  • On a DMARD/biologic: significant or progressive cytopenia, serious infection, abnormal film or drug threshold → withhold, contact specialist ≤7 days
  • Persistent unexplained severe lymphopenia → haematology/immunology ≤7 days

3Assess

☐ Previous FBCs: new, persistent or progressive? ☐ Fever, sweats, weight loss ☐ Recurrent or opportunistic infections ☐ Steroids, DMARDs, biologics, chemotherapy ☐ Sexual history, injecting drugs, travel ☐ Nodes, spleen, oral candida, skin

☐ Repeat FBC; film if persistent or marked ☐ HIV 4th-generation test ☐ Immunoglobulins if recurrent bacterial infections. EBV/CMV, CRP/ESR, ANA, LDH, CXR only as history directs; subsets only with HIV, opportunistic infection, severe persistent lymphopenia or specialist advice. Not routine: CD4 for mild isolated lymphopenia, or zinc testing.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Reactive — recent viral illness, surgery, stress → repeat FBC at 4–8 weeks.
  • Drug — steroids (commonest), DMARDs, biologics, chemotherapy, radiotherapy → review with the prescriber (shared-care protocol).
  • HIV — risk factors or indicator conditions (unexplained leukopenia, oral candida, nodes, weight loss, mononucleosis-like illness) → offer and recommend a test.
  • Autoimmune — SLE, sarcoidosis, RA → rheumatology if active.
  • Antibody deficiency (CVID) — recurrent sinopulmonary infections, low IgG/IgA/IgM → clinical immunology.
  • Uncertain — persistent and unexplained: film, HIV test, haematology/immunology per local pathway.

5Treat today

  • Steroids: review dose with the prescriber; never stop abruptly. Prednisolone ≥5 mg/day for ≥4 weeks → Steroid Emergency Card and sick-day rules.
  • DMARD, isolated mild lymphopenia: usually continue and repeat; falling trend → discuss with rheumatology.
  • Vaccines: Green Book chapter 6, by the specific immune defect, not the count. Inactivated vaccines generally safe; Shingrix, not Zostavax.
  • Oral candida: topical nystatin or miconazole if mild (not fluconazole 150 mg single dose); recurrent → check HIV, diabetes, inhaled steroid. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer routinely — and leave to specialists

  • Clinical immunology: recurrent or unusual infections, low immunoglobulins, suspected primary immunodeficiency.
  • Rheumatology: active SLE or inflammatory disease needing immunosuppression adjustment.
  • Specialist-led only: ART, immunoglobulin replacement, PCP, antifungal and herpes prophylaxis. Non-immune, immunosuppressed, exposed to chickenpox or shingles → Same-day advice (oral antivirals per UKHSA).

7Never reassure by

  • Blaming a virus or steroid without a repeat FBC to confirm recovery.
  • Not offering an HIV test because the patient "is not at risk".
  • A total lymphocyte count to infer CD4, or to start PCP prophylaxis.
  • Labelling CD4 <200 as AIDS without confirmed HIV.
  • Stopping a DMARD only because lymphocytes are below range.
  • A single "mild" result with no trend, film or other cell lines.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “If you get a fever or signs of a new infection, or feel very unwell, contact us the same day — don't wait it out.” 999 On steroids: “If you keep vomiting or have persistent diarrhoea, use your emergency hydrocortisone injection if you have one, and call 999.”

Follow-up: repeat FBC booked at 4–8 weeks with a named result owner; not normalised → film, HIV test and targeted tests.
