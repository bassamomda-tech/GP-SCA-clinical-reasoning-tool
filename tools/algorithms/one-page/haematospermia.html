Haematospermia (blood in semen) in primary care

For: men presenting with blood in the semen. Not for: testicular pain, haematuria alone or a raised PSA alone (see those algorithms).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12 (April 2026)NICE NG110BASHH 2026 · STI testing 2023GOV.UK PSA testingEAU haemospermia
Core rule: a single episode in a man under 40 with a normal examination and urine is benign — but age over 40 with persistence or recurrence, haematuria, or an abnormal prostate or testis needs urine testing, PSA/DRE and the NG12 routes.

0Act now — before reassuring

  • Fever >38°C, rigors, perineal pain, tachycardia or hypotension (acute bacterial prostatitis or epididymo-orchitis with sepsis) → Same-day / emergency assessment.
  • Painful inability to pass urine (prostatitis, clot) → Same-day catheterisation and urology.
  • Sudden severe testicular pain → possible torsion: 999 or A&E immediately.

1Triage — age, persistence, associated features

PatternRoute
<40, single episodeNo red flags, haematuria or STI risk; normal examination and urine → reassure, safety-net

2Screen — NG12 routes (haematospermia alone is not one)

  • Prostate feels malignant on DRE → Suspected cancer referral, whatever the PSA.
  • PSA above age range (40–49 >2.5 · 50–59 >3.5 · 60–69 >4.5 · 70–79 >6.5 ng/mL) → Suspected cancer; under 40 or 80+: judgement.
  • Aged 45+, unexplained visible haematuria, or persisting after treated UTI → Suspected cancer (bladder / renal).
  • Painless testicular enlargement, or change in shape or texture → consider Suspected cancer; persistent testicular symptoms → consider direct-access ultrasound.
  • Penile mass or ulcer, STI excluded or persisting after treatment → consider Suspected cancer.
  • Weight loss, bone pain, night sweats → examine, PSA, relevant NG12 route.

3Assess

☐ Truly semen? (partner, urine) · episodes · procedures · anticoagulants, bleeding disorder, liver disease · family history, BRCA2, Black ethnicity · sexual history · freshwater travel  ☐ BP, temperature, pulse, abdomen, testes  ☐ Offer DRE (chaperone) with prostate symptoms, persistent episodes, abnormal PSA or visible haematuria  ☐ All: dipstick + MSU  ☐ PSA after counselling; not during UTI / prostatitis or within 6 weeks of treated UTI or biopsy; no ejaculation or vigorous exercise for 48 h  ☐ STI risk: NAAT by exposure, HIV, syphilis  ☐ Bleeding elsewhere: FBC, clotting, INR, LFTs

Not in primary care: transrectal ultrasound, MRI, semen culture. Tender boggy prostate: no prostatic massage.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Idiopathic — commonest in younger men → reassure; usually resolves.
  • Iatrogenic — biopsy, vasectomy, catheter, cystoscopy, radiotherapy → reassure.
  • Infective — prostatitis, epididymo-orchitis, urethritis (chlamydia, gonorrhoea, M. genitalium), UTI → treat the cause.
  • Vascular / systemic — high BP, anticoagulation, bleeding disorder, liver disease → control BP; review medicines, clotting.
  • Structural — seminal vesicle or ejaculatory duct cysts, calculi, enlarged prostate → urology imaging. Schistosomiasis after travel → terminal urine ova, serology, eosinophils; infectious diseases advice.
  • Uncertain — say so; review, and refer if it persists.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Reassure; no medicine. If infection possible: condoms or no sex until STI assessment complete.
  • Acute prostatitis: MSU if no delay; antibiotic per NG110 and local guidance, review at 14 days; fluoroquinolones only if others inappropriate (MHRA). Unwell, retention, immunocompromised, no oral intake or not improving → Same-day referral.
  • Chlamydia: doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days; partner notification; abstain until treatment complete; test for gonorrhoea, syphilis, HIV.
  • BP to NG136 target (under 80: clinic <140/90). Anticoagulants: do not stop automatically — review indication and risk.

6Refer routinely

  • Persistent or recurrent (especially over 40), unexplained or troublesome after urine, PSA/DRE; persisting despite treatment of a cause; suspected cyst or calculus → local urology pathway.
  • STI confirmed or suspected → sexual health; Urgent GUM for suspected gonorrhoea or M. genitalium.
  • Coagulopathy or INR out of range → haematology / anticoagulation service.
  • Schistosomiasis positive or strongly suspected → tropical medicine; do not treat automatically.

7Never reassure by

  • Colour or amount — dark blood does not localise the source or exclude cancer or infection.
  • A normal PSA when the prostate feels malignant.
  • Haematospermia not being an NG12 criterion — persistence over 40 still needs review.
  • Blaming visible haematuria on anticoagulation without investigation.
  • Transillumination or absence of pain in a testicular mass.
  • A PSA taken during infection — repeat it once the infection has settled.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you can't pass urine at all, have a fever with shivering and feel very unwell, or get sudden severe pain in a testicle, call 999 or go to A&E — don't drive yourself.” Same-day “Blood in your urine or a fever: contact us the same day.” Review after treatment: resolved? repeat MSU if haematuria; repeat PSA if taken during infection; persisting or recurring, weight loss or bone pain → reassess and urology.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
