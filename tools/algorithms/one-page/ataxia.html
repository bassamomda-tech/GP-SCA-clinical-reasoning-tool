Ataxia — acute or chronic unsteadiness

For: adults with new or progressive incoordination or unsteady gait; children for the red flags only. Not for: vertigo or falls without ataxia (use the vertigo or falls algorithm).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG127NICE NG128NICE CG100ABN nitrous oxide 2024DVLA
Core rule: sudden-onset new ataxia is posterior-circulation stroke until excluded (FAST may be negative), and confusion, ophthalmoplegia or ataxia in an at-risk patient is Wernicke's — the full triad is not required.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Sudden-onset ataxia with ↓GCS, severe or occipital headache, vomiting, or a focal deficit (dysarthria, diplopia, dysphagia, weakness, sensory loss, visual symptoms, new deafness) → 999 stroke pathway.
  • Acute vestibular syndrome with abnormal HINTS by a trained examiner (normal head impulse, direction-changing nystagmus, skew) → 999; no trained examiner → stroke pathway immediately.
  • Fever + ataxia + meningism; unstable suspected Wernicke's; lithium toxicity with collapse or severe neurological features → 999
  • While waiting: ABCDE, capillary glucose and treat hypoglycaemia now; do not delay transfer for bloods or outpatient MRI.

1Triage — tempo of onset decides the route

OnsetThink → route
Minutes–hoursStroke, drug toxicity → emergency
Hours–daysWernicke's, intoxication, demyelination, cerebellitis (children) → emergency/same-day
WeeksTumour, paraneoplastic, B12/thiamine, nitrous oxide → urgent neurology
Months–yearsHereditary, MS, alcohol, hypothyroidism → bloods, then neurology

2Screen — serious causes

  • Wernicke's (alcohol misuse, hyperemesis, malnutrition) → Same-day emergency assessment + urgent parenteral thiamine.
  • Child, sudden-onset or rapidly progressive ataxia → Same-day acute paediatrics; 999 if ↓GCS, persistent vomiting, severe headache, meningism, focal deficit, ingestion or unable to walk.
  • Headache worse on waking, vomiting, papilloedema → Same-day imaging.
  • Phenytoin, carbamazepine or lithium with nystagmus, slurred speech, drowsiness → Same-day review + urgent levels; emergency assessment if significant toxicity.
  • Rapidly progressive over days–weeks → Urgent suspected cancer pathway (NG127); same-day if headache, vomiting, papilloedema or focal deficit.
  • Nitrous oxide + paraesthesia, numbness, weakness or urinary symptoms → Urgent neurology; treat without delay.

3Assess

☐ Glucose, BP, conscious level, temperature  ☐ Finger-nose, heel-shin, dysdiadochokinesia, nystagmus, speech  ☐ Romberg only if safe standing eyes open; gait with assistance  ☐ Eye movements, fundi, hearing  ☐ Tone, power, reflexes, proprioception, vibration  ☐ Alcohol, drug and nitrous oxide history  ☐ Chronic: FBC, U&E, LFT, glucose/HbA1c, B12/folate, TFT, calcium; consider coeliac serology; drug levels

Not in primary care: MRI, EEG or genetics without specialist input.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Cerebellar — wide-based gait, intention tremor, nystagmus, past-pointing; alcohol, MS, tumour, hereditary, paraneoplastic.
  • Sensory — Romberg positive, stamping gait, falls in the dark; B12 (incl. nitrous oxide), neuropathy, dorsal columns.
  • Vestibular — veers, vertigo, no limb incoordination; severe truncal ataxia or new deafness → think central.
  • Reversible — alcohol, antiepileptic or lithium toxicity, thiamine or B12 deficiency, hypothyroidism → treat.
  • Uncertain, or progressive despite normal bloods → neurology.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Neurological B12 deficiency: IM hydroxocobalamin loading, B12 before folate; nitrous oxide → parenteral B12 + urgent specialist advice.
  • Harmful or dependent drinker at risk: offer prophylactic oral thiamine (NICE CG100) + alcohol support.
  • Drug toxicity: agreed plan with specialist/acute advice; never stop antiepileptics or lithium abruptly without one.
  • Driving: advise not to drive if symptoms could impair safe control; check DVLA standards, notify where required; document.

6Refer routinely and review

  • Gradually progressive unsteady gait: neurology (NG127), alongside the reversible-cause screen; hereditary → genetics.
  • Rehabilitation: physiotherapy, OT, falls prevention; SALT for dysarthria. Choking, wet voice, chest infections → urgent swallow assessment.
  • After treating the cause: reassess; persisting or progressive → neurology. Recheck levels after dose changes.

7Never reassure by

  • A negative FAST — posterior-circulation stroke can show isolated ataxia.
  • An incomplete Wernicke triad.
  • A normal serum B12 in a nitrous oxide user.
  • A normal blood screen when ataxia is progressive.
  • "Labyrinthitis" when acute hearing loss comes with vertigo.
  • A "negative" HINTS outside acute vestibular syndrome or by an untrained examiner.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if the unsteadiness suddenly gets worse, or you get a new headache, vomiting, drowsiness, confusion, double vision, weakness, numbness, trouble swallowing or breathing, or a fever with a stiff neck or dislike of light.”

Same day: “Contact us the same day if the unsteadiness worsens over days, or you get new nerve symptoms, slurred speech or drowsiness on your medicines, or a fever with new symptoms. Take care to avoid falls.”
