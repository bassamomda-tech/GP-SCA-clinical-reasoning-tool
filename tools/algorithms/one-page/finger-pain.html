Finger pain in adults

For: adults with finger pain, swelling, stiffness, locking or deformity. Not for: children (local paediatric pathway); wrist or whole-hand pain, nail problems or Raynaud's alone (their own algorithms).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG100NG219NG226NG12BSSH
Core rule: a hot swollen joint, a sausage finger with tendon-sheath tenderness or a cold pale digit is an emergency until proven otherwise. Persistent synovitis → urgent rheumatology, without waiting for blood results.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • Threatened digit or hand: cold, pale or blue digit, rapidly progressive colour change, sensory loss, absent Doppler signals → 999. A palpable radial pulse does not exclude digital artery occlusion.
  • Ischaemia with systemic illness, major trauma or vascular compromise; finger infection with sepsis or instability → 999. Other acute digital ischaemia → Immediate ED / vascular or hand surgery. Do not delay transfer for Allen's testing, bloods or imaging.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Flexor tenosynovitis (Kanavel: fusiform swelling, held flexed, pain on passive extension, sheath tender) → Same-day ED / hand surgery
  • Septic finger joint: acutely swollen, hot, tender; fever may be absent → Same-day hospital / hand surgery
  • Spreading infection from a wound or paronychia, red streak up the forearm → Same-day ED / hand surgery; ask about bites, water.
  • Major tendon injury; mallet finger that is open, with volar subluxation, a large bony avulsion or extension not held → Same-day hand surgery
  • Sensory loss, intrinsic muscle wasting and Horner syndrome (Pancoast) → Urgent CXR (NG12) + CT chest; lung cancer pathway if suggestive.
  • Persistent focal bone pain or swelling, lytic lesion, pathological fracture → X-ray; sarcoma suspected → Suspected-cancer pathway (NG12)

3Assess — only once blocks 0–2 are answered

☐ Exact digit and joint; active vs passive restriction  ☐ Swelling: bony, synovial, tendon-sheath or diffuse; warmth, fever  ☐ Neurovascular status, capillary refill  ☐ Skin and nail psoriasis; trauma, bite or puncture  ☐ Triggering, extensor lag, tabletop test  ☐ Diabetes, immunosuppression, anticoagulants, vibration tools

Synovitis: RF (anti-CCP if RF negative), ESR/CRP, X-ray hands and feet, without delaying referral. Gout: serum urate; if <360 µmol/L in a flare, repeat ≥2 weeks after it settles. Not routine: imaging for typical OA; HbA1c just for trigger finger or Dupuytren's.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Inflammatory — symmetrical MCP/PIP synovitis, stiffness >30 min (RA); DIP, nail pits, dactylitis (psoriatic) → Urgent rheumatology
  • OA — Heberden's or Bouchard's nodes, thumb base, stiffness ≤30 min. MCP swelling: think RA, gout, CPPD, haemochromatosis.
  • Gout — acute, very painful, red, hot; any finger joint. Uncertain → aspirate.
  • Tendon — trigger finger (locking, A1 nodule); de Quervain's (Finkelstein's supports only); mallet (drooping DIP; boutonnière is not the mallet pathway).
  • Structural — Dupuytren's (nodule, cord, contracture); ganglion. Secondary Raynaud's features → local rheumatology pathway.
  • Uncertain hot swollen joint — say so → Same-day hospital. Crystals do not exclude infection.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • OA: topical NSAID first; oral NSAID + PPI if ineffective or unsuitable, after GI, renal, liver and CV risk. No routine paracetamol or weak opioids. Hand therapy, joint protection.
  • Gout flare: NSAID (consider a PPI), colchicine or a short oral steroid, by renal function, age, anticoagulants, ulcers, interactions, pregnancy. Keep any allopurinol going; discuss urate-lowering therapy after every flare.
  • Trigger finger, de Quervain's: activity change, analgesia, splint; consider steroid injection if trained and locally permitted — never if infection suspected.
  • Mallet finger: continuous DIP extension splint, typically 6–8 weeks; any DIP flexion usually restarts the period.

6Refer routinely

  • Trigger finger locked, disabling, persisting or injection unsuitable → hand surgery / MSK.
  • Dupuytren's affecting function, MCP ≈30°+, progressive PIP, hand not flat → hand surgery (sooner if rapid).
  • Failed mallet splinting, symptomatic ganglion, de Quervain's failing → hand surgery.
  • Vibration tools with white or numb fingers (HAVS) → occupational physician.

7Never reassure by

  • Normal ESR/CRP or negative RF or anti-CCP when synovitis persists.
  • No fever in a hot swollen joint.
  • Crystals or a negative Gram stain: infection can coexist.
  • A palpable radial pulse with a cold, pale digit.
  • A normal serum urate during a flare.
  • Calling MCP swelling "OA".

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if a finger or your hand turns cold, white or blue, or you feel very unwell.” Same day “Contact us, NHS 111 or A&E today for rapidly increasing pain or swelling, fever, a finger you cannot move, spreading redness or red streaks, new numbness or weakness, worsening night pain or bone swelling, or increasing pain after a wound, bite or puncture.”

On allopurinol: “Stop it and get urgent advice if you get a rash, mouth ulcers or fever.”

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
