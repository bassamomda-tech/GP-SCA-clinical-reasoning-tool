Itch in pregnancy

For: pregnant women with new itch, with or without a rash, at any gestation. Not for: itch outside pregnancy (use the widespread itch algorithm). Suspected ICP, pre-eclampsia and fetal concerns are managed by maternity — the GP recognises, tests and refers.

Core rule: new unexplained itch in pregnancy, especially without a primary rash and worse on the palms, soles or at night, is intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) until bile acids and LFTs say otherwise. ICP carries a stillbirth risk; normal LFTs or a normal CTG exclude nothing.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Lip or tongue swelling, throat tightness, breathlessness (anaphylaxis) → 999. IM adrenaline first; antihistamines must not delay it.
  • Maternal collapse, haemodynamic instability, seizure or severe neurological symptoms (eclampsia) → 999
  • Jaundice with confusion, hypoglycaemia, severe vomiting, severe abdominal pain or features of acute liver failure (acute fatty liver, HELLP) → 999
  • Chickenpox with breathlessness or chest symptoms (varicella pneumonia) → 999. Reduced fetal movements alone → maternity triage now; 999 only if maternal collapse, severe illness or unable to transfer safely.

PatternAction

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Reduced or changed fetal movements → Maternity triage now. Do not wait for the next antenatal appointment.
  • New BP ≥140/90 from 20 weeks → Same-day maternity. Severe headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain or sudden swelling → Same-day even if BP is normal.
  • Persistent itch with normal initial bile acids/LFTs → Same-day obstetric repeat bloods; ICP can develop later.
  • Polymorphic eruption with fever, facial or mucosal involvement → Same-day
  • Chickenpox: significant exposure, not immune → Same-day post-exposure prophylaxis advice. New rash → Same-day obstetric; isolate from other pregnant patients.

3Assess

☐ BP; urinalysis if hypertension or pre-eclampsia concern  ☐ Whole skin: primary lesion or excoriations only; periumbilical involvement or sparing  ☐ Sclerae  ☐ Fundal height, fetal heart, fetal movements documented  ☐ Random (non-fasting) bile acids + LFTs — do not delay for fasting  ☐ Medicines (co-amoxiclav, erythromycin, nitrofurantoin)

Not in primary care: CTG as reassurance; routine clotting.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • ICP — itch without rash plus peak bile acids ≥19 µmol/L (severe ≥100) → obstetric-led care.
  • Polymorphic eruption (PEP) — urticarial papules in striae, spares the umbilicus, late third trimester → GP; benign.
  • Pemphigoid gestationis — starts periumbilically, blisters, any trimester → urgent dermatology (biopsy + immunofluorescence).
  • Atopic eruption — atopy, excoriated papules, dry skin, earlier than ICP, normal bile acids → GP.
  • Candida / scabies — vulval itch with white discharge; burrows in web spaces, contacts itchy → GP.
  • Uncertain — no clear local cause → bile acids + LFTs; “dry skin” only once ICP is excluded.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • All: emollient at least 4× daily, cool baths, loose cotton clothing. Chlorphenamine 4 mg at night for sleep (weigh sedation near delivery).
  • ICP: ursodeoxycholic acid, vitamin K and birth timing are obstetric decisions.
  • PEP / atopic eruption: topical steroid of appropriate potency, smallest effective quantity. Candida: 7-day topical clotrimazole; avoid oral fluconazole. Scabies: permethrin 5%, repeat at 1 week, treat contacts together; avoid lindane.
  • Chickenpox: non-immune exposure → aciclovir 800 mg QDS or valaciclovir 1 g TDS, day 7–14 after exposure. Rash onset valaciclovir 1 g TDS, day 7–14. Rash <24 h atlt;24 h ago at ≥20 weeks → oral aciclovir should be prescribed.

6Refer routinely

  • LFTs not explained by ICP, positive hepatitis serology → hepatology.
  • Atopic eruption not responding to emollient + mild topical steroid; dermatosis worsening or spreading → dermatology.
  • After ICP: itch, bile acids, LFTs ≥4 weeks after birth. Record ICP; bile acids + LFTs at next booking; contraception per UKMEC.

7Never reassure by

  • Normal LFTs — bile acids are more sensitive early.
  • One normal result while the itch persists.
  • A normal CTG — it does not remove stillbirth risk.
  • No rash — that is the ICP pattern, not a reason to relax.
  • Calling it dry skin before ICP is excluded.
  • A normal surgery BP when pre-eclampsia symptoms are present.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you collapse or have a fit, if your lips or tongue swell or your throat feels tight, if you are breathless with chickenpox, or if you are yellow with confusion, severe vomiting or severe tummy pain.” Now “If your baby's movements slow down or change, call maternity triage straight away — don't wait for your next appointment.”

Same day “Contact us or maternity today if the itch persists or worsens despite treatment or normal blood tests, your eyes go yellow or urine dark, you get a bad headache, vision problems, pain below the ribs or sudden swelling, a fast-spreading rash or fever with the rash, or chickenpox contact or rash.”
