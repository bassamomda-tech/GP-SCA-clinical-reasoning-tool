Foreign body in the eye

For: anyone with foreign-body sensation, an eye injury, a chemical splash or welding exposure, in hours or OOH. Not for: removing anything beyond irrigation or a moistened cotton bud — needle, burr and slit-lamp work belong to a trained clinician on the local (Bolton/Greater Manchester) emergency eye pathway.

Core rule: the mechanism sets the urgency. High-velocity metal (grinding, drilling, hammering) is an intraocular foreign body until proven otherwise, even with good vision and little pain; a chemical splash is irrigated before anything else.

0Stop — act now — before a full history

  • Chemical splash (alkali worse than acid) → irrigate now with tap water or 0.9% saline; do not wait for history, acuity or pH. Remove lenses, sweep the fornices, continue until pH is near-neutral → Emergency eye unit / ED same day
  • High-velocity injury or open-globe signs (wound, peaked or teardrop pupil, hyphaema, reduced vision, positive Seidel, subconjunctival haemorrhage with no posterior border) → stop examining → Emergency eye unit / ED now
  • Blunt trauma with orbital haematoma, enophthalmos/proptosis, restricted movement or diplopia → Emergency eye unit / ED + CT orbits. Reduced acuity after injury or RAPD → same route.
  • While waiting: rigid shield, not a pad; no pressure, irrigation (unless chemical), tonometry or removal; no nose-blowing; nil by mouth; analgesia, antiemetic; tetanus. No MRI until a metallic IOFB is excluded. 999 if systemically unstable, major trauma, severe chemical exposure, an eye too painful to open, or no safe transport.

1Triage by mechanism

MechanismMeans → action
High velocityGrinding, drilling, hammering metal, explosion → block 0
Low velocityDust, grit, insect → blocks 3–5
Organic / soilPlant, soil: fungal risk → lower referral threshold
Contact lensPseudomonas risk → urgent assessment
UV / weldingBilateral pain hours later → arc eye, not a FB

2Screen — same-day ophthalmology

  • Worsening pain, redness, discharge or photophobia, infiltrate or ulcer → keratitis → Same-day. Organic injury worsening = suspect fungal.
  • Rust ring central, deep, extensive, reduced acuity, or you are not trained/equipped → Same-day
  • Corneal FB central, deep, not fully seen or not removable by cotton bud → Same-day
  • Contact lens + corneal defect with infiltrate, discharge, photophobia, reduced vision, significant pain, worsening or incomplete examination → Same-day
  • Abrasion or arc eye not improving within 24–48 h, or arc eye with reduced vision, opacity or severe unilateral features → Same-day
  • Dendritic staining (herpes simplex) → Same-day; ring lesion in a lens wearer → Emergency

3Assess — in this order; stop if open globe suspected

☐ Acuity each eye (after irrigation starts if chemical)  ☐ Pupils, RAPD  ☐ Globe position  ☐ Cornea, anterior chamber: FB, laceration, hyphaema  ☐ Fluorescein + cobalt blue  ☐ Seidel, no pressure  ☐ Evert upper lid if FB sensation or vertical staining  ☐ Fornices if chemical  ☐ Tetanus status

Never: needle or burr without slit-lamp training; tetracaine for home; steroid drops without ophthalmology advice.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Superficial conjunctival or corneal FB — visible, focal staining → block 5.
  • Subtarsal FB — vertical linear staining → evert the upper lid.
  • Corneal abrasion — epithelial defect, no FB; usually heals within days.
  • Arc eye — bilateral, hours after UV; topical anaesthetic for examination only.
  • Recurrent erosion — pain waking from sleep weeks after an abrasion → ophthalmology or optometry.
  • Uncertain — FB not fully seen or any doubt → refer.

5Treat today — only these

  • Remove only if the globe is intact, the FB clearly visible, acuity preserved and you are trained: tetracaine 0.5% 1 drop, then irrigation or a moistened cotton bud; recheck acuity and fluorescein. Not if central, deep, rust ring, reduced acuity or RAPD, uncertain Seidel, infiltrate or hypopyon, or a lens wearer (unless the local service supports it). Child or anxious patient: no forceful manoeuvres.
  • After removal or abrasion: topical antibiotic per the local eye-injury protocol (chloramphenicol is common) — not for lens wearers (no Pseudomonas cover; fluoroquinolone via local pathway); lubricant; no eye patch. Paracetamol 1 g QDS + ibuprofen 400 mg TDS if no contraindication. Doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • Contact lenses: out now; none until the cornea is healed and the eye symptom-free.

6Refer non-urgently

  • Within 24–48 h ophthalmology: FB removed but residual staining or symptoms; uncomplicated lens-wearer abrasion per local pathway; any doubt about complete removal.
  • Work injury: record mechanism and PPE; the employer reports to HSE under RIDDOR; occupational health if recurrent.

7Never reassure by

  • Good vision or little pain after a high-velocity injury.
  • A negative Seidel test.
  • A CT scan alone — it may miss wood and organic material.
  • No visible FB when the upper lid has not been everted.
  • "I got it out" while pain or staining persists.
  • Chloramphenicol given to a contact-lens wearer.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same day “If the pain, redness or light sensitivity gets worse, your vision changes, a discharge starts, or it is not clearly better in 24–48 hours, contact us or eye casualty today.” “Don't rub it while numb; no lenses until fully healed.” Work and driving: no driving until acuity is normal; welders do not weld until ophthalmology clears them if an IOFB has not been excluded.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
