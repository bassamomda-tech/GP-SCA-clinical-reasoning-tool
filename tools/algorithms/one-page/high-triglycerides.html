High triglycerides in adults

For: adults with a raised triglyceride (TG) result, in hours or OOH. Not for: raised cholesterol alone or familial hypercholesterolaemia (use those protocols). Pregnancy with severe TG → obstetric and lipid-specialist co-management.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG238NG104TA805HST13MHRA / SPC
Core rule: raised TG at any level with severe abdominal pain or vomiting is acute pancreatitis until proven otherwise — do not wait for a repeat. Without symptoms, TG ≥10 mmol/L is about pancreatitis prevention; below that, it is about overall CVD risk (statin first).

0Stop — act now — before repeating any lipids

  • Severe epigastric pain radiating to the back + nausea or vomiting, any TG level (pancreatitis can occur below 10 mmol/L, especially after fasting or vomiting) → Same-day hospital, 999 if unwell. Do not wait for a repeat fasting profile.
  • TG >20 mmol/L with abdominal pain, vomiting or systemic illness, or chylomicronaemia with acute symptoms → Same-day hospital
  • Hyperglycaemic symptoms, vomiting, abdominal pain or acute illness → capillary glucose and blood ketones now; ketones raised, DKA/HHS suspected or unwell → Same-day hospital, 999 if very unwell. HbA1c does not exclude an acute emergency.
  • Very high TG can mask pancreatitis: a normal amylase or lipase does not exclude it when the picture fits — the hospital team decides.

1Triage by TG level — no pain or vomiting (NICE NG238)

TG (mmol/L)Action
<4.5Assess overall CVD risk; manage LDL-C/non-HDL-C, usually with a statin where indicated. Routine
4.5–9.9Fasting repeat if non-fasting or LDL-C unreliable; find secondary causes; risk tools may underestimate CVD risk. Non-HDL-C >7.5 → specialist advice.
10–20Review secondary causes urgently; fasting repeat after 5 days but within 2 weeks. Still >10 → specialist advice.
>20Not explained by excess alcohol or poor glycaemic control → Urgent specialist review

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Chylomicronaemia: fasting TG >20 + eruptive xanthomata, lipaemia retinalis or recurrent pancreatitis → Urgent specialist lipid assessment. Never manage solely in primary care.
  • Milky (lipaemic) sample: TG almost certainly >10 → repeat; symptomatic → Same-day assessment
  • Nephrotic syndrome (heavy proteinuria, oedema, low albumin), any TG → Urgent nephrology discussion; severe oedema, AKI, thrombosis, infection or instability → Same-day hospital
  • Pregnancy: TG can reach pancreatitis-risk levels → obstetric and lipid-specialist co-management.
  • Previous TG-induced pancreatitis: specialist-agreed target; alcohol abstinence; steatorrhoea → gastroenterology.

3Assess — secondary causes first

☐ Alcohol units (AUDIT-C), sugary drinks, weight, diabetes symptoms, family history of premature CVD or pancreatitis  ☐ Drugs: corticosteroids, thiazides, beta-blockers, isotretinoin, PI antiretrovirals, oestrogen (OCP/HRT)  ☐ BMI, waist, BP, xanthomata, fundi  ☐ Lipids (fasting if TG ≥4.5), HbA1c + fasting glucose, TSH, LFTs + GGT, renal function + urinalysis

TG >4.5: calculated LDL-C is unreliable — use non-HDL-C. ApoB is not routine in primary care.

4Classify — only once blocks 0–2 are answered

  • Secondary (most common) — uncontrolled diabetes, hypothyroidism, obesity, alcohol, CKD, nephrotic syndrome, pregnancy, drugs → treat the cause, then recheck TG.
  • Metabolic syndrome — central obesity, low HDL, raised BP or glucose → lifestyle first.
  • Familial — hypertriglyceridaemia (TG usually 3–10) or combined hyperlipidaemia (TG and LDL raised; family history of premature CVD → lipid clinic).
  • Chylomicronaemia (familial or multifactorial) — TG >20, pancreatitis → specialist (block 2).
  • Uncertain — unexpected or discordant result → fasting repeat; act on the confirmed band in block 1.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Lifestyle: no sugary drinks or fruit juice, less refined carbohydrate; avoid or cut alcohol (abstain if TG severely raised or after pancreatitis); weight loss; regular aerobic activity. TG >10 → specialist-directed low-fat diet with a dietitian.
  • CVD risk: statin first for LDL-C/non-HDL-C. Do not routinely prescribe a fibrate solely for CVD prevention.
  • Fenofibrate: only on specialist advice for persistent or severe TG; renal function and LFTs first; avoid in severe renal impairment and pregnancy. Never gemfibrozil with a statin.
  • Icosapent ethyl 2 g twice daily: consider only within TA805 — established CVD, on a statin, fasting TG ≥1.7, LDL-C >1.04 to ≤2.60; review AF and bleeding risk.

6Refer routinely

  • Specialist advice: TG still >10 on fasting repeat; TG 4.5–9.9 with non-HDL-C >7.5 mmol/L.
  • Dietitian: TG >10, recurrent pancreatitis, suspected chylomicronaemia, pregnancy, significant diabetes or renal disease.
  • Lipid clinic: mixed dyslipidaemia with QRISK3 >10% and uncertain cause; suspected familial combined hyperlipidaemia.

7Never reassure by

  • A normal amylase or lipase when TG is very high and the picture fits pancreatitis.
  • A TG below 10 mmol/L in a patient with pain and vomiting.
  • A normal calculated LDL-C when TG is >4.5 mmol/L.
  • A low QRISK3 in mixed dyslipidaemia or metabolic syndrome.
  • A normal HbA1c in an acutely unwell, vomiting patient.
  • Oily fish or over-the-counter fish oil as treatment for severe TG.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Severe pain in the centre or top of your tummy, or being sick — call 999 or go to A&E. Don't wait for the repeat blood test.” Same day: “Very thirsty, passing a lot of urine, losing weight or feeling unwell — contact us today for a sugar and ketone check.”

TG >10: explain pancreatitis risk, triggers (alcohol, high-fat meals, uncontrolled diabetes) and book the fasting repeat. Remote or OOH: any TG with abdominal pain or vomiting → same-day hospital assessment, not a repeat test.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented

No pain, vomiting or acute illness (block 0) Glucose and ketones checked if symptomatic TG band set; fasting repeat booked if 10–20 Secondary causes, CVD risk and non-HDL-C reviewed Pancreatitis safety-net given; specialist route chosen
