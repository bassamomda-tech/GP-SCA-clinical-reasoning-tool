Anal pain in primary care

For: adults with new anal pain. Not for: painless rectal bleeding (rectal bleeding algorithm), a painless lump (anal lumps), itch alone (anal itchiness); children (constipation in children algorithm, paediatric assessment).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12 (April 2026)NICE DG56 (FIT)BASHH (herpes 2024, LGV)ESCP anal fistula 2023ACPGBI
Core rule: a perianal abscess needs same-day drainage, not antibiotics alone; and always examine — never label pain or bleeding as fissure or piles until a mass, ulcer or sepsis is excluded.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Any one of: rapidly spreading perianal/perineal redness or swelling, pain out of proportion to the signs, skin discoloration, crepitus, or systemically unwell → suspected Fournier's gangrene → 999. Do not wait for crepitus (a late sign). Diabetes and immunocompromise raise risk; never delay for antibiotics.
  • Rectal trauma with sudden severe abdominal pain, peritonism or systemically unwell (possible perforation) → 999.

2Screen — serious causes

  • Tender perianal swelling, ± fever → abscess → Same-day surgical drainage (phone the registrar); never drain in practice.
  • Rectal trauma or foreign body → Same-day hospital / surgical retrieval. Unexplained trauma → safeguarding.
  • Rectal mass, or unexplained anal mass or ulcer → consider Suspected cancer referral by site; no FIT wait.
  • Rectal bleeding or change in bowel habit → examine; FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g → colorectal Suspected cancer pathway. Negative FIT never delays referral if concern remains.
  • Tenesmus + weight loss → Urgent assessment, routed by findings (or FIT per NG12).
  • HIV or immunosuppressed → low threshold for proctoscopy + biopsy (CMV, HSV, cancer); GUM / colorectal.

LGV), HSV swab, HIV / syphilis by risk  ☐ FBC, CRP if infection; HbA1c by diabetes risk or recurrent sepsis; calprotectin if atypical fissure

Not in primary care: draining an abscess or biopsy. Lidocaine must not delay referral or mask a lesion.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Typical fissure — posterior midline tear; chronic (≥6 weeks): sentinel pile, indurated edges → block 5.
  • Atypical fissure — lateral, multiple, painless, non-healing → Crohn's, HIV, TB, syphilis, cancer → assess further.
  • Thrombosed external pile — most settle; severe within about 72 h → urgent surgical assessment for excision may be considered.
  • Fistula-in-ano — persistent discharge, recurrent abscess, external opening → colorectal.
  • STI proctitis → sexual health; don't delay empirical treatment if convincing (LGV: doxycycline 21 days).
  • Proctalgia fugax / levator ani — only once fissure, abscess, proctitis, IBD and cancer are excluded → reassure, warmth.
  • Uncertain — say so; specialist examination; safety-net.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Soft stool: gradual fibre, adequate fluid, one laxative by stool consistency; no straining.
  • Persistent or chronic fissure: GTN 0.4% per local protocol, usually 6–8 weeks; warn about headache; not with PDE-5 inhibitors or nitrates, or in pregnancy. Alternative: diltiazem 2% (off-label).
  • Pain: paracetamol; NSAID only if no bleeding, anticoagulant, renal, heart failure, ulcer, frailty or GI risk; lidocaine, sitz baths, ice.
  • Abscess antibiotics: adjunct only — systemic illness, spreading cellulitis, immunosuppression, diabetes, extensive or recurrent disease; never instead of drainage.

6Refer routinely

  • Fissure not healing after 6–8 weeks of topical treatment, recurrent or atypical → colorectal.
  • Fistula-in-ano, recurrent abscess → colorectal. Haemorrhoids with persistent prolapse, bleeding, pain, irreducibility or failed conservative care → colorectal.
  • GUM: STI or HSV proctitis, MSM or HIV-positive with anal symptoms.

7Never reassure by

  • Antibiotics alone for a perianal abscess.
  • The absence of crepitus or fever when infection is spreading.
  • Blaming bleeding on piles or a fissure without examination.
  • A negative or unreturned FIT when there is a mass or ongoing concern.
  • Labelling proctalgia fugax before excluding serious causes.
  • Repeating ointment for an atypical or non-healing fissure.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Rapidly spreading redness or swelling around the anus or genitals, severe pain, crackling under the skin, feeling very unwell, or sudden severe tummy pain: call 999.” Same-day “A new painful swelling, a drained wound that won't heal, much worse symptoms, or new bleeding during treatment: contact the GP today.” Review fissure at 6–8 weeks; no cancer appointment → patient phones the hospital.
