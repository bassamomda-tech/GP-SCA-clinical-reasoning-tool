Fever in adults

For: adults with fever, GP or OOH/111, face to face or remote. Not for: children (fever in children pathway, NG143). Neutropenic, asplenic, immunosuppressed, pregnant, device or travel → block 2.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG253NG240NG12NG33UKHSA malaria
Core rule: judge severity from physiology and risk group, not the thermometer. No fever, a normal CRP or a fever that "breaks" with paracetamol excludes nothing. Neutropenia, asplenia and malaria-area travel make an ordinary fever an emergency.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • NG253 high risk: systolic ≤90 (or >40 below baseline), pulse >130, RR ≥25, SpO₂ <92% on air, new altered mental state, mottled or ashen skin, no urine for 18 h; shock or rapid deterioration → 999
  • Non-blanching rash or meningism (headache, neck stiffness, photophobia, vomiting, seizure) → 999 + pre-alert. No rash or a glass test excludes nothing. Also suspected severe malaria, necrotising fasciitis, epiglottitis (stridor, drooling).
  • Unwell within 30 days of anticancer treatment, neutropenia or important immunosuppression → 999 / urgent secondary care via alert card. No FBC wait; no oral antibiotic.
  • While waiting: ABCDE, oxygen if hypoxic, IV access only if no delay; hand over observations including RR. Meningococcal strongly suspected and transfer significantly delayed (or per local pathway): benzylpenicillin 1.2 g IV/IM or ceftriaxone, not if anaphylaxis; record time. Never delay transfer for bloods, cultures, urine or antibiotics.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Asplenia, sickle cell → Same-day admission, low threshold for 999. Immunosuppressed, device → Same-day acute
  • Malaria-area travel, even months ago, despite prophylaxis → Same-day malaria test; one negative may not exclude. VHF risk → isolate, call UKHSA.
  • Back pain plus neurological deficit, sphincter change, saddle anaesthesia, focal spinal tenderness or toxicity → Emergency + MRI
  • Endocarditis (new murmur, emboli, prosthetic valve, injecting drugs) or hot swollen joint → Same-day hospital; cultures or aspirate before antibiotics if stable.
  • GCA: ≥50, new headache, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness → prednisolone 40–60 mg now (60 mg if visual), Same-day rheum; visual → Emergency eye
  • Unexplained fever → consider FBC ≤48 h (NG12); nodes or spleen → consider lymphoma referral. Cough >3 weeks, sweats, weight loss → CXR, consider TB.

3Assess — once blocks 0–2 are answered

☐ Full observations incl. RR ☐ Whole skin undressed; percuss spine; joints, lines ☐ Murmur, chest, abdomen, throat, teeth, ears, nodes

Well, <5 days, viral: no routine tests. Unwell or persistent: FBC, U&E, LFTs, CRP, glucose; urine if urinary symptoms; CXR if respiratory or unexplained. Echo, CT, MRI via acute route.

4Classify — three exits, not two

  • Serious cause likely — red flag, deranged physiology, risk group → blocks 0–2 destination.
  • Self-limiting — well, <5 days, normal physiology, coherent viral or minor local source, no risk group, reliable support → block 5, safety-net, named review.
  • Non-infective — GCA, Still's, vasculitis, lymphoma, PE (low-grade fever); drug fever 7–10 days after a new drug.
  • Uncertain — no source, remote, frail or >5 days → not "viral"; targeted bloods and a defined review, or admit.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Sepsis, malaria, endocarditis, septic joint, spinal infection: transfer is the treatment; no oral antibiotic and review.
  • Bacterial source, stable: amoxicillin or doxycycline (CAP, CRB-65), nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim (lower UTI), flucloxacillin (cellulitis), phenoxymethylpenicillin (strep throat); cultures first where feasible; shortest course; review at 48–72 h.
  • Viral: no antibiotic; usually 3–5 days. Paracetamol for comfort, within maximum dose; ibuprofen may be unsuitable (AKI, dehydration, pregnancy, varicella).
  • Sick-day rules: don't stop basal insulin without specialist advice; follow the individual adrenal plan; adrenal crisis → emergency hydrocortisone and admit; SGLT2i, metformin, ACEi/ARB, diuretics may need pausing if poor intake or AKI.

6Refer routinely

  • Fever >3 weeks or recurrent, undiagnosed → general medicine or infectious diseases; no repeated empirical antibiotics.
  • Still's, vasculitis or CTD (GCA excluded) → rheumatology. Resolved undiagnosed travel fever → infectious diseases.
  • Suspected notifiable infection → Health Protection Team promptly.

7Never reassure by

  • A fever that "breaks" with paracetamol.
  • No fever: frail or steroid-treated patients may be afebrile.
  • A normal CRP or ESR, or early normal bloods.
  • A positive dipstick in an older or catheterised patient.
  • A low NEWS2 score when you are still worried.
  • Fever with back pain called musculoskeletal.

8Safety-net — written; document the words used

999 “A rash that does not fade, new confusion or drowsiness, severe headache with stiff neck, breathlessness, cold mottled hands and feet, a fit, or no urine.” Same day: “Much worse, fever beyond 5 days, can't keep fluids down, much less urine, new pain or swelling including your back.” Asplenic or immunosuppressed: within the hour. No source, frail or antibiotic started → review at 48 h.

Remote or OOH: go face to face if observations incl. RR or a skin check are impossible, high-risk group, changing mental state, caller is not the patient, or review is not assured.
