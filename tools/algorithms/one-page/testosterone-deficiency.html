Testosterone deficiency in men

For: adult men with symptoms of testosterone deficiency (TD) or a low testosterone result, in primary care, before and during testosterone therapy (TRT). Not for: women or children; breast enlargement (use the gynaecomastia algorithm). TRT is started only under a documented local endocrine/urology shared-care protocol.

Core rule: one low level is not enough — diagnose only with symptoms plus two 7–11am testosterone levels and LH/FSH. Never start TRT in secondary TD before endocrine assessment, or in a man who may want children.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Severe chest pain, severe breathlessness, face or throat swelling or collapse during or after a Nebido injection (POME or anaphylaxis) 999
  • Sudden severe headache with visual loss (possible pituitary apoplexy) · suspected adrenal crisis (vomiting, low blood pressure, collapse) in hypopituitarism 999

1Triage — total testosterone (TT), two 7–11am samples

TT resultMeaning → next step
Single, afternoon or illNot diagnostic → repeat ×2, 7–11am, ~4 weeks apart, avoid acute illness
<8 nmol/L ×2Strongly supports TD if symptomatic → LH, FSH, FBC; prolactin if LH/FSH low or inappropriately normal
8–12 nmol/LBorderline → SHBG and calculated free testosterone
>12 nmol/LTD less likely, though context matters

BSSM action thresholds, not universal cut-offs — use the local assay and reference range.

2Screen — before any TRT

  • TT <5.2 nmol/L + low LH/FSH, raised prolactin, visual or neurological symptoms, other pituitary-axis abnormalities → Urgent endocrinology; consider pituitary MRI. No TRT alone.
  • Prostate feels malignant on DRE → refer Suspected cancer pathway (NG12). PSA above age threshold with possible prostate symptoms (40–49 >2.5; 50–59 >3.5; 60–69 >4.5; 70–79 >6.5 microgram/L) → consider the same route. No testosterone until resolved.
  • Low testosterone + anaemia + bone pain + fatigue, especially in an older man → exclude myeloma and prostate bone metastases before any TRT.
  • Low LH/FSH with liver disease, diabetes, arthropathy or bronze skin → ferritin and transferrin saturation Urgent (haemochromatosis).
  • Fertility wanted now or in future, or haematocrit >54% → do not start TRT.

3Assess

Screen: ED, loss of morning erections or low desire; T2DM, CKD, BMI >30 or waist >102 cm; long-term opiates, antipsychotics, anticonvulsants.

☐ Testes, BMI, breasts  ☐ Ask directly about anabolic steroids  ☐ STOP-BANG  ☐ Mood (depression mimics TD)  ☐ FBC/haematocrit, TSH, HbA1c, LFT + ferritin, CV risk  ☐ PSA per local protocol; DRE if indicated

4Classify — LH is the branch point

  • Primary — high LH/FSH: Klinefelter (small firm testes → karyotype), orchitis, chemo/radiotherapy, trauma. No pituitary work-up needed.
  • Secondary — low or inappropriately normal LH: prolactinoma, pituitary disease, opioids, glucocorticoids, anabolic steroids, haemochromatosis → endocrine assessment before TRT.
  • Functional — obesity, T2DM, OSA, alcohol, opioids, systemic illness → correct the cause first.
  • Late-onset — symptoms + confirmed deficiency, reversible causes excluded; many older men need no treatment.
  • Uncertain — borderline TT or symptoms only → free testosterone, treat functional causes, reassess; symptoms alone never justify TRT.

5Treat today

  • Functional: weight loss, treat OSA (CPAP), alcohol ≤14 units/week, opioid review (consider buprenorphine), stop anabolic steroids. No supplements to raise testosterone.
  • ED: a PDE5 inhibitor remains first line after the usual checks (nitrates, CV disease); TRT is not an ED treatment.
  • TRT only for confirmed TD, no contraindication, under the local protocol: counsel on fertility, prostate, polycythaemia; gel — wash hands, cover site; Nebido — observe ~30 min. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.
  • On TRT (3, 6, 12 months, then yearly): haematocrit 0.50–0.54 → consider dose cut; ≥0.54 → withhold or reduce + specialist advice. PSA rise >1.4 ng/mL in 12 months or >0.4 ng/mL/year over >2 years → urology. No benefit by 6 months → review, look for other causes.

6Refer routinely

  • Reproductive endocrinology / andrology: fertility wanted — before any TRT; stop TRT if new wish for children.
  • Endocrinology: primary TD without a GP pathway, Klinefelter, borderline or complex cases, previous prostate cancer, persistent suppression after anabolic steroids.

7Never reassure by

  • A single, afternoon or private testosterone result.
  • A "normal" LH — inappropriately normal LH with low TT is secondary TD.
  • "Children in a couple of years" — that man is not a TRT candidate.
  • Low mood and fatigue alone — depression, OSA, hypothyroidism mimic TD.
  • Good testosterone levels on TRT while haematocrit or PSA climbs.
  • Repeat prescriptions — TRT needs a documented annual review.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Severe chest pain or breathlessness, face or throat swelling or collapse during or after your injection, or a sudden severe headache with loss of vision — call 999.” Same day “Milder cough or breathlessness after the injection that is settling, severe breast swelling or pain, or new aggression or very high mood — contact us today.”

Review: two morning levels before any decision; on TRT at 3, 6 and 12 months, then a formal yearly review.
