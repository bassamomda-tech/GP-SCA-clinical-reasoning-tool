Acute and chronic scrotal pain

For: adults and young people with acute or chronic (≥3 months) scrotal pain. Not for: a painless lump or swelling (use Scrotal Lumps and Swelling). Children: local paediatric surgical pathway.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BASHH 2020NICE NG12GIRFTMHRA 2024NG193
Core rule: sudden-onset or severe unilateral scrotal pain is torsion until proven otherwise, at any age. No single sign, no normal Doppler and no response to analgesia excludes it — transfer, do not scan.

0Stop — emergency transfer now — before any test

  • Possible torsion: sudden severe unilateral pain, high or horizontal testis, nausea or vomiting, absent or uncertain cremasteric reflex, or torsion not confidently excluded → Emergency transfer to a service able to operate; 999 if unstable or unsafe to transfer.
  • Fournier's gangrene (severe pain, systemic illness, skin necrosis, bullae, crepitus, rapidly spreading swelling) or sepsis / haemodynamic instability → 999
  • Trauma with expanding haematoma, haematocele or suspected rupture; irreducible inguinoscrotal hernia (vomiting, abdominal pain) → Emergency transfer
  • While arranging: analgesia now; do not wait for ultrasound, tests or response to analgesia. Surgery is still worthwhile after 6 hours. Children → a centre able to explore, not just for an ultrasound.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Vomiting or pain preventing oral treatment, suspected abscess or infarction, immunocompromise with significant infection → Same-day hospital
  • Epididymo-orchitis not improving at 48–72 h → Same-day hospital; reconsider torsion, abscess or the wrong organism.
  • Non-painful enlargement or change in shape or texture of the testis → consider Suspected cancer pathway (NG12). Solid intratesticular lesion on USS → refer on it.
  • Unexplained or persistent testicular symptoms, persistent swelling or poor response → consider Urgent direct-access USS (NG12).

3Assess — only once block 0 is excluded

☐ Temperature, HR, BP, RR, SpO₂, sepsis check  ☐ Chaperone offered; examine standing and supine: lie, epididymis, cord, skin, hernias, abdomen, loins  ☐ Dipstick and MSU  ☐ First-pass urine NAAT before antibiotics, if no delay  ☐ HIV and syphilis tests if STI suspected

Not in primary care: ordering or awaiting USS when torsion is suspected; Prehn's sign; tumour markers that delay referral; PSA during active infection.

4Classify — only if block 0 is excluded

  • Appendage torsion — upper-pole tenderness ± blue dot; only if confident: analgesia, safety-net.
  • Epididymo-orchitis — gradual onset, tender epididymis, ± fever, urinary or urethral symptoms. Pathogen from sexual and urinary risk, not age alone.
  • Referred — scrotum often normal: renal colic, hernia, groin nerve, lumbar spine, hip.
  • Structural — hydrocele or cyst transilluminates; varicocele right-sided or non-reducing → imaging.
  • Uncertain — torsion not confidently excluded → Emergency transfer.

5Treat today — doses: NICE, SPC and local formulary

  • Likely sexually acquired: ceftriaxone 1 g IM once + doxycycline 100 mg BD 10–14 days (BASHH) via sexual health; no sex until treated, improved and partners treated.
  • Likely enteric: MSU first; BASHH/local guidance (ofloxacin 200 mg BD 14 days); fluoroquinolone only if other antibiotics are inappropriate (MHRA). Mixed risk → discuss with sexual health or microbiology.
  • Analgesia for all, never delaying transfer: paracetamol, NSAID if no contraindication, scrotal support.
  • Chronic pain: explain; activity, psychological therapy, pelvic-floor physio; no prolonged antibiotics without infection.

6Refer routinely

  • Sexual health: positive NAAT, partner notification, M. genitalium, gonorrhoea test of cure where indicated.
  • Urology: varicocele if symptomatic, right-sided, bilateral, large or with subfertility; large symptomatic hydrocele; chronic pain persistent, disabling or post-vasectomy (or pain service). Uropathogen with recurrent infection or tract abnormality → Urgent

7Never reassure by

  • A normal Doppler ultrasound, or waiting for one.
  • Pain easing with analgesia.
  • A present cremasteric reflex, a normal testicular lie or Prehn's sign.
  • Age — torsion occurs at any age; age alone does not set the pathogen.
  • Known chronic pain when new acute severe pain appears.
  • Persisting "infection" — a tumour can hide behind it.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Any new sudden or severe testicular pain — even if you have had pain there before — or scrotal skin turning dark, blistering or crackly with feeling very unwell: call 999 or go to A&E now.” Same day: “Fever not settling after 48–72 hours of antibiotics, swelling getting worse, feeling more unwell or unable to keep the tablets down — contact us today. On ofloxacin, stop and get advice if you get tendon pain, tingling, mood change or confusion.”

Follow-up: review at 48–72 h with MSU and NAAT results, then at 1–2 weeks. A new painless change in the testis → back promptly, whatever a previous scan showed.
