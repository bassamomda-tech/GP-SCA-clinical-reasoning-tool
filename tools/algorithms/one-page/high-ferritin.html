Raised ferritin in adults

For: adults with a raised serum ferritin in primary care. Not for: low ferritin or iron deficiency (use the low ferritin algorithm); known transfusional iron overload is managed by specialist haematology.

Core rule: ferritin is an acute-phase protein. Read it with CRP and transferrin saturation; a high ferritin with a normal saturation is usually inflammation, alcohol or metabolic liver disease, not iron overload. Very high ferritin in an ill patient is HLH until shown otherwise.

0Stop — act today — before anything else

  • Haemodynamically unstable, hypoxic, confused, bleeding, organ failure or clinically septic → 999 / ED
  • Ferritin >10,000 µg/L + fever, cytopenias, splenomegaly, liver dysfunction, coagulopathy or organ failure → suspect HLH/MAS → Same-day haematology or acute medicine discussion, even if stable. Untreated HLH is rapidly fatal.
  • Blood film suggesting acute leukaemia (blast cells) → Same-day haematology
  • While arranging: FBC, film, LFTs, U&E, CRP, coagulation/fibrinogen, triglycerides, LDH. Ferritin alone does not diagnose HLH; do not wait for a bone marrow.

1Triage by ferritin level

FerritinAction

>10,000 µg/LAssess for organ failure, sepsis, HLH/MAS, Still disease, liver injury, malignancy → block 0.

2Screen — serious causes

  • Haemochromatosis with organ damage: ferritin >1,000 + raised ALT/AST, hepatomegaly, bronzing or diabetes; or MCP arthritis, bronzing, diabetes, hypogonadism → Urgent (same-week) hepatology / iron-overload service
  • Unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly → consider suspected cancer pathway (NG12, lymphoma). Hepatosplenomegaly or generalised nodes → very urgent FBC within 48 h.
  • Spiking fever, salmon-pink rash, arthritis, neutrophilia, liver dysfunction (possible Still disease, once infection, malignancy and HLH excluded) → Same-day rheumatology / acute medicine
  • Many transfusions (thalassaemia, sickle cell, myelodysplasia) → specialist haematology; heart failure or arrhythmia → Urgent specialist / cardiology

3Assess

☐ Ferritin with CRP/ESR  ☐ Transferrin saturation, serum iron, transferrin/TIBC (fasting not routinely essential; repeat a borderline result)  ☐ FBC, U&E/eGFR, LFTs + GGT, HbA1c  ☐ AUDIT-C, BMI, BP; iron supplements, IV iron, transfusions; family history  ☐ Hepatitis B/C and liver ultrasound if LFTs abnormal, hepatomegaly or risk factors

TFTs, electrophoresis and malignancy tests only if symptoms or results point there. Never send HFE testing without transferrin saturation.

4Classify — transferrin saturation decides

  • Reactive (saturation normal; commonest) — inflammation, infection, liver or renal disease, malignancy → treat the cause; recheck ferritin + CRP once CRP has normalised.
  • Alcohol — often missed; ask AUDIT-C, track GGT; repeat LFTs + ferritin after 3 months' abstinence.
  • Metabolic (obesity, insulin resistance, fatty liver; saturation normal) → lifestyle (NG49); no venesection.
  • Iron overload — saturation persistently >50% men / >40% women with raised ferritin → HFE testing (C282Y/H63D) or specialist advice. Genotype alone does not diagnose.
  • C282Y/H63D or H63D/H63D — low penetrance; look for other causes. C282Y carrier — no routine monitoring.
  • Uncertain — persistent unexplained rise → hepatology / iron-overload referral.

5Treat today — only these

  • Alcohol: AUDIT-C, brief intervention, community alcohol service if needed.
  • Metabolic: weight loss, 150 min/week aerobic exercise; optimise diabetes and lipids.
  • Stop iron supplements and iron-containing multivitamins unless indicated; avoid high-dose vitamin C.
  • Haemochromatosis: balanced diet, no extreme iron restriction; no raw oysters or shellfish if significant overload or liver disease (Vibrio). Venesection is specialist-led.

6Refer routinely

  • Hepatology / iron-overload service: persistent ferritin ≥1,000; raised saturation + raised ferritin; C282Y homozygote with raised ferritin; abnormal LFTs or hepatomegaly.
  • Haemochromatosis cirrhosis: liver ultrasound every 6 months (± AFP); new nodule or rising AFP → refer immediately.
  • Relatives of a C282Y homozygote: offer adults HFE genotype, ferritin and saturation; no routine testing of children.
  • Pregnancy: venesection usually paused; cirrhosis, cardiac iron or severe overload → specialist obstetric / hepatology care.

7Never reassure by

  • A ferritin read without a CRP.
  • "It's just inflammation" without a recheck once CRP is normal.
  • Ancestry — it never rules out testing or referral.
  • An HFE genotype on its own, either way.
  • Lowering a reactive or metabolic ferritin with venesection.
  • Skipping the alcohol history.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you become confused, very breathless, collapse or are bleeding.” Same day “Contact us today if you get a fever, unusual bruising or bleeding, yellow skin, or feel very unwell.” Give the raw-shellfish warning in writing at a haemochromatosis diagnosis; book the recheck with a date.

Result received remotely: ferritin >10,000, or very high with fever or abnormal FBC → speak to the patient the same day; do not file it as a routine result.
