Suspected cancer in children and young people

For: children (under 16) and young people (16–24) with unexplained or persistent symptoms in primary care. Not for: a critically unwell child (block 0 first) or known cancer (local acute oncology pathway). Age 16–24 → local teenage and young adult pathway: paediatric, TYA or adult services.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NG127HeadSmartCCLG
Core rule: "unexplained" or "persistent" means act at the NG12 time frame, not reassure. A normal test never overrides continuing clinical or parental concern.

0Stop — call 999 now — emergency admission, not an outpatient pathway

  • Toxic or septic-looking, reduced consciousness, respiratory distress or airway compromise (possible mediastinal mass) → 999 / A&E
  • New-onset seizure, signs of raised intracranial pressure, acute severe pain with haemodynamic or neurological compromise → 999 / A&E
  • Petechiae with fever, toxic appearance, altered consciousness, shock or a rapidly progressive rash → manage as suspected sepsis 999. It may be meningococcal sepsis as well as leukaemia.
  • Do not wait for an FBC or any other test before referring these children.

1Triage by NG12 action level — write it on the referral

LevelMeaning
ImmediateAcute referral or admission within a few hours; 999 only if critically unwell
Very urgentTest or appointment within 48 hours, with an explicit plan to review and act on the result
Suspected cancer pathwayFormer "2-week wait": diagnosed or ruled out within 28 days
OtherTest, non-urgent referral or "consider referral", with safety-netting and review

2Screen — immediate features first

  • Unexplained petechiae or hepatosplenomegaly → Immediate specialist assessment for leukaemia; do not wait for an FBC.
  • Reduced consciousness, focal deficit, rapidly progressive symptoms, persistent morning headache with vomiting; infant with bulging fontanelle or regression → Immediate emergency assessment.
  • New squint with loss of the red reflex (one or both eyes) or neurological abnormality → Immediate ophthalmology (NG127).
  • Nodes with new breathlessness (possible mediastinal mass) → Same-day assessment.
  • Absent or abnormal fundal ("red") reflex, or white reflex in photos → consider Suspected cancer pathway ophthalmology.
  • Unexplained bruising or petechiae → leukaemia and non-accidental injury; FBC and clotting fit both.

3Assess — every worrying child, fully

☐ Growth; head circumference if under 2  ☐ Temperature, full skin  ☐ Nodes  ☐ Abdomen, gently and once; BP if a mass  ☐ Eyes: red reflex, squint, proptosis  ☐ Neuro: gait, coordination, cranial nerves, fundi  ☐ Bones and joints

Higher risk: Down syndrome, NF1, Li–Fraumeni, previous cancer treatment, family history of retinoblastoma, immunodeficiency.

Do not delay referral for primary-care imaging unless that is the local pathway.

4Classify — NG12 very urgent (48 h) actions

  • Leukaemia — pallor, persistent fatigue, unexplained fever, unexplained persistent infections, generalised lymphadenopathy, persistent or unexplained bone pain, unexplained bruising or bleeding → very urgent FBC ≤48 h
  • Brain/CNS — newly abnormal cerebellar or central neurology → consider referral ≤48 h. HeadSmart features (headache waking or on waking, vomiting on waking, visual, gait) → discuss or refer via local pathway.
  • Neuroblastoma / Wilms' — palpable abdominal mass, unexplained enlarged organ or visible haematuria → consider referral ≤48 h
  • Lymphoma — unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly → consider referral ≤48 h; weigh fever, night sweats, breathlessness, itch, weight loss.
  • Sarcoma — unexplained bone pain or swelling → consider X-ray ≤48 h; enlarging lump → consider ultrasound ≤48 h; suggests sarcoma or uncertain with concern → consider referral ≤48 h
  • Uncertain — repeat visits → diagnostic review: re-examine, second opinion.

5Act on results — name who checks them

  • FBC with differential, film if leukaemia suspected; clotting if bruising; urine dip.
  • Blasts, pancytopenia, severe or falling counts, abnormal film, or an unwell child → discuss with paediatrics/haematology Same-day
  • Mild isolated abnormality, well child → interpret with the laboratory, context and trend.
  • Named clinician checks the result within 48 h; tell the family how and when they will hear.

6Other action — planned review or advice

  • Persistent parental concern → consider referral, even if a benign cause is likely; reassess and review past consultations.
  • Symptoms not resolving, predisposition syndrome, or abnormal non-urgent results → planned review, paediatric advice or referral.
  • Local Greater Manchester paediatric pathway; speak clinician to clinician if concern is high.

7Never reassure by

  • A normal FBC while concern persists; reassess and discuss, do not repeat FBCs indefinitely.
  • A normal first X-ray when bone pain persists or progresses.
  • "Growing pains" or minor trauma for night pain, limp or refusal to walk.
  • The absence of fever, sweats or weight loss in a child with unexplained nodes.
  • The first explanation for bruising: leukaemia and non-accidental injury both stay open.
  • Calling the parent "anxious", or a young person presenting late or alone.

8Safety-net — say it, write it, document it

999 “Call 999 if spots or bruises come with a fever, or your child is drowsy, floppy or very unwell.” Same-day “If new spots or bruises appear, or they become more unwell, come straight back today, or call 111 out of hours.”

Explain the 48-hour plan as precautionary and who will contact them. Give written advice; investigate until symptoms are explained or resolved.
