Thrombocytopenia — low platelet count

For: adults with platelets <150 × 10⁹/L on an FBC, well or unwell, including pregnancy (block 2). Not for: children (use paediatric pathways). Local haematology pathway sets thresholds and repeat intervals.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BSH TTP 2023BSH HIT 2023NICE NG24ITP consensus 2019Green Book ch 7 & 25
Core rule: the count alone does not set bleeding risk or urgency. Bleeding, a rapid fall, other cytopenias or a haemolytic (TTP) picture do. Never delay urgent referral to exclude pseudothrombocytopenia.

0Stop — call 999 now — at any platelet count

  • Major bleeding (GI, haemoptysis, heavy haematuria, retinal) or severe headache, confusion, focal weakness, seizures, reduced consciousness (ICH, TTP) → 999
  • TTP/TMA: low platelets + haemolysis (schistocytes, raised LDH, low haptoglobin) ± neurological, renal, cardiac or GI features → Emergency discussion with the regional TTP/haematology service. Do not wait for the pentad or ADAMTS13.
  • DIC (bleeding from several sites, hypotension, confusion); non-blanching rash with fever or unwell; HIT with thrombosis → 999. Pregnant with hypertension, headache, visual change or epigastric pain → Emergency obstetric (pre-eclampsia/HELLP).
  • While waiting: suspected HIT → stop all heparin (including flushes). Do not stop anticoagulants or antiplatelets automatically — discuss. No routine platelet transfusion in ITP, HIT or TTP.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Non-blanching petechiae, extensive purpura, gum or nose bleeding → Same-day FBC, film and haematology discussion
  • Schistocytes or blasts on film, pancytopenia, clinically significant bleeding → Same-day haematology
  • Recent heparin, quinine, antibiotics or chemotherapy + platelet fall → Same-day haematology (drug-induced or HIT); HIT: 4Ts score.
  • Pregnant, new, falling or moderate fall → Same-day obstetric/haematology: exclude pre-eclampsia/HELLP, ITP, TTP, acute fatty liver.

3Assess — only once blocks 0–2 are answered

☐ Previous counts, lab comment  ☐ Repeat FBC + film  ☐ Obs; skin, mucosa, spleen, liver, nodes  ☐ Clotting + fibrinogen if DIC, liver disease or bleeding  ☐ LFT, reticulocytes, LDH, haptoglobin  ☐ Drugs, alcohol, recent viral illness

Suspected ITP: HIV, hepatitis B/C, ANA, immunoglobulins per context. Pseudothrombocytopenia: only in a well patient without bleeding — citrate sample if clumping. Not in primary care: bone marrow biopsy, antiplatelet antibodies.

4Classify — read the film

  • Clumps, well patient — pseudothrombocytopenia (EDTA artefact) → citrate count; no treatment.
  • Isolated, normal Hb/WCC — ITP (diagnosis of exclusion, haematology-confirmed), drugs, alcohol/liver, viral. Marrow disease not excluded.
  • Other cytopenias — marrow (MDS, leukaemia, aplasia), B12 deficiency, hypersplenism → pancytopenia is Same-day.
  • Gestational — only if late, isolated, no hypertension or proteinuria, normal LFTs and haemolysis markers, no bleeding or past ITP.
  • Post-viral — recovering on repeat; escalate if falling, symptomatic or other lines affected.
  • Uncertain — persistent, unexplained after initial tests → haematology.

5Treat today — only these

  • Suspected culprit drug: stopping may be right, but not an essential medicine without an alternative. HIT needs a non-heparin anticoagulant.
  • Avoid NSAIDs/aspirin with significant thrombocytopenia or bleeding unless haematology advises; paracetamol instead. Reduce alcohol.
  • ITP: observe or treat (steroids, IVIg) is a haematology decision. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • <100, persistent or falling; splenomegaly; dysplasia on film; B symptoms → discuss or refer per local pathway.
  • Suspected or known ITP → haematology (confirmation and management are haematology-led).
  • Surgery, dental work, IM injection, neuraxial anaesthesia → agree thresholds with haematology.
  • Splenectomy → vaccinate ideally ≥2 weeks before (Green Book ch 7 and 25).

7Never reassure by

  • A count above a "safe" number — a rapid fall matters more.
  • Calling it EDTA artefact with bleeding, other cytopenias or an abnormal film.
  • Waiting for the full TTP pentad or an ADAMTS13 result.
  • Calling it gestational before pre-eclampsia/HELLP is excluded.
  • Stopping heparin alone in suspected HIT.
  • Normal Hb and WCC — marrow disease is not excluded.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for a severe headache, weakness, a fit or confusion; vomiting blood or black stools; coughing blood or severe breathlessness; sudden loss of vision; bleeding from several places; heavy blood in the urine or feeling faint; or a rash that does not fade under pressure with a fever.” Pregnant: severe headache, vision changes or upper abdominal pain → emergency maternity.

Same day “New spreading spots or bruises, a nosebleed not stopping after 10–15 minutes of pressure (go to A&E), soaking a pad every hour, gums that keep bleeding, or blood in the urine — contact us today.” Any bleeding with platelets <20 is same-day. Follow-up: repeat FBC + film; re-refer if falling, new bleeding or <20.
