Excessive daytime sleepiness — adults

For: adults who doze or struggle to stay awake in the day. Not for: children; tiredness without dozing (use the fatigue algorithm); poor night sleep as the main problem (insomnia algorithm).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG202DVLANICE CKS
Core rule: separate acute drowsiness (an emergency) from chronic sleepiness, and give the driving advice today — sleepiness that affects driving means no driving until it is controlled.

0Stop — call 999 now — acute drowsiness is not sleepiness

  • New drowsiness over hours to days with confusion, headache, fever, neck stiffness, focal signs or vomiting → 999
  • Several people at home drowsy or with headaches → possible carbon monoxide: leave the property, 999
  • Unconscious or fitting with hypoglycaemia → 999

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Breathlessness, morning headache, cyanosis, ankle swelling, low resting SpO₂ → hypercapnic failure Same-day
  • Hypoglycaemia on insulin or a sulfonylurea → glucose, treat Same-day
  • Falling asleep at the wheel or a near miss → stop driving now, document, expedite referral Same-day
  • Group 2 driver or safety-critical worker with sleepiness → expedited sleep service Urgent
  • Sudden sleep attacks on a dopamine agonist → no driving, review medicine Urgent
  • Low mood with suicidal thoughts → crisis Same-day

3Assess — scores support, history decides

☐ Sleep on work days and days off; 2-week sleep diary  ☐ Partner account: snoring, apnoeas, kicking  ☐ Sedating medicines, alcohol, cannabis  ☐ BMI, neck, BP, airway, thyroid, SpO₂  ☐ Epworth (above 10 excessive) and STOP-BANG (3 or more raises OSA risk)  ☐ Licence group, occupation

Bloods by indication: FBC + ferritin, TSH, HbA1c, U&E, LFTs. Sleep studies are arranged by the sleep service.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Insufficient sleep (commonest) — catch-up at weekends.
  • OSA — 2+ features (NG202) → sleep service.
  • Drugs or alcohol — sedatives, gabapentinoids, opioids, antihistamines → reduce or re-time.
  • Depression, hypothyroidism, anaemia, diabetes, restless legs → treat the cause.
  • Narcolepsy or hypersomnia → neurology or sleep medicine.
  • Uncertain — sleep service after the primary-care work-up.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Driving today: must not drive while sleepiness affects driving; suspected or mild OSA — notify the DVLA if not controlled within 3 months; moderate or severe OSA or narcolepsy — must notify. Document.
  • Suspected OSA: refer; weight loss, less evening alcohol, avoid sedatives, side sleeping.
  • Sleep: 7–9 hours for most adults; regular times; protected sleep after night shifts.
  • Do not start modafinil or other wake-promoting drugs in primary care.

6Refer routinely

  • Suspected OSA with 2+ features, or unexplained sleepiness → sleep service.
  • Suspected narcolepsy, idiopathic hypersomnia, REM sleep behaviour disorder → neurology or sleep medicine.
  • Weight management; occupational health for shift work; drug and alcohol services.

7Never reassure by

  • A low Epworth score when the history describes dozing at the wheel.
  • "Just snoring" without asking about apnoeas and sleepiness.
  • Fatigue and sleepiness treated as the same complaint.
  • Leaving driving advice until the sleep study result.
  • Sedatives for poor sleep in suspected OSA without caution.
  • Missing carbon monoxide when the household is affected.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for sudden drowsiness with confusion, severe headache, weakness or fits — or if several people at home are drowsy or have headaches: leave and call 999.” Same-day “Contact us today if you get breathless, swollen ankles or blue lips. Don’t drive while you feel sleepy — not until it is controlled; we will guide you on telling the DVLA.”

Follow-up: 2–4 weeks — results, sleep diary, Epworth; re-check driving at each contact.
