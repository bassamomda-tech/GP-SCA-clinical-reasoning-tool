Abnormal immunoglobulins and paraprotein

For: adults in primary care with a paraprotein, abnormal serum free light chains (FLC), a polyclonal rise or low immunoglobulins. Not for: diagnosing or treating myeloma, Waldenström, amyloid or CVID (specialist-led). Suspected cord compression: MSCC protocol. Local laboratory comments and haematology advice take precedence.

NICE NG35NG12 (April 2026)NG234BSH MGUS 2023BSH transfusion 2023
Core rule: a paraprotein level alone neither diagnoses nor excludes myeloma, and no single test excludes it. Attributable CRAB features, serum FLC and symptoms decide the route; MGUS is monitored only to a written haematology plan.

0Emergency now — do not delay transfer for imaging or specialist confirmation

  • Leg weakness, gait disturbance, sensory loss, radicular pain, saddle anaesthesia, bladder or bowel dysfunction (suspected MSCC) → Immediately MSCC coordinator / acute oncology; whole-spine MRI within 24 h; dexamethasone 16 mg orally as soon as possible.
  • IgM paraprotein + visual disturbance, headache, mucosal bleeding, confusion, dizziness or breathlessness (hyperviscosity) → Immediately haematology / acute oncology discussion; severe → emergency admission.
  • Severe or symptomatic hypercalcaemia → Same-day hospital; emergency with confusion, reduced consciousness, vomiting, dehydration, arrhythmia, AKI or calcium around/above 3.0 mmol/L.
  • AKI with suspected myeloma · sepsis on myeloma treatment · unstable pathological fracture → Emergency acute oncology / emergency pathway.

1Triage — what is the pattern?

ResultMeans / next

Polyclonal riseInfection (HIV, hepatitis, TB), cirrhosis, autoimmune, sarcoid, lymphoma → find the cause

2Screen — suspected myeloma

  • Paraprotein or abnormal FLC + attributable CRAB: calcium >2.75 mmol/L (or >0.25 above upper limit) · creatinine >177 µmol/L or clearance <40 mL/min · Hb <100 g/L (or >20 g/L below lower limit) · lytic lesion → Suspected cancer haematology.
  • Paraprotein or abnormal FLC with bone pain, fracture, IgD/IgE, IgG >15 g/L, IgA/IgM >10 g/L → Suspected cancer haematology.
  • 60+ with persistent bone or back pain, or unexplained fracture → myeloma bloods incl. SPEP and serum FLC (NG12).
  • Restrictive cardiomyopathy, nephrotic syndrome, neuropathy, macroglossia → amyloid screen (SPEP, immunofixation, FLC).

3Assess

☐ SPEP + immunofixation  ☐ Serum FLC and ratio (renal-adjusted range)  ☐ FBC, adjusted calcium, eGFR, IgG/IgA/IgM, ESR or PV  ☐ Urine Bence Jones only if FLC unavailable

Not in primary care: myeloma imaging (via haematology; no isotope bone scan), bisphosphonate, denosumab, immunoglobulin replacement.

4Classify — a haematology diagnosis; only if blocks 0–2 negative

  • MGUS — paraprotein <30 g/L, marrow plasma cells <10%, no CRAB; about 1% a year progress.
  • Smouldering myeloma — paraprotein >30 g/L or 10–60% plasma cells, no CRAB → haematology-led.
  • Myeloma — clonal plasma cells + CRAB or SLiM (e.g. FLC ratio ≥100, involved FLC ≥100 mg/L).
  • IgM paraprotein — Waldenström more often than myeloma; ask about hyperviscosity.
  • Uncertain — local haematology advice and guidance.

5Treat today — only these

  • Coeliac testing: always check total IgA with anti-tTG IgA; IgA deficient → anti-tTG IgG or anti-DGP IgG.
  • Possible myeloma: keep hydrated; avoid NSAIDs, iodinated contrast, aminoglycosides.
  • Myeloma or antibody deficiency: prompt antibiotics; vaccines per Green Book, no live vaccines. Lenalidomide: thromboprophylaxis and pregnancy prevention in place. Doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • IgA deficiency: transfusion alert, not drug allergy; past severe reaction → ≤7 days transfusion medicine advice.

6Refer routinely

  • New paraprotein, no concerning features or CRAB → Routine haematology for MGUS characterisation and risk staging.
  • Persistent low IgG with recurrent sinopulmonary infections, bronchiectasis or poor vaccine responses → clinical immunology.
  • GP-monitored MGUS only if the haematology letter delegates it: about 6 months, then yearly or longer if low risk.
  • MGUS: M-protein up >25% and ≥5 g/L → ≤7 days re-refer; new anaemia or renal impairment → Suspected cancer.

7Never reassure by

  • A low paraprotein, or a normal SPEP when suspicion persists.
  • Never diagnose myeloma from a paraprotein level or isolated FLC ratio.
  • A negative anti-tTG IgA without a total IgA.
  • A polyclonal rise without looking for its cause.
  • Never start bisphosphonate or denosumab in primary care for suspected myeloma.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 or go to A&E straight away for new leg weakness or numbness, loss of bladder or bowel control, confusion, sudden blurred vision, severe headache or unusual bleeding, or fever during myeloma treatment.” Same-day “Contact us the same day for new or severe bone or back pain, extreme thirst or passing lots of urine, or a new infection.”

Review: MGUS checks per the haematology plan; contact us sooner for tiredness, repeated infections or new swelling. MGUS is not myeloma; most never progress.
