Unilateral leg swelling

For: adults with one swollen leg in primary care. Not for: bilateral swelling (ankle swelling algorithm); pregnancy or postpartum (same-day maternity/medical pathway); children.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG158NG141NG12NG253
Core rule: one swollen leg is a DVT until excluded — Wells score, D-dimer and a scan within 4 hours, or interim anticoagulation and a scan within 24 hours. Ask about the chest every time.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Breathless, pleuritic pain, haemoptysis or collapse (PE) → 999
  • Massive blue or white leg (phlegmasia) or cold pulseless limb → 999
  • Pain out of proportion: compartment syndrome (pain on passive stretch) or necrotising infection → 999
  • High-risk sepsis with spreading cellulitis → 999

1Triage — two-level Wells (NG158)

WellsAction

2Screen

  • Cellulitis with moderate sepsis risk or Eron III–IV → Same-day
  • Superficial thrombosis in the thigh or near a junction → Same-day scan
  • Hot swollen foot in diabetic neuropathy (Charcot) → Same-day foot team
  • Pelvic mass, groin nodes, unexplained lymphadenopathy → Suspected cancer pathway per NG12

3Assess

☐ Obs incl. SpO₂ ☐ Both calves measured (3 cm or more scores) ☐ Pitting, warmth, edge marked, entry wound ☐ Knee (Baker's), compartments ☐ Pulses before compression ☐ Abdomen, pelvis, groin nodes ☐ Before anticoagulation: FBC, U&E (Cockcroft–Gault), LFT, PT, APTT — do not wait

Avoid: Homan's sign; treating a "Baker's cyst" before a scan.

4Classify

  • DVT — scan positive → DOAC at least 3 months.
  • Cellulitis — acute spreading redness, fever, portal of entry.
  • Baker's cyst / calf tear — knee arthritis, sudden pain; scan shows it.
  • Venous / post-thrombotic — gradual, skin changes, old DVT.
  • Lymphoedema or extrinsic compression — non-pitting, Stemmer, pelvic mass.
  • Uncertain — scan negative: re-examine in 1–2 weeks; image abdomen/pelvis if unexplained.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Interim or confirmed DVT: apixaban or rivaroxaban, dose per SPC; specialist if cancer, APS, severe renal impairment.
  • Cellulitis: flucloxacillin 500 mg–1 g four times a day 5–7 days; allergy: clarithromycin 500 mg twice a day.
  • Baker's / tear (after scan): analgesia, elevation.
  • Venous: compression only after pulses ± ABPI.

6Refer routinely

  • Suspected lymphoedema (after DVT and pelvic cause excluded) → lymphoedema service.
  • Venous skin changes or ulcer → vascular / leg-ulcer pathway.

7Never reassure by

  • A negative Homan's sign.
  • No risk factors — many DVTs are unprovoked.
  • "Baker's cyst" before an ultrasound.
  • Redness — cellulitis and DVT coexist.
  • A raised D-dimer "because they are old" without a scan.
  • "Lymphoedema" without examining abdomen, pelvis and groin.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for sudden breathlessness, chest pain, coughing blood or collapse, or if the leg turns cold, white or blue.” Same-day “Contact us today if the redness spreads past the line, you get a fever, or the swelling or pain keeps getting worse.” Confirm the scan within 24 h.
