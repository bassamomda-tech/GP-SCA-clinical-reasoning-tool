Low folate in adults

For: adults with a low serum folate or suspected folate deficiency in primary care, including pregnancy, preconception and methotrexate. Not for: B12 deficiency with normal folate (use the vitamin B12 deficiency algorithm) or macrocytosis with normal folate (use the macrocytosis algorithm).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG239NG247CG32BSR / SPS methotrexateMHRA valproate
Core rule: check B12 before (or alongside) folic acid. Folic acid can correct the blood count while B12 nerve damage carries on.

0Stop — act today — before anything else

  • Unstable, septic, actively bleeding, confused or unable to travel safely → 999
  • Severe or symptomatic anaemia, pancytopenia with infection or bleeding, suspected acute leukaemia/MDS, rapidly falling counts → Same-day hospital / haematology
  • Gait disturbance, loss of proprioception, weakness, cognitive or visual change (possible subacute combined degeneration) → take FBC, B12/active B12 and folate, then start B12 Same-day + urgent neurology/haematology advice. Never folic acid alone.
  • Confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia (Wernicke) or severe malnutrition → Same-day emergency hospital; suspected Wernicke's → immediate transfer, parenteral thiamine first if available (oral not adequate); never delay it for glucose.

1Triage — B12 first, then the context

SituationAction

2Screen — serious causes

  • Methotrexate + mouth ulcers, cytopenia or macrocytosis: withhold if significant cytopenia, severe mucositis, suspected sepsis, renal or significant LFT deterioration; FBC, LFTs, U&E → specialist / acute team Same-day. Folinic acid is specialist-led.
  • Pregnant + severe anaemia, significant vomiting/dehydration, neurological disease or another obstetric concern → Same-day maternity. Low folate alone and well → primary care.
  • Chronic haemolysis + sudden Hb fall, rising bilirubin/LDH, low reticulocytes (aplastic crisis, marrow disease) → Urgent haematology
  • Refeeding risk (BMI <16, little or no intake >10 days, >15% weight loss in 3–6 months) → thiamine before feeding (CG32), dietitian.

3Assess

☐ FBC, MCV, reticulocytes; film if macrocytosis or cytopenias  ☐ Serum folate  ☐ B12 or active B12 before folic acid  ☐ Anti-tTG IgA + total IgA, while eating gluten  ☐ LFTs + GGT, ferritin; TFTs if macrocytosis unexplained  ☐ Diet, AUDIT-C, medicines, pregnancy plans, haemolysis, bowel surgery

Not routine: red-cell folate, homocysteine, methylmalonic acid.

4Classify — find the cause

  • Diet / alcohol (commonest) — older people living alone, eating disorders, alcohol.
  • Coeliac disease — iron + folate deficiency together; the MCV can look normal → anti-tTG; positive → gastroenterology.
  • Increased demand — pregnancy, lactation, haemolysis, exfoliative skin disease, malignancy, dialysis.
  • Medicines — methotrexate, trimethoprim, sulfasalazine, phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, primidone.
  • B12 + folate together → treat B12 first, or both at once.
  • Uncertain / poor response → reconsider B12, iron, haemolysis, alcohol, thyroid, MDS, adherence.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Deficiency: folic acid 5 mg daily; 4 months suits many adults, longer if malabsorption or haemolysis continues.
  • Preconception to 12 weeks: 400 micrograms daily. 5 mg daily if previous NTD pregnancy, personal/family NTD history, type 1 or 2 diabetes, sickle cell/thalassaemia, some anti-epileptic or HIV medicines. BMI alone does not need 5 mg (NG247).
  • Methotrexate: folic acid 5 mg once weekly on a different day, for as long as methotrexate continues.
  • Anti-epileptics: review folate with neurology if pregnant or planning; never stop them abruptly. Valproate → Pregnancy Prevention Programme.

6Refer routinely

  • Gastroenterology: positive coeliac serology, suspected malabsorption. Dietitian: severe or multiple deficiencies, eating disorder, new coeliac disease.
  • Recheck FBC at about 4–8 weeks if anaemic or macrocytic; do not recheck serum folate on treatment.

7Never reassure by

  • A rising Hb or falling MCV on folic acid — B12 nerve damage can still progress.
  • A normal MCV — combined iron and folate deficiency can hide it.
  • A normal serum folate after a recent good diet — it reflects the last few days.
  • Folic acid in someone taking valproate — it does not remove the risk to a baby.
  • Methotrexate prescribed without weekly folic acid.
  • A negative coeliac test done on a gluten-free diet.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you collapse, become confused, are bleeding heavily, or are very unwell with a fever.” Same day “Contact us today if you get numbness, tingling, unsteady walking, weakness, memory or eyesight changes, unusual bruising or bleeding, or — on methotrexate — mouth ulcers or fever.” Book the FBC recheck with a date.
