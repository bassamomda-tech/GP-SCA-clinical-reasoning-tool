Vaginal mass

For: women and girls with a vaginal lump, bulge or mass in primary care. Not for: vulval lesions and Bartholin disease (vulvar disorders algorithm) or long-term prolapse care (prolapse protocol). Pregnancy and prepubertal children go straight to blocks 0 and 2.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG123NG12NG126RCOG/BSUG GTG 46
Core rule: first decide what the “mass” is. Most are prolapse or benign cysts, but an irregular, friable or bleeding lesion, postmenopausal bleeding, or any vaginal mass in a child needs the cancer route. Manage prolapse by symptoms, not stage.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any further examination

  • Rapidly enlarging pelvic mass with severe pain, peritonism, instability or collapse in a woman of reproductive age (torsion, ruptured ectopic) → 999 IV access; hCG in any reproductive-age woman.
  • Prolapsed mass that will not reduce, with haemorrhage, vascular congestion or necrosis (incarcerated prolapse, prolapsed fibroid with torsion) → 999 Do not reduce a necrotic, bleeding, severely painful or suspicious mass.
  • Swelling with fever, spreading erythema or crepitus (necrotising fasciitis) → 999
  • Pregnant, antepartum haemorrhage, friable cervix → Same-day obstetric; 999 if heavy bleeding or unstable. No digital vaginal examination until placenta praevia is excluded.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Suspicious cervix (irregular, friable, bleeding), suspicious vaginal lesion or mass, suspected VAIN → Suspected cancer pathway for colposcopy and biopsy. No smear from it.
  • Postmenopausal bleeding, not attributable to HRT: aged 55 and over → Suspected cancer pathway; under 55 consider it (NG12).
  • New unexplained discharge at 55 or over → check the NG12 direct-access ultrasound criterion.
  • Prepubertal child: vaginal mass, bleeding or persistent discharge → Urgent paediatric gynaecology via the local sarcoma pathway + safeguarding. No biopsy in primary care.
  • New Bartholin mass at 40 or over → Urgent gynaecology. Bartholin abscess → Same-day gynaecology
  • Acute urinary retention with prolapse → Same-day bladder decompression; urgent gynaecology or urology advice.

3Assess — face to face

☐ Inspect vulva and introitus  ☐ Speculum where appropriate and consented; see the cervix if bleeding or discharge  ☐ Prolapse on cough and strain; urinary, bowel and sexual symptoms  ☐ Pregnancy test where relevant; urine if urinary symptoms; swabs only if infection suspected

Transvaginal ultrasound for uterine or adnexal pathology or an unclear mass; none for obvious uncomplicated prolapse. Not in primary care: urodynamics; CA125 just because of a vaginal mass.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Prolapse (cystocele, rectocele, uterine, vault) — dragging, worse standing, eased lying. Simplified stages are not POP-Q.
  • Gartner duct cyst — smooth, non-tender, anterolateral. Observe only if small, typical and asymptomatic.
  • Cervical polyp — smooth, red, pedunculated. Remove only if trained, clearly benign and locally supported; histology always.
  • Nabothian cyst, cervical ectopy — benign; reassure.
  • Pyometra — older woman, offensive discharge, fever → ultrasound + Urgent gynaecology (exclude endometrial cancer).
  • Uncertain — say so and refer to gynaecology; do not label it a cyst.

5Treat today — prolapse

  • Asymptomatic: reassure, advise, safety-net. Weight loss if BMI over 30; treat constipation and cough; stop smoking; limit heavy lifting.
  • Symptomatic stage 1–2: consider supervised pelvic-floor muscle training for at least 16 weeks (NG123).
  • Pessary: consider for symptomatic prolapse at any stage. Removed and vagina inspected at least every 6 months, by trained clinicians.
  • Vaginal oestrogen: consider for genitourinary symptoms, atrophy or pessary mucosal problems; specialist advice after hormone-sensitive breast cancer. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • Prolapse affecting quality of life, bladder or bowel dysfunction, failed conservative care, or wishes pessary or surgery → urogynaecology (not stage alone).
  • Atypical, enlarging or uncertain vaginal-wall cyst; large or atypical polyp; symptomatic vault prolapse → gynaecology.
  • Persistent postcoital bleeding with a normal cervix → local colposcopy or gynaecology pathway. Mesh symptoms → NHS mesh centre.

7Never reassure by

  • A normal smear or HPV result — not a test for a symptomatic cervix.
  • Calling a vaginal-wall mass a Gartner cyst when features are not clearly typical.
  • Labelling postmenopausal discharge as BV, atrophy or prolapse without assessment.
  • A low prolapse stage — symptoms rarely match stage.
  • A normal-looking cervix when postcoital bleeding persists.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for sudden severe pelvic pain, fainting or collapse, or a lump that is stuck out, very painful, bleeding heavily or turning dark.” Same day: “Contact us today if you cannot pass urine, have fever with a painful swelling, or bleed in pregnancy.”

Remote or OOH: a vaginal mass needs a face-to-face examination; any block 0 feature → 999. Pessary users: clear instructions and access to review for bleeding, pain, discharge, retention or expulsion.
