Raised prolactin (hyperprolactinaemia)

For: adults with a raised prolactin, or galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea or oligomenorrhoea, low libido or ED, in primary care. Not for: cabergoline titration, withdrawal trials or prolactinoma in pregnancy (endocrinology leads; do not measure prolactin in pregnancy).

Core rule: exclude a stressed sample, pregnancy, hypothyroidism, drugs, renal or liver disease and macroprolactin before imaging — but a new visual-field defect or a sudden severe headache never waits for those tests.

0Stop — call 999 now — possible pituitary apoplexy

  • Sudden severe (thunderclap) headache with a known pituitary adenoma, or with visual disturbance, double vision, vomiting, collapse or altered consciousness — even with no known pituitary disease (often the first presentation) → 999 / A&E. It can mimic subarachnoid haemorrhage.
  • While waiting: if adrenal insufficiency is suspected or the patient is unstable → hydrocortisone 100 mg parenterally per the emergency protocol. Do not delay transfer.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Visual or cranial nerve symptoms, significant or progressive headache, hypopituitarism → Urgent endocrine advice ± MRI
  • Prolactin >5000 mIU/L, no drug cause → strongly suggests prolactinoma → Urgent endocrinology
  • On an antipsychotic, prolactin >2000 mIU/L, symptomatic or rising (Greater Manchester trigger) → Urgent endocrine / psychiatry discussion
  • Pregnant with a macroprolactinoma or tumour near the chiasm → Urgent endocrinology
  • Aged ≥50, discharge, retraction or other change in one nipple only → Suspected cancer pathway (NG12), whatever the prolactin. Under 50: local breast pathway.
  • Pituitary mass with a modest prolactin → ask the lab to dilute (hook effect).

3Assess — before any imaging

☐ Visual fields (confrontation screens only; formal perimetry where indicated), eye movements, neurology  ☐ Pregnancy test  ☐ TSH ± free T4  ☐ Renal and liver function  ☐ Full medicine and recreational drug review  ☐ Pituitary function if a lesion is suspected

Use the lab's sex-specific range and units. Not in primary care: MRI for a single mildly raised result; repeated breast stimulation.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Physiological — pregnancy, lactation, stress, sex, nipple stimulation → repeat rested.
  • Macroprolactin — PEG positive, normal monomeric prolactin, no symptoms or visual, neurological or pituitary signs → reassure, explain, discharge.
  • Drugs (commonest pathological) — antipsychotics, metoclopramide, domperidone, SSRIs, opioids, methyldopa, verapamil → prescriber review.
  • Systemic — hypothyroidism (TSH first), CKD, cirrhosis, PCOS, chest-wall injury or zoster.
  • Prolactinoma or stalk compression — unexplained persistent rise → endocrine advice ± MRI.
  • Uncertain — still raised after medication review → endocrine advice ± MRI; do not blame the drug.

5Treat today — only these

  • Drug cause: discuss stopping, reducing or switching with the prescriber where safe; repeat after the agreed interval. Never stop an antipsychotic without the mental-health team; no empirical dopamine agonist.
  • Hypothyroidism: treat it; prolactin normalises.
  • On cabergoline (specialist-initiated): check BP, adherence, impulse-control, psychiatric effects and sleepiness; never change the dose or stop it without specialist advice. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.
  • Driving: visual-field defect → do not drive, notify DVLA; cabergoline sleepiness → do not drive if affected. Document it.

6Refer routinely

  • Microprolactinoma on MRI; complex macroprolactin or persistent monomeric rise → endocrinology.
  • Symptomatic drug-induced rise, drug cannot change → endocrinology; antipsychotic → psychiatry (any switch is their decision).
  • Prolonged hypogonadism or amenorrhoea → fracture-risk assessment. Planning pregnancy on cabergoline → endocrinology.

7Never reassure by

  • No known pituitary disease, when a sudden severe headache comes with visual signs.
  • Normal confrontation fields — they only screen.
  • A convincing drug cause, when prolactin stays raised after review.
  • A modest prolactin with a pituitary mass (hook effect or stalk compression).
  • "It's just prolactin" when one nipple changes at ≥50.
  • The prolactin level alone — it does not reliably predict symptom severity.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for a sudden, severe headache, especially with loss of vision, double vision, vomiting, collapse or confusion.” Same day “Any new blurring or loss of side vision — contact us today.” Within a week “If the discharge becomes one-sided or bloodstained, or the nipple pulls in, contact the practice within a week.”

On cabergoline: “Tell us about urges to gamble, spend or binge, mood change or sudden sleepiness — and don't drive if sleepy.” Macroprolactin: explain it in plain words so the patient does not feel dismissed.
