Breast lump and breast symptoms

For: adults (women and men) with a breast lump, nipple or skin change, axillary lump or breast pain in primary care. Not for: breast pain alone with a normal examination (use the mastalgia algorithm); nipple eczema or galactorrhoea (nipple problems algorithm).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NICE CKSNICE CG164ABS
Core rule: the referral is decided by age and examination findings, not by how benign a lump feels. If an NG12 criterion is met, refer first — do not arrange imaging first.

0Stop — call 999 now — rare in breast presentations

  • Breast infection with septic shock — hypotension, confusion, mottled skin → 999
  • Known breast cancer + back pain with leg weakness, sensory level or sphincter change (spinal cord compression) → 999 / immediate MSCC pathway

1Triage by age and finding — NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)

FindingAction
Lump, 30 and overUnexplained, with or without pain (women and men) → Suspected cancer referral
One nipple, 50 and overDischarge, retraction or other change of concern → Suspected cancer referral
Skin changeSuggestive of cancer, any age → consider Suspected cancer referral
Axilla, 30 and overUnexplained lump → consider Suspected cancer referral
Lump, under 30Consider non-urgent breast clinic; sooner if hard, fixed, enlarging or strong family history

2Screen

  • Hot, tender breast with fever, rigors, spreading cellulitis or fluctuant mass → Same-day (abscess, sepsis)
  • "Mastitis" not breastfeeding, or not settling after one course → breast clinic; suspicious skin or lump → suspected cancer referral (inflammatory cancer)
  • Male: hard, eccentric or fixed lump, nipple or skin change → Suspected cancer referral
  • Known cancer + bone pain, confusion or thirst → Same-day acute oncology

3Assess — chaperone offered and documented

☐ Inspect sitting, arms down then raised  ☐ Palpate both breasts, axillary tail, behind the nipple  ☐ Axillary and supraclavicular nodes  ☐ Describe lump: site, size in cm, mobility, tethering  ☐ Family history (CG164), HRT, pregnancy  ☐ Men: testes, liver, drugs

Do not: request mammography or ultrasound before referral, do fine-needle aspiration, or squeeze the nipple repeatedly.

4Classify — examination cannot exclude cancer

  • Fibroadenoma — under 30, smooth, very mobile → non-urgent clinic.
  • Cyst — 35 to menopause, smooth, may appear suddenly → over 30 still suspected cancer referral.
  • Nodularity — diffuse, bilateral, cyclical; no discrete lump → reassure, re-examine.
  • Mastitis — lactational or periductal (smokers) → treat; review at 48 hours.
  • Gynaecomastia — rubbery disc under the areola → bloods, testes, drug review.
  • Uncertain — any doubt in a discrete lump → refer on the NG12 age criteria.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • NG12 met: refer today; explain, check contact details for the next 2 weeks.
  • Lactational mastitis: keep feeding or expressing, analgesia; if indicated flucloxacillin 500 mg four times daily for 10–14 days.
  • Periductal mastitis: antibiotic per local pathway (consider co-amoxiclav) + stop smoking.
  • Pain, normal exam: supportive bra, paracetamol or topical NSAID (mastalgia algorithm).

6Refer routinely

  • Under 30 with a lump; nipple discharge under 50 per local pathway; persistent focal pain → breast clinic.
  • Family history at moderate or high risk (CG164) → family history service or genetics.
  • Gynaecomastia with abnormal bloods → endocrinology; raised hCG/AFP or testicular lump → urgent urology.

7Never reassure by

  • A smooth, mobile or tender lump in a patient aged 30 and over.
  • A recent normal screening mammogram.
  • A normal scan arranged from primary care.
  • Calling a male breast lump gynaecomastia without examining.
  • Repeated antibiotics for a breast that will not settle.
  • A previous benign result for a new change.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if your breast is hot and painful and you have a high fever, shivering, confusion or feel faint.” Same day “Contact us the same day if redness spreads quickly, you feel unwell with a breast infection, or you are worse after 48 hours of antibiotics.”

Referred: contact the practice if no appointment within a week; diagnosis or cancer ruled out within 28 days. Return with any new lump, dimpling, nipple turning in or one-sided bloodstained discharge.

✓Safe to manage in primary care only if — all five, documented

No NG12 criterion met; no block 0–2 feature Both breasts, axillae and nodes examined; chaperone offered Age, family history, pregnancy and medicines recorded Diagnosis named; review booked Safety-net given; screening and breast awareness discussed
