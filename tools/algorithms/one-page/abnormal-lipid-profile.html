Abnormal lipid profile in adults

For: adults with an abnormal cholesterol, HDL or triglyceride (TG) result in primary care. Not for: children (paediatric FH pathways) or treatment detail (High cholesterol protocol). TG-led results → High triglycerides pathway.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG238CG71QS100QRISK3HEART UK
Core rule: a lipid result is a long-term risk factor, not an emergency — except TG with abdominal pain or vomiting. Exclude secondary causes and suspected FH before you reach for QRISK3; QRISK3 never applies to FH or established CVD.

0Stop — act now — the lipid result never lowers the urgency

  • Acute, ongoing or recent chest pain → 999 as possible ACS, whatever the cholesterol shows.
  • Raised TG with abdominal pain or vomiting, any level (can occur below 10 mmol/L) → Same-day hospital, 999 if unwell. Do not wait for a fasting repeat.
  • Thirst, polyuria, weight loss or vomiting with raised TG → capillary glucose and ketones now; raised or unwell → Same-day hospital (DKA/HHS).
  • Heavy proteinuria, oedema, low albumin (nephrotic syndrome) → Urgent medical assessment; AKI, thrombosis, infection or instability → Same-day hospital.

2Screen for FH — NICE CG71, before any risk score

  • Suspect FH: TC >7.5 or LDL >4.9, and/or CHD before 60 in the patient or a first-degree relative; tendon xanthomata.
  • Assess: exclude secondary causes, 2 LDL measurements, family history, then Simon Broome or DLCN.
  • Possible or definite Simon Broome, or DLCN >5 → specialist FH service for DNA testing and cascade testing of relatives.
  • Records search: under 30, TC >7.5; 30 or over, TC >9.0.

3Assess — is it real, and why?

☐ Non-fasting full profile is enough; fasting only if TG ≥4.5 or unexpected  ☐ Repeat when well (acute illness lowers cholesterol); pregnancy raises lipids  ☐ TSH, HbA1c, eGFR + urine ACR, LFTs + GGT, alcohol (AUDIT-C), BMI and BP  ☐ Drugs: antipsychotics, corticosteroids, retinoids, HIV antiretrovirals, ciclosporin, amiodarone, thiazides, beta-blockers, oestrogens

TG >4.5: calculated LDL is unreliable — use non-HDL (total minus HDL).

4Classify — frame the patient, then risk

  • Secondary cause — hypothyroidism, diabetes, CKD or nephrotic syndrome, cholestasis, alcohol, obesity, drugs → treat it, then recheck.
  • Established CVD → secondary prevention; no risk score.
  • Suspected FH → block 2; QRISK3 underestimates risk.
  • Type 1 diabetes, CKD (eGFR <60 and/or albuminuria) → disease-specific criteria, not QRISK3. Aged 85 or over → individualise.
  • Primary prevention, 25–84 → QRISK3 (may underestimate in severe mental illness, HIV, antipsychotics, steroids).
  • Uncertain — unexpected or discordant result → repeat with secondary-cause tests before labelling.

5Treat today — doses per SPC and local formulary

  • QRISK3 ≥10%: offer atorvastatin 20 mg after an informed discussion. <10%: lifestyle; do not rule out a statin if preferred.
  • Established CVD: atorvastatin 80 mg. CKD: atorvastatin 20 mg. FH: lipid clinic.
  • Targets: primary >40% fall in non-HDL; secondary LDL ≤2.0 or non-HDL ≤2.6; FH ≥50% LDL fall.
  • Monitor: lipids at 2–3 months; transaminases baseline, within 3 months and at 12 months. CK only if muscle symptoms. ALT below 3× ULN is no reason to withhold a statin.

6Refer routinely

  • Lipid specialist: TC >9.0 or non-HDL >7.5; TG still >10 on repeat; suspected familial combined hyperlipidaemia.
  • Specialist advice: TG 4.5–9.9 with non-HDL >7.5.
  • Lipid clinic: not at target on maximum tolerated treatment, or statin intolerance (local lipid pathway).

7Never reassure by

  • A "normal" cholesterol in a patient with chest pain.
  • A low QRISK3 in suspected FH, CKD or type 1 diabetes.
  • A normal calculated LDL when TG is >4.5 mmol/L.
  • A high HDL when LDL or non-HDL is raised.
  • A cholesterol taken during acute illness.
  • One result, without a secondary-cause check or family history.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Chest pain means 999, whatever your cholesterol. Severe tummy pain or being sick — call 999 or go to A&E.” On a statin: “Severe muscle pain or weakness, or dark urine — stop the tablet and contact us the same day.”

FH: explain that relatives need cascade testing through the specialist service. Remote or OOH: any TG with abdominal pain or vomiting → same-day hospital assessment, not a repeat test.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented

No chest pain, abdominal pain or vomiting (block 0) Result confirmed; secondary causes checked FH screened; specialist route if criteria met Patient framed; QRISK3 only where valid Target, 2–3 month recheck and safety-net set
