Scrotal lumps and swelling

For: adults and adolescents (men, trans women and non-binary people with male reproductive organs) with a new scrotal lump or swelling. Not for: scrotal pain without a lump (use Scrotal pain). Children: local paediatric acute-scrotum pathway.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12BASHH 2020GIRFT/BAUSMHRA 2024EAU 2026
Core rule: if the lump is not clearly separate from the testis, arrange urgent ultrasound and referral to exclude cancer — both at once. Transillumination, pain and normal tumour markers exclude nothing.

0Stop — emergency transfer now — before any scan

  • Possible torsion: sudden or severe unilateral pain, high-riding or horizontal testis, nausea or vomiting, marked tenderness → Immediate ED / urology; 999 if severely unwell, uncontrolled pain, collapse or no safe urgent transfer.
  • Painful irreducible groin or scrotal swelling (obstructed or strangulated hernia); trauma with suspected rupture or expanding haematoma → Emergency hospital
  • Fournier's gangrene: severe pain with systemic toxicity, skin discolouration, bullae, crepitus or rapidly spreading perineal or scrotal oedema → Emergency hospital; 999 if septic.
  • While arranging: do not delay for ultrasound. A present cremasteric reflex does not exclude torsion. Urgency is greatest within 6 hours, but salvage may still be possible later.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Rapid growth over days or weeks → Same-day urology. Pain or tenderness does not exclude cancer.
  • Lump + back pain, weight loss or gynaecomastia → Suspected cancer pathway; do not wait for markers.
  • New adult hydrocele with testis impalpable, or suspicious USS → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Testicular mass + generalised lymphadenopathy (lymphoma, older men) → Urgent haematology / urology
  • Varicocele sudden, right-sided, large, non-reducible or not emptying supine → investigate for renal or retroperitoneal cause.

3Assess — only once block 0 is excluded

☐ Consent and chaperone; standing and supine  ☐ Both testes, epididymides, cords: inside or outside the testis, get above it, cough impulse, transillumination  ☐ Abdomen, liver, gynaecomastia, groins, nodes  ☐ Risk: undescended testis (even after orchidopexy), previous or family testicular cancer, Klinefelter's

Not in primary care: aspiration or biopsy; staging CT or routine CXR; AFP, β-hCG or LDH that delay referral (only if the local pathway supports them).

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Epididymal cyst / spermatocele — smooth, posterior, clearly separate, transilluminates → reassure; USS if uncertain.
  • Hydrocele — surrounds and obscures the testis, transilluminates → USS if testis impalpable, new, atypical, painful or with a mass.
  • Varicocele — "bag of worms" above the testis, worse standing, usually left; USS confirms.
  • Epididymo-orchitis — tender swollen epididymis ± fever; cause from sexual and urinary risk, not age alone.
  • Inguinal hernia — cannot get above it, cough impulse → surgical referral.
  • Uncertain — testis not felt separately or swelling unexplained → Urgent USS; refer if solid.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Epididymo-orchitis, STI likely: ceftriaxone 1 g IM once + doxycycline 100 mg BD 10–14 days once swelling and tenderness are objective; first-pass urine NAAT first where feasible, HIV and syphilis tests.
  • Enteric or urinary likely: MSU + STI screen; local guidance; fluoroquinolone only if alternatives are inappropriate (MHRA).
  • Benign lump confirmed on USS: reassure; testicular awareness.
  • Suspected cancer: refer and scan together; check sperm banking has been discussed.

6Refer routinely

  • Urology: symptomatic cyst or spermatocele; varicocele painful, progressive, with atrophy or fertility concerns; symptomatic adult hydrocele.
  • Sexual health: STI-related epididymo-orchitis, partner notification. Uropathogen, recurrent or obstructive → urology.
  • No routine surveillance USS for high-risk men unless urology advises.

7Never reassure by

  • Transillumination — fluid can hide a tumour.
  • Pain, tenderness or a dull ache.
  • Normal tumour markers.
  • A hydrocele you cannot feel the testis through.
  • "Infection" not improving at 48–72 h — a tumour can mimic it.
  • A present cremasteric reflex or a normal scan when torsion is suspected.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Sudden severe testicular pain, vomiting, rapid swelling, fever, skin colour change, worsening pain or being unable to pass urine — go to hospital now; call 999 if very unwell.” Same day: “The lump growing fast, new fever or weight loss while you wait, a new lump in the other testis, or infection not better by 72 hours — contact us today.” Rapid enlargement after referral → phone urology the same day.

Follow-up: confirm the USS is booked and the referral received; no appointment in 2 weeks → patient phones the hospital. Epididymo-orchitis review at 48–72 h, then about 2 weeks if it persists.
