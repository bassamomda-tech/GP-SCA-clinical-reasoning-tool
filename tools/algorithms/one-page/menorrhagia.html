Heavy menstrual bleeding — new presentation

For: women with heavy menstrual bleeding in primary care. Not for: pregnancy (bleeding in pregnancy algorithm), postmenopausal bleeding or bleeding on HRT (separate pathways), under-16s with first presentation (consider bleeding disorder).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG88NG12CoSRH UKMECBSG iron
Core rule: HMB is defined by impact, not volume. Check an FBC in all, ask about pregnancy plans, and start treatment today — but intermenstrual, postcoital or postmenopausal bleeding is a different route.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Torrential bleeding with tachycardia, hypotension, syncope or collapse → 999
  • Positive pregnancy test with collapse, shoulder-tip pain or peritonism (ectopic) → 999

1Triage — what kind of bleeding?

PatternRoute

2Screen

  • Abnormal cervix → consider Suspected cancer pathway (colposcopy)
  • Pelvic mass not obviously fibroids, or ascites → Suspected cancer pathway
  • IDA out of proportion → FIT; ≥10 µg Hb/g → colorectal Suspected cancer pathway
  • Symptomatic anaemia → Same-day
  • Bleeding since menarche + bleeding history → haematology (von Willebrand)

3Assess

☐ Impact, pattern, pain, bleeding history, drugs (DOAC, copper IUD) ☐ Pregnancy plans and contraception ☐ Pregnancy test ☐ FBC in all; consider ferritin ☐ TFTs only if thyroid symptoms

Examine if IMB/PCB, pain, pressure, suspected structural disease or before LNG-IUS. Pelvic US if palpable uterus, suspected mass, or examination inconclusive or difficult; TVUS for suspected adenomyosis (NG88).

4Classify

  • No structural cause — regular, normal examination → treat on history.
  • Fibroids — bulk, palpable uterus → TVUS; 3 cm or more or cavity distortion → consider gynaecology referral.
  • Adenomyosis — pain, bulky tender uterus → TVUS.
  • Endometrial pathology — IMB, obesity, PCOS, tamoxifen → hysteroscopy.
  • Coagulopathy or drug — lifelong HMB, DOAC, copper IUD.
  • Uncertain — treat, diary, review at about 3 months.

5Treat today — doses: SPC and local formulary

  • LNG-IUS 52 mg first-line if not trying to conceive; allow at least 6 cycles.
  • Else: tranexamic acid 1 g three times a day up to 4 days (max 4 g a day) ± mefenamic acid 500 mg three times a day; or CHC (UKMEC) or norethisterone 5 mg three times a day, day 5–26.
  • Trying to conceive: tranexamic acid ± mefenamic acid only.
  • Iron once daily or alternate days; Hb at 2–4 weeks.

6Refer routinely

  • Consider gynaecology: treatment failure, fibroids 3 cm or more, persistent IMB (outpatient hysteroscopy) or endometrial risk.
  • Suspected bleeding disorder or HMB on anticoagulation → haematology.

7Never reassure by

  • A "normal" measured volume — impact defines HMB.
  • A normal Hb when ferritin has not been considered.
  • Periods explaining iron-deficiency anaemia without asking if they fit.
  • A recent normal cervical screen when the cervix looks abnormal.
  • Age under 55 in a woman with postmenopausal bleeding.
  • A normal clotting screen in lifelong HMB.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you faint, or you soak through pads every few minutes.” Same-day “Contact us today if heavy bleeding makes you dizzy or breathless, or you have a positive pregnancy test with bleeding or pain; or leg swelling, chest pain or breathlessness on tranexamic acid or the pill.” Review at about 3 months.
