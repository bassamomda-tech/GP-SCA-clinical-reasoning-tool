Taste disorders — loss of taste and distorted taste

For: adults with loss of taste (ageusia, hypogeusia), distorted (dysgeusia) or phantom taste. Not for: smell loss alone → loss of smell algorithm.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG127NICE NG12NICE NG188NICE CG151BNF
Core rule: most "loss of taste" is loss of smell, and the commonest fixable cause is a drug — but taste change with facial weakness, cranial-nerve signs or drug toxicity is never "just viral".

0Stop — call 999 now — stroke, seizure, sepsis on chemotherapy, toxicity

  • Facial weakness with limb weakness, speech disturbance or forehead sparing (stroke); seizure, reduced consciousness or meningism; rapidly progressive bulbar or breathing symptoms → 999
  • Fever or unwell on chemotherapy (neutropenic sepsis) → 999 / immediate acute oncology or ED; do not wait for an FBC. Unstable digoxin or lithium toxicity → 999. Suspected DKA → Same-day emergency

1Triage — loss or distortion? taste or smell?

PatternMeaning → next step
"No flavour" but tastes sweet, saltSmell loss → loss of smell + drug/oral review

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Facial palsy: Bell's within 72 h → oral corticosteroid + eye care. Ear pain, vesicles, hearing loss or vertigo (Ramsay Hunt) → Same-day specialist; antiviral ideally ≤72 h.
  • Dysphagia, dysarthria, palatal weakness, tongue deviation/wasting or several cranial nerves → Urgent neurology / ENT + imaging
  • Hoarseness ≥45, unexplained neck lump, persistent oral ulcer, lump or red/white patch → Suspected cancer pathway; dysphagia → urgent upper-GI.
  • One-sided nasal blockage, bleeding, facial pain, orbital symptoms, mass → Urgent ENT
  • Metallic taste + vomiting, visual change, confusion, bradycardia (digoxin) or tremor, ataxia (lithium) → Same day ECG, level, U&E; withhold.
  • Mouth ulcers or sore throat on methotrexate → withhold, Same-day FBC.

3Assess

☐ Taste vs smell; onset; viral/COVID; head injury  ☐ All medicines incl. OTC, supplements, terbinafine in past 12 months  ☐ Mouth: dryness, candida, glossitis, dentures  ☐ CN VII, IX/X, XII; ear, nose, neck

Bloods by risk only: HbA1c, TFTs, B12/folate, FBC/ferritin, U&E/LFTs; zinc if deficiency risk; HIV by risk. Not routinely: zinc, ESR/CRP, taste-solution or smell tests, COVID serology, CD4.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Post-viral / COVID (commonest) — often with smell loss; recovery variable over months → olfactory training ≥12 weeks.
  • Drug-induced — metronidazole, metformin, ACE inhibitors, clarithromycin, terbinafine, lithium → review with the prescriber.
  • Oral and dry mouth — candida, periodontal disease, anticholinergic burden → treat the cause, dental review.
  • Nutritional or systemic — zinc, B12, folate, iron; diabetes, hypothyroidism, CKD, liver disease, Sjögren's → targeted tests, treat the cause.
  • Neurological — Bell's, ear/parotid surgery, dental anaesthesia, head injury, MS → blocks 0–2.
  • Uncertain — say so; unexplained beyond 3 months → consider MRI brain (NICE NG127) ± ENT.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Culprit drug: stop, reduce or switch if safe, with the prescriber; never stop important treatment automatically.
  • Confirmed candida: miconazole oral gel (check warfarin, statins) or nystatin; fluconazole 50 mg daily for 7 days if extensive or severe.
  • Zinc, B12, folate, iron: confirmed deficiency only. No empirical high-dose zinc, omega-3 or steroids for post-viral dysgeusia.
  • Flavour: umami, herbs, spices, texture; plastic cutlery, bicarbonate rinse if metallic; oral hygiene; stop smoking.

6Refer routinely

  • Unexplained loss beyond 3 months → consider MRI brain (NICE NG127) and ENT; do not routinely refer if imaging is normal or loss began straight after a head injury.
  • Persistent post-viral loss despite olfactory training → consider ENT. Burning mouth, chronic candida → oral medicine; Sjögren's → rheumatology.
  • Weight loss or malnutrition risk → dietitian. Low mood → NHS Talking Therapies.

7Never reassure by

  • "It's only taste" without asking about face weakness, ear pain, swallowing and speech.
  • No ear vesicles — Ramsay Hunt can occur without them.
  • A digoxin level in range: toxicity occurs within range.
  • A planned FBC in a patient on chemotherapy with fever.
  • Taste or smell loss alone as a sign of Parkinson's — refer only with motor features.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if your face droops with arm or leg weakness or slurred speech, or you have a fit. If you have a fever or feel unwell during chemotherapy, call your oncology helpline or 999 now.”

Same day “Contact us today for new face weakness, ear pain or a rash in or around the ear, trouble swallowing or speaking, eye pain or an eye you cannot close; or, on digoxin or lithium, sickness, confusion, blurred vision or shakiness.”

Within a week “Book an appointment if your taste gets worse, or you get a hoarse voice, a neck lump, a mouth ulcer or patch lasting over 3 weeks, one-sided nose blockage or bleeding, or weight loss.”
