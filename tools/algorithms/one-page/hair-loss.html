Hair loss (alopecia)

For: adults and children with hair loss in primary care. Not for: full management of pattern hair loss, tinea or eating disorders (use their own pages).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
PCDS alopeciaBAD alopecia areata 2024BAD tinea capitis 2014MHRA finasteride 2026BNF/BNFC
Core rule: look at the scalp in good light. Loss of follicular openings, inflammation, scale, pustules or tufting may be scarring alopecia: refer urgently before the loss becomes permanent. A normal-looking scalp does not exclude early scarring.

0Stop — call 999 now — rare in hair loss

  • Suicidal thoughts with immediate risk of self-harm (ask about mood, especially on finasteride) → 999 or A&E; suicidal thoughts without immediate risk → stop finasteride, Same-day assessment.
  • Otherwise the danger is irreversible scarring, kerion, a missed cancer, syphilis or an eating disorder: screen with block 2 first.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Suspected scarring or rapidly progressive inflammatory alopecia, pustules, tufting or significant pain → Urgent dermatology, local pathway.
  • Kerion: boggy painful swelling, pustules, neck nodes (especially a child) → Same-day paediatric/dermatology; do not delay systemic antifungal.
  • Ulcerated or bleeding scalp lesion, fast-growing nodule, suspicious pigmented lesion → Suspected cancer skin pathway.
  • “Moth-eaten” loss + rash, nodes or oral ulcers → Same-day STI clinic / GUM.
  • Rapid diffuse loss + weight loss, night sweats, nodes or known cancer → Urgent investigation for malignancy.
  • Very low BMI, amenorrhoea, lanugo, wasting → eating-disorder service immediately; Same-day medical review, admit if unstable.

3Assess — investigate selectively

☐ Scalp in good light ± dermatoscope  ☐ Brows, lashes, beard, nails  ☐ Thyroid, anaemia, androgen, lupus signs; nodes  ☐ Drugs, triggers, hair care  ☐ Distress, mood, suicidal thoughts

Diffuse shedding: FBC, ferritin/iron studies, TSH ± free T4. Testosterone, SHBG ± DHEAS only with hirsutism, acne, irregular periods, virilisation or suspected PCOS. Not routinely: bloods in classic male pattern loss; ANA, hormone panel, B12, zinc.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Pattern hair loss — commonest, normal scalp → primary care (block 5).
  • Telogen effluvium — diffuse, after a trigger → treat the cause; recovery 6–12 months or longer.
  • Alopecia areata — assess extent (SALT), brows, lashes, nails, distress → topical steroid.
  • Tinea capitis → systemic antifungal; screen household contacts.
  • Traction — tight styles → looser styles; can scar if chronic.
  • Uncertain → dermatology; never reassure suspected scarring away.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Pattern loss: topical minoxidil (OTC), long-term, ≥4–6 months to respond. Finasteride 1 mg (adult men): ask about depression, suicidal thoughts, sexual dysfunction first; alert card. Spironolactone (women, off-label): consider only after assessment, preferably with specialist advice; check pregnancy, potassium, renal function, BP.
  • Telogen effluvium: treat confirmed iron deficiency (ferrous sulfate 200 mg daily or alternate days), thyroid disease, B12 deficiency (NG239); do not promise regrowth.
  • Alopecia areata: potent or very potent topical steroid, e.g. betamethasone valerate 0.1% or clobetasol. Tinea: oral griseofulvin or terbinafine per BNFC + antifungal shampoo.
  • Distress: validate; NHS Talking Therapies, Alopecia UK; NHS wigs via hospital referral.

6Refer routinely

  • Dermatology ≤18 weeks: non-severe alopecia areata not responding; uncertainty without scarring. Severe, progressive, distressing or resistant alopecia areata → Urgent
  • Rheumatology: connective-tissue features, not a positive ANA alone. Endocrine: rapid virilisation or raised DHEAS → Urgent
  • Adult trichotillomania: NHS Talking Therapies (habit reversal).

7Never reassure by

  • A normal-looking scalp: early scarring can be subtle.
  • A “receding hairline” in a postmenopausal woman: check brows, think frontal fibrosing alopecia.
  • Non-specialist dermoscopy when scarring is suspected.
  • A boggy scalp swelling treated with topical antifungal alone.
  • A normal ferritin with inflammation: it can be falsely normal.
  • A history taken without looking at brows, lashes and nails.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you have thoughts of harming yourself and might act on them, call 999 or go to A&E.” Same day “Contact us today if the hair loss gets rapidly worse over days or weeks, you get a painful boggy swelling or pus on the scalp (especially a child), a new sore or bleeding scalp lump, fever, weight loss or a rash, or suicidal thoughts on finasteride (stop it).”

Review: 6–8 weeks after starting iron, thyroid or tinea treatment; recheck ferritin/TSH and reassess alopecia areata at 3–4 months; telogen effluvium not resolving by 6–12 months → reassess the diagnosis. Remote: book face to face; the diagnosis needs scalp examination in good light.
