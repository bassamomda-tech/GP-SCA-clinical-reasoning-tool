Developmental and speech delay in children

For: children with motor, speech and language, social or global developmental concerns in primary care, mainly 0–5 years. Not for: the acutely unwell child (acute pathways), established epilepsy or autism management (their own protocols). Use corrected age to 2 years.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG127CG128NG233NG217Working Together 2026
Core rule: loss of skills is never normal — route it by speed, age and type. For any speech delay, refer to audiology and SALT (and autism or paediatric services) in parallel, never one after the other.

0Stop — act today — before any milestone check

  • Infantile spasms: clusters of head drops, salaam attacks, brief flexor or extensor spasms → Same-day specialist. Do not treat in primary care.
  • Rapidly progressive regression, or regression with seizures, altered consciousness, focal signs, acute gait change or suspected raised ICP → Same-day paediatrics
  • New neurological signs (hypotonia, hyperreflexia, abnormal movements) or rapidly progressive weakness → Same-day paediatrics / neurology
  • Seizure over 5 minutes, cannot be woken, struggling to breathe → 999. Serious safeguarding risk → Same-day paediatrics + safeguarding referral.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Boy with Gowers', calf hypertrophy or proximal weakness → consider CK (NG127); Urgent paediatrics
  • Progressive weakness, faltered growth, chest infections → Urgent neurology / genetics
  • Dysmorphism, anomalies or abnormal head growth with delay → Urgent paediatrics ± genetics
  • First suspected seizure → Urgent, seen within 2 weeks (NG217)
  • Severe or profound hearing loss suspected → Urgent audiology; if none → Same-day paediatrics
  • Unexplained injury, inconsistent history, failure to thrive → local safeguarding pathway.

3Assess — all four domains

☐ Gestation, corrected age, trajectory, Red Book  ☐ Height, weight, head circumference plotted  ☐ Dysmorphism, skin (ash-leaf, café-au-lait, port-wine)  ☐ Tone, reflexes, gait, Gowers'  ☐ Red reflex, squint, visual behaviour  ☐ Otoscopy; newborn hearing and bloodspot results  ☐ Joint attention, pointing

TFTs only if clinical features or newborn result uncertain. No routine bloods for isolated speech delay. Genetic, metabolic, MRI and EEG: specialist-led.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Expressive delay — understands well, gestures and points → SALT + audiology; prognosis varies.
  • Receptive or mixed delay — poor comprehension, more concerning → SALT, audiology, community paediatrics.
  • Autism features — reduced joint attention, no pointing, repetitive behaviour → local multidisciplinary autism pathway. M-CHAT-R/F (16–30 months) is a screen, not a diagnosis.
  • Glue ear — dull, retracted drum → audiology; bilateral with hearing loss, reassess at 3 months (NG233). SALT regardless.
  • Motor-only delay — suspected cerebral palsy or muscle disease → child development service; physiotherapy.
  • Uncertain — say so and refer in parallel; name who reviews and when.

5Treat today — only these

  • Refer in parallel today: audiology + SALT (+ autism or paediatrics). Do not wait for one before another.
  • Language-rich home: talk, read, sing; less background TV. Keep the home language.
  • Glue ear: no antibiotics, oral or nasal steroids, antihistamines or decongestants.
  • Family: ask about parental mental health (NHS Talking Therapies); carer's assessment, DLA, support organisations.

6Refer routinely

  • Isolated speech concern → SALT directly (local route; many accept parent or nursery referral).
  • Gross motor → physiotherapy; fine motor or coordination (DCD) → OT.
  • Selective mutism → child mental health / psychology with school.
  • Family history of intellectual disability, or consanguinity → clinical genetics. School needs → SENCo, local SEND team (EHCP).

7Never reassure by

  • Bilingualism — assess all languages; delay across all of them matters.
  • A passed newborn hearing screen.
  • A negative M-CHAT-R/F, good eye contact or smiling.
  • "Late talker" with good comprehension — still refer to SALT.
  • Slow loss of skills as "a phase" — regression is never normal.
  • Waiting for audiology before SALT or autism referral.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if a seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, your child cannot be woken, or is struggling to breathe.” Same-day “Contact us or NHS 111 the same day if your child loses skills they had, has fits or switching-off episodes, becomes weak or unsteady, has persistent vomiting, headache, odd eye movements, a fast-growing head, new swallowing or breathing problems, or becomes unwell.”

Follow-up: no fixed interval — document the next review. Check growth, medicines, safeguarding and parental wellbeing; at 3 years confirm SALT and hearing assessment done. Not attending with significant delay is a safeguarding concern.
