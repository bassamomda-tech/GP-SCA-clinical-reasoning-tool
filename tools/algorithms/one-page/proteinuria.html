Proteinuria in adults

For: adults with dipstick or incidental proteinuria in primary care. Not for: children (separate paediatric pathway) or proteinuria in pregnancy (obstetric pathways, NICE NG133); suspected pre-eclampsia is in block 2.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG203NG12NG133NG136TA1075 / TA942
Core rule: proteinuria is a marker, not a diagnosis — quantify with an ACR, never the dipstick alone, confirm it persists and stage by eGFR and ACR. A normal ACR does not exclude myeloma.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Sudden severe breathlessness, chest pain, a seizure or collapse → 999
  • Pregnant after 20 weeks with a seizure or collapse (suspected eclampsia) → 999
  • On an ACEi/ARB with K⁺ ≥6.5 mmol/L, ECG changes or symptoms (palpitations, syncope, chest pain, muscle weakness) → 999. K⁺ ≥6.0 → stop the ACEi/ARB and other contributors, urgent ECG and Same-day assessment; check for AKI.

1Triage — quantify first (NICE NG203)

Stage A1 <3 · A2 3–30 · A3 >30 mg/mmol, plus G1–G5. CKD = ACR >3 or eGFR <60 for >3 months.

2Screen — renal and haematological emergencies

  • Rapidly progressive GN / AKI: proteinuria + haematuria + rising creatinine + hypertension ± rash, haemoptysis; oliguria → Same-day
  • Nephrotic: oedema, low albumin, frothy urine (PCR >300 or ACR >250 approx.) → Urgent nephrology discussion; Same-day if severe oedema, AKI, thrombosis, infection or unstable.
  • Myeloma: aged ≥60 with persistent bone pain or unexplained fracture → offer FBC, calcium, ESR/PV, serum electrophoresis, free light chains; suggestive → Suspected cancer. AKI, symptomatic hypercalcaemia, cord compression → Same-day
  • Pre-eclampsia: >20 weeks, new proteinuria (PCR ≥30 or ACR ≥8, not a first-morning sample) + BP ≥140/90 → Same-day maternity assessment.
  • Malignant hypertension: BP ≥180/120 with retinal haemorrhage or papilloedema, or life-threatening symptoms → Same-day specialist.
  • Haematuria (NG12): unexplained visible at ≥45, or non-visible at ≥60 with dysuria or raised WCC → Suspected cancer urology, plus renal work-up.

3Assess

☐ U&E/eGFR, bicarbonate, HbA1c, FBC, lipids, bone profile, albumin  ☐ Dipstick for blood  ☐ Culture if UTI possible  ☐ BP; drugs incl. NSAIDs, lithium, OTC and herbal  ☐ Pregnancy?  ☐ KFRE at G3–G5

Consider a myeloma screen if protein is disproportionate to ACR, or unexplained anaemia, renal impairment, hypercalcaemia, bone pain, weight loss or raised ESR/PV. GN picture → immunology with urgent renal advice.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Transient — fever, UTI, exercise, heart failure → retest when well.
  • Orthostatic — younger, absent on early-morning ACR → reassure.
  • Diabetic / hypertensive (commonest) — long duration, retinopathy, gradual ACR rise, bland urine → block 5.
  • Glomerulonephritis — haematuria, casts, hypertension, rapid change, systemic features → urgent renal advice.
  • Overflow (myeloma) — older, anaemia, high calcium or ESR, ACR low for total protein → myeloma screen.
  • Uncertain — confirm ACR, eGFR trend; renal advice and guidance.

5Treat — renoprotection; doses per BNF and local formulary

  • ACEi or ARB: offer if diabetes + ACR ≥3, hypertension + ACR >30, or any ACR ≥70; titrate to maximum tolerated. Not routinely if K⁺ >5.0. U&E 1–2 weeks after each change. Never ACEi + ARB; stop if pregnant.
  • SGLT2 inhibitor add-on: eGFR 20 to <45, or 45–90 with ACR ≥22.6 or type 2 diabetes. Not in type 1 diabetes.
  • BP 120–129/<80 if ACR ≥70, else 120–139/<90. Offer atorvastatin 20 mg. Stop NSAIDs.
  • Lifestyle: less salt, stop smoking, weight; sick-day advice; vaccines per Green Book.

6Refer routinely and review

  • Nephrology: ACR ≥70 (unless diabetic and treated), ACR ≥30 + haematuria, eGFR fall ≥25% with category change or ≥15 mL/min in 12 months, KFRE >5%, BP uncontrolled on ≥4 drugs, genetic or rare cause.
  • Possible MGUS (small paraprotein) → discuss with haematology, do not reassure.
  • Monitor eGFR per NG203 Table 2, ACR individualised; code CKD.

7Never reassure by

  • A normal ACR in an older patient — overflow light chains are missed.
  • A negative dipstick — it does not detect Bence Jones protein.
  • A single dipstick or a single eGFR.
  • "Orthostatic" without an early-morning ACR.
  • A urology referral — do not delay urgent renal assessment.
  • Labelling it chronic before retesting once the patient is well.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you suddenly become very short of breath, get chest pain, have a fit or collapse, call 999.” Same-day “If you notice swelling of your ankles or face, very frothy urine, blood in your urine or you pass much less urine, contact us the same day.”

Recheck U&E after each ACEi/ARB/diuretic change and in dehydrating illness. The myeloma safety-net never fully closes in older patients with unexplained proteinuria.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
