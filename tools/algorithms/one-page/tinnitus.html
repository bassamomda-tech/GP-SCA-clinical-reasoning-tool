Tinnitus in adults

For: adults with new or persistent tinnitus, daytime GP and OOH/111. Not for: under-16s → Urgent paediatric audiology; confirmed Ménière's (ENT pathway).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG155NG98NG12NG128NG225
Core rule: triage by side, rhythm and company (NG155). Bilateral non-pulsatile tinnitus with symmetrical hearing is benign and needs support, not imaging. Unilateral, pulsatile, sudden hearing loss, neurology or suicidal distress each leave by a faster route. Never say “nothing can be done”.

0Stop — act now — 999 or same-day, before any history

  • Central or cranial-nerve sign (facial weakness, diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia, limb weakness, ataxia, cannot walk unaided), acute severe vertigo with deafness, suspected stroke, altered consciousness, severe systemic illness, airway compromise → 999 Say “query posterior stroke”: CT misses about half; MRI is the test.
  • Thunderclap headache with vomiting, or meningism (SAH, meningitis) → 999
  • Sudden hearing loss (over ≤3 days) within the past 30 days, not explained by wax or middle-ear disease → Immediate ENT/ED, seen within 24 h — not automatically 999. Do not wait for audiometry.
  • Suicidal ideation or intent, or acute psychological crisis → Immediate crisis assessment if high risk (NG155). Record onset date, side and rhythm; make the call rather than arranging review.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Persistent pulsatile → Urgent ENT/neurovascular imaging, even if examination normal. Papilloedema (IIH) → Same-day ophthalmology/neurology; neurology or bruit after trauma → 999
  • Unilateral tinnitus, even after wax or infection treated → ENT/audiovestibular per local pathway; consider MRI IAM. With asymmetric hearing loss → Urgent ENT
  • Sudden hearing loss >30 days ago or rapidly progressive → Urgent ENT within 2 weeks (NG98)
  • Necrotising otitis externa (diabetes, granulation), mastoiditis, new facial palsy → Same-day ENT. Cholesteatoma → Urgent ENT
  • Head and neck cancer: neck lump, adult unilateral effusion, persistent hoarseness or dysphagia, oral ulcer >3 weeks → Suspected cancer, 2 weeks (NG12)

3Assess — face to face only

☐ Otoscopy both ears  ☐ Whisper, Weber, Rinne (Weber to better ear = sensorineural)  ☐ Cranial nerves, gait  ☐ Neck, mouth, jaw  ☐ Pulsatile: auscultate ear, orbit, neck; BP; fundi  ☐ Drugs: aminoglycosides, cisplatin, high-dose aspirin/NSAIDs, loop diuretics, quinine

Audiology for all persistent tinnitus. Bloods only if indicated. Not routinely: CT head, blood panel, ECG.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Age- or noise-related — bilateral, symmetrical loss, worse in quiet → audiology; aids help the tinnitus too.
  • Wax or effusion — conductive loss → treat, re-test, re-ask; persisting or unilateral → block 2.
  • Drug-related — match dates to onset. Aminoglycoside or cisplatin → advice same day; never stop essential drugs without the specialist.
  • Ménière's — vertigo 20 min–12 h, fluctuating unilateral loss → ENT with audiometry; still image.
  • TMJ / neck — changes with jaw or neck, normal ear → dental or physio, plus audiology.
  • Unclassified / high impact — asymmetry, fluctuation or disabling impact → state uncertainty, refer, name reviewer and timeframe.

5Treat today — only these

  • Explain: very common, not a tumour in the bilateral non-pulsatile pattern; habituation usual; distress is treatable.
  • Sound and sleep: sound enrichment, not silence; sleep hygiene; no alcohol as a sedative. Protect from loud noise, not ordinary noise.
  • Reversible contributors: remove wax and re-test; review ototoxic drugs; treat anaemia, thyroid disease, hypertension.
  • No drug treats tinnitus: no antidepressant, benzodiazepine, betahistine or ginkgo for the noise. Steroids for sudden loss only on ENT advice — doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • Bilateral symmetrical, no red flags → direct audiology; hearing aids where loss affects communication.
  • Distress without crisis (promptly if it stops normal activities, whatever the score) → stepped tinnitus CBT: digital → group CBT, mindfulness-based CBT or ACT → individual. Treat mood disorder on its own pathway, in parallel.
  • Otosclerosis (Rinne negative, normal drum) → ENT. Severe loss not helped by aids → cochlear implant assessment.

7Never reassure by

  • “Just stress” or “just wax” for a unilateral or pulsatile noise.
  • A normal examination in persistent pulsatile tinnitus — image it.
  • “Normal for your age” without an audiogram.
  • Quiet tinnitus — distress and seriousness are not proportional to loudness.
  • Treating an adult unilateral effusion as glue ear with a nasal spray.
  • A remote consultation: no otoscopy, tuning forks, bruit check or fundi.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for new facial weakness, double vision, unsteadiness, difficulty swallowing, or a sudden severe headache.” Same day “Contact us the same day if the tinnitus becomes one-sided, starts beating in time with your pulse, or comes with sudden hearing loss, dizziness, facial weakness, severe headache or visual change — and straight away if you feel unable to cope or have thoughts of harming yourself.”

Remote or OOH: convert to face-to-face or emergency assessment if new and unilateral, pulsatile, or with hearing loss, vertigo, facial weakness, headache or crisis-level distress. Review new presentations at 2–4 weeks.
