Unsettled baby

For: infants aged 0–6 months with excessive crying, unsettled behaviour or feeding-related distress, in UK primary care. Not for: replacing local paediatric, surgical, safeguarding or sepsis pathways; fever work-up follows the fever in children algorithm, poor weight gain the faltered growth algorithm.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG143NG1NG224CG116 / iMAPCG89
Core rule: colic is a diagnosis of a well, thriving infant with a normal examination — exclude serious illness and safeguarding first, because a sick infant can present only as inconsolable crying, and crying is a recognised trigger for abusive head trauma.

0Stop — call 999 now — do not delay transfer for primary-care investigations

  • Weak, high-pitched or continuous cry; reduced responsiveness; bulging fontanelle; non-blanching rash; seizures or focal signs; mottled, pale, blue or ashen; grunting or respiratory distress → 999. Any one is enough.
  • Bilious (green) vomiting, marked abdominal distension, peritonism, collapse or failure to pass stool; paroxysmal screaming + legs drawn up + "redcurrant jelly" stool (intussusception) → 999 for immediate emergency paediatric/surgical assessment.
  • Pallor + collapse + rigid abdomen, shock or apnoea; change in cry + eye deviation or twitching (consider abusive head trauma) → 999.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Temperature ≥38°C under 3 months, even after vaccination → Urgent hospital/paeds (NG143); not colic.
  • Tense irreducible groin swelling; tender swollen testis; hair tourniquet → Immediate emergency surgical/ED assessment; no ultrasound first for torsion.
  • Suspected UTI under 3 months → Immediate paediatric referral; urine for urgent microscopy and culture (NG224).
  • Persistent projectile non-bilious vomiting under 2 months → Same-day paediatric/surgical assessment + urgent ultrasound.
  • Swollen painful limb → Same-day ED + safeguarding. Bruising in a non-mobile infant, fracture, inconsistent history → Same-day paediatrics (immediate if head trauma suspected) + safeguarding lead/Named GP. Parent unable to cope or urge to harm → Same-day risk assessment.

3Assess — undress fully if injury or rash is possible

☐ Temperature, HR, RR, SpO2; CRT ≥3 s = amber  ☐ Fontanelle, mouth (thrush, tongue tie, cleft), ears if indicated  ☐ Abdomen, hernial orifices, testes, anus, fingers and toes  ☐ Skin and limbs  ☐ Weigh and plot  ☐ Parental mood and coping

No routine tests in typical colic (no urine, stool pH, breath tests); no IgE tests for non-IgE CMPA. GPs do not request skeletal surveys; GP fundoscopy is not definitive.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Infantile colic — well, feeding, normal exam; clinical diagnosis after assessment → reassure and support.
  • Reflux — thriving "happy spitter" = GOR → reassure. GORD (troublesome symptoms) → NG1 steps. Crying alone is not reflux.
  • CMPA — colic alone is not enough; consider with features in more than one system (eczema, blood or mucus in stool, vomiting, diarrhoea, faltering growth).
  • Feeding — under- (latch, tongue tie, over-diluted formula) or overfeeding → feeding review.
  • Other — thrush, otitis media, constipation. Uncertain — re-examine; don't anchor on colic.

5Treat today — doses per BNFc and local formulary

  • Colic: explain the pattern; ICON — crying is normal, comfort may help, OK to walk away safely, never shake. No routine simeticone, probiotics, antispasmodics, gripe water, lactase or acid suppression.
  • Reflux, formula-fed: cut excess volume → smaller, more frequent feeds → thickened formula → then alginate 1–2 weeks (never with thickener). Breastfed: feeding assessment, consider alginate. No PPI/H2RA for crying.
  • Suspected non-IgE CMPA: eHF (formula) or maternal cow's-milk exclusion only if indicated (breastfed), 2–6 weeks with dietetic support → reintroduce to confirm.
  • Safe sleep: on the back, flat, separate surface; no co-sleeping when exhausted or after alcohol/drugs; never prone, side or inclined.

6Refer and review routinely

  • Health visitor for all; lactation consultant; perinatal mental health if depression or severe anxiety; dietitian for CMPA.
  • Routine paediatrics: moderate–severe CMPA, complicated GORD, unclear diagnosis; crying beyond 3–4 months → reassess, persistent with no cause → refer.

7Never reassure by

  • A "colic" label before examining undressed — digits, groin, testes, skin.
  • A normal fontanelle or no neck stiffness — meningitis is not excluded.
  • "It's just the vaccine" — fever under 3 months still needs urgent assessment.
  • Labelling crying alone as reflux or CMPA — no PPI or formula switch without a clinical indication.
  • Accepting an explanation for bruising in a non-mobile infant.
  • Not asking about coping — "Has anyone shaken or dropped the baby?" may be appropriate.

8Safety-net — say it, write it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if your baby goes floppy or unresponsive, has a weak, high-pitched or constant cry, looks mottled or blue, has green vomit, a fit, pauses in or difficulty breathing, redcurrant-jelly stool, a tense tummy, a bulging soft spot, a rash that does not fade under a glass, or a tense, tender swelling in the groin or scrotum.”

Same day “Call us or 111 today if feeds drop below half, there are fewer wet nappies, projectile vomiting, blood in the stool, a swollen tummy, unusual sleepiness, or you are struggling to cope. Under 3 months, a temperature of 38°C or more needs urgent hospital assessment.” ICON: “If the crying gets too much, put your baby down safely and walk away for a few minutes — never shake.”
