Sleep problems in children

For: children and young people aged 0–18 with settling or waking problems, night-time events, snoring or daytime sleepiness, Not for: adults (use the adult insomnia and OSA protocols). Melatonin decisions sit with the specialist or local shared-care pathway.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BTS 2023 paediatric SDBNICE CG170NICE NG217BNFCGMMMG melatonin
Core rule: most childhood sleep problems are behavioural, but only call it behavioural after asking about snoring with breathing pauses, stereotyped night-time events and safeguarding, and plotting growth. Melatonin is never first-line in primary care.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Breathing now: current respiratory compromise, cyanosis, persistent apnoea, not breathing, reduced consciousness or failure to recover → 999
  • Seizure lasting 5 minutes or more, repeated seizures without recovery, significant injury or failure to return to baseline → 999
  • Acute safeguarding emergency → 999 and the local safeguarding pathway.
  • Not 999: stable habitual snoring or witnessed pauses → prompt paediatric sleep/ENT referral (block 2).

1Triage by age — what to look for first

AgeThink of
0–2 yNight waking can be normal beyond 6 months. Sleep associations, reflux, eczema, pain; safe sleep.
2–5 yBedtime resistance, "curtain calls", night terrors, confusional arousals; bed-sharing and safe sleep.
5–12 yAnxiety, OSA, restless legs or "growing pains", ADHD, parasomnias.
13–18 yDelayed sleep phase, screens, anxiety and depression, substance use, narcolepsy onset, snoring.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Possible OSA: habitual snoring with witnessed apnoeas, choking, laboured or mouth breathing, restless sleep, daytime sleepiness or behaviour change → Prompt sleep/ENT referral (local pathway)
  • Seizure features: stereotyped, clustered events, tonic stiffening or rhythmic jerking, lateral tongue biting, injury, incontinence, post-event confusion, daytime events, abnormal neurology → Urgent paediatrics, seen ≤2 weeks (NG217). Ask for a video.
  • Developmental regression or serious underlying illness (weight loss, growth faltering) → Urgent paediatrics, discussed same day
  • Falling asleep in hazardous situations (roads, water, cycling, school travel) → Same-day GP assessment: OSA, narcolepsy, insufficient sleep, medicines.
  • Safeguarding: neglect, abuse, unsafe supervision or co-sleeping, unexplained bruising, fear of going to bed → Same-day local safeguarding pathway. Co-sleeping or parental exhaustion alone is not abuse.

3Assess

☐ 2-week sleep diary  ☐ Growth chart (faltering = medical cause)  ☐ Tonsils, mouth breathing, nasal obstruction  ☐ Development, mood, ADHD/autism, ACEs, safeguarding  ☐ BEARS screen

Tests only if targeted: FBC and ferritin for restless legs or suspected iron deficiency; TFTs or HbA1c only with symptoms. Oximetry, sleep studies and EEG via the specialist pathway.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Sleep association — only falls asleep with feeding, rocking or a parent; persistent and impairing → behavioural plan.
  • Limit-setting (age 2–8) — refuses bed, repeated requests → firm consistent routine, bedtime pass, rewards.
  • Night terrors or sleepwalking — first third of night, no memory → do not wake; make home safe.
  • Nightmares — second half of night, recalls the dream, comforted → reassure; treat anxiety if persistent.
  • Delayed sleep phase (teenager) — late onset, normal sleep at weekends → regular wake time and morning daylight.
  • Uncertain — identical, clustered events may be nocturnal frontal lobe epilepsy: treat as block 2.

5Treat today

  • Family-agreed behavioural plan: graduated checking, parental presence fading, camping out, positive reinforcement. Controlled crying is not mandatory.
  • Sleep hygiene: consistent wake time incl. weekends, screens out of the bedroom, no caffeine or energy drinks, dark quiet room. Safe sleep for infants: on the back, clear cot, smoke-free; never share a sofa or armchair with a baby.
  • Treat itch, reflux, constipation and pain; support parental wellbeing and consistency.
  • Melatonin only specialist-initiated (usually neurodevelopmental, after a plan fails); GP continues under shared care, doses per BNFC and local protocol. No antihistamines, benzodiazepines, Z-drugs or antipsychotics.

6Refer routinely

  • Infant or toddler behavioural problems → Health Visitor or sleep support service (Sleep Charity helpline 03303 530541).
  • Persistent despite a structured plan, complex neurodisability → community paediatrics. Anxiety, PTSD nightmares, teenage chronic insomnia → CAMHS.
  • Daytime sleepiness with cataplexy (narcolepsy), or teenage delayed sleep phase needing melatonin or light → paediatric sleep specialist.

7Never reassure by

  • A "behavioural" label without a snoring and breathing history.
  • Small tonsils or a normal oximetry study.
  • Calling identical, clustered night events "night terrors".
  • "Growing pains" without considering restless legs and iron deficiency.
  • Teenage late sleep put down to laziness or poor sleep hygiene.
  • Melatonin repeat prescriptions continued without specialist review.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if your child turns blue, stops breathing or keeps pausing and can't recover, can't be woken properly, or has a fit lasting 5 minutes or more or fits without recovering in between.” Same day: “Contact us today if your child falls asleep somewhere dangerous, night episodes become longer or more frequent, or there is tongue biting, wetting or confusion after an episode.”

Follow-up: review the diary at 2 weeks and again at 4–6 weeks. If the plan fails, check consistency, then reassess for OSA, a medical, mood or neurodevelopmental cause, and safeguarding.
