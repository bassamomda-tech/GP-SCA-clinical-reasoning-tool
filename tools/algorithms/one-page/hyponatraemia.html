Hyponatraemia in adults

For: adults with Na⁺ <135 mmol/L, in hours or OOH. Not for: pregnancy or peripartum (NICE NG235, specialist advice); children; hospital correction (full algorithm, step 7).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
Soc Endocrinology 2022European guideline 2014NICE NG243NG12CG174
Core rule: symptoms and the speed of the fall drive urgency, not the sodium alone. Urine osmolality, urine sodium (sent before treatment) and volume status name the cause; always review drugs. Correction limits are maximums, not targets: never more than 10 mmol/L in 24 h (about 8 if high risk) — osmotic demyelination is irreversible.

0Stop — call 999 now — emergency admission at any sodium level

  • Any symptom attributable to it: seizure, GCS ≤14, drowsiness, confusion, vomiting, respiratory distress, unequal pupils; also headache or nausea without vomiting → 999
  • Na⁺ <120 mmol/L with neurological symptoms, acute onset or a rapidly falling sodium; acute fall (<48h) with any neurology; symptomatic postoperative (within 3–5 days), exercise, MDMA, bowel-preparation or water-intoxication hyponatraemia → 999
  • Shock: HR >120, SBP <90, postural collapse → 999. Suspected adrenal crisis (low Na⁺ with hypotension, vomiting, collapse or raised K⁺; after stopping long-term steroids) → 999
  • While waiting: adrenal crisis → IM or IV hydrocortisone now if trained and authorised; never wait for cortisol. 3% saline is hospital only. Do not delay transfer for tests.

1Triage by sodium — if asymptomatic

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Adrenal insufficiency? (postural hypotension, pigmentation, K⁺ raised) → Same-day admission
  • Hypervolaemic (HF, cirrhosis, nephrotic); with hypokalaemia, malnutrition, alcohol dependence or liver disease; worsening despite stopping the drug; poor intake → Same-day admission
  • New SIADH, no obvious cause → Urgent malignancy screen: chest X-ray (SCLC); CT if high suspicion.
  • NG12, 40+: ≥2 of cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, weight or appetite loss (≥1 if ever smoked) → urgent chest X-ray; unexplained haemoptysis or suggestive X-ray → suspected cancer pathway. Not for hyponatraemia alone.

3Assess — face to face if Na⁺ <130

☐ GCS, orientation, gait; lying and standing BP (drop >20 mmHg systolic = hypovolaemia)  ☐ JVP, oedema, ascites, crackles, pigmentation, nodes  ☐ U&E, serum osmolality, glucose; spot urine Na⁺ and osmolality, before IV fluids; TFTs; 8–9 am cortisol if adrenal insufficiency possible; LFTs; lipids, protein if pseudo ☐ All medicines

4Classify — osmolalities first; examination refines

  • Serum osmolality 275–295 → pseudohyponatraemia (lipids, paraprotein); >295 → hyperglycaemia.
  • Urine osmolality ≤100 → primary polydipsia, low solute (beer potomania), excess water.
  • Urine osm >100, urine Na⁺ ≤30 → reduced effective volume: GI or skin losses (dry, postural drop), or HF, cirrhosis, nephrotic (oedema).
  • Urine Na⁺ >30 → SIAD, diuretics, Addison's, renal salt loss (unreliable with diuretics, CKD, saline). SIADH only once hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency and diuretics are excluded; find its cause (SCLC, CNS, chest, drugs).
  • Drugs — thiazides (commonest in elderly), SSRIs/SNRIs, carbamazepine, tricyclics, PPIs, NSAIDs, opioids, antipsychotics.
  • Uncertain — say so; specialist advice; never "SIADH" by default.

5Treat today — mild, stable, well

  • Remove the cause: stop the offending drug if safe; recheck Na⁺ promptly. Overt hypothyroidism → thyroid pathway.
  • Hypovolaemic, stable, drinking: oral rehydration, stop thiazide — never fluid-restrict.
  • SIADH: individualised fluid restriction, all drinks counted (no generic 500 mL/day without specialist input); review at 1 week. Hypervolaemic: specialist-led; no reflex IV fluids.
  • Specialist only: urea, demeclocycline, tolvaptan — never start in primary care. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • Endocrinology: confirmed SIADH, stable 130–134. Nephrology: CKD-related, refractory. Hepatology: cirrhosis. Falls service if Na⁺ <130 with falls. After discharge: recheck per plan, within 1 week for many.

7Never reassure by

  • The patient seeming well below 120 — same-day hospital assessment regardless.
  • A "mild" number in a patient with symptoms or a fast fall.
  • Low Na⁺ plus raised K⁺ each looking mild — think adrenal crisis.
  • "SIADH" without TFTs, cortisol, urine tests and a drug review.
  • Fluid restriction in a hypovolaemic patient, or saline in a hypervolaemic one.
  • A rapidly rising sodium — over-correction causes osmotic demyelination.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for a fit, collapse or fainting, unusual drowsiness or being hard to wake, new or worsening confusion, a severe headache, vomiting, or breathing difficulty.” Same day: “Contact us or 111 today if you feel sick, dizzy on standing, unsteady or fall, get muscle cramps, cannot keep up with fluids, or are very thirsty with little urine. Attend the repeat blood test even if well.”

Remote or OOH: Na⁺ <130 needs face to face — volume status cannot be judged by phone. Convert to face to face or emergency if unable to attend, vomiting, diarrhoea or poor intake, a relative reports confusion, or no one can act on an out-of-hours result.
