Raised CRP or ESR in adults

For: adults (16 and over) with a raised CRP and/or ESR, face to face or remote, in hours or OOH. Not for: children (use the fever in children algorithm) or ongoing care of confirmed GCA or PMR (use their protocols).

Core rule: CRP and ESR are prompts to reassess the patient, not diagnoses. Physiology, symptoms, examination and trajectory set the urgency. A modest, discordant or normal marker excludes neither sepsis nor GCA.

0Stop — call 999 now — whatever the CRP

  • Suspected infection with NEWS2 ≥7, or NEWS2 5–6 with a single parameter scoring 3 or clinical concern (high-risk sepsis) → 999 pre-alert, immediate transfer.
  • Shock or altered consciousness → 999
  • New neurological deficit, cauda equina or cord compression symptoms → Emergency admission and urgent MRI.
  • While waiting: do not delay transfer for bloods. NEWS2 does not override clinical judgement.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • GCA → prednisolone today + Same-day fast-track. Visual loss, amaurosis, diplopia → steroid now + Same-day emergency eye/acute assessment.
  • Spinal infection: new back pain + focal tenderness, night pain, fever, bacteraemia, immunosuppression or IV drug use → Same-day hospital + MRI.
  • ANCA vasculitis: rapidly progressive renal impairment, active urinary sediment, pulmonary haemorrhage, mononeuritis multiplex, purpura or ENT disease → Same-day renal/rheumatology.
  • Possible myeloma + AKI, hypercalcaemia or cord compression; major cytopenia; film suggesting leukaemia → Same-day
  • Myeloma (NG12): 60+ with persistent bone (especially back) pain or unexplained fracture → FBC, calcium, ESR/PV, SPEP + free light chains; suggest myeloma → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Lymphoma: unexplained nodes or splenomegaly → consider Suspected cancer pathway

3Assess — localise the inflammation

☐ Obs + NEWS2 if unwell; source  ☐ Temporal arteries, girdle, spine, nodes, spleen  ☐ FBC + film, renal, LFTs, calcium, albumin, urinalysis + ACR/PCR  ☐ SPEP + free light chains if myeloma plausible  ☐ Targeted autoimmune tests, imaging. Not: routine procalcitonin or blanket autoimmune screens.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Infection — commonest acute cause; CRP does not separate bacterial from viral. Treat the source.
  • PMR — aged 50 or over, new bilateral shoulder and/or hip girdle pain, prolonged morning stiffness. Exclude GCA, infection, cancer, RA, hypothyroidism, myopathy, drugs.
  • Inflammatory disease — RA, SLE (CRP often normal in flares), IBD (CRP can be normal; faecal calprotectin), vasculitis.
  • High ESR, mild CRP — anaemia, renal disease, age, pregnancy, paraprotein/myeloma, SLE.
  • Persistent mild CRP (5–20 mg/L) — obesity, smoking, diabetes, NAFLD, gums. Investigate more if older, symptomatic or rising.
  • Uncertain — persistent + weight loss or night sweats → site-specific NG12 route. Document the differential.

5Treat today — only these

  • Suspected GCA: prednisolone 40–60 mg daily per the local fast-track protocol, without waiting for tests. Visual symptoms: specialist-directed high dose (may be IV).
  • Infection: antibiotic by clinical assessment, likely source and NEWS2 — not CRP alone.
  • PMR (GCA excluded): prednisolone 15 mg daily, individualised taper; steroid monitoring plan and fracture-risk assessment. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • Urgent rheumatology: PMR needing confirmation; ANA positive + multisystem; vasculitis without organ threat.
  • PMR treatment beyond 2 years, relapsing GCA or steroid toxicity → specialist.
  • Use GM GCA fast-track and rheumatology/nephrology Advice & Guidance routes.

7Never reassure by

  • A modest or normal CRP/ESR in suspected sepsis or GCA.
  • Waiting for CRP/ESR, ultrasound or biopsy before prednisolone in GCA.
  • A CRP number to give antibiotics, diagnose sepsis or prove source control.
  • CRP/ESR on steroids, or CRP and fever on tocilizumab — suppressed.
  • An isolated raised ESR as a cancer referral — use the NG12 tests.
  • Repeating CRP/ESR without reassessing the patient.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 or go to A&E if you are very unwell with an infection — confused, breathing very fast, blue or blotchy skin, a rash that does not fade, no urine all day — or get new leg weakness, numbness around the bottom, or lose bladder or bowel control.”

Same-day “Any new loss of vision, blurred or double vision — eye casualty or A&E today. New headache, scalp tenderness, jaw pain on chewing, fever or feeling worse, back pain with fever, much less urine, thirst, confusion or vomiting — contact us today.” Book a review without waiting for the recheck for weight loss, night sweats or growing lumps.
