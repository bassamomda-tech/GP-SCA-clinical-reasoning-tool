Penile discharge

For: people aged 16 and over with penile (urethral) discharge or balanoposthitis in primary care. Not for: under-16s — safeguarding assessment and paediatric sexual-health advice (BASHH children and young people guideline, not adult regimens). Sexual assault → offer SARC referral.

BASHH 2025–26NICE NG12NG110MHRA
Core rule: discharge from the meatus is an STI until tested — first-void urine NAAT for chlamydia and gonorrhoea, suspected gonorrhoea to GUM for culture and ceftriaxone, and partner notification for every STI.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • Severe genital or perineal pain, swelling, erythema, fever or systemic illness (possible Fournier's gangrene), SGLT2 inhibitor or not → 999 immediate emergency hospital assessment
  • Possible torsion — sudden severe scrotal pain, especially under 25, high-riding testis, absent cremasteric reflex → Immediate emergency urology. Do not wait for a scan.
  • Acute urinary retention, septic illness or severe disseminated gonococcal infection (DGI) → Emergency hospital; 999 if clinically unstable
  • While waiting: never delay transfer or treatment for specimens.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Fever + joint pain, tenosynovitis, pustules (DGI) → Same-day hospital/GUM
  • Epididymo-orchitis, STI risk → Same-day GUM/emergency; cover gonorrhoea + chlamydia
  • Possible resistant gonorrhoea (sex abroad, resistant contact) → Same-day GUM
  • Painless penile/oral ulcer + inguinal nodes (syphilis) → serology + Same-day GUM
  • Penile mass/ulcer, STI excluded; lesion persisting after STI treatment; persistent foreskin/glans symptoms → consider Suspected cancer pathway (NG12)
  • Progressive poor stream (stricture), blood-stained discharge without infection → Urgent urology

3Assess

☐ Sexual history (3 months), PrEP, chemsex  ☐ Chaperone, confidentiality, safeguarding  ☐ Meatus, foreskin, ulcers, nodes, epididymis, bladder  ☐ First-void urine NAAT (not MSU); throat/rectal by exposure  ☐ Syphilis, HIV; hepatitis B/C if risk  ☐ Glucose/HbA1c if balanitis severe, persistent or recurrent

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Gonorrhoea — copious pus, within a week of a new partner → same-day GUM.
  • NGU / chlamydia — scant mucoid, 1–3 weeks → NAAT; doxycycline.
  • Balanoposthitis — candidal (white, itchy) or bacterial (foul) → swab if significant or recurrent.
  • Prostatitis — perineal pain, fever → MSU, antibiotics (NG110). Reactive arthritis → STI screen, GUM.
  • Uncertain — NAATs negative, symptoms persist → GUM to confirm urethritis. Pre-ejaculate or dribble → reassure.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Gonorrhoea: ceftriaxone 1 g IM via GUM/local pathway; no oral substitute.
  • Severe NGU, confirmed chlamydia: doxycycline 100 mg BD 7 days (mild NGU: consider awaiting results). Never azithromycin 1 g stat. Avoid doxycycline in pregnancy.
  • Candidal balanitis: clotrimazole 1% BD 7–14 days, or fluconazole 150 mg once if more severe. Bacterial: water/saline; antibiotics only if indicated.
  • Every STI: document partner-notification advice, offer GUM; no sex until partners are treated.

6Refer routinely

  • Persistent NGU after doxycycline → GUM (M. genitalium testing).
  • Phimosis after topical steroid, lichen sclerosus not settling → urology. Red glazed glans not settling → dermatology/GUM.

7Never reassure by

  • The look of the discharge — NGU needs objective confirmation.
  • Oral antibiotics in surgery for suspected gonorrhoea.
  • A normal scan or examination when torsion is possible.
  • Repeated azithromycin or doxycycline for persistent NGU.
  • Treating the patient without partner notification.
  • A penile lesion "still settling" after completed STI treatment.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you get sudden severe pain in the testicle, severe pain or swelling around the penis with fever, a fever with joint pains or a rash, or cannot pass urine, go to A&E straight away — call 999 if very unwell.”

Same day: “Contact us the same day if scrotal pain starts or you are no better 48 hours into treatment. Not settled a week after finishing treatment? Come back.”
