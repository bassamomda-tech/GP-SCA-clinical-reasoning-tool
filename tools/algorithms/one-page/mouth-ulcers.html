Mouth ulcers

For: adults and children with mouth ulcers in primary care. Not for: dental treatment (dentist, urgent dental service or NHS 111) or confirmed oral cancer (oncology leads).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12BSG coeliacNICE/UKHSA antimicrobialSPC (emc)
Core rule: unexplained oral ulceration lasting more than 3 weeks, or a persistent unexplained neck lump → NICE NG12 says consider a suspected cancer pathway referral; local safety policy is to refer by default. Do not delay it with further topical treatment trials.

0Stop — call 999 now — before the full history

  • Possible SJS/TEN: mouth erosions or blistering with a target or blistering rash, skin pain, fever, eye symptoms or a recent high-risk drug → stop the suspected drug → Same-day hospital (usually admission); 999 if critically unwell, airway compromise, shock or cannot travel safely. No Nikolsky testing first.
  • Possible Ludwig's angina: floor-of-mouth or neck swelling with difficulty swallowing, breathing or opening the mouth, drooling or stridor → 999. No tongue depressor.
  • Fever or unwell on chemotherapy or immunosuppression, or on carbimazole, clozapine or methotrexate → Immediate acute oncology / ED; no waiting for FBC.
  • Unable to eat or drink (often a child with primary herpes) or systemically unwell → Same-day hospital

2Screen — serious non-cancer causes

  • Behçet's: oral plus genital ulcers, uveitis, skin lesions → rheumatology / dermatology; painful red eye or photophobia → Same-day eye
  • Pemphigus / pemphigoid: widespread, skin or eye → Same-day; mouth only → Oral medicine ≤2 wk
  • Syphilis: painless indurated ulcer, nodes, sexual risk → serology, Urgent GUM
  • HIV: severe atypical ulcers plus risk factors → offer an HIV test.
  • Methotrexate mucositis → Urgent review of dosing, renal function and FBC.
  • Tonsillar asymmetry (persistent) → urgent ENT; loose teeth → OMFS.

3Assess — good light, whole mouth

☐ Site, size, base, edges, induration  ☐ Lateral tongue, floor of mouth (bimanual), soft palate, retromolar  ☐ Neck nodes  ☐ Red or white patches  ☐ Drugs, skin, eyes, genitals, gut

Tests only if adult-onset, severe, atypical or systemic features: FBC, ferritin, B12, folate, TTG IgA + total IgA (eating gluten). None for typical long-standing minor aphthae.

4Classify — only once blocks 0–2 are clear

  • Minor aphthous — <1 cm, non-keratinised mucosa, heals in 7–14 days.
  • Major aphthous — >1 cm, deep, 2–6 weeks; mimics cancer → oral medicine/OMFS if persistent or atypical.
  • Traumatic — at a sharp tooth or denture; heals 7–10 days once removed. Not healed at 3 weeks → cancer pathway.
  • Viral — primary herpes (gums, fever), hand, foot and mouth → supportive.
  • Drug — nicorandil, NSAIDs, methotrexate, alendronate, chemotherapy.
  • Uncertain — treat as unexplained; review by 3 weeks.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Relief: analgesia, saline rinses, benzydamine 0.15% mouthwash; SLS-free toothpaste.
  • Topical steroid only if the diagnosis is clear and infection excluded: hydrocortisone buccal tablets or betamethasone mouthwash (off-label, spit out). Systemic: specialist only.
  • Primary herpes: consider early oral antiviral if severe, immunocompromised or poor intake.
  • Nicorandil: never stop without an angina plan with the prescriber; record as an adverse reaction.

6Refer routinely

  • Recurrent aphthae no better at 6–8 weeks, no deficiency → oral medicine.
  • Positive TTG, or GI symptoms → gastroenterology.
  • Nicorandil ulcers persisting after withdrawal → Oral medicine ≤2 wk
  • Facial swelling, trismus, spreading dental infection → Same-day dental / OMFS

7Never reassure by

  • Another topical treatment trial for an ulcer over 3 weeks.
  • A "traumatic" label on an ulcer that has not healed.
  • A non-smoker (HPV-related cancer is rising).
  • Looking only at the front of the mouth.
  • A normal haemoglobin: iron deficiency needs a ferritin.
  • An FBC before transfer on chemotherapy.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for swelling under the tongue or in the neck with difficulty swallowing, breathing or opening your mouth, or drooling; or mouth blistering with a rash, skin pain or eye symptoms.” Now: “Fever on chemotherapy — acute oncology line or ED now.” Same day: “Cannot eat or drink — contact us today.” In writing: “Come back if any ulcer has not healed within 3 weeks, grows, the edge feels hard or raised, or a white or red patch won't rub off.” Track every cancer referral to an outcome.
