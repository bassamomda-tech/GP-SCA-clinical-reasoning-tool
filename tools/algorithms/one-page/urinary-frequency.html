Urinary frequency, urgency and nocturia

For: adults with frequency, urgency or nocturia in primary care. Not for: children (children's pathways); pregnancy (antenatal care); established incontinence plans (use the protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG123CG97NG12TA290MHRA
Core rule: first ask big volumes or small volumes? Polyuria needs glucose, calcium and a drug review; small-volume frequency needs a dipstick, examination and a 3-day bladder diary.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Saddle anaesthesia, new bladder or bowel dysfunction, leg weakness (cauda equina, cord compression) → 999 or emergency pathway
  • High-risk sepsis from urinary source; drowsy with polyuria and vomiting (DKA, hyperosmolar) → 999

1Triage — the volume question

PictureFirst move
Large volumes, thirstGlucose/HbA1c, U&E, calcium, drug review

2Screen

  • Retention, acute or with renal impairment → Same-day
  • Fever, loin pain → Same-day
  • Visible haematuria aged 45 or over (unexplained or after UTI) → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Non-visible haematuria aged 60 or over + dysuria or raised WCC → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Hard irregular prostate → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Woman aged 40 or over, persistent urgency/frequency + bloating or pelvic pain → CA125 (NG12)

3Assess

☐ Dipstick ± culture ☐ 3-day bladder diary ☐ Abdomen (palpable bladder) ☐ Men: genitalia, DRE, IPSS ☐ Women: pelvic exam (prolapse, atrophy) ☐ Glucose, U&E, calcium if polyuria ☐ Post-void residual if emptying concern

Nocturnal polyuria: more than about 33% of 24-hour output at night (older adults).

4Classify

  • UTI / prostatitis — acute, dysuria, nitrites → NG109.
  • OAB — urgency, small volumes, normal urine → bladder training.
  • Polyuria — diabetes, hypercalcaemia, diuretics, lithium, intake.
  • Nocturnal polyuria — HF, OSA, evening fluids.
  • Prostate, prolapse, bladder pain syndrome — by examination and pattern.
  • Uncertain — diary, review in 6 weeks; persistent sterile pyuria → urology.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • Bladder training at least 6 weeks; fluids about 1.5–2 L; cut caffeine.
  • Antimuscarinic (e.g. solifenacin 5 mg daily) — weigh anticholinergic burden; no IR oxybutynin in frail older women.
  • Mirabegron 50 mg daily if antimuscarinic unsuitable — BP first; not if 180/110 mmHg or more.
  • Men, voiding: tamsulosin MR 400 micrograms daily.

6Refer routinely

  • Refractory OAB, symptomatic prolapse, voiding dysfunction → urogynaecology / urology.
  • Recurrent UTI in men, bladder pain syndrome, suspected stone → urology.

7Never reassure by

  • "Just OAB" without a dipstick and diary.
  • A normal PSA when the prostate feels hard.
  • Haematuria explained by a UTI without a repeat dipstick.
  • No pain — chronic retention is painless.
  • "Old age" for nocturia before checking for HF, diabetes and calcium.
  • Irritative symptoms with sterile pyuria in a smoker.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you get numbness between the legs, new leg weakness, or lose bladder or bowel control.” Same-day “Contact us today if you can't pass urine, get a fever with back pain or shivering, see blood in your urine, or have extreme thirst with weight loss or vomiting.” Drug review at 4 weeks.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented
