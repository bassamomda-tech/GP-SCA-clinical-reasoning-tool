Ear discharge (otorrhoea)

For: adults and children with ear discharge in primary care. Not for: ear pain without discharge (otalgia algorithm); head injury assessment itself (NICE NG232).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE CKSNICE NG91NICE NG232ENT UK
Core rule: document whether you saw the drum. If you cannot, assume it may be perforated. Discharge that keeps coming back needs re-examination, not another course of drops.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Intracranial complication — severe headache, fever with drowsiness, neck stiffness, seizure, focal neurology → 999
  • Head injury with reduced GCS → 999. Clear fluid after a head injury → A&E for CT within 1 hour; nothing in the ear.

1Triage by host and drum

FindingAction
Diabetic or olderSevere pain, granulation, not settling → Same-day ENT
Drum intactOtitis externa → acetic acid or antibiotic ± steroid drops

Discharge 2 weeks or moreCSOM or cholesteatoma → ENT

2Screen

  • Mastoid swelling, pinna pushed forward → Same-day
  • Facial weakness with discharge → Same-day ENT
  • Button battery or drum injury → Same-day ENT
  • Sudden hearing loss or severe vertigo → Same-day (within 24 hours)
  • Canal mass, ulcer, persistent bloody discharge → Urgent ENT

3Assess

☐ Tragal tenderness, nodes, mastoid  ☐ Canal: debris, fungus, granulation, foreign body  ☐ Drum seen and intact?  ☐ Hearing, facial nerve  ☐ Glucose if diabetic or recurrent  ☐ Swab only if failure, recurrent, fungal or high risk

Do not: syringe a perforated ear, a battery or vegetable matter.

4Classify

  • Otitis externa — itch, pain moving the pinna, swollen canal.
  • Fungal — fluffy white or black debris after repeated drops.
  • AOM with perforation — pain then discharge with relief.
  • CSOM — painless discharge 2 weeks or more, central perforation.
  • Cholesteatoma — scanty, foul, recurrent; attic retraction → ENT.
  • Uncertain — drum not seen: treat as perforated; review in 7 days.

5Treat today — doses per SPC, BNF/BNFC and formulary

  • Mild OE: acetic acid 2% one spray three times daily for 7 days.
  • Moderate OE: antibiotic ± steroid drops 7 days; perforation → ciprofloxacin drops.
  • Fungal: clotrimazole 1% solution; stop antibiotic drops.
  • AOM perforation: analgesia; amoxicillin 5–7 days if needed (NG91).

6Refer routinely

  • ENT or aural care: CSOM, uncomplicated cholesteatoma, canal too swollen for drops, failure after two courses.
  • Perforation not healed by 6 weeks; recurrent otitis externa.

7Never reassure by

  • "Just otitis externa" in a diabetic with severe pain.
  • Drops that reduced the discharge of a cholesteatoma.
  • Clear fluid after a head injury.
  • A drum you did not actually see.
  • Repeat prescriptions without re-examination.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for a severe headache, stiff neck, high fever with drowsiness or confusion, or a fit.” Same day “Contact us the same day for swelling behind the ear, facial weakness, sudden hearing loss or dizziness, severe pain that keeps you awake, or clear fluid after a head injury.”

Return if discharge lasts more than 2 weeks or hearing does not recover.
