Widespread or blistering rash in adults

For: adults with a new widespread or blistering rash in primary care. Not for: children (children's pathways); localised single lesions; non-blistering maculopapular rashes (maculopapular rash algorithm).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BADNICE NG240NG253NG153BASHH
Core rule: look at the mouth, eyes and genitals, take observations and list every drug started in the last 8 weeks before naming the rash. Painful skin with mucosal erosions is SJS/TEN until proven otherwise.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Mucosal erosions + painful dusky or peeling skin after a new drug (SJS/TEN) → stop drug, 999 or same-day emergency admission
  • Non-blanching rash, unwell → 999; benzylpenicillin or ceftriaxone if no delay to transfer
  • Pain out of proportion, necrotic skin (necrotising infection) → 999
  • Airway, breathing or circulation compromise with wheals → IM adrenaline 500 micrograms, 999

2Screen

  • Erythroderma (90% or more red skin) → Same-day
  • Eczema herpeticum → aciclovir now + Same-day dermatology
  • DRESS: fever, facial swelling, nodes 2–8 weeks after a drug → Same-day
  • Disseminated zoster → Same-day
  • Palms and soles, sexual risk → syphilis/HIV tests; sexual health within a week

3Assess

☐ Obs, NEWS2 ☐ Mouth, eyes, genitals ☐ Nikolsky sign ☐ Blister type, distribution, % body surface ☐ Drug list with start dates ☐ Bloods if unwell (FBC + eosinophils, LFT, U&E) ☐ HSV/VZV PCR swab ☐ tTG IgA if dermatitis herpetiformis

Do not start oral steroids for an undiagnosed blistering rash without specialist advice.

4Classify

  • Severe drug reaction — new drug, fever, painful skin → admit.
  • Immunobullous — pemphigoid (tense, older), pemphigus (erosions, mouth), dermatitis herpetiformis.
  • Viral — chickenpox, zoster, HSV, mpox.
  • Urticaria / urticarial vasculitis.
  • Papulosquamous — guttate psoriasis, pityriasis rosea, secondary syphilis.
  • Uncertain — photo to dermatology; review in 48–72 hours.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Stop the culprit drug; record allergy; Yellow Card.
  • Eczema herpeticum: aciclovir 400 mg five times a day. Adult chickenpox within 24 h: 800 mg five times a day for 7 days.
  • Pemphigoid (on advice): clobetasol 0.05% + emollient. Urticaria: cetirizine 10 mg daily.
  • Bullous impetigo: flucloxacillin 500 mg four times a day for 5 days.

6Refer routinely

  • Localised pemphigoid, dermatitis herpetiformis, urticarial vasculitis → Urgent dermatology.
  • Extensive guttate psoriasis, atypical pityriasis rosea → dermatology.

7Never reassure by

  • A normal temperature in early SJS/TEN or DRESS.
  • A photo without looking in the mouth and eyes.
  • "Eczema" or "scabies" in an itchy older patient with urticated plaques.
  • "Infected eczema" when erosions are punched-out and painful.
  • "Viral rash" on the palms and soles without syphilis serology.
  • The drug was started "weeks ago" — DRESS takes 2–8 weeks.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if your skin starts peeling or blistering quickly, you get sore eyes with mouth or genital sores, a rash that doesn't fade under a glass, or swelling of the lips or difficulty breathing.” Same-day “Contact us today for a new fever, painful or spreading blisters, eye pain, facial swelling, or if you can't eat or drink.” Review in 48–72 hours.
