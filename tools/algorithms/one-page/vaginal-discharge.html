Vaginal discharge in adults

For: adults aged ≥16 with vaginal discharge. Not for: children and young people — local paediatric and safeguarding pathways; no internal examination in primary care. Local sexual-health guidance takes precedence.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
BASHHIUSTI 2023NICE NG12NG253GM antimicrobials
Core rule: discharge features are suggestive, not diagnostic. Treat PID early and empirically — delay risks tubal infertility and ectopic pregnancy — and examine the cervix in any blood-stained or persistent discharge.

0Stop — call 999 now — before routine management

  • Suspected sepsis (NEWS2) with instability, confusion or poor perfusion; peritonism; suspected ruptured tubo-ovarian abscess → 999 / sepsis pathway (NG253).
  • Toxic shock: fever, rash, vomiting or diarrhoea, hypotension or confusion with a tampon, cup or foreign body in place → remove it if easily accessible → 999
  • Anaphylaxis, angioedema or severe rash after treatment → 999; document and avoid the drug.
  • Pregnant and unstable → 999. Suspected intra-amniotic infection, PPROM, significant bleeding or maternal or fetal compromise → Same-day maternity, discussed promptly.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • PID with systemic illness, severe pain, suspected abscess, pregnancy, diagnostic doubt, can't take oral treatment or no better at 72 h → Same-day gynae / admit
  • Suspicious cervical lesion → Urgent suspected cancer. Unexplained postmenopausal bleeding → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Age 55+, unexplained discharge: first presentation, or with thrombocytosis or haematuria → consider urgent direct-access USS (NG12)
  • Gonorrhoea confirmed or strongly suspected → Urgent sexual health
  • Immunocompromised, severe or atypical → low threshold for admission. Assault or coercion → local safeguarding pathway.

3Assess — chaperone offered and documented

☐ Obs; NEWS2 if unwell  ☐ Vulva: ulcers, lichen sclerosus  ☐ Speculum if acceptable: friable cervix, contact bleeding, foreign body  ☐ Vaginal pH: >4.5 BV or TV; ≤4.5 candida or physiological  ☐ Bimanual if PID is possible  ☐ Self-taken vulvovaginal NAAT; gonorrhoea culture before treatment if feasible, never delaying it  ☐ HIV and syphilis tests by risk

Don't order: CA-125, routine urine culture, HVS if asymptomatic, Gardnerella or Ureaplasma tests.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • BV — thin grey/white, fishy odour, pH >4.5, clue cells → treat.
  • Candida — itch, thick white curdy, pH ≤4.5 → antifungal.
  • Trichomonas — frothy yellow-green, pH >4.5 → NAAT; treat with partners.
  • Chlamydia, gonorrhoea, cervicitis — mucopurulent, friable cervix; often silent → NAAT.
  • Atrophic — postmenopausal, dry → topical oestrogen. Physiological — cyclical, no odour or itch → reassure once pathology excluded.
  • Uncertain or persistent → review and escalate; don't repeat empirical treatment.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • PID, today, not waiting for swabs: ceftriaxone 1 g IM + doxycycline 100 mg BD + metronidazole 400 mg BD 14 days; review at 72 h. No routine fluoroquinolone.
  • BV: metronidazole 400 mg BD 5–7 days, 0.75% gel or clindamycin cream (weakens latex); no 2 g stat in pregnancy. Candida: clotrimazole 500 mg pessary or fluconazole 150 mg; pregnant: topical imidazole 7 days, no fluconazole.
  • TV: metronidazole 400–500 mg BD 7 days. Chlamydia: doxycycline 100 mg BD 7 days; pregnant: azithromycin 1 g then 500 mg daily 2 days, test of cure ≥3 weeks. Gonorrhoea: ceftriaxone 1 g IM, specialist-led.
  • STI: partner notification; no sex until both treated.

6Refer routinely

  • Sexual health: STI screen and contact tracing; possible LGV, syphilis, M. genitalium; BV or candida not responding.
  • Recurrent candida (≥4 a year): culture, HbA1c, fluconazole induction then weekly for 6 months. Gynaecology: candida not responding, vulval biopsy, suspected fistula.

7Never reassure by

  • Colour or odour alone — it doesn't separate BV, trichomonas, gonorrhoea and cervicitis.
  • A negative trichomonas wet mount.
  • Waiting for swab results before treating suspected PID.
  • A normal cervical screening result when discharge is blood-stained or persistent.
  • Skipping vulval inspection (lichen sclerosus, herpes, syphilis).
  • Repeated empirical courses for persistent unexplained discharge.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for worsening fever and pelvic pain on antibiotics, severe tummy pain or fainting; face, lip or tongue swelling or difficulty breathing after treatment; or fever, rash, vomiting or feeling faint with a tampon or other item inside.” Same day “Contact us today for pelvic pain with fever, feeling unwell, no better after 72 hours of PID treatment, new fever, blood-stained discharge or new bleeding, any pregnancy worry, or can't keep tablets down.”

Return if discharge persists or recurs → reassess, examine the cervix. Chase swabs; confirm partner notification. Remote: PID possible → examine face to face the same day.
