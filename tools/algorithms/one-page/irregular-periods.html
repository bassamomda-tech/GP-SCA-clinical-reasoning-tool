Irregular periods (oligomenorrhoea / AUB)

For: women of reproductive age with irregular, infrequent, frequent or intermenstrual/postcoital bleeding, in primary care. Not for: known pregnancy (bleeding in pregnancy pathway) or expected bleeding on HRT (BMS/NICE HRT pathway). Postmenopausal bleeding is kept separate — block 2.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NG23NG88NG126Int. PCOS Guideline 2023
Core rule: urine hCG first in anyone who could be pregnant; then exclude cervical and endometrial cancer — look at the cervix for postcoital or intermenstrual bleeding, and treat any postmenopausal bleeding as endometrial cancer until proven otherwise. Only then settle on a benign label, and document it.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • Heavy bleeding with dizziness, syncope, tachycardia or hypotension, or with chest pain, breathlessness or collapse → 999 (haemorrhage).
  • Possible pregnancy with shoulder-tip pain, collapse or peritonism, or haemodynamic instability → 999 / A&E (ruptured ectopic).

1Triage by urine hCG — if pregnancy possible (NG126)

+ unstableInstability, significant pain/bleeding, shoulder-tip pain, syncope, peritonism → A&E / 999
+ painPelvic pain, tenderness or cervical motion tenderness → Immediate EPAU / OOH gynae
+ bleedingPain, ≥6 weeks or unsure dates → EPAU by status. Else (<6 weeks, no risk factors) → assess ± expectant, safety-net.
NegativeScreen (block 2). Recheck hCG if cycles change.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Postmenopausal bleeding, unexplained, not due to HRT: 55+ → Suspected cancer pathway; under 55 → consider it.
  • Cervix looks cancerous → consider Suspected cancer pathway. Persistent PCB/IMB, normal cervix, STI tests negative → Urgent gynae
  • Endometrial risk: infrequent heavy bleeding with obesity or PCOS, tamoxifen, failed treatment, pelvic mass, thick endometrium → Urgent gynae
  • Persistent bloating, early satiety, pelvic pain: ≥40 CA125 + urgent USS; ≤39 consider urgent USS. Ascites, or mass not obviously fibroids → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Visual-field loss, neuro deficit or severe headache + raised prolactin/amenorrhoea → Same-day specialist. Rapid virilisation → Urgent endocrinology
  • Low weight + amenorrhoea: suspected eating disorder → Refer without delay; bradycardia, hypotension, syncope, electrolyte upset → Same-day acute medical. Not BMI alone.

3Assess — once blocks 0–2 are answered

☐ Bleeding diary ~3 cycles; contraception, drugs, sexual history, fertility wishes ☐ BMI, BP, hirsutism, acne; visual fields if prolactin raised ☐ Speculum + chlamydia/gonorrhoea NAAT for PCB or IMB; bimanual if pain, mass or CMT ☐ hCG, TSH, prolactin as indicated; FBC if heavy; PCOS: testosterone + FAI/SHBG, HbA1c, lipids; FSH ×2, 4–6 weeks apart, only if POI suspected

Not in primary care: blind endometrial biopsy, random "hormone profiles", pituitary MRI before prolactin confirmed.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

Infrequent: cycles >35 days, <8 a year or any >90 days. Frequent: <21 days. Irregular in year 1 after menarche is normal.

  • PCOS — 2 of 3 after excluding others: ovulatory dysfunction, hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovaries. Cycles + hyperandrogenism → no USS.
  • Thyroid / prolactin — repeat a raised prolactin; review antipsychotics, metoclopramide, domperidone, opioids; macroprolactin.
  • POI — under 40, menopausal symptoms, raised FSH on 2 samples, never one. Perimenopause — 45+: clinical, no FSH; still needs contraception.
  • Hypothalamic — low weight, restriction, exercise, stress; low/normal FSH. Exclusion diagnosis.
  • Structural / cervical — IMB, PCB, heavy, enlarged uterus → TVUSS or outpatient hysteroscopy.
  • Iatrogenic — hormonal contraception (common first 3 months), anticoagulants, tamoxifen.
  • Uncertain — say so; name who reviews and when.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • PCOS, no pregnancy wish: combined hormonal contraception 1st line; check UKMEC. Weight loss 5–10% if overweight.
  • Prolonged anovulation: after excluding pregnancy, protect the endometrium — CHC, LNG-IUS or intermittent progestogen (bleed at least every 3–4 months).
  • Trying to conceive: folic acid, weight optimisation; fertility referral by duration, age and risk.
  • Hypothyroid: levothyroxine per NG145. Drug prolactin: change only with the prescriber; never stop an antipsychotic abruptly.

6Refer routinely — and follow up

  • Confirmed POI → specialist service (HRT or CHC to at least average menopause age). Unexplained high prolactin → endocrinology. Fibroids, polyps → gynaecology.
  • Review: results 1–2 weeks; 3 months — diary, endometrial protection; PCOS yearly — weight, BP, glycaemia, lipids, mood.

7Never reassure by

  • A normal cervical screening result — it does not explain PCB.
  • A withdrawal bleed — abnormal bleeding still needs investigation.
  • Blaming contraception before pregnancy, STI and cervix are checked.
  • A normal CA125, or CA125 alone under 40.
  • A single FSH or a single prolactin result.
  • "Stress" or PCOS under 40 before POI is considered.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “If you ever feel faint with heavy bleeding, or have strong tummy pain and could be pregnant, call 999 or go to A&E.” Same day: positive pregnancy test with pain or bleeding; new visual loss, neurological symptoms or severe headache. Book now, don't wait for review: “bleeding after sex, between periods, or at all once periods have stopped for a year”; heavier or continuous bleeding, a new pelvic mass, severe pelvic pain or bloating, rapidly progressive virilisation.

Document: hCG result, speculum findings, red flags asked, endometrial-protection plan and safety-net given.

✓Safe to leave primary care only if — all five, documented

Pregnancy excluded; no block 0–2 row applies Speculum and swabs done if PCB or IMB One cause explains it, or uncertainty stated Endometrial protection planned; review owner and date set Safety-net given and documented
