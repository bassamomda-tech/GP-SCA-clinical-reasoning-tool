Subcutaneous lumps (lumps and bumps)

For: adults, children and young people with a palpable soft-tissue lump in primary care. Not for: breast, axillary, neck, thyroid, testicular or skin lesions as a generic lump — they have their own NG12 routes (block 2). Children run on a 48-hour clock.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12UK sarcoma guideline 2024NG253EBI programme
Core rule: growth is the trigger and imaging comes before the knife. An unexplained lump increasing in size → consider urgent ultrasound; never excise or "shell out" a lump you have not characterised.

0Stop — emergency first — infection and vascular lumps

  • Signs of sepsis with high-risk features (abscess, cellulitis, deep infection) → 999 (NICE NG253).
  • Pulsatile or expansile mass with haemodynamic instability or uncontrolled bleeding → 999. Never aspirate or biopsy it.
  • Rapidly spreading cellulitis, expanding painful vascular mass, active bleeding, distal ischaemia or neurovascular compromise, irreducible tender hernia, suspected deep infection or joint involvement → Same-day
  • Stable pulsatile mass → Urgent vascular or radiology advice and Doppler imaging. No office drainage of a possible deep abscess, vascular lesion, or hand, face or joint lump.

2Screen — any one row changes the route

  • >5 cm, deep to fascia, painful, fixed, rapid growth, recurrence after excision, indeterminate scan → image or discuss. Do not wait for 5 cm.
  • Unexplained breast lump ≥30 y → suspected cancer referral; axillary lump ≥30 y → consider it.
  • Neck lump unexplained ≥45 y, or persistent at any age; unexplained thyroid lump → consider suspected cancer referral.
  • Testis painless enlargement or change in shape/texture → consider suspected cancer referral; unexplained or persistent symptoms → consider urgent ultrasound.
  • Nodes persistent, enlarging, hard, fixed, supraclavicular, generalised or systemic features → bloods, urgent tests, consider lymphoma referral.
  • Skin lesion suggesting SCC or nodular melanoma → consider suspected cancer referral.

3Assess — history, the lump, then imaging

☐ Growth (old photos), pain, previous excision and histology, systemic symptoms, known cancer, NF1, radiotherapy  ☐ Size in cm, depth (tense the muscle), mobility, skin, pulsatility, cough impulse, nodes, distal neurovascular status. Uncertain → ultrasound first, "?sarcoma" on the request. Targeted bloods only.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Lipoma — small, soft, mobile, superficial, stable → reassure if secure. Deep, large, painful, enlarging or recurrent → image first.
  • Cyst — punctum (epidermoid) or smooth on the scalp (pilar) → leave, or excise when quiet.
  • Ganglion — firm, by a joint or tendon sheath → observe if symptoms are minimal.
  • Reactive node — tender, mobile, local infection → treat the cause and re-examine.
  • Abscess — hot, fluctuant. Hernia — cough impulse; irreducible and tender → same-day.
  • Uncertain — say so → ultrasound, not excision.

5Treat today — only these

  • Fluctuant abscess (not deep or vascular): incise and drain. Antibiotics only for cellulitis, systemic upset or immunocompromise (local guideline; doses per BNF and local formulary).
  • Inflamed cyst: often sterile inflammation; antibiotics only with a clear indication; excise with its wall once settled.
  • Growth unclear: record size in cm; book a dated re-measure by the same method.
  • Anything excised → histology, labelled with site and differential; result reviewed and acted on.

6Refer routinely

  • Symptomatic, clearly benign lumps → Routine surgery or dermatology, per local Evidence-Based Interventions policy.
  • Ganglion painful, limiting or with nerve symptoms → discuss hand/orthopaedic options.
  • Recurrent abscesses → HbA1c; assess for hidradenitis, PVL Staphylococcus aureus or immunodeficiency.

7Never reassure by

  • A lump that is painless, under 5 cm or mobile.
  • An old "lipoma" label — recurrence at an excision site is suspicious.
  • A slip sign or transillumination.
  • A normal FBC or LDH when lymphoma is possible.
  • A normal X-ray when bone pain or swelling persists.
  • A benign look — every excised lump goes for histology.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you feel very unwell — new confusion, very fast breathing, mottled or ashen skin, or not passing urine.” Same-day “Spreading redness, fever, or the lump quickly becoming bigger and tender — contact us today.”

Benign label: “Come back if it grows, becomes painful or fixed, the skin changes or it comes back — don't wait for it to reach 5 cm. Photograph it beside a ruler every few weeks.”
