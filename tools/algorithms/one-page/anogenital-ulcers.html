Anogenital ulcers

For: sexually active adults with a new genital or perianal ulcer, in primary care. Not for: children, or a confirmed diagnosis already under care (use the genital herpes, HIV and chlamydia protocols). Pregnancy, immunosuppression and HIV exposure ≤72 h go straight to block 2.

Core rule: a painless, indurated genital ulcer is syphilis until serology proves otherwise. Test for every STI at the same visit (co-infection is common), and send a persistent or unexplained ulcer for specialist assessment without delaying STI testing.

0Stop — call 999 now

  • Rapidly spreading painful ulceration, black eschar, crepitus or perineal spread with sepsis (Fournier's, necrotising fasciitis), or progressive ulceration with systemic collapse → 999
  • Hospital gives IV antibiotics and immediate debridement: transfer is the treatment; do not wait for swabs or bloods.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Urinary retention, parenteral-level pain, severe systemic illness, progressive necrosis, ocular or neurological symptoms → Same-day hospital
  • Pregnant with suspected primary herpes or syphilis → Same-day hospital with obstetric input. First-episode herpes in the third trimester → Same-day obstetric contact
  • HIV exposure ≤72 h → PEP, ideally within 24 h → Same-day sexual health / ED
  • HIV-positive or immunocompromised, or ulcer persisting or unexplained → Urgent GUM reassessment
  • Behçet's with painful red eye, photophobia or visual change → Urgent ophthalmology
  • NG12: unexplained vulval lump, ulceration or bleeding; penile mass or ulcer once STI excluded or persisting after STI treatment; unexplained anal mass or ulceration → consider Suspected cancer pathway

3Assess — chaperone, good light

☐ Sexual history (anal sex, travel, skin-to-skin contact, exposure ≤72 h), pregnancy, immunosuppression, medicines, eye/joint/bowel symptoms  ☐ Ulcer edge, base, number; inguinal nodes; anus, mouth, palms and soles

☐ HSV PCR with typing from a fresh lesion base  ☐ Syphilis serology + quantitative RPR  ☐ 4th-generation HIV test  ☐ Gonorrhoea/chlamydia NAAT by site  ☐ Mpox/VZV PCR if features  ☐ Hepatitis B/C where indicated. Not routine: ANA, HLA-B51, Paul-Bunnell.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Genital herpes — painful vesicles → shallow ulcers, tender nodes; first episode may be systemic → treat, GUM.
  • Primary syphilis — single painless indurated ulcer, clean base, non-tender rubbery node; may be hidden on cervix or rectum → urgent GUM.
  • LGV — proctitis or buboes in MSM → rectal NAAT + LGV genotyping via GUM; not standard chlamydia treatment.
  • Non-STI — Lipschütz ulcer (exclusion, after STI tests), aphthae, fixed drug eruption, lichen sclerosus/planus, Crohn's, Behçet's, trauma or assault.
  • Uncertain — say so: full STI screen, urgent GUM, review until healed or explained.

5Treat today — doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary

  • First-episode herpes (within 5 days or new lesions forming): aciclovir 400 mg TDS or valaciclovir 500 mg BD for 5–10 days; review at day 5.
  • Symptom relief: saline bathing, paracetamol + ibuprofen, lidocaine 5% gel; pass urine in a warm bath.
  • Known recurrent herpes: aciclovir 800 mg TDS for 2 days; consider suppression (400 mg BD) if painful, distressing, disruptive or transmission concern; review yearly.
  • Not GP prescribing: syphilis, LGV, PEP, HIV → GUM/HIV services. Fixed drug eruption → withdraw the drug.

6Refer routinely

  • Partner notification for syphilis, HIV, LGV, gonorrhoea, chlamydia → sexual-health service.
  • Recurrent or multisystem Behçet's → rheumatology/dermatology. Atypical perianal fissures or fistulae → gastroenterology/colorectal.
  • Recurrent aphthae → ferritin, B12, folate, coeliac screen. Ulcerated, indurated or irregular wart → specialist.
  • HSV distress → NHS Talking Therapies; HSV history → tell midwife at booking.

7Never reassure by

  • A painless ulcer — syphilis and early cancer are painless.
  • Spontaneous healing — a chancre heals in 3–6 weeks untreated.
  • Negative syphilis serology very early — repeat if suspicion remains.
  • Negative HSV PCR from an old or crusted lesion.
  • Negative HIV test within 45 days of exposure — repeat.
  • One positive result or a Lipschütz label — test for every STI first.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if the sore spreads fast, the skin turns black, or you feel very unwell or collapse.” Same day: “Contact us today if you cannot pass urine, have severe pain, eye pain or vision change, a severe headache or new weakness, or you are pregnant.”

Remote or OOH: an ulcer is not assessed by phone or photo alone — arrange face-to-face examination or direct urgent sexual-health access. If the ulcer persists or stays unexplained, reassess urgently. Before syphilis treatment, warn about the Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction (usually within 24 h); in pregnancy it needs obstetric advice.
