Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) in adults

For: adults with new difficulty swallowing, oropharyngeal or oesophageal, face to face or remote. Not for: a lump sensation with normal swallowing (globus, block 4) or children. Reflux without dysphagia → GORD protocol.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12 (updated April 2026)NG128CG184BSGRCSLT · IDDSI
Core rule: unexplained dysphagia at any age → suspected cancer pathway referral for oesophageal or stomach cancer (NG12). Never delay it to trial a PPI. Localise and investigate alongside the referral, never instead of it.

0Stop — call 999 now — or transfer to hospital now

  • Can't swallow saliva, drooling or persistent regurgitation (complete obstruction, food bolus) → immediate hospital transfer Same-day for urgent endoscopy; airway compromise, respiratory distress, severe chest pain, suspected perforation or instability → 999
  • Haematemesis: active, with instability or known varices → 999; otherwise, or melaena → Same-day hospital
  • Chest pain with dysphagia: suspected ACS or aortic dissection → 999. Troponin is a hospital test. Suspected acute stroke → 999 via the stroke pathway.
  • While waiting: no blind removal, no forced oral fluids; stroke → nil by mouth, no oral medicines or fluids until a swallow screen (NG128).

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Food impaction now, managing secretions → Same-day hospital. Recurrent food bolus → Urgent gastroenterology.
  • Aspiration signs (wet voice, coughing at meals, recurrent chest infections), well → Urgent SALT and dietetic assessment
  • Severe weight loss with inability to swallow fluids → Same-day
  • Dysarthria, tongue fasciculation, fatigable swallow (MND, myasthenia, Parkinson's) → Urgent neurology and SALT
  • Aged ≥55, weight loss plus upper abdominal pain, reflux or dyspepsia → Suspected cancer pathway (NG12)

3Assess — alongside the referral

☐ Weight, BMI, % weight loss, MUST  ☐ Mouth: candida, teeth, mass  ☐ Neck: nodes, thyroid, pouch  ☐ Neuro: dysarthria, wet voice, tongue fasciculation  ☐ Chest, SpO₂  ☐ Abdomen: epigastric mass, liver

Targeted bloods (FBC, ferritin if anaemia, U&E, LFTs). OGD comes via the referral. Not in primary care: CT staging, manometry. CXR only if aspiration or chest disease suspected.

4Classify — a probability, never a reason not to refer

  • Oropharyngeal — trouble starting the swallow, choking at onset, nasal regurgitation → SALT; treat the neuro cause.
  • Mechanical — solids then liquids, progressive → cancer, peptic stricture, ring → suspected cancer pathway.
  • Motility — solids and liquids from onset, long history (achalasia, spasm) → OGD first; gastroenterology arranges manometry.
  • Eosinophilic oesophagitis — young, atopic, food impaction → OGD with biopsies, gastroenterology.
  • Pharyngeal pouch — late regurgitation, halitosis, gurgling → ENT / upper GI. Globus — lump sensation only; food and liquids pass, no alarm features.
  • Uncertain — refer on the suspected cancer pathway. Never label it reflux or globus.

5Treat today — only these

  • Prominent reflux: a PPI may be given for symptoms while the referral proceeds; document it and tell the endoscopy service.
  • Oral candidiasis: miconazole oral gel first line (nystatin if unsuitable or on warfarin), or systemic antifungal if extensive; fluconazole interacts with warfarin and QT-prolonging medicines.
  • Pill oesophagitis: review the culprit (bisphosphonate, doxycycline, NSAID, iron, KCl) with the prescriber; take with water, upright.
  • Nutrition: supplements via a dietitian or local ACBS criteria, safe for the swallow; texture or fluid change (IDDSI) only as SALT recommends. Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • Suspected oropharyngeal dysphagia → SALT (urgent for stroke, MND or aspiration).
  • Suspected achalasia or motility disorder, EoE, GORD not responding to PPI → gastroenterology.
  • Smoking and alcohol: advise stopping; stop smoking service.

7Never reassure by

  • A response to a PPI.
  • Young age: dysphagia has no age threshold.
  • No weight loss: it adds urgency but is not needed.
  • An intermittent or long-standing history.
  • Where the patient feels the hold-up: a throat sensation can be distal.
  • Calling it globus when food or liquids stick.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you can't swallow your saliva, choke and struggle to breathe, vomit blood, or get sudden severe chest pain.” Same day: “Contact us today if swallowing gets quickly worse, you cough or choke with a fever, or your weight is falling faster.”

Referral: confirm it was received and explain the local time frame; chase if not heard. Do not wait for the appointment if swallowing deteriorates or intake cannot be maintained.
