Unintentional weight loss in adults

For: adults with unexplained, unintentional weight loss. Not for: children (paediatric pathways) or planned weight loss; known eating disorder or malnutrition → use those protocols.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NG69CG32RCPsych MEEDBAPEN MUST
Core rule: confirm it is unintentional and measure it. Most is not cancer, but if weight keeps falling with normal tests, reassess and use the non-specific symptoms pathway.

0Stop — act now — before the full work-up

  • Physiological instability, haemodynamic instability from GI bleeding, suspected sepsis (NG253) or acute decompensation → 999
  • Severe malnutrition (do not rely on BMI alone) or unable to eat or drink → Same-day medical assessment
  • Possible eating disorder with syncope, severe bradycardia, hypotension or postural drop, hypothermia, ECG abnormality, severe electrolyte disturbance, dehydration, rapid loss, purging or high suicide risk → Same-day medical assessment (MEED)
  • Alcohol excess with confusion, ataxia or eye-movement signs (Wernicke's) → Emergency hospital transfer; parenteral thiamine (e.g. Pabrinex) first if available, without delaying transfer — oral is not adequate.

2Screen — NG12 (updated April 2026); one row is enough

  • Dysphagia (any age); 55+ with upper abdominal pain, reflux or dyspepsia → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Lower GI (40+ with abdominal pain; <50 with rectal bleeding; 50+; bowel change, iron deficiency, abdominal mass) → FIT; ≥10 µg Hb/g → Suspected cancer pathway. Rectal mass or high concern → refer without waiting for FIT.
  • Haemoptysis, 40+ → Suspected cancer pathway. 40+ with cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain or appetite loss → CXR within 2 weeks
  • Jaundice, 40+ → Suspected cancer pathway. 60+ with diarrhoea, back or abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, constipation or new diabetes → consider urgent direct-access CT.
  • Visible haematuria, 45+ (urology); postmenopausal bleeding, ascites or pelvic mass (gynaecology) → Suspected cancer pathway
  • Haematology: concerning nodes → lymphoma criteria; unexplained bruising or bleeding → FBC within 48 h; 60+ persistent bone pain or unexplained fracture → myeloma screen incl. SPEP and serum free light chains.

3Assess — guided by history and examination

☐ Weight, BMI, frailty  ☐ Nodes (incl. supraclavicular), thyroid, chest, abdomen, teeth  ☐ Rectal, breast, testes, pelvis if indicated  ☐ Mood, suicide risk

☐ FBC, U&E, LFTs, albumin, calcium, CRP/ESR, HbA1c, TFTs, urine dipstick. Selectively: coeliac serology (eating gluten), ferritin, B12/folate, HIV, CXR. Not routinely: CEA, CA-125, CA 19-9, AFP; PSA on age alone.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Malignancy — older age, alarm features → NG12 (block 2).
  • Appetite kept — hyperthyroidism, uncontrolled diabetes, coeliac, IBD, pancreatic insufficiency.
  • Organic — TB, HIV, COPD, heart failure, CKD, cirrhosis, Addison's.
  • Mental health — depression (common in older people), dementia, eating disorder, alcohol.
  • Drugs, social — GLP-1, SGLT2, metformin, SSRIs, digoxin; poverty, isolation, teeth.
  • Uncertain — say so; review in 2–4 weeks; still losing → non-specific symptoms pathway.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • MUST ≥2: nutrition plan, food first, consider dietitian; supplements only if clinically indicated.
  • Hyperthyroidism: carbimazole only once confirmed; sore throat or fever → urgent FBC.
  • Low mood: assess first; mirtazapine may suit depression with poor appetite, not as an appetite stimulant.
  • Alcohol: AUDIT-C; oral thiamine if malnourished or at risk. Review appetite-suppressing drugs.

6Refer routinely

  • Stable eating disorder → community eating-disorder service (NG69); BEAT.
  • Abnormal result → the matching specialty pathway.
  • Food insecurity, isolation → social prescribing, food banks; dentist.

7Never reassure by

  • Normal first-line bloods while the weight keeps falling.
  • BMI alone, in malnutrition or an eating disorder.
  • Young age: dysphagia and postmenopausal bleeding need referral at any age.
  • A FIT below 10 µg Hb/g when symptoms persist.
  • A negative electrophoresis: it misses light-chain myeloma.
  • "Depression" or "old age" before organic causes are assessed.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you vomit blood, pass black stools, cough up blood and feel faint, collapse or become very confused.” Same day: “Contact us today if the weight loss speeds up, you cannot eat or drink, food sticks when you swallow, you cough up blood, bleed from the back passage or turn yellow.”

Review in 2–4 weeks if loss continues, tests are abnormal or pending, concern persists, nutritional risk is significant, or frail or vulnerable.
