Insomnia and sleep problems — adults

For: adults with difficulty getting to sleep, staying asleep or early waking. Not for: children (use the sleep problems in children algorithm). Daytime dozing is the main problem: use the excessive daytime sleepiness algorithm.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE TA922HTG624TA77NG202DVLA
Core rule: find the cause first (mood, mania, OSA, withdrawal), then CBT-I is first-line for chronic insomnia; hypnotics only for severe impairment, shortest course, with an agreed stop date and no automatic repeat.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • Hypnotic overdose, especially with alcohol, or immediate danger to life → 999
  • Nocturnal chest pain or breathlessness → acute chest pain, breathlessness or heart-failure pathway — not insomnia

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Suicidal intent, imminent risk or unable to stay safe → crisis assessment Same-day; PHQ-9 item 9 is not a risk assessment
  • Mania: reduced need for sleep with elevated mood, energy, grandiosity → Same-day if acute mania, psychosis or risk
  • OSA with sleepiness (2+ features, NG202) → must not drive until controlled; Group 2 or safety-critical → rapid sleep-service assessment
  • Nocturnal seizures → epilepsy specialist within 2 weeks
  • Night sweats with unexplained lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly → consider Suspected cancer pathway (NG12)
  • Substance withdrawal → manage the withdrawal, not sedation

3Assess — diary first, tests only on suspicion

☐ 2-week sleep diary  ☐ ISI for severity  ☐ Epworth and STOP-BANG as aids  ☐ BMI, neck, airway  ☐ Caffeine, alcohol, nicotine, cannabis; medicine timing (SSRI, steroid, diuretic)  ☐ PHQ-9/GAD-7

Tests by suspicion: ferritin and iron status (restless legs); TFTs (thyroid features). No fixed insomnia blood panel; no polysomnography for insomnia alone.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Chronic insomnia disorder — hyperarousal, unhelpful sleep beliefs → CBT-I.
  • OSA → sleep service per NG202; sedatives only with individual caution.
  • Restless legs → ferritin, correct iron deficiency, review caffeine, alcohol, medicines.
  • Depression, anxiety, PTSD → treat alongside CBT-I; assess risk.
  • Circadian or shift work → timing, not sedation.
  • Uncertain — re-run the causes at review before any more tablets.

5Treat today — doses per SPC, BNF and local formulary

  • CBT-I: sleep restriction or compression (window usually not below 5 hours), stimulus control, cognitive work; face-to-face preferred, Sleepio where commissioned. Supervise in bipolar, epilepsy, pregnancy, frailty, untreated OSA.
  • Severe short-term: Z-drug — zopiclone 7.5 mg (3.75 mg older or frail); a few days, rarely beyond 2 weeks; stop date; no automatic repeat.
  • Chronic, CBT-I tried or unsuitable: daridorexant 50 mg (TA922 criteria); review within 3 months. Melatonin MR 2 mg (55+) not routine locally.
  • Every prescription: next-day driving warning; avoid alcohol, opioids, sedatives. Long-term users: 5–10% reduction every 2–4 weeks, patient-paced; never stop abruptly.

6Refer routinely

  • Suspected OSA, severe parasomnia, circadian disorder not responding → sleep service.
  • Narcolepsy, REM sleep behaviour disorder, complex RLS → neurology or sleep medicine.
  • Refractory despite genuine CBT-I → insomnia service. Complex dependence → drug service.

7Never reassure by

  • A sleep-hygiene leaflet as the treatment for chronic insomnia.
  • An automatic hypnotic repeat.
  • "Can't sleep" without asking about mood and mania.
  • Sedating someone with untreated OSA or alcohol withdrawal.
  • Sedating antihistamines, amitriptyline or quetiapine as routine insomnia treatment.
  • Stopping a long-term benzodiazepine abruptly.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if you are in immediate danger or have taken too many tablets, especially with alcohol.” Same-day “If you have thoughts of ending your life or feel you can’t keep yourself safe, call NHS 111 option 2 today. If you feel drowsy the morning after a sleeping tablet, don’t drive. Loud snoring with pauses in breathing and daytime sleepiness needs its own assessment — tell us.”

Follow-up: 2–4 weeks after CBT-I or a hypnotic; Z-drug at the stop date; daridorexant within 3 months; melatonin by 13 weeks.
