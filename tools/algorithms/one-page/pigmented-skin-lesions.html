Pigmented skin lesions

For: adults and children with a new, changing or worrying pigmented lesion, or a growing pink or flesh-coloured nodule, in primary care. Not for: AK treatment detail (use the actinic keratosis protocol) or other nail problems (use the nail disorders protocol).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12NG14NG34BAD BCC 2021
Core rule: change is the cardinal warning sign. Score every worrying lesion on the NICE weighted 7-point checklist, remember nodular and amelanotic melanoma can lack ABCDE features, and if melanoma is suspected refer — never partial biopsy or non-specialist excision.

0Act now — rarely an emergency, but do not miss these

  • Acute uncontrolled bleeding, severe infection or airway compromise from a lesion → Same-day ED. ED is not the route for a suspicious lesion itself.
  • On checkpoint immunotherapy for melanoma with a new organ symptom (diarrhoea or bloody stool, new breathlessness, severe or blistering rash) → consider an immune-related adverse event and contact oncology Urgent
  • Suspected melanoma: suspected cancer pathway referral; do not take a partial biopsy or excise it yourself. Photography must not delay the referral.

1Triage — NICE weighted 7-point checklist

FeaturePoints
MajorChange in size · irregular shape · irregular colour — 2 each
MinorDiameter ≥7 mm · inflammation · oozing · change in sensation — 1 each
Score ≥3→ Suspected cancer pathway
Score <3High clinical concern → still refer on the suspected cancer pathway

ABCDE and the “ugly duckling” sign are supplementary prompts. Evolution (any change) is the most important single feature.

2Screen — suspected cancer pathway (diagnosis within 28 days)

  • Dermoscopy by a trained clinician suggests melanoma, or new pigmented lesion changing rapidly over weeks in any adult → Refer
  • Lesion suggesting nodular melanoma, pigmented or not (elevated, firm, growing; bleeding or ulcerated) → Consider referral
  • Suspected lentigo maligna (large, irregular, slowly spreading flat facial patch, older person) or nail: widening or irregular pigmented band, Hutchinson's sign → Refer
  • Child or young person with change, irregular border or bleeding → Refer via local paediatric dermatology.
  • Previous melanoma, new or changing lesion → specialist service per the follow-up plan; meets criteria → Refer
  • Suspected SCC; BCC where site or delay could affect outcome → Consider referral

3Assess

☐ Change, symptoms, duration, sunburn and skin type, previous melanoma or family history, immunosuppression  ☐ Whole skin and nails  ☐ Dermoscopy only if trained  ☐ Record site, maximum diameter (mm), evolution; photograph with a scale where permitted

Not in primary care: partial biopsy or excision of a suspected melanoma.

4Classify — reassure only if the diagnosis is clinically secure

  • Common naevus — symmetrical, uniform colour, regular border, stable → reassure; return if it changes.
  • Seborrhoeic keratosis — waxy “stuck-on”, well defined → reassure. Sudden eruption of many → think of internal malignancy (Leser-Trélat).
  • Solar lentigo, dermatofibroma (dimples on pinching), cherry angioma → reassure.
  • Pyogenic granuloma — fast-growing, bleeds easily; can mimic amelanotic melanoma → refer for excision. Spitz naevus in a child → refer.
  • Actinic keratosis — rough, scaly, sun-exposed. Thick, tender, ulcerated, bleeding or fast-growing → assess for SCC.
  • Uncertain or equivocal → refer rather than reassure.

5Treat today — only these

  • Awaiting a melanoma appointment: keep the area out of the sun; no manipulation or treatment of the lesion. Do not promise an appointment within 2 weeks.
  • Single uncomplicated AK (competent, not suspicious for SCC) → cryotherapy. Field AK → fluorouracil 5% with counselling: expected inflammation; stop and seek review for severe pain, ulceration, infection or eye exposure. Regimen per SPC; doses per BNF and local formulary.
  • Excised lesions → histology per local skin cancer/pathology policy; always where melanoma or another skin cancer is possible.
  • Sun protection: shade, clothing, hat, no sunburn, no sunbeds; sunscreen SPF ≥30 with good UVA protection as an adjunct. Teach self-examination.

6Refer routinely

  • Suspected BCC → local dermatology/skin-cancer pathway; low-risk only by a GPwER under local protocol.
  • Multiple or refractory AK; equivocal lesion not meeting urgent criteria → dermatology.
  • Large or atypical congenital naevus, multiple atypical naevi, suspected familial melanoma → dermatology or genetics.
  • Previous melanoma or high risk → follow the specialist surveillance plan, not an annual GP skin check.

7Never reassure by

  • Regular borders or no ABCDE features (nodular melanoma).
  • A pink or flesh-coloured colour (amelanotic melanoma).
  • A diameter under 6 mm.
  • A checklist score below 3 when you are still worried.
  • A photograph review that delays a referral.
  • Young age: childhood melanoma is rare but occurs.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

Same week “Come back within the same week if a mole changes in size, shape or colour, grows quickly, bleeds without injury, ulcerates, becomes painful, keeps itching or crusting, or a new mole appears.”

Referred: “The aim is a diagnosis within 28 days. If it changes, bleeds, ulcerates or hurts before then, contact us or dermatology the same week. Go to A&E only for bleeding that won't stop, severe infection or breathing problems.” Record the referral date and chase it. Declined referral: document informed refusal and safety-net.
