Tremor in adults

For: adults with new or changing tremor in primary care. Not for: children (use paediatric pathways). Long-term care of confirmed essential tremor → essential tremor protocol; Parkinson's treatment is neurology-led.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027

Core rule: rest vs action is the key question — asymmetric rest tremor with bradykinesia suggests Parkinson's (refer); bilateral action tremor with an otherwise normal examination suggests essential tremor. Always exclude thyrotoxicosis and a tremorgenic drug first.

0Stop — call 999 now — tremor with altered consciousness, toxicity or focal signs

  • Delirium tremens: tremor + confusion + fever, 6–72 h after stopping alcohol; high seizure risk → 999 hospital admission.
  • Serotonin syndrome: tremor + agitation + clonus/hyperreflexia after a serotonergic drug change → stop ALL serotonergic drugs → 999. NMS: lead-pipe rigidity + hyperthermia + autonomic instability on antipsychotics → stop antipsychotics → 999
  • Thyroid storm (fever + confusion + arrhythmia) → 999 emergency admission; not propranolol alone. Sudden tremor + confusion + acute headache + focal deficit (bleed, tumour, abscess) → 999 / stroke pathway.
  • While waiting: treatment is hospital-led (benzodiazepine and thiamine for DTs per hospital protocol; cyproheptadine, bromocriptine, dantrolene specialist only).

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Lithium + worsening tremor → timed level, U&E/eGFR, sodium Same-day. >1.5 mmol/L → withhold, urgent advice; toxicity signs → emergency assessment.
  • Thyrotoxicosis: tachycardia, heat intolerance, weight loss → Urgent TFTs.
  • Wilson's: under 40 + psychiatric change, liver disease, dystonia or family history → Urgent neurology/hepatology; acute liver failure → 999 / Same-day
  • Suspected Parkinson's (rest tremor + bradykinesia) → Urgent neurology; do not wait for imaging.
  • Abnormal neurology, markedly asymmetric, rapidly progressive or atypical → Urgent neurology (NG127).
  • Metoclopramide: stop; acute dystonia, oculogyric crisis, laryngospasm → Same-day acute.

3Assess — once blocks 0–2 are answered

☐ Hands on lap ☐ arms out 30 s (ET at once; PD re-emerges after 5–15 s) ☐ finger-nose ☐ bradykinesia, rigidity, gait, eyes, speech, focal signs ☐ spiral, handwriting ☐ drugs, caffeine, alcohol

☐ Targeted: TSH + free T4 if thyroid possible; glucose/HbA1c; U&E/eGFR, LFTs; lithium/valproate level if relevant. Not routine: CK, copper in every young person, NCS/EMG; MRI for typical longstanding bilateral action tremor with normal examination.

4Classify — only if the screen is negative

  • Essential tremor — bilateral upper-limb action tremor ± head/voice; family history supportive; no other signs → reassure if mild, treat if disabling.
  • Parkinson's — rest tremor + bradykinesia (isolated tremor is not Parkinson's) → refer quickly.
  • Cerebellar — intention tremor + ataxia, nystagmus, dysarthria → urgent neurology.
  • Physiological / drug — anxiety, caffeine, β-agonists, lithium, valproate, SSRIs, withdrawal → remove the cause.
  • Functional — variability, entrainment, distractibility → positive explanation; neurology / FND therapy.
  • Uncertain — say so → neurology; reassess if features evolve.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Disabling ET: propranolol may be considered — check contraindications (asthma/obstructive airways, bradycardia, heart block, hypotension, heart failure, PVD/Raynaud's, diabetes); start low, titrate by pulse, BP. Second line primidone: very low at night, slowly; check falls, driving, interactions, pregnancy. Topiramate/gabapentin specialist only.
  • Performance anxiety: propranolol 40 mg once, 30–60 min before; not for ET; no unreviewed repeats.
  • Drug-induced: valproate changes only with neurology/psychiatry (MHRA reproductive safety, incl. men); SSRI → review or switch.
  • All: less caffeine, nicotine, stimulants; manage anxiety; never alcohol as treatment; OT, weighted utensils.

6Refer routinely

  • ET refractory to propranolol and/or primidone → Routine neurology (DBS or focused ultrasound if severe).
  • Thyrotoxicosis as the driver → endocrinology. Awaiting Parkinson's diagnosis → falls assessment; disabling wait → specialist advice.

7Never reassure by

  • Improvement with alcohol — not diagnostic of ET, and a dependence risk.
  • Tremor frequency, or waiting for imaging before referring suspected Parkinson's.
  • Labelling a young adult "anxious" — consider Wilson's and functional tremor.
  • Waiting for routine monitoring when tremor worsens on lithium.
  • Calling tremor without bradykinesia Parkinson's, or starting levodopa yourself.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if the tremor comes with confusion, fever, stiffness, sweating, a sudden severe headache or new weakness.” Same week “Contact us if the tremor gets much worse on a new medicine, or Parkinson's symptoms worsen quickly.”

Follow-up: propranolol/primidone review at 6 weeks, 3-monthly at target dose, then yearly. Driving: advise checking DVLA standards and notifying where required (e.g. Parkinson's; ET only if it affects control); document.
