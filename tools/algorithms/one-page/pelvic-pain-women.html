Pelvic pain in women

For: women with acute or chronic pelvic pain, face to face or remote, in hours or OOH. Not for: ongoing management of endometriosis, PID or IBS (use the full Steps and their protocols). Pregnancy ≥20 weeks or within 6 weeks postpartum goes to maternity (blocks 0–1).

NICE NG73NG12NG126NG193BASHH PID
Core rule: do a pregnancy test early in anyone who could be pregnant — a positive test with pain is ectopic until proven otherwise. Then ask whether the pain is cyclical; a normal examination and scan do not exclude endometriosis.

0Stop — call 999 now — before any history

  • Shock or peritonism, syncope with the pain (ruptured ectopic or cyst, perforated appendix, tubo-ovarian abscess) → 999
  • Positive pregnancy test with shock, syncope, shoulder-tip pain, severe pain or significant bleeding → 999 / emergency department. Suspected sepsis from PID or abscess → 999
  • Severe pain after 20 weeks of pregnancy or within 6 weeks postpartum; BP ≥160/110, seizure or heavy bleeding → 999
  • While waiting: nil by mouth, IV access if available, analgesia that does not delay transfer. Do not wait for a scan, CA-125 or swabs; do not remove an IUD; no oral antibiotics in place of admission.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Sudden severe unilateral pain ± vomiting or adnexal mass (torsion) → Same-day gynae; normal Doppler does not exclude.
  • PID with systemic illness, abscess, pregnancy, vomiting, doubt or no better by 72 h; pain after instrumentation, IUD or termination → Same-day
  • Ascites or a pelvic/abdominal mass (not obviously fibroids) → Urgent suspected cancer referral; do not wait for CA-125.
  • Persistent bloating, satiety, pelvic pain, urgency, new IBS ≥50 → CA-125 if 40+; ≥ age threshold → Urgent USS. ≤39: consider USS.
  • Unexplained post-menopausal bleeding → Urgent suspected cancer (55+; local pathway if younger). Suspicious cervix, unexplained vulval lump or ulcer → consider suspected cancer referral.
  • Sexual violence or coercion → ask in private; safeguarding / SARC.

3Assess — consent, chaperone offered and documented

☐ Obs, NEWS2 if unwell (tachycardia + pain + positive test = emergency) ☐ Abdomen: mass, ascites, Carnett's ☐ Speculum, bimanual, pelvic floor, hip ☐ Urine dip, MSU; NAAT chlamydia/gonorrhoea ± M. genitalium, HIV, syphilis ☐ TVUS if suspected endometriosis

Not: CA-125 for acute pain, endometriosis or no symptoms; FIT for pelvic pain alone.

4Classify — causes often coexist

  • Endometriosis / adenomyosis — cyclical pain limiting daily life, deep dyspareunia, cyclical bowel/urinary symptoms, infertility → treat, TVUS.
  • PID, systemically well — cervical motion or adnexal tenderness supports, not proves → treat today.
  • Bladder pain — worse when full, eased by voiding, culture-negative "UTIs". IBS — often coexists; new aged ≥50 → CA-125 first.
  • Musculoskeletal / neuropathic — levator tenderness, positive Carnett's, scar/nerve pain → physio.
  • Uncertain — say so; not "probably endometriosis" or "just IBS". Name review owner and date.

5Treat today — without waiting for certainty

  • Endometriosis: offer paracetamol and/or NSAID trial (e.g. mefenamic acid 500 mg TDS); offer hormonal (CHC, progestogen, 52 mg LNG-IUS) in parallel with TVUS and referral; not if trying to conceive.
  • PID: treat on suspicion — ceftriaxone 1 g IM once + doxycycline 100 mg BD + metronidazole 400 mg BD, 14 days (ofloxacin regimen second line). Partner notification; review 48–72 h.
  • Bladder pain: stop needless antibiotics. Ceiling: no new opioids or gabapentinoids for chronic primary pain (NG193). Doses: NICE/SPC, BNF, local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • Gynaecology (NG73): endometriosis treatment ineffective, not tolerated or contraindicated, daily life affected, persistent or recurrent, or pelvic signs; adnexal mass; fibroids with pressure symptoms.
  • Specialist endometriosis centre: suspected deep disease of bowel, bladder or ureter.
  • Physio (myofascial); pain clinic (sensitisation, opioids); sexual health (PID partners). Review: PID 48–72 h and 2–4 weeks; endometriosis at 3 months as a decision point, not a repeat prescription.

7Never reassure by

  • A negative urine pregnancy test when timing is uncertain.
  • A normal examination or ultrasound (endometriosis).
  • A normal Doppler (torsion).
  • A normal CA-125 while symptoms persist.
  • A normal smear history with a suspicious cervix.
  • "Recurrent UTI" on negative cultures, or new "IBS" aged ≥50.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if pain suddenly becomes severe, you faint or feel faint, you get pain at the tip of your shoulder, you bleed heavily with dizziness, you feel very unwell with fever and severe pain, or you are pregnant with severe pain, fainting or heavy bleeding.” Same day: “Contact us today for a positive pregnancy test with any pain or bleeding, new or rapidly worsening pain, fever, vomiting with the pain, new swelling of your tummy, or if you cannot pass urine.”

Remote or OOH: face-to-face or emergency assessment if no pregnancy test, obs or examination, no chaperone where wanted, pain severe, sudden or changing, pregnant or postpartum, or safeguarding not explorable privately. Default to the safer destination.
