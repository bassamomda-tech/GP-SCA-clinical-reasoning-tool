Eye swelling — periorbital and eyelid

For: adults and children with eyelid or periorbital swelling in primary care. Not for: a red eye without lid swelling, or facial swelling beyond the eye (use the red eye or facial swelling algorithm). Local NHS Greater Manchester antimicrobial and ophthalmology pathways take precedence.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG141NG12NG258RCUK anaphylaxisEUGOGO
Core rule: check vision, pupils (RAPD), eye movements and proptosis in every swollen eye. Any orbital sign means orbital pathology until proven otherwise, and no proptosis does not exclude orbital cellulitis.

0Stop — call 999 now — emergency transfer

  • Airway angio-oedema (tongue, floor of mouth, hoarse voice, dysphagia, stridor) or suspected anaphylaxis → 999. IM adrenaline at once for anaphylaxis. ACE-inhibitor or hereditary angio-oedema does not respond to adrenaline.
  • Suspected orbital cellulitis: infected lid plus any orbital sign — proptosis, painful or restricted eye movements, reduced vision, RAPD, chemosis, diplopia — or systemic toxicity → 999
  • Cavernous sinus thrombosis (severe headache, high fever, bilateral proptosis, cranial nerve III/IV/VI palsies, altered consciousness) or rapidly spreading swelling with black eschar or severe facial pain in DKA or marked immunosuppression → 999
  • Do not delay referral to get CT or MRI; imaging, blood cultures and IV antibiotics are hospital-led.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Sight-threatening TED: reduced VA or colour vision, field loss, RAPD, corneal exposure, globe subluxation → Eye casualty now (999 if not available).
  • Pulsatile proptosis + bruit (carotid-cavernous fistula) → Same-day
  • Child, fever or unwell, or orbit not excluded → Same-day paediatrics
  • Persistent one-sided proptosis, no infection → Ophthalmology ≤1 week; child → very urgent.
  • Lid lesion: SCC or melanoma → Suspected cancer pathway; BCC → non-urgent (periocular may be high-risk).
  • Known cancer → Same-day. Diabetes or immunosuppression → low threshold for hospital.

3Assess — every eye swelling

☐ VA each eye  ☐ Pupils, swinging torch for RAPD  ☐ Eye movements, diplopia  ☐ Proptosis (look from above)  ☐ Lid closure, chemosis, lid margin  ☐ Temperature, pulse, BP  ☐ Mark the erythema edge

Cannot check VA or RAPD → lower threshold for same-day referral. Tests: TFTs + TRAb (TED); TFTs, albumin, urinalysis, U&E (puffiness). No CT/MRI in primary care.

4Classify — only if blocks 0–2 are negative

  • Pre-septal cellulitis — warm, tender, red lid; eye movements, globe and VA normal. Bite, skin, dental or sinus source.
  • Stye / chalazion — tender lump at lid margin / painless firm lump in tarsal plate.
  • Dacryocystitis — tender swelling below medial canthus, epiphora → local antibiotics + ophthalmology advice.
  • Allergy / angio-oedema — itchy bilateral puffiness; angio-oedema non-itchy, non-pitting. ACE inhibitor?
  • TED — lid retraction, proptosis, upgaze restriction; can be euthyroid. Systemic — morning puffiness: hypothyroid, nephrotic.
  • Uncertain — orbital involvement not excluded → Same-day hospital; lid lesion → ophthalmology.

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Mild pre-septal, adult, well, eye normal: co-amoxiclav 500/125 mg TDS for 7 days (NG141); penicillin allergy → clarithromycin with metronidazole per local guidance. Review 24–48 h.
  • Stye, chalazion, blepharitis: warm compresses, lid hygiene; no routine antibiotics; do not squeeze.
  • Allergic: non-sedating antihistamine, cool compress. ACE-inhibitor angio-oedema: stop, never restart any; advice before an alternative.
  • TED: stop-smoking support, stable euthyroidism, lubricants.

6Refer routinely

  • ≤1 week ophthalmology: suspected TED; complicated dacryocystitis; recurrent or atypical chalazion.
  • Routine: uncontrolled blepharitis; chalazion troublesome after several weeks; tear-duct obstruction.
  • Immunology: recurrent angio-oedema with no trigger, or ACE-inhibitor angio-oedema.

7Never reassure by

  • No proptosis — it does not exclude orbital cellulitis, especially in children.
  • Normal thyroid function — TED can be euthyroid.
  • Feeling better while the redness spreads past the pen mark.
  • A "recurrent chalazion" or one-sided "blepharitis" — think sebaceous carcinoma.
  • An examination without VA, pupils and eye movements.
  • Absent itch or urticaria in angio-oedema — bradykinin type still threatens the airway.

8Safety-net — in writing, then document it

999 “Call 999 or go to A&E now for pain moving your eye, difficulty moving it, the eye bulging, blurred or double vision, a severe headache, vomiting, confusion or fever with the swelling, redness spreading despite antibiotics, or swelling of the tongue or throat or noisy breathing.”

Same day: “No better after 24–48 hours of antibiotics — contact us today.” TED: loss of vision or colour vision, new double vision, eye pain or cannot close the eyes → eye casualty now.
