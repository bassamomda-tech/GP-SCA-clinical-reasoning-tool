Tongue problems

For: adults with a tongue ulcer, patch, colour change, swelling, burning, numbness or abnormal movement. Not for: mouth ulcers or a sore mouth elsewhere (mouth ulcers and sore mouth and tongue algorithms) or dental problems (own dentist).
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG12Resus Council UKNICE NG145BSG iron (2021)BHIVA
Core rule: retract the tongue with gauze and look at the lateral and ventral borders, then feel for induration and neck nodes. Unexplained oral ulceration over 3 weeks, at any site → NICE NG12 says consider a suspected cancer pathway referral; local safety policy is to refer by default.

0Stop — call 999 now — airway first

  • Tongue swelling with stridor, drooling or airway compromise (anaphylaxis or ACE inhibitor angioedema) → 999, ABCDE, anaesthetics/ENT early. Do not examine the back of the tongue.
  • IM adrenaline (1 mg/mL, thigh) if airway, breathing or circulation problems: adult and child >12 y 500 µg; 6–12 y 300 µg; 6 months–6 y 150 µg. Repeat after 5 minutes if no improvement.
  • ACE inhibitor angioedema (no urticaria; can start years into treatment): antihistamines and steroids do not work; icatibant or C1-inhibitor by hospital protocol only. Stop the ACE inhibitor for good and record it as an allergy.
  • SJS/TEN, neutropenic sepsis or deep-neck infection → Immediate hospital

2Screen — one row is enough

  • New unexplained CN XII palsy (wasting, fasciculation, deviates to the weak side) → Urgent neurology + imaging; neck pain, Horner's or other signs → Same-day emergency
  • Macroglossia progressing fast, with dysphagia or sleep obstruction → Urgent airway assessment
  • Iron deficiency + dysphagia + glossitis (Plummer-Vinson) → Urgent upper-GI pathway + haematology
  • Numbness after dental treatment: complete loss, severe dysaesthesia, transection, progressing → Urgent dental / OMFS
  • Hairy leukoplakia → offer an HIV test (indicator condition) ≤7 days
  • Macroglossia + periorbital purpura or carpal tunnel → electrophoresis, free light chains → haematology

3Assess — whole tongue, floor of mouth, neck

☐ Protrusion and side-to-side movement  ☐ Gauze retraction: lateral and ventral borders  ☐ Bimanual palpation  ☐ Neck nodes  ☐ Smoking, alcohol, ACE inhibitor, antibiotics, recent dental work

Targeted tests only: FBC, ferritin, B12, folate; tTG IgA + total IgA; TSH; HbA1c; IGF-1 if acromegaly features. Do not biopsy in primary care or test typical geographic or fissured tongue.

4Classify — only once blocks 0–2 are clear

  • Geographic — migrating smooth red patches, white borders; benign → reassure.
  • Fissured — deep dorsal grooves; normal variant → gentle tongue brushing.
  • Black hairy — antibiotics, smoking, coffee, poor hygiene → remove cause, brush.
  • Glossitis — smooth, beefy-red, sore → iron, B12, folate; find the cause.
  • Burning mouth — no visible lesion, eases with eating → exclude candida, deficiency, dry mouth, diabetes, thyroid first.
  • Uncertain — lump or induration without a clear cause → Urgent OMFS / ENT

5Treat today — doses per BNF and local formulary

  • Deficiency: treat the confirmed deficiency and its cause; no folic acid until B12 deficiency considered; zinc only if deficient.
  • Possible candida under “burning mouth”: fluconazole 50 mg daily for 7 days, or nystatin until 48 hours after resolution.
  • Burning mouth: reassure, explain, CBT (NHS Talking Therapies). Clonazepam, tricyclics, gabapentinoids: specialist only.
  • Hypothyroid macroglossia: levothyroxine (NG145); TSH every 3 months until stable. Stop smoking; alcohol ≤14 units a week.

6Refer routinely

  • Burning mouth not settling (candida and deficiency excluded); recurrent major aphthae → oral medicine.
  • Raised IGF-1 with macroglossia or acral change → endocrinology. Macroglossia affecting swallowing or speech → OMFS.

7Never reassure by

  • Asking the patient to stick out their tongue: the lateral borders are unseen without gauze.
  • A painless ulcer: tongue cancer is often painless at first.
  • Repeated topical steroid for an ulcer over 3 weeks.
  • Tongue deviation put down to a benign cause: urgent neurology.
  • A normal ear in persistent one-sided earache: examine the tongue and oropharynx.
  • A red patch seen only recently: refer at first presentation.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 if your tongue swells and you have difficulty breathing or swallowing.” Within a few days: “Contact us — do not wait for it to settle — for a red or white patch that won't rub off, a lump, a hard area, or a tongue that does not move normally.” Give a date: “Come back if any ulcer or changing area has not settled within 3 weeks.”
