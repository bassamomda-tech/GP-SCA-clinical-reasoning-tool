Tingling, numbness and paraesthesia in adults

For: adults with new or persistent tingling, numbness or paraesthesia in primary care. Not for: children. Detailed management of carpal tunnel, sciatica, cauda equina and neuropathic pain → use those protocols.
Reviewed October 2026 · next review April 2027
NICE NG127NG128NG19NG239CG173
Core rule: localise first. Upper motor neurone (UMN) signs, bilateral symptoms or rapid progression = a central or compressive cause until proven otherwise. Never label it peripheral neuropathy without a neurological examination.

0Stop — call 999 now — any one feature is enough

  • Saddle anaesthesia, or new bladder, bowel or sexual dysfunction or urinary retention, with or without bilateral leg tingling or weakness (cauda equina, cord compression) → 999 emergency MRI spine the same day.
  • Sudden one-sided face, arm or leg tingling or numbness, even without weakness: FAST and check glucose. Persisting → 999 stroke pathway.
  • Ascending tingling with weakness and areflexia (Guillain-Barré) plus breathlessness, weak cough, or swallowing or speech difficulty → 999
  • Do not wait for an FVC threshold or nerve studies in suspected Guillain-Barré.

2Screen — one row is enough

  • Fever + back pain + deficit, or autonomic instability → Same-day
  • Foot ulcer + fever, sepsis, ischaemia, deep infection, gangrene → Same-day. Warm, swollen foot (Charcot) → Foot service ≤1 working day
  • Lhermitte's, absent ankle jerks + upgoing plantars, sensory ataxia → B12 now; Urgent neurology
  • Low B12 + ataxia or cognitive change → bloods, then IM hydroxocobalamin urgently. Never folic acid alone.
  • Weight loss, sweats, nodes → Urgent neurology; NG12 pathway if cancer suggested
  • Asymmetric, painful, stepwise (vasculitis) → ESR, CRP; Urgent neurology

3Assess — once blocks 0–2 are answered

☐ Touch, pinprick, vibration, joint position ☐ Power, reflexes, plantars, clonus, Romberg, gait ☐ Sensory level, Spurling's, SLR ☐ Feet, pulses; monofilament in diabetes ☐ Postural BP if autonomic

☐ HbA1c, B12 + folate, TSH, FBC, U&E, ESR, AUDIT-C; coeliac if indicated ☐ Electrophoresis + light chains if unexplained. Not routinely: tumour markers, antineuronal antibodies, MRI without localising signs.

4Classify — the pattern localises the lesion

  • Stocking-and-glove — diabetes, alcohol, B12, CKD, thyroid, paraprotein, drugs, nitrous oxide.
  • Single nerve — entrapment: median (nocturnal, eased by shaking), ulnar, radial, peroneal, meralgia.
  • Dermatomal + neck or back pain → radiculopathy; weakness, gait change, brisk reflexes → MRI.
  • Hands and feet together — absent reflexes = polyneuropathy; any UMN sign = cord → urgent neurology + MRI spine.
  • Perioral + both hands → hyperventilation; check calcium.
  • Uncertain — say so, document the signs at each visit; progression → escalate investigation or referral.

5Treat today — the cause first

  • Painful neuropathy: offer a choice of amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin or pregabalin (CG173); none for painless numbness; no gabapentinoids for sciatica (NG59). Driving warning; taper.
  • B12 deficiency + neurology: hydroxocobalamin 1 mg IM alternate days until no further improvement, then every 2 months.
  • Carpal tunnel: neutral night splint first; a single steroid injection is an option.
  • Alcohol: safe reduction, thiamine; Wernicke's risk → Pabrinex before carbohydrate. Isoniazid → co-prescribe pyridoxine. Doses per BNF and local formulary.

6Refer routinely

  • Unexplained, small-fibre or hereditary neuropathy → neurology.
  • Carpal tunnel: severe → hand surgery; wasting, or not improved after a single injection → Urgent hand surgery
  • Radiculopathy: progressive weakness, or cervical pain after 6 weeks → spinal surgery.
  • Loss of protective sensation → podiatry. Paraprotein → Urgent haematology

7Never reassure by

  • Calling it neuropathy without a full neurological examination.
  • The absence of weakness in sudden one-sided tingling.
  • A B12 within the range when suspicion is high (consider MMA).
  • A painless warm, swollen diabetic foot.
  • Normal nerve studies when the pain is burning (small fibre).
  • Anaemia corrected by folic acid while the neuropathy progresses.

8Safety-net — say it, then document it

999 “Call 999 for new numbness between the legs or around the bottom, any new bladder or bowel change, breathlessness, a weak cough or trouble swallowing, or sudden one-sided numbness, face droop, arm weakness or speech change.” Same day “New weakness in both legs, numbness spreading quickly upwards, or you can't walk safely — go to hospital today. Any foot wound, blister, colour change, warmth or swelling — get it seen today.”

Follow-up: bloods reviewed within 1 week; review 2–4 weeks after starting or changing medication, 4–8 weeks for response. Neuropathy affecting pedal control → patient must tell the DVLA; document it.
