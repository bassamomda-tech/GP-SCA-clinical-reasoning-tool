What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
An HbA1c of 47 mmol/mol is non-diabetic hyperglycaemia (pre-diabetes), NOT "the all-clear" — it is a modifiable fork in the road with real progression risk to type 2 diabetes that intensive lifestyle change can substantially reduce (and sometimes reverse). The trap is the relief-driven "I’m fine, leave it, recheck next year"; the task is to reframe it as a warning that can be acted on, refer to the NHS Diabetes Prevention Programme with realistic lifestyle advice tailored to the patient’s actual life (here a sedentary taxi driver), and treat the WHOLE cardiometabolic risk — raised BP (146/90, confirmed by ambulatory or home monitoring before treatment — NICE NG136), weight, QRISK/lipids (NICE NG238), family history — not just the glucose number, with an active monitoring plan (NICE PH38: at least annual HbA1c; BP follow-up; the 48 = diabetes threshold). The examinable hidden agenda is that the relief masks fear and fatalism from watching his father decline and die early with diabetes; convert that into motivation ("this is the warning that lets you avoid his path"), build the plan around his real day without lecturing, and safety-net hyperglycaemic symptoms. Prevention framed as agency, not passive waiting.
