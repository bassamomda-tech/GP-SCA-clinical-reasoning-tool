What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A COPD exacerbation in a smoker must be treated competently AND used as a teachable cessation moment. First GRADE severity and exclude the sinister mimics — pneumonia (fever), PE (pleuritic pain, sudden onset, calf swelling), heart failure (orthopnoea, oedema), pneumothorax (sudden severe breathlessness) and lung cancer (haemoptysis, weight loss) — checking speech, breathing effort and conscious level on video (sats only with a home oximeter), with a same-day face-to-face assessment if in doubt; talking in full sentences with no fever, pain or haemoptysis is reassuring for a straightforward infective flare but the exclusion must be explicit, and admit if severe. Treat correctly: increase the short-acting bronchodilator, give oral PREDNISOLONE 30 mg once daily for 5 days, and decide on an ANTIBIOTIC by NICE NG114 judgement — sputum colour change with increased volume or thickness, previous exacerbations and admissions, complication risk and previous sputum results (here supporting amoxicillin, doxycycline or clarithromycin for 5 days) — not reflexively for every flare — plus inhaler optimisation and a personalised self-management/rescue-pack plan. Keep the NICE NG12 (Suspected cancer: recognition and referral, last updated April 2026) LUNG criteria in mind: an urgent CXR (within 2 weeks) for anyone aged 40 and over who has ever smoked with 1 or more unexplained symptoms (cough, fatigue, breathlessness, chest pain, weight loss, appetite loss), and a suspected cancer pathway referral for unexplained haemoptysis at 40 and over, and image rather than let repeated rescue packs mask a tumour if a flare does not resolve. The examinable communication is the cessation OFFER done as Very Brief Advice (ask permission, advise plainly that stopping is the single most effective intervention and helps even now, act by offering combination NRT/varenicline/bupropion or vaping harm-reduction plus a stop-smoking-service referral), reading the patient’s defensiveness as guilt/shame/fatalism, earning trust by treating the flare well, and respecting autonomy — neither colluding with "don’t mention it" nor hectoring. Safety-net for deterioration and follow up.
