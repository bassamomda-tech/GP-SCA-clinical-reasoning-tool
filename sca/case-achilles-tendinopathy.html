What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
ACHILLES TENDINOPATHY is an overuse condition presenting with gradual-onset posterior ankle/heel pain and stiffness, worst on STARTING activity and in the MORNING, with localised tenderness and THICKENING of the tendon (mid-portion ~2–6 cm above the insertion, or insertional where the tendon meets the heel bone), typically provoked by training-load increases, poor footwear and calf tightness. The must-not-miss is ACUTE ACHILLES RUPTURE — a sudden "kicked/popped" sensation, weakness, inability to push off/stand on tip-toes, a palpable gap and a positive SIMMONDS/Thompson calf-squeeze test (no plantarflexion on squeezing the calf) — which is easily missed (some patients still weakly plantarflex) and needs same-day orthopaedic or fracture-clinic assessment. Consider gout, retrocalcaneal bursitis, spondyloarthritis enthesitis (NICE NG65), xanthomata and referred pain, and review risk factors including training-load spikes, FLUOROQUINOLONE antibiotics (ciprofloxacin — tendinopathy/rupture risk; MHRA Drug Safety Update March 2019, and since January 2024 only when other recommended antibiotics are inappropriate), corticosteroids and inflammatory arthropathy, stopping/avoiding fluoroquinolones if implicated. Manage conservatively: relative rest/load modification (reduce running volume rather than stop entirely), analgesia, and the cornerstone — a structured progressive LOADING programme (eccentric/heavy-slow resistance) via physiotherapy with calf stretching and footwear/heel raise — which is evidence-based (JOSPT/APTA 2024, international) and effective but slow (about three months). AVOID corticosteroid injection into the Achilles tendon (rupture risk), refer to physiotherapy (and orthopaedics/sports medicine if refractory, urgently if rupture suspected), and set realistic expectations (review at around 12 weeks; shockwave only with special arrangements, NICE HTG426).
