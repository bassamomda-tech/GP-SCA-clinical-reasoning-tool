What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Isolated macrocytosis (raised MCV without anaemia, normal white cells/platelets) is usually NOT sinister — leukaemia is not the typical cause — and is a finding to work up through its common causes: ALCOHOL (very common), B12/folate deficiency, hypothyroidism, drugs, and liver disease, with a structured panel (B12, folate, TFTs, LFTs/GGT, reticulocytes/film, medication review) and haematology/marrow referral reserved for unexplained, progressive or multi-lineage abnormalities. The examinable hidden agenda is that the likely cause — rising alcohol since a recent bereavement/relationship stress — is something the patient is ashamed of and has framed the call around a leukaemia fear partly to avoid; the result is a DOOR into a sensitive, important issue. Reassure genuinely about leukaemia, take a non-judgemental alcohol/lifestyle history that enables disclosure, and address the underlying distress (screen mood, offer bereavement and alcohol-reduction support) — treating the abnormal result as an opportunity to help, not just a number to recheck. Catching rising alcohol early (the macrocytosis is a useful biological flag) and addressing the bereavement/low mood is genuinely valuable — the abnormal result is an opportunity, not just a number to recheck. Done well, it opens help she might not otherwise have sought.
