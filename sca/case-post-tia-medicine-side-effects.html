What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
When a patient blames new medicines, take the symptom seriously and look for a mechanism before stopping anything. Ask exactly how each tablet is taken — duplicated doses after a change (old stock in a pill organiser plus a new box) are common. Remote consultations can still gather data: a validated home monitor gives seated and standing readings (a systolic fall of 20 mmHg or more, or a diastolic fall of 10 or more, is postural hypotension). After a TIA, antiplatelet therapy and high-intensity statin are the key preventive treatments; the BP target is usually below 130/80, but postural symptoms and falls take priority. An ARB dose increase needs U&E (creatinine, potassium) in 1–2 weeks. For statin muscle symptoms: check CK; if 4–10 × the upper limit of normal or symptoms are intolerable, stop for 4–6 weeks (more than 10 ×: same-day assessment); otherwise consider a pause and rechallenge, a lower dose or another statin. Change one medicine at a time so the cause can be identified. A Black African or Caribbean family origin is relevant to antihypertensive choice (ARB rather than ACE inhibitor) — and here she also has a documented ACE-inhibitor cough. Car drivers must not drive for one month after a TIA.
