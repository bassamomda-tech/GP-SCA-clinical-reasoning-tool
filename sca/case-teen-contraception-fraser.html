What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Young people under 16 can consent to contraception if they are competent (Gillick) and the Fraser guidelines are met; confidentiality applies, with limits where there is a risk of serious harm. Every under-16 sexual health consultation needs a safeguarding screen — partner age and power imbalance, coercion, alcohol or drugs, gifts, older contacts, online images — and sex under 13 is always a child protection matter. Always ask about recent unprotected sex: a condom failure turns a contraception request into an emergency contraception consultation. The copper IUD is the most effective emergency contraception (up to 5 days after unprotected sex or up to 5 days after the earliest expected ovulation). Ulipristal acetate is more effective than levonorgestrel near ovulation; after ulipristal, wait 5 days before starting hormonal contraception and use condoms. Offer LARC, condoms for STIs, STI testing (2 weeks after exposure for chlamydia and gonorrhoea) and a pregnancy test 3 weeks after the episode. On video, confirm identity and privacy first.
