What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A 10-week dry cough, nocturnal, triggered by cold air / laughter / exertion, on an atopic background with childhood asthma and family history, is cough-variant asthma — not an infection, and a second antibiotic is the wrong reflex. The skill is twofold: hold the differential cleanly (reflux, post-infective/pertussis, and the red flags that aren’t there) and treat correctly per NICE NG245: objective testing first (eosinophils or FeNO — FeNO 50 ppb or more confirms; then spirometry with reversibility), then as-needed low-dose ICS/formoterol, never a SABA alone. But the marks live in the hidden agenda: the antibiotic request masks a newly-discovered pregnancy and a terror that treatment will harm the baby. Make disclosure safe (vaping shame, then the pregnancy), then deliver the counter-intuitive truth — uncontrolled asthma harms the fetus, the inhaler does not — and weave proportionate pregnancy housekeeping (folic acid per NICE NG247, stop smoking service support to come off the vape without relapsing to cigarettes per NICE NG209, booking) without hijacking the consultation.
