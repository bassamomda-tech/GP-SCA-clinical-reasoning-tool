What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
An incidental X-ray reported as Paget’s disease, with an isolated raised (bone-specific) ALP and normal calcium, is a benign disorder of bone remodelling — NOT bone cancer or metastases. The first and central task is a CONFIDENT benign diagnosis that answers the patient’s real question ("is it cancer?") clearly and early, using his own classic symptoms (bony ache, headache, the hat feeling tighter from bony enlargement) to make the benign explanation cohere. The plan is to confirm/characterise (ALP with liver function tests, calcium, vitamin D, and a radionuclide bone scan to map the disease — Paget’s Association guideline, Ralston et al., JBMR 2019), treat bone pain with a bisphosphonate (zoledronic acid most likely to help; symptom-directed rather than treating the ALP number) plus analgesia, and monitor; complications (skull-base hearing loss, fracture, rare sarcomatous change) are mentioned proportionately as reasons to monitor, not as new threats. The examinable hidden agenda is a brother who died of cancer that "went to the bones", onto which he has pattern-matched a raw portal phrase ("abnormal bone") read before any explanation. Address the portal misreading, acknowledge the brother-grief, check the unspoken death-sentence fear, offer to involve his wife, and safety-net — leaving him fully, not half, reassured. Paget’s is benign and treatable; symptomatic disease is managed with bisphosphonates, and monitored. Rare complications (deafness if skull base involved, fractures, very rarely sarcomatous change — emphasise rare) need awareness but should not dominate the reassurance.
