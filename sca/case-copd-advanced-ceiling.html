What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A patient with advanced COPD (MRC dyspnoea 5, home oxygen, recurrent admissions) who, after a frightening admission, raises "what happens from here" is opening the door to ADVANCE CARE PLANNING, and the skill is to seize it sensitively — exploring his understanding, values, fears (suffocation, machines, being a burden) and wishes at his pace and with permission, not as an imposed checklist. Explore the ceiling-of-care/escalation gently: hospital vs home, intensive care/invasive ventilation, NON-INVASIVE ventilation (often appropriate and reversible in exacerbations — clarify, because "no machines" frequently conflates NIV with ITU), and resuscitation status framed around what CPR can realistically achieve in advanced COPD; document a values-based, revisitable escalation plan (e.g. ReSPECT/DNACPR) shared with ambulance and out-of-hours services (NICE NG142), never coerced — and if he wants a legally binding refusal, an advance decision under the Mental Capacity Act 2005 must be written, signed, witnessed and state that it applies even if life is at risk. Centrally, name and address the BREATHLESSNESS–ANXIETY cycle, which amplifies suffering and drives crises: non-pharmacological measures (handheld FAN to the face, pursed-lip/controlled breathing, forward-lean positioning, pacing/energy conservation, CBT-based anxiety support, a written crisis plan) give agency and reduce panic, while for REFRACTORY breathlessness low-dose OPIOIDS (carefully titrated; NICE NG115, dose per BNF) ease the sensation of breathlessness and benzodiazepines can help panic in selected cases — alongside optimised COPD therapy/oxygen and treatment of reversible factors. Involve SUPPORTIVE/PALLIATIVE care early (it is not only for cancer or the final days; NICE NG115: access to palliative and hospice care), support any CARER, and check home-oxygen fire safety (smoking, naked flames; BTS 2015). Communicate with honesty and compassion held together — validate both fears, reassure he will be kept comfortable and not abandoned, clarify NIV/ITU/CPR, avoid both false reassurance and fatalism, pace the discussion and offer to continue.
