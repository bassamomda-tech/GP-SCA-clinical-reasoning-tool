What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A NEW change in bowel habit at 59 with rectal bleeding and weight loss is a colorectal red-flag cluster, not IBS — and IBS is a diagnosis of exclusion that should never be newly applied at this age without investigation. The NICE NG12/FIT pathway applies: offer quantitative FIT (NICE HTG690) and refer on a colorectal suspected cancer pathway if FIT ≥10 µg Hb/g (a low FIT does not end it while concern persists; the Faster Diagnosis Standard aims for an answer within 28 days), with baseline bloods (FBC/ferritin) and examination, and without simply prescribing an antispasmodic. Distinguish genuine IBS (NICE CG61: at least 6 months of symptoms; chronic/lifelong, pain related to defaecation, bloating, NO bleeding/weight loss/nocturnal symptoms/anaemia) from this picture. The examinable hidden agenda is self-soothing denial — a colleague her age recently diagnosed with bowel cancer drove her to the least frightening label — plus a practical dread of taking time off mid-term. Surface the fear gently, decline the convenient label without catastrophising or dismissing her, address the practical barrier, and complete FIT and the suspected cancer pathway referral with follow-up. The skill is refusing the anchored self-diagnosis while keeping her on side.
