What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Most newborn jaundice is physiological, but every baby with visible jaundice needs a bilirubin level: within 2 hours if jaundice appears in the first 24 hours, and within 6 hours if it is noticed after 24 hours. Do not rely on visual inspection to estimate the level, particularly in darker skin. Sleepiness and poor feeding are both a cause and a consequence of rising bilirubin. Always ask about stool and urine colour: pale chalky stools or dark urine suggest conjugated jaundice (for example biliary atresia) and need urgent specialist referral. Prolonged jaundice is beyond 14 days in term babies (21 in preterm). Encourage frequent breastfeeding and arrange skilled support rather than advising formula or water; check the mother’s mood and support.
