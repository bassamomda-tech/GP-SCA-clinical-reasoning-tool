What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
In the very old, "UTI" is the label that stops people thinking. UKHSA advises against dipsticks over 65 (up to half of older adults have asymptomatic bacteriuria); smelly, concentrated urine usually means dehydration, and new confusion needs a cause hunted, not a reflex antibiotic. New dribbling plus new confusion are two UKHSA over-65 criteria, so infection is not dismissed — the urine goes for culture while the other causes are examined for. Here the cause is iatrogenic: opioid-induced constipation causing retention with overflow. The hidden agenda — the daughter’s terror of hospital after her husband died there — drives the antibiotic demand, and only naming it lets the right plan land.
