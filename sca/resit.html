Failed the SCA? A calm plan for the resit

A failed SCA is common, survivable and fixable. This page explains how to read your RCGP feedback without over-reading it, how to build a 4–8 week plan, how to time the next diet and where to find support.

First, the result is not a verdict on you

Failing the SCA is common. Roughly three in ten sittings in the RCGP's first year of SCA data did not pass (RCGP interim report), and plenty of very good doctors are among them. The SCA samples consulting habits across twelve cases, and habits can be trained. A clear plan works better than doing the same thing harder.

Give yourself a few days before you plan anything. Then tell your educational supervisor or trainer. You do not have to work this out alone, and they will want to help.

How to read your RCGP feedback

Your results show your score against the pass mark and feedback on your consultations under the marking domains. The RCGP is clear about what its feedback statements are and are not:

  • They are not performance indicators and play no part in marking. The examiner marks the case first and only then chooses statements. They are not a justification of the mark.
  • Passing candidates may get them too. A statement on a case you passed is a suggestion, not evidence that you failed it.
  • A failed domain always gets feedback. So look at the domains you failed across all twelve cases.
  • They describe those cases on that day, not your consulting in general. Look for statements that repeat across several cases: that pattern is your diagnosis.

Go through the statements with your trainer, alongside the RCGP's educational notes for each statement and the marking and results page. Then compare them with what your trainer sees in real surgeries. Where they agree, you have found the problem.

A 4–8 week resit plan

The Resit Clinic builds a personal 8-week plan from your results and an honest questionnaire. It names the likely failure patterns and the order to fix them in, and nothing you enter leaves your browser. The outline below works for most people and can be compressed to four weeks if your next diet is close.

WeeksFocus
1Diagnose. Map the feedback statements across all twelve cases, complete the Resit Clinic, and record two or three real consultations for your trainer and for real-consultation feedback.
2–3Fix one domain at a time. Pick cases from the case bank that stress your weakest domain. Do fewer cases, mark them strictly in Examiner Marking, and repeat the ones that went wrong.
4–5Mixed practice with people. Run three-person sessions with the Live Group Mock or role-play packs, with a colleague who will be honest with you. Use the AI patient for extra repetitions in between.
6–7Full diets. Two 12-case mock exams, one in each week, marked by domain. Check that the old pattern has stopped repeating.
8Taper. Short circuits, rest and logistics: room, kit, connection. No new topics.

Choosing your next diet

There are nine SCA diets a year, so the next one is rarely far away, but booking closes well before the exam. From the published dates, the final booking deadline is about nine to ten weeks before each diet. See SCA dates and fees for the current table, and always confirm on the RCGP site.

  • Do not rebook out of panic. If your feedback shows the same pattern in many cases, give yourself at least six weeks of changed practice.
  • Check your training time. Attempt limits, extensions to training and what happens to your CCT date are set by the MRCGP regulations and your deanery. Talk to your training programme director early.
  • Reasonable adjustments: if health, disability or personal circumstances affected you, ask about adjustments for the next sitting before that diet's deadline.

Looking after yourself

Failing a high-stakes exam can knock your confidence and your sleep, and it can feel isolating, especially if you are also working full time, far from family or worried about visas or money. Please use the support available:

  • Your deanery's professional support and wellbeing service (names vary by region), via your training programme director.
  • NHS Practitioner Health, a free and confidential service for doctors with mental health concerns.
  • The BMA wellbeing support services, including confidential counselling and peer support.
  • If you are struggling to cope or having thoughts of harming yourself, contact your GP, call NHS 111 (option 2 for mental health), or call Samaritans on 116 123. In an emergency, call 999.

Frequently asked questions

I failed the SCA. What should I do first?
Take a few days, then go through your results and feedback with your trainer. Look for feedback that repeats across several cases, because that pattern is what to fix. Then book a diet that gives you enough time to change it.
Are SCA feedback statements used to mark me?
No. The RCGP states that feedback statements are not performance indicators and have no role in marking. Examiners choose them after marking, and passing candidates can receive them too. See the RCGP feedback statements page.
How long should I prepare for an SCA resit?
Most resitters benefit from four to eight weeks of targeted work: diagnose the pattern, fix one domain at a time, practise with people, then sit two full mock exams.
How many times can I sit the SCA?
There is a limit on attempts, and rules about extended training and leaving training. These are set by the MRCGP regulations and your deanery. Check the current rules on the RCGP website.
Is there wellbeing support after failing the SCA?
Yes. Deanery professional support services, NHS Practitioner Health and the BMA wellbeing services all support doctors. If you feel unable to cope, call NHS 111 (option 2) or Samaritans on 116 123. In an emergency, call 999.
