What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A refusal is the start of the conversation, not the end. Explore the reason (here: a child at home, fear of losing contact, previous stigma), give honest information about risk including death, then assess capacity for the specific decision (understand, retain, use or weigh, communicate — Mental Capacity Act 2005). An unwise decision does not mean incapacity, and recent alcohol does not automatically remove capacity. If he has capacity, respect the refusal (GMC decision-making and consent guidance) and reduce risk: speak to the on-call medical or gastroenterology team today (many units offer next-day ambulatory endoscopy for patients who decline admission), agree PPI with them, stop NSAIDs, prescribe thiamine for a dependent drinker, advise against abrupt alcohol cessation, avoid starting oral iron before endoscopy (it blackens stools and confuses monitoring), give clear 999 advice, and document thoroughly. When a dependent child is at home with a parent at risk of collapse, secure another adult now. H. pylori status should be rechecked (at endoscopy, or by stool antigen or breath test at least two weeks off PPI).
