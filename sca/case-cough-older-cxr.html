What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
The whole case is a single refusal: do not convert a normal chest X-ray into a false all-clear. A plain film misses lung cancer and mesothelioma, and an 11-week unremitting cough in a 40-pack-year ex-smoker with a fleck of blood in the sputum and a hoarse voice is NICE NG12 territory regardless of that film. Two acts of clinical curiosity carry the marks: taking the occupational history no template prompts (30 years of asbestos behind one question), and actively re-screening the symptoms the patient minimises (progressive breathlessness, hoarseness, weight loss, a possible fleck of blood). State the NG12 routes explicitly — unexplained haemoptysis at 40+ means a suspected cancer pathway lung referral despite the normal CXR; persistent hoarseness at 45+ means considering a laryngeal suspected cancer pathway referral; persisting symptoms after a normal film are discussed with the lung team, who usually go to CT — and do it while managing a man who is actively asking to be discharged because a dead workmate has made the truth unbearable. Override his wish kindly; meet the dread; close with reliability, not "ring if worried".
