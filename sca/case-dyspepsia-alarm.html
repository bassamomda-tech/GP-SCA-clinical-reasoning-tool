What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Long-standing dyspepsia that has CHANGED and become persistent despite a PPI, with unintentional weight loss, early satiety and — critically, on direct questioning — melaena (black tarry stools), is a cluster of ALARM features that mandates an urgent suspected cancer pathway referral, not a phone PPI dose increase. NICE NG12: refer using a suspected cancer pathway referral for oesophageal or stomach cancer (this replaced urgent direct-access endoscopy in 2025) at 55 and over with weight loss and upper abdominal pain, reflux or dyspepsia; the black stools also mean an in-person assessment the same day (pulse, BP, FBC, U&E), with emergency admission if bleeding is active. "Test and treat" (H. pylori) and a 4-week full-dose PPI (NICE CG184) are for uncomplicated dyspepsia WITHOUT alarm features — not here; with a possible bleed, H. pylori is tested at endoscopy. Two things are easily missed: melaena (the patient won’t volunteer it — ask specifically), and a hidden NSAID driver (daily ibuprofen not on the record, a major peptic-ulcer/bleeding risk) which must be stopped, alongside H. pylori testing at endoscopy and an FBC; the back pain then needs an NG59 plan (exercise-based care; not paracetamol alone). The examinable hidden agenda is work-driven avoidance (short-staffed, job insecurity) plus a quieter fear of bad news he can’t afford; name the seriousness without panic, problem-solve the work barrier, stop the NSAID, and safety-net the GI-bleed red flags clearly.
