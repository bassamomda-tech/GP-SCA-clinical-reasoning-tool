What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Drenching night sweats (soaking nightclothes/sheets) persisting for weeks must be taken seriously and worked up structurally — NOT reassured away as stress/overheating or met with a sleep aid. In a young adult especially, drenching night sweats are a classic LYMPHOMA "B symptom": screen the constellation — unexplained weight loss, fevers, drenching night sweats, pruritus/itch, fatigue, and lymphadenopathy (painless rubbery nodes) — and elicit the red flags the patient may be downplaying. Examine (lymph nodes, hepatosplenomegaly), arrange urgent bloods (FBC/film, ESR, LDH), and consider the broader differential (thyroid/hyperthyroidism; infection — TB, HIV; drugs), treating anxiety as a diagnosis of EXCLUSION, not the opening assumption. Given an unexplained lump with drenching sweats, weight loss and itch, arrange a suspected cancer pathway referral under NICE NG12 (Suspected cancer: recognition and referral, last updated April 2026), which is triggered by unexplained lymphadenopathy (or splenomegaly for non-Hodgkin lymphoma) with these symptoms taken into account. The examinable hidden agenda is "just stress / something to help me sleep" as AVOIDANCE masking a noticed lump and real fear; surface it gently without colluding, balance honesty with calm reassurance (lymphoma is highly treatable; many causes are benign), and act — structured work-up of the can’t-miss causes, not a sleep aid.
