What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A new, discrete, unexplained breast lump is taken seriously and EXAMINED face to face (both breasts, axillae and nodes, with a chaperone offered and documented; this cannot be done on video) regardless of the patient’s age, the absence of pain, or how well she feels — "painless" is not reassuring (most breast cancers are painless) and "too young" does not exclude it. The NICE NG12 (Suspected cancer: recognition and referral, last updated April 2026) threshold is explicit: people aged 30 AND OVER with an unexplained breast lump (with or without pain) are referred using a SUSPECTED CANCER PATHWAY referral (appointment within 2 weeks) for TRIPLE ASSESSMENT (clinical examination + imaging ± biopsy); under 30 with an unexplained breast lump, consider a non-urgent referral; aged 50 and over with discharge, retraction or other changes of concern in one nipple only are also referred; and a suspected cancer pathway referral should be CONSIDERED for skin changes that suggest breast cancer, or at 30 and over with an unexplained axillary lump (NICE NG12). A benign-feeling examination does NOT override referral. The examinable hidden agenda is that "it’s probably just a cyst / I’m too young / just tell me it’s fine" is FEAR — of cancer, hospitals and the impact on young children — half-hoping to be told it is nothing; colluding with that wish to spare distress is a serious error. Correct the misconceptions, arrange a face-to-face examination with dignity, refer on the NG12 breast pathway with the honest, reassuring message that most referred lumps prove benign, surface and hold the fear, and safety-net and follow up.
