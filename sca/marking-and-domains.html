How the SCA is marked, in plain English

Twelve cases, three domains, four grades and a pass mark set by borderline regression. This page explains how SCA marking works, from the RCGP’s own published descriptions, and how to use that when you mark your own practice.

The three marking domains

Every SCA case is marked in the same three domains. These are the RCGP's names, from its marking and results page:

Data gathering and diagnosis

Gathering and using the right information, including checking for red flags and drawing out the psychological and social context, then reaching an appropriate working diagnosis.

Clinical management and medical complexity

A safe, appropriate plan for this patient, including follow-up and safety-netting, and handling more than one problem, risk or uncertainty at once.

Relating to others

How you communicate and work with the patient: rapport, exploring their ideas, concerns and expectations, explaining clearly and sharing the decision.

The summaries above are ours. The RCGP publishes the official descriptors for each domain on its marking page, and its SCA consultation toolkit breaks the skills down further. Read the RCGP's own wording before your diet.

How each case is graded

  • Four grades per domain. For each case, the examiner gives each domain one of four grades: clear pass, pass, fail or clear fail. They judge against the standard of a newly qualified, independent GP, using the RCGP's published descriptors rather than a tick list.
  • A different examiner for each case. The RCGP's SCA adjustments page notes that each of the twelve cases is marked independently by a different examiner. One examiner's view cannot colour your whole exam.
  • Clinical management carries more weight. The RCGP weights clinical management and medical complexity more heavily than the other two domains. For the exact weighting: check RCGP.
  • A total mark across all twelve cases. Results are reported as a mark out of 126. The RCGP's per-diet results give the highest mark for each diet in that form, for example 118 out of 126 in June 2025.

How the pass mark works

There is no fixed pass mark for each case, and you do not have to pass a set number of cases. The RCGP explains on its marking and results page that, as well as grading the domains, each examiner makes an overall judgement of the case. Those judgements feed a standard-setting process called borderline regression, which sets the pass mark for each case. Your overall result depends on your aggregated mark across the twelve cases compared with the pass mark for that diet.

In plain terms:

  • The pass mark can move a little between diets, because it reflects how examiners judged the cases on that occasion, not a fixed percentage.
  • One bad case does not fail you. Falling short in a case does not mean you have failed the exam; it is the aggregate that counts.
  • Consistent weakness does fail you. Because every case adds to the total, the same domain slipping across several cases costs more than one disaster.

The rules on results, appeals and attempts are in the MRCGP regulations on marking and results.

Feedback statements

After the exam you receive feedback statements linked to the domains. The RCGP's feedback statements page is clear that they are not performance indicators and play no part in marking. The examiner marks the case first and then chooses statements. They are not a justification of the mark, and passing candidates may receive them too. Any domain an examiner fails always receives feedback.

The RCGP advises that a statement appearing more than once should guide your future preparation. If you are resitting, see how to read SCA feedback and plan a resit.

Marking yourself in practice

We have two practice tools for marking. Neither is the RCGP's marking scheme, and neither can predict your result.

Examiner Marking

Examiner Marking lets you grade each case in the three RCGP domains with the same four grades (clear pass to clear fail). It keeps your averages over time, so you can see which domain is holding you back.

The Hot Seat scorecard

Every case in The Hot Seat has a red, amber and green scorecard. It is our own practice tool, not an RCGP document. It borrows the column layout of the RCGP's consultation toolkit (Global skills, Tasks, Relating to others) and holds 7 Global skills items and 11 Relating to others items that are the same for every case, plus 11 Tasks written for that case. Green items add up to a score in each of the three exam domains, with clinical management given the most marks.

Use marks to find habits, not to forecast results. Self-marking, peer marking and AI feedback are practice opinions. The RCGP's pass mark is set by borderline regression on the day, so no practice score converts into a pass or fail.

To put this into practice, mark every case straight after you finish it, write one line of evidence for each fail grade, and look for the domain that keeps slipping across a full mock exam.

Frequently asked questions

What are the three SCA marking domains?
Data gathering and diagnosis; Clinical management and medical complexity; and Relating to others. Each case is marked in all three. See the RCGP marking and results page for the official descriptors.
What grades are used in the SCA?
Each domain in each case gets one of four grades: clear pass, pass, fail or clear fail. Examiners judge against the standard of a newly qualified, independent GP.
What is the SCA pass mark?
There is no fixed pass mark. Examiners' overall judgements of each case feed a standard-setting process called borderline regression, which sets the pass mark. Your result depends on your aggregated mark across all twelve cases, so the pass mark can differ slightly between diets.
Do I need to pass a certain number of SCA cases?
No. The RCGP says there is no fixed pass mark per case. Doing badly in one case does not mean you have failed the exam, because the overall result is based on your total across the twelve cases.
Do feedback statements affect my SCA mark?
No. The RCGP states that feedback statements are not performance indicators and play no part in marking. Examiners choose them after marking the case, and passing candidates can receive them too.
Is the Hot Seat scorecard the RCGP marking scheme?
No. It is our own practice scorecard, laid out in the columns of the RCGP's consultation toolkit. It has 7 Global skills items, 11 case-specific Tasks and 11 Relating to others items. It helps you find habits to work on, but it cannot predict an RCGP result.
