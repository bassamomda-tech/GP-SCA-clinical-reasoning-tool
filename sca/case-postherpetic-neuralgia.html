What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
POSTHERPETIC NEURALGIA (PHN) is neuropathic pain persisting in the affected dermatome 90 days or more after the shingles rash began (timed from rash onset; Dworkin 2007, international) — burning, stabbing or electric-shock pain with ALLODYNIA (pain from light touch such as clothing/bedsheets), hyperalgesia and itch — with risk rising with age. Recognise it, reassure that it is a known after-effect rather than a new disease (excluding atypical features), and — crucially — explain why ordinary analgesics fail: paracetamol and NSAIDs target nociceptive/inflammatory pain and are largely ineffective for neuropathic pain, so "nothing works" reflects the wrong tool, not patient failure. Institute the NICE CG173 neuropathic pain ladder (2013, updated 2020): offer a first-line CHOICE of amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin or pregabalin, one at a time, started low and titrated, with realistic expectations (meaningful relief and improved sleep/function built over weeks, rather than zero pain), switching to one of the remaining first-line agents if ineffective or intolerable and tailoring choice to comorbidity and elderly-specific risks (anticholinergic/falls/sedation; gabapentinoid sedation/dependence — Schedule 3 controlled drugs, MHRA Drug Safety Update April 2019, with respiratory depression risk alongside opioids, MHRA October 2017). Add TOPICAL options well-suited to localised pain and allodynia in older people (capsaicin cream per CG173; lidocaine 5% plasters are licensed for PHN but not recommended by CG173, so only as the local formulary allows), address sleep and consider physiotherapy/psychological support and pain-clinic referral if refractory. Avoid long-term opioids (CG173: tramadol only as acute rescue; no other opioids started in non-specialist settings without specialist advice), endless escalation of paracetamol/NSAIDs, and polypharmacy harms. Address the bio-psycho-social toll — sleep, mood, function, isolation — validate it, treat the whole person, and mention shingles VACCINATION for prevention (UKHSA Green Book chapter 28a: Shingrix for adults aged 70 to 79).
