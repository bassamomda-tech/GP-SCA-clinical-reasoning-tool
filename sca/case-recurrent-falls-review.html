What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Three falls in three months is recurrent falls requiring a structured MULTIFACTORIAL assessment (NICE NG249: 2 or more falls, injury or being unable to get up each qualifies — she has all three), not acceptance of "just the carpet". The highest-yield intervention is almost always the MEDICATION review/deprescribing — here a polypharmacy cluster (two antihypertensives including a diuretic, amitriptyline, zopiclone) driving postural drop and sedation. The full assessment spans postural BP (NICE NG136: a systolic fall of 20 mmHg or more after standing for at least 1 minute is significant), vision (expedite the cataracts), gait/balance/strength (physio/falls service), continence, cognition, footwear, alcohol, home hazards (OT), and — importantly — bone health (a bruised hip + recurrent falls warrants fracture-risk assessment per NICE NG259 and NOGG 2024, with DXA where it would change management) and the long lie (40 minutes on the floor is a red flag prompting a personal alarm). The examinable hidden agenda is the fear that admitting falls means losing her home; the minimisation is a shield and the daughter’s worry is the reliable signal. Reframe every intervention around PRESERVING independence — the assessment is how she STAYS home, not the road to a care home — and safety-net the long lie. Dignity protected, risk reduced.
