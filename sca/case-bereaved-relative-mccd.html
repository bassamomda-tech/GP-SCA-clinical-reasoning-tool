What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Since 9 September 2024 the medical examiner system is statutory in England and Wales: any doctor who attended the deceased during life can complete the MCCD (the old 28-day rule has gone), and every death not investigated by a coroner is independently scrutinised by a medical examiner, who also speaks with the bereaved and can raise concerns or notify the coroner. Families usually need three things: a plain explanation of the certificate (part I is the causal sequence; part II contributing conditions), a clear picture of the process and timing (registration within 5 days, faith needs considered), and a safe place to raise concerns about care. Respond to concerns openly — listen, say sorry for the distress, explain the routes (medical examiner, practice review, meeting, complaint) honestly including the possible effect on timing — and look after the bereaved, whose anger often carries guilt.
