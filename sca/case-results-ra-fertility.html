What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
In suspected inflammatory arthritis, serology is supportive, not decisive: around a third of people with rheumatoid arthritis are seronegative, and early erosions with raised inflammatory markers and clinical synovitis of the small joints make the diagnosis probable whatever the anti-CCP shows. NICE NG100 recommends urgent referral for persistent synovitis of unknown cause, especially if the small joints of the hands or feet or more than one joint are affected, and the quality standard is assessment within 3 weeks of referral — erosions at ten weeks make this even more pressing. The GP role is not to start disease-modifying treatment but to refer quickly, give honest information, manage symptoms safely and spot what will change specialist decisions. Here the key question is about pregnancy: NSAIDs may reduce fertility and are generally avoided in pregnancy, methotrexate and leflunomide are teratogenic, while hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine and some biologics are compatible with conception and pregnancy. Telling her that a pregnancy plan is part of rheumatology care — not an obstacle to it — turns fear into a plan.
