What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A persistent dry cough in someone on an ACE inhibitor should prompt a medicines timeline: ACE-inhibitor cough affects around 1 in 10 people, can begin weeks or months after starting or increasing the dose, and usually settles within 1–4 weeks of stopping (occasionally up to 3 months). After myocardial infarction, NICE NG185 recommends an ACE inhibitor and, for people who are intolerant, an angiotensin-II receptor blocker instead — a switch that can be made safely in primary care with monitoring of renal function, potassium and BP. Do not let a probable drug cause close the differential: a man over 40 with a cough who has smoked meets NICE NG12 criteria for a chest X-ray within 2 weeks. Side effects often cause silent non-adherence, here driven by work; asking about missed doses in a normalising way protects secondary prevention. Vocational drivers raise specific DVLA questions — after acute coronary syndrome Group 2 relicensing depends on functional testing, and cough syncope is a separate driving issue — so ask about faintness with coughing and give honest information about what must be reported.
