What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Heavy menstrual bleeding with small fibroids, a normal endometrium, a regular cycle and no intermenstrual/postcoital bleeding is a benign picture — fibroids are common and not cancer, and the normal lining is reassuring. Two clinical tasks are easily missed: actively treating the iron-deficiency anaemia (Hb 104, ferritin 8) that the patient has normalised into "just tired", and laying out the HMB options (NICE NG88 for fibroids of 3 cm or more: tranexamic/mefenamic acid, LNG-IUS, hormonal options, and consideration of specialist referral to discuss uterine artery embolisation, myomectomy or hysterectomy — LNG-IUS-first applies to no identified pathology or fibroids under 3 cm, and hormonal options are contraceptive, so they do not fit a wanted pregnancy; hysterectomy only as a last-resort choice) rather than jumping to surgery. Treat the anaemia per BSG 2021 (oral iron once daily, Hb recheck at about 4 weeks, coeliac serology). The examinable hidden agenda is a fertility fear: she has "coped" for years because she believed treatment meant a hysterectomy and the end of a possible baby with her new partner. Surfacing and dismantling that — fertility-sparing options exist, hysterectomy is a choice not an inevitability — restores agency and is the heart of the consultation. Confirm the benign/red-flag picture, safety-net for what would change management, and agree a shared, stepped plan.
