Best SCA preparation resources 2027, compared honestly

There is no shortage of SCA preparation on offer. This page compares the main types, from the RCGP’s own free material to courses and case banks, with fair pros and cons. We run gpreasoning.uk, so we say plainly where we fit and where we do not.

Last checked: October 2026
Disclosure: we run gpreasoning.uk. This page compares types of SCA preparation, including our own. We have tried to be fair, and we do not name or rate other companies. Read it with that in mind, and start with the free official material whatever else you use.

The short answer

The best preparation for most candidates is a mix: the RCGP's own material to understand the standard, a study group or supervisor to practise with real people and get honest feedback, and enough cases to build range. Courses and online banks can add structure and volume, but none replaces practising consultations aloud, regularly, with someone watching.

The main options compared

RCGP official materials

The SCA pages, the marking and results page, the consultation toolkit, the feedback statements with their educator notes, and the examination day guide.

  • For: Free, and written by the people who run the exam.
  • For: The only authoritative description of the domains and the standard.
  • For: The toolkit is designed to be used with a trainer to analyse your consultations.
  • Against: Not a case bank: you need cases from elsewhere to practise.
  • Against: Best used with a supervisor, which needs time to arrange.

Your trainer, deanery and VTS teaching

Joint surgeries, recorded consultations, tutorials and any SCA sessions your programme runs.

  • For: Feedback from experienced GPs who know you.
  • For: Real patients, not role play.
  • For: Usually free to you as part of training.
  • Against: Varies a lot between practices and schemes.
  • Against: Real surgeries do not run to a 12-minute exam rhythm.

Study groups with colleagues

Two to four trainees taking turns as doctor, patient and examiner.

  • For: Free, flexible, and the closest thing to live role play.
  • For: Marking someone else teaches you what examiners notice.
  • For: Keeps you accountable.
  • Against: Feedback depends on the group; peers can be too kind or too harsh.
  • Against: Needs reliable cases and a shared marking frame.

Group practice tools can supply cases and timing.

Commercial courses

One- or two-day courses, live or online, often with role players and examiner-style feedback.

  • For: Intensive practice with trained role players.
  • For: Structured feedback in a short time.
  • For: May be claimable from a study budget in some regions.
  • Against: Expensive compared with other options.
  • Against: A single course is a snapshot; skills need repetition afterwards.
  • Against: Quality varies; ask colleagues who have been.

See paying for SCA preparation for what study budgets may cover.

Online case banks and question banks (including ours)

Libraries of SCA-style cases, sometimes with AI patients, timers and self-marking.

  • For: Volume and range: many presentations and consultation types.
  • For: Practice at any hour, alone or with others.
  • For: Usually cheaper than a course.
  • Against: Self-marking and AI feedback are practice opinions, not the RCGP standard.
  • Against: Easy to read cases instead of practising them aloud.
  • Against: Cases are teaching material, not exam recalls.

How to choose

  1. Start with the RCGP. Read the marking page, toolkit and feedback statements before spending money.
  2. Find people. Book time with your supervisor and set up a study group. This is free and often the most useful thing you can do.
  3. Then add cases. Use free cases first to find your baseline, from us or elsewhere. Pay for a bank only if you need more range or volume.
  4. Consider a course for feedback. If you want intensive feedback from people outside your practice, a course may help, especially four to eight weeks before the exam.
  5. Check the budget rules. Study budget rules vary by region; check before you pay.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best resource for the SCA?
There is no single best resource. Most candidates do well with a mix: the RCGP's free official materials, practice with a supervisor and a study group, and a source of cases for range. Courses and online banks can add structure.
Are the RCGP SCA materials free?
Yes. The SCA pages, marking information, consultation toolkit, feedback statements and examination day guide are on the RCGP website at no cost.
Do I need to pay for an SCA course?
No. Many candidates pass using free material, their supervisor and a study group. A course can help if you want intensive feedback from people outside your practice. Check whether your study budget covers it first.
Is gpreasoning.uk independent of the RCGP?
Yes. We are not affiliated with or endorsed by the RCGP. Our cases are original teaching material, not exam recalls.
Is AI feedback reliable for the SCA?
It can help you spot habits, but it is a practice opinion, not the RCGP standard. Use it alongside feedback from people. See our AI simulator page for what AI marking can and cannot judge.
