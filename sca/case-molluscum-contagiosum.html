What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Molluscum contagiosum is a common, benign poxvirus infection in children, especially those with atopic eczema. Lesions are small, firm, pearly, dome-shaped papules with a central dimple, often in clusters on the trunk, limbs and flexures. It is self-limiting: individual lesions last weeks, and the infection usually clears within about 6–18 months, sometimes longer. NICE CKS advises that treatment is not routinely recommended in children: physical removal (cryotherapy, curettage) is painful, can scar and does not shorten the course, and topical products can irritate the skin, particularly in eczema. Eczema around lesions (molluscum dermatitis) is common and should be treated with emollients and a topical corticosteroid to inflamed skin (check the SPC/local formulary), which reduces itch, scratching and spread. Inflammation of individual lesions often heralds resolution. Red flags: lesions on the eyelid margin with conjunctivitis need referral to ophthalmology; also consider referral for very extensive lesions, suspected immune deficiency, or uncertainty. UKHSA guidance for education and childcare settings sets no exclusion for molluscum; practical measures are not sharing towels, not scratching or squeezing, and covering lesions for swimming if wished. A letter to the childcare provider is a legitimate, helpful action. Here the real challenge is a single working parent whose job depends on childcare, so acknowledging that pressure and solving the practical problem is central.
