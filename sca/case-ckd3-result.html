What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A CKD stage-3a result (eGFR ~52, stable, ACR normal/mildly raised) in a well 64-year-old is a RISK-COMMUNICATION consultation: the word "disease" in a letter, with no conversation, lands as a dialysis-and-death sentence — here amplified by a neighbour’s end-stage disease mapped onto herself. The task is to reframe ACCURATELY without minimising: CKD3a is common (age, BP, diabetes), usually stable, the large majority never need dialysis (they die with it, not of it), and at this stage it is largely a cardiovascular risk marker to act on. Lay out the protective levers (BP and diabetes control, ACE inhibitor/ARB where indicated — NICE NG203: with diabetes, if ACR is 3 mg/mmol or more — avoid NSAIDs, atorvastatin 20 mg per NICE NG238, dose review, periodic eGFR+ACR monitoring), address guilt without shaming, and know the genuine NICE NG203 referral criteria (5-year kidney failure risk over 5%, ACR 70 or more, ACR 30 or more with haematuria, a sustained significant eGFR fall, hypertension uncontrolled on 4 or more drugs, suspected rare or genetic cause) that distinguish her stable picture from those needing nephrology. Respond to the distress first, dismantle the neighbour-driven catastrophe, and safety-net (NSAID avoidance, sick-day guidance, monitoring) so she leaves reassured and engaged. eGFR 52 (CKD stage 3a) that is STABLE over a year with normal/mildly-raised ACR is common, usually age- and blood-pressure-related, and the large majority of people at this stage NEVER need dialysis and die WITH it, not OF it. The label "disease" frightens people far more than the mild, manageable reality. If reached: nobody explained the result before the letter; the term landed as a death sentence. The consultation is largely RISK COMMUNICATION — reframing a number into a manageable, mostly-stable condition without either minimising or terrifying.
