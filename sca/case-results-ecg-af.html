What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
New AF found incidentally needs a structured telephone consultation: confirm the ECG findings (irregularly irregular, no P waves, rate 112, no ischaemia), screen for features requiring emergency or same-day care (haemodynamic instability, chest pain, heart failure, syncope, stroke symptoms, onset under 48 hours if rhythm control is being considered), and make a shared anticoagulation decision. NICE uses CHA2DS2-VASc for stroke risk (anticoagulate if 2 or more; consider in men with 1) and ORBIT for bleeding risk, addressing modifiable bleeding risks (here aspirin without a vascular indication and over-the-counter NSAIDs). Offer a DOAC (apixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban or rivaroxaban) dosed by creatinine clearance, age and weight. Do not withhold anticoagulation on the basis of falls risk or age alone, and do not use aspirin for stroke prevention in AF. Rate control (beta-blocker or rate-limiting calcium-channel blocker) is first-line for most; arrange echocardiography; check TFT, U&E, FBC, LFT; consider triggers such as alcohol and sleep apnoea. DVLA: Group 1 drivers need not notify AF unless it causes distracting or disabling symptoms. Explain risk with natural frequencies and address personal fears, such as a relative’s bleed on warfarin and travel plans.
