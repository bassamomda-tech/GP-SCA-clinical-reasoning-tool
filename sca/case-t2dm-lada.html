What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
The classic SCA diabetes trap: a slim, young adult labelled "type 2" on the HbA1c alone who is actually insulin-deficient — LADA / late-onset type 1. The flags are unmistakable once looked for: slim build, unintentional weight loss, marked osmotic symptoms, no family history of T2DM, a viral prodrome, and rapid failure of oral agents. The danger is twofold: ongoing wrong treatment (a sulfonylurea that depends on the beta cells he is losing, plus dietary restriction that worsens catabolism and ketosis) and an evolving DKA that no one has screened for. Assess acute safety first (ketones, DKA features, low threshold to admit), get urgent specialist input, consider antibodies/C-peptide because the type is uncertain (NICE NG17: not routine, but considered when the type is in doubt) without delaying safety, and recognise he needs insulin not stronger tablets. The examinable hidden agenda is the blame: family and the initial diagnosis framed it as his dietary fault, and he is shamefully starving himself to atone. Lift that blame explicitly — autoimmune diabetes is not caused by eating badly — stop the self-starvation, and deliver the reframe with both compassion and urgency.
