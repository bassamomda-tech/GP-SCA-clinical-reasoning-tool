What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A sticky eye in a newborn is usually a benign blocked nasolacrimal duct / mild sticky eye (managed with cleaning and lacrimal-sac massage, resolving by ~12 months) — but the key task is to TRIAGE for OPHTHALMIA NEONATORUM (conjunctivitis in the first ~28 days). COPIOUS PURULENT discharge with marked LID SWELLING/redness (as here) is NOT a simple blocked duct and needs urgent assessment, swabs and treatment. GONOCOCCAL ophthalmia (the hyperacute picture; onset timing alone does not identify it) is hyperacute and SIGHT-THREATENING — a same-day emergency needing ophthalmology/paediatric assessment and systemic treatment; CHLAMYDIAL (now the commonest cause; onset timing alone does not identify it) is usually less acute but needs systemic treatment and signals a maternal STI. Take conjunctival swabs (including gonococcus/chlamydia), and handle the maternal-infection implication sensitively, confidentially and without blame (maternal/partner sexual-health follow-up). The examinable hidden agenda is family-reassurance minimisation ("just a blocked duct, don’t want to overreact") and possible STI-related shame; validate the family wisdom for the common case but act on the red flags here, arrange urgent assessment, and support an anxious new mother without blame or panic.
