What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
In AF with a CHA₂DS₂-VASc of 2 or more — here 3 — NICE NG196 says offer a DOAC, whatever the person’s sex, taking bleeding risk into account; anticoagulation substantially reduces the risk of a disabling/fatal stroke — the dominant risk — and the consultation is shared decision-making, not coercion (GMC Decision making and consent, 2020; Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board, 2015). Two misconceptions must be corrected: ASPIRIN is NOT effective for stroke prevention in AF (it gives false reassurance with its own bleeding risk), and modern DOACs are a genuinely different proposition from the warfarin that may haunt a patient’s family memory (no routine INR, fewer interactions, generally lower intracranial-bleed risk). Put the bleeding risk in proportion (it is usually far outweighed by the stroke benefit at high CHA₂DS₂-VASc), address modifiable bleeding factors, and counter the commonly over-stated falls-risk argument. The examinable hidden agenda is TRAUMA: witnessing his father bleed to death on warfarin, so "I’d rather take my chances" is fear, not informed choice — acknowledge it with compassion. Offer the empathic reframe that a stroke causes exactly the dependency he fears, so anticoagulation protects his independence. Respect his autonomy to decline after a genuinely informed discussion, provide a decision aid, and keep the door open with follow-up.
