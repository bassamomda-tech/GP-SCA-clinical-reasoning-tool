What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
Interpreting a "tiredness panel" means reading the GESTALT, not handing over the single number the patient googled: a raised ALT with normal bilirubin and near-normal ALP is a HEPATOCELLULAR pattern, and when it sits with a high BMI, a pre-diabetic HbA1c and raised triglycerides, the most likely cause is NON-ALCOHOLIC FATTY LIVER DISEASE (NAFLD/MASLD) — a marker of insulin resistance and cardiometabolic risk, not a trivial finding, and the fatigue is far more likely multifactorial than caused by vitamin D alone. Do not diagnose NAFLD by assumption: exclude the alternatives with a focused liver screen — alcohol (quantified honestly, using AST:ALT), viral hepatitis B/C, haemochromatosis (ferritin + transferrin saturation), ± autoimmune and drug causes. In NAFLD the number that matters is FIBROSIS risk, not the ALT: use a non-invasive score (FIB-4 ± ELF or transient elastography) to identify the minority needing hepatology, managing the low-risk majority in primary care. Treat the drivers — weight loss (7–10% per international EASL guidance; no UK figure), activity, alcohol reduction, and pre-diabetes/lipid/cardiovascular risk — and replace the vitamin D proportionately without pretending it explains everything. The hidden agenda is the wish for a single quick fix that sidesteps the harder metabolic conversation; engage without lecturing and avoid over-medicalising. The duty is correct interpretation, fibrosis risk and lifestyle, not a cancer referral.
