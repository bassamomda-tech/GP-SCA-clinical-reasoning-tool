What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
TINEA PEDIS (athlete’s foot) is a dermatophyte fungal infection recognised by itchy, flaky, MACERATED/cracked skin between the toes (especially the 4th web space), scaling in a "moccasin" distribution on the soles, and odour, provoked by sweating, occlusive footwear and communal showers/gyms. Distinguish it from contact dermatitis/eczema, erythrasma (coral-red under Wood’s lamp) and pitted keratolysis, using skin scrapings for mycology if uncertain or before oral therapy. Check for TINEA UNGUIUM (onychomycosis — thickened/discoloured/crumbly nails), which is a reservoir for recurrent infection, and for tinea elsewhere, sampling nails before oral treatment. Manage with a topical ANTIFUNGAL for the full licensed course (terbinafine 1% cream once daily for 1 week for tinea pedis per its SPC; clotrimazole for its longer licensed duration), asking how previous creams were used, plus foot HYGIENE — keep feet dry, dry thoroughly between the toes, change socks and avoid occlusive footwear, use antifungal powder, avoid sharing towels and wear footwear in communal showers — and consider an ORAL antifungal for extensive/refractory disease or nail involvement (after sampling; toenails usually 12 weeks of terbinafine, 12–16 weeks per BAD 2014, with baseline LFTs). Importantly, ask about DIABETES or immunocompromise: in these people, tinea pedis/interdigital maceration is a portal for bacterial entry and CELLULITIS, so foot care, prompt treatment and vigilance for secondary infection matter more and the threshold for review is lower. Reassure, address recurrence (nail reservoir/hygiene), and safety-net for secondary infection (NICE NG19 foot checks in diabetes).
