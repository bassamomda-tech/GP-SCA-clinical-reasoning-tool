What this case tests

Show the learning points (spoiler: read after you have practised)
A guarded "tell my mum I’m fine" teenager seen alone is a confidentiality-and-risk consultation. The hinge is establishing confidentiality and its limits explicitly and early, in age-appropriate language — only then will disclosure follow. Conduct an explicit, sensitive risk assessment (ask directly about self-harm method/frequency and about suicidal thoughts, plan, intent, means, and protective factors — asking does not plant ideas), assess Gillick competence (decision-specific; Fraser guidelines apply to contraception advice, not to confidentiality in mental health), avoid risk tools, scales and low/medium/high labels (NICE NG225), and screen the psychosocial picture (HEEADSSS) to surface the real triggers — here online bullying and questioning her sexuality, with terror of being outed. Respond to self-harm by exploring its function (emotion regulation) without shame or alarm, not by simply demanding it stop. Make a proportionate plan (NICE NG225: referral for psychosocial assessment with consent, safety-planning; CAMHS/early help, school/bullying support, crisis routes, follow-up; NICE NG134: psychological therapy first-line for low mood), and judge parental involvement by risk and competence — negotiating any disclosure WITH her wherever safe. The marks are won by ethics held under genuine warmth.
